The basic idea

Preserve the normative problem without turning it into a fake answer key.

SACRE begins with a Scenario and source-tagged Policy candidates. Bioethics Bench creates reusable versions of those objects so the same normative material can be inspected, rerun, compared across evaluators, and revised without silently changing what is being analyzed.

01

A Scenario

A sufficiently specified practical ethical situation. Facts and assumptions are made explicit where different hidden assumptions would change the analysis.

02

Natural Policy positions

The resource recovers the substantively distinct positions that the relevant sources actually support. It does not require every case to have the same number of candidates.

03

Visible provenance

Sources, reasoning bridges, editorial stipulations, versioning, hashes, and review history remain inspectable rather than disappearing behind the final text.

From Bench to SACRE

The resource supplies the represented normative material. SACRE supplies the procedure.

Keeping those layers separate matters. Bioethics Bench does not contain the morally correct answer. It preserves Scenarios and candidate positions well enough for SACRE, human evaluators, or other methods to study their relationships.

STEP 1

Scenario

A stable representation of the normative situation enters the analysis.

STEP 2

Policy candidates

Public, Expert, and Framework positions enter with source identity preserved.

STEPS 3–4

Convergence

QCCS measures cross-source normative relations across the represented field.

STEP 5

Coherence

Declared aggregation yields an inspectable ranking and provisional Final Policy.

STEP 6

RE-Iteration

Selected positions can be revised and the complete analysis run again with lineage preserved.

The completion standard

Good citations are necessary. Faithful normative representation is another task.

A case can have excellent provenance and still be a weak computational object if distinct source positions have been merged into the same cautious formulation, or if candidates were created merely to fill a preferred geometry.

Source diversity ≠ Policy diversity. Different source classes can genuinely converge, and that convergence should remain. But the construction process must not manufacture symmetry, compress distinct positions, or make an editorial framing question more authoritative than the sources themselves.
Source fidelityDoes the source actually warrant the represented Policy position?
Natural distinctnessHave materially different positions been preserved rather than merged or paraphrased?
Comparable abstractionAre cross-source relations meaningful rather than comparisons of unlike granularities?
Scenario adequacyAre enough facts fixed for interpretation without stipulating away the normative tension?
Natural geometryCandidate counts follow the evidence rather than a mandatory 2 × 2 × 2 template.
Revision valueFor demonstration cases, can RE-Iteration alter a substantive normative relation rather than merely polish wording?
