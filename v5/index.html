Bioethics cases, with the positions people actually hold and where each one comes from.

Every case sets out a decision someone really has to make — whether to keep a feeding tube, who gets a funded IVF cycle, when a clinician may refuse. Alongside it sit the serious positions on that decision: what surveyed publics want, what professional bodies recommend, and what ethical frameworks imply. Each position says which source it came from, and how directly.

It exists so that reasoning about these cases — by people or by machines — can be compared against something stable and checkable, instead of against an answer key nobody agrees on.

How a case is built

Three things, kept separate on purpose.

The decision

A real situation, with the facts that matter stated and held fixed — so that two people disagreeing are disagreeing about the ethics, not about what happened.

The positions

The serious answers, not a spread of plausible-sounding options. Where a professional body and a surveyed public genuinely differ, both appear. Where they agree, we do not invent a disagreement.

The sources

Each position names what it came from, and says whether the source states that policy outright or whether we drew it out — because those are not the same kind of claim.

Where this currently stands. The cases are a release candidate. Every source identifier has been checked, and every position claiming a source states its policy outright has been read against that source. What has not happened is independent review by someone outside the project. Until it does, treat these as carefully built research material rather than settled reference material — and the review record says exactly what has and has not been checked.
