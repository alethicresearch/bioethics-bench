# M085 — First-in-human high-risk gene/cell therapy

**Case-family identity:** M085  
**Primary domain:** translational research / early-phase trials  
**Tags:** first in human, gene therapy, cell therapy, irreversible risk, therapeutic misconception, preclinical evidence  
**Research status:** deep case draft  
**Evidence date:** 2026-08-27

## Decision architecture

**Decision-maker:** research ethics committee and regulator.  
**Decision question:** When should a first-in-human gene/cell intervention proceed when preclinical evidence is promising but the intervention may persist or cause irreversible harms?

## Scenario pair

**Concise:** Adults with a severe progressive disease have no curative standard therapy. A gene-modified cell intervention has a plausible mechanism, replicated preclinical efficacy and an acceptable manufacturing program, but it has never been given to humans and may persist for years. The first cohort would receive a low staged dose with intensive monitoring and long-term follow-up. The committee must decide whether the evidence and unmet need justify cautious first-in-human testing, whether stronger preclinical replication should be required before any exposure, or whether enrollment should be limited to patients with the most severe disease and no reasonable alternatives.

**Detailed:** A translational program proposes first-in-human administration of a gene-modified cell product for a severe progressive disorder. Existing therapy slows disease but does not cure it. The product has a biologically plausible mechanism, replicated efficacy in disease-relevant models and manufacturing controls designed for clinical use. Important uncertainty remains about immune effects, off-target biological consequences, cell persistence and late toxicity. The protocol uses a small sentinel cohort, staged dosing, stopping rules, independent data monitoring, enhanced consent aimed at therapeutic misconception and long-term follow-up. The committee must decide whether the current evidence and unmet need justify initiation; whether more preclinical evidence is required because the harms may be irreversible; or whether first exposure should be confined to the most severely affected patients who lack reasonable alternatives.

## Evidence architecture

ISSCR 2025 requires compelling scientific rationale, systematic appraisal of preclinical evidence, rigorous manufacturing, independent review, risk minimization, appropriate subject selection, enhanced early-phase consent and long-term monitoring where interventions persist. Trial initiation must be justified by unmet need and credible evidence, not mere technical ability. These provisions form one risk-adapted translational architecture.

## Serious policies

- proceed under sentinel/staged first-in-human oversight;
- require stronger preclinical evidence before initiation;
- limit first exposure to highest-need/no-alternative patients.

## Frameworks

- **Responsible innovation:** severe unmet need can justify carefully controlled human uncertainty once preclinical evidence is credible.
- **Precaution/irreversibility:** irreversible interventions demand a higher evidentiary threshold than ordinary early-phase drugs.
- **Fair subject selection:** using the sickest can reduce opportunity cost but may increase vulnerability and biological risk; selection should track risk-benefit rather than desperation alone.

## Construction risks

Do not make “no alternatives” synonymous with valid consent. Do not promise therapeutic benefit. Preserve long-term follow-up and staged dosing. Do not split components of the ISSCR architecture into fake expert candidates.

## Executable judgment

`research-complete-not-executable`. The case is strong, but present professional guidance is one conditional risk-adapted architecture and direct participant policy evidence on trial-initiation thresholds is inadequate.
