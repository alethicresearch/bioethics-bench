# M081 — Publication of negative and inconclusive trial results

**Case-family identity:** M081  
**Primary domain:** research governance / publication ethics  
**Tags:** trial reporting, negative results, publication bias, transparency, sponsor obligations  
**Research status:** deep case draft  
**Evidence date:** 2026-08-27

## Decision architecture

**Decision-maker:** research institution and commercial sponsor.  
**Decision question:** What public-reporting policy should apply when a completed human trial is negative, null, inconclusive or commercially unfavorable?

## Concise representation

A completed randomized clinical trial fails to show the sponsor's product is superior to control. The study is scientifically interpretable, contains no unexpected safety crisis and was prospectively registered. A journal article would require additional analysis and staff time, while a registry results summary can be posted promptly. The institution and sponsor must decide whether all completed trial results should be made public promptly through a registry with journal publication pursued when scientifically useful, whether every interpretable trial should also be submitted as a full manuscript within a defined period, or whether sponsors may choose manuscript detail and timing so long as complete core results are publicly disclosed.

## Detailed representation

A multicenter randomized trial sponsored by a company is complete. The primary efficacy endpoint is negative and several secondary endpoints are inconclusive. The trial was appropriately designed, sufficiently enrolled and prospectively registered. There is no reason to retract the study as invalid. The sponsor worries that a full journal manuscript will consume resources and attract unfavorable commercial attention but accepts that registry reporting is required. Investigators argue that complete negative evidence is necessary to reduce publication bias and prevent future patients, researchers and decision-makers from relying on a distorted evidence base. The institution must set policy on the minimum form and timing of public disclosure and on whether journal publication itself should be mandatory.

## Critical facts

- completed interpretable human trial;
- negative/inconclusive rather than fraudulent or invalid;
- prospectively registered;
- core results can be publicly posted promptly;
- manuscript preparation has real but modest cost;
- sponsor has commercial interests.

## Evidence

Declaration of Helsinki 2024 ¶36 requires negative and inconclusive as well as positive results to be published or otherwise made publicly available, with timely, complete and accurate reporting. CIOMS Guideline 24 likewise requires public availability and says sponsors must not suppress unwelcome findings. WHO/funder joint statements require timely registration/results disclosure. Trial participants strongly value receiving study results, including negative results, but participant evidence does not clearly distinguish registry disclosure from mandatory journal publication.

## Serious policies

- prompt registry/public disclosure of all completed results, with manuscript publication where scientifically useful;
- mandatory timely manuscript submission plus registry reporting for every interpretable trial;
- sponsor discretion over manuscript form/timing after complete core public disclosure.

## Frameworks

- **Public accountability:** participants accepted burdens to create generalizable knowledge → all findings must enter the public evidence base.
- **Proportional dissemination:** ethical duty is complete accessible evidence, not necessarily a journal article for every null study → require timely public results while allowing reasonable format discretion.

## Construction risks

Do not create suppression/nonpublication as a serious professional candidate. Distinguish public availability from journal publication. Do not let commercial inconvenience count as scientific invalidity.

## Executable judgment

`research-complete-not-executable` for now. Professional guidance strongly converges on public disclosure; remaining disagreement concerns format/detail rather than a sufficiently deep source-class policy split.

## Sources

WMA Declaration of Helsinki 2024 ¶36; CIOMS Guideline 24; WHO Joint Statement on Public Disclosure of Clinical Trial Results; adult trial-participant result-reporting studies including PMID 27559022.
