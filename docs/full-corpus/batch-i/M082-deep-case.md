# M082 — Cluster-randomized public-health trial and community authorization

**Case-family identity:** M082  
**Primary domain:** research ethics / cluster trials  
**Tags:** cluster randomized trial, consent, gatekeeper, community engagement, waiver  
**Research status:** deep case draft  
**Evidence date:** 2026-08-27

## Decision architecture

**Decision-maker:** research ethics committee reviewing a school-level cluster trial.  
**Decision question:** What combination of individual consent, gatekeeper permission and community engagement is required when a low-risk public-health intervention is assigned to whole schools?

## Scenario pair

**Concise:** Twenty schools will be randomized to two evidence-based approaches for increasing routine adolescent vaccination. The intervention changes school reminder/outreach procedures; no student is required to receive vaccination, and individual clinical consent rules remain unchanged. Researchers will use de-identified aggregate uptake data and a brief optional student survey. Requiring prior consent from every family before a school can receive its assigned outreach strategy would make cluster randomization infeasible. School leaders can authorize institutional participation and parent/student representatives can be consulted. The ethics committee must decide what consent and community-authorization process is required.

**Detailed:** A public-health research team proposes a pragmatic cluster-randomized trial in twenty secondary schools comparing two lawful, evidence-based reminder and scheduling systems intended to improve access to routine adolescent vaccination. The school-level intervention changes communications and clinic logistics, not whether an individual student is vaccinated; ordinary consent for vaccination is preserved. Primary outcomes use routinely collected de-identified school-level uptake data. A separate optional survey asks identifiable students about communication acceptability and therefore can obtain individual consent/assent. Requiring advance individual permission from every student/parent merely for the school to receive its assigned reminder system would make the cluster intervention infeasible. Each school has a legitimate administrative gatekeeper, and parent/student/community consultation is feasible.

## Evidence architecture

The Ottawa Statement provides a layered rule: individual informed consent is required for identifiable individual research procedures unless a valid waiver applies; gatekeepers cannot give proxy consent for individuals; gatekeepers may legitimately authorize cluster participation where organizational interests are affected; cluster/community consultation is recommended. Waivers are limited by feasibility and low-risk conditions.

## Serious policies

- layered rule: school permission + community consultation for the cluster intervention, individual consent for optional identifiable research procedures;
- require individual permission for all research-associated exposure wherever feasible;
- waive individual consent for the low-risk school-level intervention while preserving public notice/opt-out or consent for individual procedures.

## Frameworks

- **Individual autonomy:** cluster assignment does not erase personal research rights.
- **Collective legitimacy/proportionality:** organizational interventions sometimes cannot be meaningfully authorized person-by-person; legitimate gatekeeper authority and community engagement can govern cluster-level interests while waivers remain risk-limited.

## Construction risks

Do not let a gatekeeper consent on behalf of individuals. Distinguish the cluster intervention from vaccination itself and from the optional survey. Do not treat waiver, gatekeeper permission and consultation as interchangeable.

## Executable judgment

`needs-additional-evidence`. The professional layer is one integrated layered architecture; direct student/parent/community evidence comparing the alternative authorization rules is insufficient.

## Sources

Ottawa Statement on the Ethical Design and Conduct of Cluster Randomized Trials; CIOMS guidance on consent waivers/community engagement; cluster-trial ethics literature.
