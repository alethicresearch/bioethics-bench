# M083 — Return of individual research findings

**Case-family identity:** M083  
**Primary domain:** genomic research / participant obligations  
**Tags:** individual research results, actionable findings, validity, right not to know, return of results  
**Research status:** deep case draft  
**Evidence date:** 2026-08-27

## Decision architecture

**Decision-maker:** genomic research program and IRB.  
**Decision question:** When research sequencing identifies a clinically valid, potentially actionable finding unrelated to the main study question, what should investigators offer the participant?

## Scenario pair

**Concise:** An adult research participant consented to genome sequencing for a cardiovascular study and indicated willingness to be contacted about health-relevant findings. Researchers identify an unrelated pathogenic variant associated with a serious condition for which established surveillance can substantially reduce risk. The result is analytically reliable but was generated in a research laboratory and requires clinical confirmation before medical action. The program must decide whether to offer confirmed actionable findings, offer a broader menu of valid individual findings according to participant preference, or restrict return to urgent/high-actionability findings while leaving lower-value findings within the research record.

**Detailed:** An adult with capacity participates in a longitudinal genomic research study. The consent process asked about return preferences but did not promise that every individual result would be returned. Sequencing identifies a pathogenic variant unrelated to the research question that is associated with a serious condition for which established surveillance or preventive care can materially reduce morbidity. The analytical result is sufficiently reliable to justify confirmatory testing but should not be used clinically without confirmation. The participant previously expressed interest in health-relevant results and can receive genetic counseling. The program must choose whether to offer clinically confirmed actionable findings as a routine obligation; whether to offer participants a broader preference-based menu of valid findings that may have personal value even if actionability is limited; or whether to return only urgent/high-actionability results where the expected participant value clearly outweighs uncertainty, burden and cost.

## Evidence architecture

National Academies 2018 recommends study-specific planning, stronger justification as participant value and feasibility rise, return of urgent clinically actionable results, and avoidance of uninterpretable/invalid results. NIH is actively developing a broader return-of-results policy in 2026. Participant evidence is exceptionally broad: a systematic review of 221 studies and 118,874 stakeholders found overwhelming interest in return, particularly reliable/actionable findings; Jackson/Framingham cohorts found 88–92% wanted secondary genomic findings; Project Baseline and newer eMERGE work show heterogeneity in breadth and format, with a minority opting out.

## Serious policies

- routinely offer clinically confirmed actionable findings with informed choice;
- offer a broader participant-selected menu of valid findings, including some non-actionable but meaningful information;
- restrict routine return to urgent/high-actionability results and decide other findings study-by-study.

## Frameworks

- **Respect/reciprocity:** participants contribute data and may reasonably value information learned about themselves → offer meaningful valid results according to preference.
- **Clinical responsibility/epistemic restraint:** research findings can be uncertain and resource-intensive → return should track validity, actionability and potential participant value rather than a blanket right to all data.

## Construction risks

Do not equate raw research data with clinically interpreted findings. Preserve participant opt-out. Do not use overwhelming interest in receiving results as evidence for one particular institutional default.

## Executable judgment

`needs-additional-evidence` pending candidate audit. Public evidence strongly favors return but may not furnish two action-distinct institutional rules; professional guidance is deliberately study-specific rather than a simple universal return policy.
