# M084 — Sponsor control over publication

**Case-family identity:** M084  
**Primary domain:** research governance / academic independence  
**Tags:** sponsor control, publication, clinical trial agreement, IP, data access, academic freedom  
**Research status:** deep case draft  
**Evidence date:** 2026-08-27

## Decision architecture

**Decision-maker:** academic institution negotiating a commercial clinical-trial agreement.  
**Decision question:** What prepublication review or delay rights may a commercial sponsor receive without compromising investigators' duty to analyze and publish trial results independently?

## Scenario pair

**Concise:** A company will fund a multicenter clinical trial at an academic medical center. Its proposed contract gives investigators full access to study data but requires manuscripts to be sent to the sponsor before submission. The sponsor requests the right to remove confidential proprietary information, delay publication long enough to file patents, and coordinate the first multicenter publication. The institution must decide whether to allow only a short defined review/delay, allow a longer sponsor-coordinated first-publication period with a firm investigator publication backstop, or reject any agreement that gives the sponsor power to block, rewrite or indefinitely delay unfavorable results.

**Detailed:** An academic medical center is negotiating a trial agreement with a company developing a new treatment. Investigators will participate in analysis and must retain meaningful access to supporting data. The sponsor has legitimate confidential manufacturing information and possible patentable discoveries and therefore asks to review manuscripts before submission. It also wants to coordinate publication across multiple sites. The institution will not accept a sponsor veto over whether results can be published or contractual language allowing the sponsor to rewrite scientific interpretation. The remaining policy question is how much finite review/delay is acceptable: a short review period limited to identifying confidential information and patent needs; a longer defined sponsor first-publication/coordination window with an automatic investigator right to publish afterward; or essentially no sponsor delay beyond removal of genuine confidential material.

## Evidence architecture

CIOMS Guideline 24 says sponsors must not prevent publication of unwelcome findings and researchers should not enter agreements unduly interfering with data access, independent analysis, manuscript preparation or publication. ICMJE requires authors to have access to supporting data and disclosure of publication restrictions. A survey of 107 US academic centers found >85% rejected sponsor powers to revise manuscripts or decide whether results are published, while almost all accepted limited prepublication review, commonly 60–90 days. Germany's 2025 standard clinical-trial clauses permit sponsor first-publication coordination and defined review periods with investigator publication rights after deadlines.

## Serious policies

- short sponsor review for confidential/IP issues only;
- defined longer first-publication coordination period with automatic investigator publication backstop;
- no sponsor publication delay beyond immediate removal of genuine confidential material.

## Frameworks

- **Scientific independence/public accountability:** trial evidence belongs in the public knowledge base and investigators must be able to stand behind analysis → sponsor veto or indefinite delay is impermissible.
- **Reciprocal commercial fairness:** legitimate patent/confidentiality interests can justify a finite review period when they do not suppress or distort findings.

## Construction risks

Do not include sponsor suppression as a serious option. Distinguish limited delay from veto. Preserve investigator data access. Do not make a single numerical delay universal across jurisdictions.

## Executable judgment

`research-complete-not-executable` for now. Professional/institutional evidence converges strongly against suppression and supports bounded review; the remaining duration question is not supported by a direct affected-public candidate layer.
