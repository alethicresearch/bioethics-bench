# M062 — Intrafamilial gamete donation or gestational carrying

**Case-family identity:** M062  
**Featured crosswalk:** none  
**Primary domain:** reproductive ethics / third-party reproduction  
**Subdomain:** intrafamilial donation; family coercion  
**Tags:** gamete donation, gestational carrier, family, coercion, kinship, counseling  
**Research status:** deep case draft  
**Evidence date:** 2026-08-27

## 1. Decision architecture

**Decision-maker:** assisted-reproduction program.  
**Decision question:** When should a fertility program accept an adult family member as a gamete donor or gestational carrier?

The benchmark concerns voluntary non-consanguineous arrangements between adults. It excludes arrangements that would create consanguineous reproduction or simulate incestuous unions.

## 2. Concise representation

An adult patient's sister voluntarily offers to donate oocytes so the patient can have a genetically related child. Both adults report a close relationship and deny family pressure. The donor would receive independent counseling, medical screening and legal advice, and the resulting child would be told the donor's identity at a developmentally appropriate time. The fertility program must decide whether intrafamilial donation should be permitted under enhanced independent safeguards, restricted to cases in which unrelated donation is unavailable or impracticable, or generally declined because family obligations create unacceptable risks of coercion and role confusion.

## 3. Detailed representation

A fertility program receives a request for an adult sister to donate oocytes to her infertile sibling. The genetic and gestational relationships would not be consanguineous and the arrangement would not simulate sexual reproduction between relatives. The prospective donor initiated the offer and both siblings report that she is free to withdraw. The program can provide separate medical and psychological assessment, independent legal counseling, private interviews to assess pressure, and counseling about disclosure and future family roles. Longitudinal research on some sister-to-sister egg-donation families suggests that family roles can remain stable and relationships can be positive, but evidence is limited and family expectations can create subtle pressure even without overt coercion. The program must choose whether to permit adult intrafamilial donation under enhanced safeguards; restrict it to cases where unrelated donation is unavailable or would impose major barriers; or generally decline intrafamilial donation because family obligation, lineage complexity and long-term relational risks are judged too difficult to manage reliably.

## 4. Decision-critical facts

- all participants are competent adults;
- arrangement is non-consanguineous and does not simulate incest;
- donor initiated the offer and may withdraw;
- independent counseling/legal assessment is available;
- donor identity will not be hidden from the family indefinitely;
- question is program acceptance, not legal parentage.

## 5. Explicit uncertainty

Subtle family pressure may be difficult to detect, and long-term child/family outcomes are incompletely studied. Positive experience in some families does not establish universal safety.

## 6. Jurisdiction and time strategy

Jurisdiction-neutral clinic policy with legality stipulated.

## 7. Benchmark stipulations

- For this benchmark, assume the arrangement is legally available and non-consanguineous.
- For this benchmark, assume independent private counseling and legal advice are feasible.
- For this benchmark, assume the donor may withdraw before use of donated material without family penalty imposed by the clinic.

## 8. Serious policy / position families

- permit intrafamilial arrangements with enhanced independent safeguards;
- allow only when unrelated donation is unavailable/impracticable;
- generally decline because family-pressure/role risks are too difficult to neutralize.

## 9. Public / affected-community evidence

Longitudinal UK research on a small group of families using sisters or sisters-in-law as egg donors found generally positive recipient relationships and stable social roles through childhood. The evidence is reassuring but small. Other intrafamilial reproductive arrangements remain sparsely studied. This does not support a clean public-policy split.

## 10. Expert / professional recommendations

ASRM's 2024 Ethics Committee opinion says adult intrafamilial gamete donation and gestational carrying are generally ethically acceptable when informed and noncoercive; consanguineous/incest-simulating arrangements should be prohibited, and intergenerational arrangements may require particular caution. Programs should invest additional counseling time and assess undue influence. This is one strong conditional professional architecture.

## 11. Normative / framework positions and reasoning bridges

- **Relational autonomy / family solidarity:** family relationships can support meaningful voluntary assistance rather than undermine autonomy → with private safeguards and withdrawal rights, kinship is not itself disqualifying → permit enhanced-review arrangements.
- **Least-coercive means:** family obligation can be subtle and costly to resist → when an unrelated donor is reasonably available, avoid exposing relatives to those pressures → reserve intrafamilial arrangements for substantial need.
- **Role-boundary precaution:** reproductive kinship can restructure family identity for donors, recipients and children across decades → some relational risks cannot be screened away at consent → decline as a program policy.

## 13. Construction and representation risks

- treating kinship as proof of coercion;
- treating reported voluntariness as proof coercion is impossible;
- conflating non-consanguineous donation with incest;
- assuming donor/child roles must become confused;
- using very small positive studies as general outcome guarantees.
