# M068 — Transfer of an embryo known to carry a serious monogenic condition

**Case-family identity:** M068  
**Featured crosswalk:** none  
**Primary domain:** reproductive genetics / embryo transfer  
**Subdomain:** affected embryo transfer; child welfare; reproductive liberty  
**Tags:** PGT-M, affected embryo, reproductive autonomy, disability, child welfare, IVF  
**Research status:** deep case draft  
**Evidence date:** 2026-08-27

## 1. Decision architecture

**Decision-maker:** fertility-clinic policy committee.  
**Decision question:** When an embryo is known to carry a serious but treatable/manageable monogenic condition, should a fertility clinic honor a patient's request for transfer?

The case deliberately avoids a lethal early-debility condition, for which current ASRM guidance is much less permissive. It also stipulates no unaffected embryo is available.

## 2. Concise representation

A patient undergoing IVF with PGT-M has one remaining embryo. The embryo is highly likely to result in a child with a serious inherited condition that requires lifelong medical management but is treatable, compatible with meaningful functioning and not expected to cause severe early debility. No unaffected embryo is available. The patient understands the prognosis and says this embryo represents the only remaining opportunity for a genetically related child. The clinic must decide whether to honor the transfer request after specialist counseling, decline transfer because clinicians should not knowingly facilitate avoidable health burdens in a future child, or use a threshold policy that permits transfer for manageable conditions but declines when expected harm crosses a defined severe-early-debility boundary.

## 3. Detailed representation

A fertility clinic has completed PGT-M for a patient at risk of transmitting a monogenic disorder. The only remaining embryo carries a pathogenic variant with high penetrance. For this benchmark, assume the expected phenotype is serious and requires lifelong treatment and monitoring, but established therapies allow many affected people to attain meaningful function and substantial life expectancy; the condition is not expected to produce severe early debility with no reasonable function. The patient has received genetic counseling and consultation with a specialist familiar with the condition, understands the likely burdens and uncertainty, and has no unaffected embryos remaining. The patient says another IVF cycle is not realistically feasible and transfer of this embryo is the only remaining opportunity for a genetically related child. The clinic must choose whether to honor the transfer request; adopt a policy declining transfer of embryos expected to produce serious avoidable disease even when manageable; or use a severity-threshold policy that permits transfer for treatable/manageable conditions but refuses requests only when a future child is highly likely to face life-threatening severe early debility with no reasonable function.

## 4. Decision-critical facts

- condition is serious but treatable/manageable;
- phenotype is highly likely rather than merely uncertain;
- no unaffected embryo is available;
- transfer is the patient's only realistic remaining path to a genetically related child;
- counseling and condition-specific specialist review have occurred;
- case is below the severe-early-debility/no-reasonable-function threshold.

## 5. Explicit uncertainty

Phenotypic severity can vary even with a highly penetrant condition, and predictions about future quality of life can embed disability bias. “Manageable” does not mean low burden.

## 6. Jurisdiction and time strategy

Jurisdiction-neutral clinic ethics, evidence-dated 2026. The benchmark assumes all represented clinic policies are legally available.

## 7. Benchmark stipulations

- For this benchmark, assume no unaffected embryo remains and another IVF cycle is not realistically feasible.
- For this benchmark, assume the condition is serious but treatable/manageable and compatible with meaningful functioning.
- For this benchmark, assume informed genetic and disease-specific counseling has occurred.

## 8. Serious policy / position families

- honor transfer after counseling for a manageable condition;
- decline transfer of any embryo highly likely to result in a serious avoidable disorder;
- use a severity threshold: permit manageable disease, decline only severe early life-threatening debility with no reasonable function.

## 9. Public / affected-community evidence

Direct empirical evidence from patients making this rare request is limited. ASRM notes that requests often arise when no unaffected embryos are available, when patients accept raising a child with the condition, or when the condition is part of their own/family lived experience. Disability-community perspectives caution against treating a diagnosis as equivalent to an unacceptable life. However, current evidence is not strong enough to generate two direct affected-public institutional policies without further research.

## 10. Expert / professional recommendations

ASRM's 2025 Ethics Committee opinion provides unusually genuine professional pluralism. For conditions that are treatable or effectively manageable, it says physicians may ethically transfer affected embryos on patient request, and refusals can also fall within appropriate ethical boundaries if applied without discrimination. For embryos highly likely to cause life-threatening severe early debility with no possibility of reasonable function, transfer is highly discouraged. Clinics should publish policies before treatment and provide genetic/specialist counseling. These are action-distinct professional positions, not paraphrases.

## 11. Normative / framework positions and reasoning bridges

- **Reproductive liberty / disability equality:** requiring ART patients to meet a genetic-health standard not imposed on fertile parents can be discriminatory → when the future life is expected to be worth living and the patient understands burdens, honor transfer.
- **Procreative beneficence / nonmaleficence:** clinicians need not participate in knowingly creating avoidable serious disease when they control embryo transfer → decline transfer even if the expected child could have a meaningful life.
- **Harm-threshold pluralism:** professional authority to refuse should rise with the magnitude and certainty of harm, not diagnosis alone → permit manageable conditions but decline only above a severe-harm threshold.

## 13. Construction and representation risks

- treating disability as equivalent to a life not worth living;
- choosing a condition label without specifying expected phenotype;
- hiding the absence/presence of unaffected embryos;
- turning physician refusal into a claim that the resulting child would necessarily be harmed by existence;
- creating public candidates from theoretical positions without affected evidence.
