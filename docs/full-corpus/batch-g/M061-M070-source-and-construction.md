# Bioethics Bench — Batch G source and construction packet (M061–M070)

**Evidence date:** 2026-08-27  
**Scope:** assisted reproduction governance, reproductive cloning, ectogenesis/artificial-womb technology, HLA-matched embryo selection, prenatal sequencing, fertility preservation for minors, affected-embryo transfer, ART error disclosure, and scarce gene-therapy allocation.

## M061 — Compensation in gestational-carrier arrangements

**Decision object:** fertility-program policy on whether and how gestational carriers may be compensated while preserving carrier autonomy and avoiding undue inducement/exploitation.

**Professional evidence:** ASRM 2023 concludes gestational-carrier arrangements and financial compensation can be ethically permissible if carriers remain sole decision-makers for their medical care, receive independent legal counsel and psychological/medical counseling, have appropriate health coverage, and compensation is for time, risk and inconvenience rather than contingent on birth of a healthy child.

**Affected evidence:** recent carrier survey (n=28) found largely positive experiences; most reported primarily altruistic motives, while 25% also considered compensation important. Larger US carrier studies document varied screening, compensation and outcomes.

**Likely policies:** permit reasonable compensation plus independent safeguards; permit reimbursement only/no additional compensation; allow compensation but cap or structure it to reduce undue inducement.

**Risk:** carrier experience evidence does not automatically support the reimbursement-only policy; do not make intended parents' preferences stand in for carrier interests.

## M062 — Intrafamilial gamete donation or gestational carrying

**Decision object:** whether programs should accept adult family-member donation/carrier arrangements and what enhanced safeguards should apply.

**Professional evidence:** ASRM 2024 says adult intrafamilial gamete donation/gestational carrying is generally ethically acceptable when fully informed and noncoercive; consanguineous/incest-simulating arrangements should be prohibited; some intergenerational arrangements require heightened caution and counseling.

**Affected evidence:** longitudinal small-sample UK intra-family egg-donation research found generally positive recipient-family relationships over ten years, with family roles usually remaining clear. Evidence is limited.

**Likely policies:** permit with enhanced independent counseling/consent; prohibit specific consanguineous/coercive structures; broadly avoid intrafamilial arrangements because of relational pressure. The last position is philosophically serious but weakly supported as professional policy.

**Risk:** avoid equating kinship with coercion or assuming family donation is inherently benign.

## M063 — Human reproductive cloning

**Decision object:** whether a regulator should ever allow reproductive cloning using nuclear-transfer embryos, separate from therapeutic/research cloning.

**Professional/governance evidence:** current ISSCR guidelines place implantation of human embryos produced by nuclear reprogramming in prohibited Category 3B; UNESCO's Universal Declaration says reproductive cloning is contrary to human dignity and should not be permitted.

**Public evidence:** historical public attitudes are overwhelmingly cautious/opposed, but contemporary policy-comparison evidence is thin because reproductive cloning is not a live clinical option.

**Likely policies:** categorical prohibition; future reconsideration only after safety and broad democratic authorization; reproductive-liberty permission is a philosophical position without current professional support.

**Risk:** likely research-complete/non-executable because source classes collapse around prohibition.

## M064 — Artificial-womb technology for extreme prematurity

**Decision object:** future neonatal policy governing transfer of extremely premature fetuses/newborns to artificial-womb/extrauterine support technology when clinical translation becomes possible.

**Current evidence:** 2026 scoping review identified 247 ethics papers, with major themes of benefits/harms, decision-making, moral/legal status, justice/access and research ethics; it emphasizes that stakeholder engagement is still needed. AWT for extreme prematurity must be distinguished from speculative complete ectogenesis.

**Public evidence:** very limited direct contemporary patient/public policy evidence; an older Israeli pilot exists but is not sufficient for current executable construction.

**Likely policies:** offer as a neonatal rescue option after parental consent; initially restrict to trials/high-risk gestational thresholds; require use where expected outcomes substantially exceed conventional neonatal intensive care. Professional clinical architecture is not mature.

**Risk:** moving technology and terminology; likely research-complete/non-executable.

## M065 — PGT/HLA matching to conceive a donor sibling

**Decision object:** whether an IVF program should use PGT with HLA typing to conceive a healthy HLA-matched child whose cord blood or later low-burden tissue may treat a seriously ill existing sibling.

**Professional evidence:** ESHRE good-practice guidance says HLA-matched embryo selection can be acceptable after other clinical options are exhausted where an affected child is likely to be cured or have substantially prolonged survival through an HLA-matched sibling transplant; timing and prognosis must be considered.

**Affected evidence:** parents facing Fanconi anemia and other serious disorders often want the option discussed; Hong Kong couples and Malaysian stakeholders generally supported donor-sibling conception under safety/last-option conditions; 2026 Greek ART-patient survey found 68.2% considered savior-sibling creation ethically acceptable. Concerns include instrumentalization, donor-child welfare and cost.

**Likely policies:** allow after alternatives exhausted with child-welfare limits; allow more broadly when parents intend an independently valued child and donation begins with cord blood/low-burden tissue; prohibit selection for instrumental donation purposes. Candidate translation requires caution because personal support does not equal institutional policy.

## M066 — Prenatal genome-wide sequencing and secondary findings

**Decision object:** what fetal/parental secondary findings a prenatal sequencing service should analyze and offer when testing is ordered for fetal structural anomalies.

**Professional evidence:** updated ISPD position statement recommends detailed pre/post-test counseling and states there is no universal consensus on incidental/secondary findings; each center should clearly state its policy and address inclusion/exclusion of actionable secondary findings in fetal and parental sequences.

**Affected evidence:** in 682 prenatal/pediatric families, 84% accepted secondary findings; prenatal acceptance was 86.2%. Earlier survey of pregnant patients found strong interest not only in treatable childhood conditions but also adult-onset findings.

**Likely policies:** offer actionable fetal/parental secondary findings with opt-out; report only findings relevant to fetal/childhood management and defer adult-onset risk; allow parents to opt into broader categories after counseling.

**Risk:** fetal future-autonomy interests differ from adult M054; parental findings and fetal findings must not be conflated.

## M067 — Fertility preservation for minors facing gonadotoxic treatment

**Decision object:** when parents may authorize invasive or experimental fertility-preservation procedures for a minor before urgent gonadotoxic therapy.

**Professional evidence:** ASRM 2026 requires discussion of fertility-preservation options, parental consent based on potential benefit, developmentally appropriate assent, respect for a postpubertal minor's objection, avoidance of embryo creation from minors' gametes, and IRB oversight for experimental procedures. Tissue cryopreservation may be authorized for children too young to assent if potential benefit justifies risk.

**Affected evidence:** pediatric oncology literature documents high long-term importance of fertility and variable parent/child priorities, but policy-comparison evidence is limited.

**Likely policies:** routinely offer established preservation and assent-sensitive experimental protocols; allow parents to authorize low-burden tissue preservation when child cannot assent; defer invasive experimental preservation when treatment delay/burden is material.

**Risk:** treatment urgency and procedure burden must be explicit; likely professional convergence.

## M068 — Transfer of an embryo known to carry a monogenic condition

**Decision object:** fertility-clinic policy when patients request transfer of their only available embryo known to carry a serious but treatable/manageable monogenic condition.

**Professional evidence:** ASRM 2025 explicitly concludes that in many clinical cases physicians may ethically assist or refuse transfer; transfer of embryos highly likely to cause life-threatening severe early debility with no reasonable function is highly discouraged, while transfer of embryos associated with treatable/manageable conditions may ethically be provided or refused. Written clinic policy and genetic counseling are encouraged.

**Affected evidence:** direct empirical evidence on these rare requests is limited; reproductive-liberty and disability perspectives are important, and some requests arise because the affected embryo is the only chance for genetic parenthood or reflects a condition present in the family.

**Likely policies:** honor transfer for manageable disease after counseling; decline transfer on child-welfare/nonmaleficence grounds; allow only when no unaffected embryos remain and severity falls below a defined harm threshold.

**Risk:** avoid discriminatory assumptions about disability; specify phenotype/prognosis rather than a diagnostic label alone.

## M069 — Disclosure of ART laboratory errors

**Decision object:** clinic policy after a clinically consequential gamete/embryo error or misdirection is discovered.

**Professional evidence:** ASRM 2024 states clinics have an ethical obligation to disclose clinically significant errors and all gamete/embryo misdirection immediately to impacted patients; even no-harm errors are generally recommended for disclosure. Root-cause analysis and system correction are required.

**Affected evidence:** patient-specific error literature is sparse, but general medical-error literature strongly favors disclosure and reproductive errors can uniquely affect genetic parenthood and reproductive opportunity.

**Likely policies:** immediate disclosure of all consequential/misdirection errors with ongoing updates; staged disclosure after facts are verified; nondisclosure of clearly harmless near misses. For clinically consequential errors the professional layer is strongly convergent.

**Risk:** do not create concealment as a serious candidate merely to manufacture disagreement; distinguish actual error from near miss.

## M070 — Allocation of scarce gene-therapy capacity

**Decision object:** how a specialized program should prioritize eligible patients when curative gene-therapy manufacturing/bed capacity is insufficient for all eligible patients.

**Professional/implementation evidence:** a 2025 Blood Advances framework for scarce SCD/TDT gene therapy uses Accountability for Reasonableness and combines disease-prevalence proportionality, impending loss of future eligibility ('sickest first'), absence of an allogeneic donor and lottery.

**Affected evidence:** SCD discrete-choice work with 174 adults and 109 parents shows that treatment risk tolerance and desired efficacy vary substantially with disease severity; patients with worsening/severe disease accept more treatment risk. This supports urgency/severity as patient-relevant but is not itself a direct allocation-policy survey.

**Likely policies:** prioritize highest clinical urgency/loss of future eligibility; maximize expected benefit/safety; lottery among clinically eligible patients after transparent priority tiers. Direct affected-community allocation evidence should be sought before execution.

**Risk:** high cost, manufacturing scarcity and clinical eligibility are distinct rationing questions; race/equity is especially important in SCD and must not be reduced to an abstract utility calculation.
