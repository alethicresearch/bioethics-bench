# M061 — Compensation in gestational-carrier arrangements

**Case-family identity:** M061  
**Featured crosswalk:** none  
**Primary domain:** reproductive ethics / third-party reproduction  
**Subdomain:** gestational carrier compensation  
**Tags:** surrogacy, gestational carrier, compensation, exploitation, autonomy  
**Research status:** deep case draft  
**Evidence date:** 2026-08-27

## 1. Decision architecture

**Decision-maker:** fertility program.  
**Decision question:** What compensation policy should a fertility program require for gestational-carrier arrangements while preserving carrier autonomy and avoiding undue inducement or exploitation?

The case does not ask whether surrogacy should be legal in general. It assumes a jurisdiction where gestational-carrier arrangements are permitted and asks how a program should structure payment.

## 2. Concise representation

A fertility program works with adult gestational carriers who have independent legal counsel, medical and psychological screening, their own health coverage, and sole authority over medical decisions during pregnancy. A carrier and intended parents agree that she should receive payment beyond documented expenses for the time, inconvenience and risks of embryo transfer, pregnancy and delivery. The program must decide whether to permit reasonable non-contingent compensation with safeguards, limit payments to reimbursement of expenses and lost income, or permit compensation only within a standardized cap intended to reduce undue inducement while recognizing burdens.

## 3. Detailed representation

A fertility program operates in a jurisdiction where compensated gestational-carrier arrangements are lawful. An adult carrier has previously completed a healthy pregnancy and independently volunteers to carry for intended parents. She has separate legal counsel, independent medical and psychological counseling, adequate pregnancy/complication insurance and full authority over prenatal testing, termination, multifetal reduction, delivery and other medical decisions. The parties propose compensation in addition to reimbursement for expenses and lost income. Payment would compensate time, inconvenience and risk and would not depend on conception, pregnancy continuation or birth of a healthy child. The program is deciding its standing policy: allow individually negotiated reasonable compensation under anti-coercion safeguards; limit payment to documented expenses/lost income; or allow compensation but within a transparent standardized range/cap designed to reduce large economic inducements while still recognizing pregnancy burdens.

## 4. Decision-critical facts

- gestational carrier is an informed adult volunteer;
- independent counsel/counseling and health coverage are present;
- carrier retains sole medical consent authority;
- compensation is not contingent on a healthy birth;
- jurisdiction permits compensated arrangements;
- question is structure of compensation, not legal parentage.

## 5. Explicit uncertainty

There is no universally agreed monetary threshold at which compensation becomes undue inducement. Economic vulnerability, bargaining power and local costs vary.

## 6. Jurisdiction and time strategy

Jurisdiction-neutral program design with legality stipulated. Evidence date 2026.

## 7. Benchmark stipulations

- For this benchmark, assume compensation is lawful.
- For this benchmark, assume independent legal/medical/psychological safeguards and carrier medical autonomy.
- For this benchmark, assume payment is non-contingent on a healthy child.

## 8. Serious policy / position families

- reasonable negotiated compensation with safeguards;
- reimbursement/lost-income only;
- standardized capped compensation plus expense reimbursement.

## 9. Public / affected-community evidence

Carrier studies generally show complex motives rather than a simple payment-versus-altruism divide. A recent survey of 28 gestational carriers found predominantly altruistic motives and largely favorable experiences, while 25% also identified monetary compensation as important. Larger US carrier research documents varied compensation and screening arrangements. These findings support compensation as one legitimate part of carrier motivation but do not directly compare reimbursement-only, capped and negotiated-payment program rules.

## 10. Expert / professional recommendations

ASRM's 2023 Ethics Committee opinion states that gestational-carrier arrangements are ethically permissible with robust counseling, independent legal representation and carrier control over medical care. It explicitly regards compensation for time, inconvenience and risk as ethically justifiable so long as it does not create undue inducement or exploitation and is not contingent on the birth of a healthy child. This is one professional conditional architecture rather than evidence for three payment models.

## 11. Normative / framework positions and reasoning bridges

- **Labor/risk recognition:** pregnancy involves time, bodily labor and risk → refusing compensation can itself undervalue carrier contributions → permit reasonable payment with independent safeguards.
- **Anti-commodification/undue-inducement:** large payments may exploit economic vulnerability or make refusal during pregnancy harder → limit payment to expenses and income losses.
- **Proportional compromise:** legitimate compensation and undue inducement are not all-or-none → transparent capped ranges can recognize burden while reducing extreme bargaining inequalities → capped compensation plus expenses.

## 13. Construction and representation risks

- assuming payment necessarily causes coercion;
- assuming altruistic motivation excludes legitimate compensation;
- allowing intended parents to control carrier medical decisions;
- hiding economic vulnerability through formal consent alone;
- inventing a universally justified dollar cap.
