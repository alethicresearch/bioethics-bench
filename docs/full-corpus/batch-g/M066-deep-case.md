# M066 — Prenatal genome-wide sequencing and secondary findings

**Case-family identity:** M066  
**Featured crosswalk:** none  
**Primary domain:** prenatal genomics / consent  
**Subdomain:** fetal and parental secondary findings  
**Tags:** prenatal sequencing, secondary findings, adult-onset risk, future autonomy, parental choice  
**Research status:** deep case draft  
**Evidence date:** 2026-08-27

## 1. Decision architecture

**Decision-maker:** prenatal genomic medicine service.  
**Decision question:** What secondary-findings policy should a prenatal sequencing service use when genome-wide testing is ordered because of fetal structural anomalies?

The case distinguishes the fetal diagnostic indication from unrelated fetal or parental findings.

## 2. Concise representation

A pregnant patient undergoes trio exome/genome sequencing because fetal structural anomalies remain unexplained after standard testing. The laboratory can also identify medically actionable variants unrelated to the fetal anomalies, including findings in the fetus and in one of the parents. Some would affect childhood management; others primarily predict adult-onset disease. The family can receive detailed genetic counseling. The service must decide whether to offer actionable fetal and parental secondary findings with an informed opt-out, restrict routine return to findings relevant to pregnancy, neonatal or childhood management while deferring adult-onset fetal risk, or allow parents to choose among broader secondary-finding categories before sequencing.

## 3. Detailed representation

A prenatal genetics service performs trio exome/genome sequencing after ultrasound identifies multiple fetal structural anomalies and chromosomal microarray is nondiagnostic. The sequencing analysis may identify pathogenic or likely pathogenic variants that explain the fetal phenotype, but it can also reveal unrelated medically actionable variants in the fetus or one of the parents. Some secondary findings could change pregnancy, neonatal or childhood surveillance; others predict serious adult-onset conditions for which prevention is available only later. The service provides pre- and post-test counseling and can technically include or exclude categories before analysis. It must choose a standing policy: routinely offer a defined set of actionable fetal and parental secondary findings with informed opt-out; limit routine return to findings relevant to pregnancy, neonatal care or childhood health and defer adult-onset fetal findings to protect the future child's later choice; or let parents opt into broader secondary-finding categories after counseling, including adult-onset and parental findings.

## 4. Decision-critical facts

- sequencing is clinically indicated for fetal anomalies;
- unrelated secondary findings can arise in fetus and parents;
- some findings are childhood-actionable, others adult-onset;
- parents can make informed category choices before analysis;
- genetic counseling is available;
- analytical confirmation is required before clinical return.

## 5. Explicit uncertainty

Penetrance, timing of disease and clinical actionability vary. Prenatal discovery can create information about a future child before that person can exercise a right not to know. Parental findings may also affect relatives.

## 6. Jurisdiction and time strategy

Jurisdiction-neutral evidence-dated 2026 prenatal-genomics policy. Current ISPD guidance explicitly recognizes international variation and no universal consensus on incidental/secondary findings.

## 7. Benchmark stipulations

- For this benchmark, assume sequencing is otherwise clinically appropriate for fetal anomalies.
- For this benchmark, assume returned secondary findings are clinically confirmed.
- For this benchmark, assume detailed counseling and pre-analysis category selection are feasible.

## 8. Serious policy / position families

- offer a defined actionable fetal/parental set with opt-out;
- restrict routine fetal return to pregnancy/childhood actionability and defer adult-onset risk;
- broad parent-directed opt-in among secondary-finding categories.

## 9. Public / affected-community evidence

Families generally show high interest in secondary findings but not universal uptake. In a cohort of 682 prenatal/pediatric families, 84% accepted secondary findings and 86.2% of prenatal families accepted them. An earlier survey of 186 expectant patients found 83% thought prenatal whole-exome sequencing should be offered and majorities wanted both childhood and adult-onset results, including non-treatable conditions. These studies support broad-information and preference-sensitive orientations but do not directly tell us which default policy families prefer.

## 10. Expert / professional recommendations

The updated ISPD position statement says there is **no universal consensus** on incidental and secondary findings in prenatal genome-wide sequencing. Centers should clearly state whether such findings are reported and address the option to include or exclude secondary findings in fetal and parental sequences. This is genuine professional policy openness rather than one fixed recommendation, but the statement does not itself endorse every broad-return option.

## 11. Normative / framework positions and reasoning bridges

- **Beneficence/family actionability:** actionable findings can improve care for fetus, child or parent and may inform relatives → make a defined actionable set routinely available with opt-out.
- **Future autonomy:** adult-onset fetal information can permanently remove the future person's chance not to know → return only pregnancy/childhood-relevant findings by default.
- **Parental informational authority:** parents already make many prenatal information choices and family values vary → after counseling, let them affirmatively choose which broader categories are analyzed and returned.

## 13. Construction and representation risks

- conflating fetal findings and parental findings;
- assuming parental desire automatically overrides future-child autonomy;
- treating all adult-onset findings as equally actionable;
- attributing a universal secondary-finding policy to ISPD when it explicitly says there is no consensus;
- using interest in information as direct evidence for a particular default.
