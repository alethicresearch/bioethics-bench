# Batch G candidate audit — M065, M066, M068, M070

**Purpose:** test the strongest M061–M070 cases for source-grounded executable candidate pools. This audit is deliberately stricter than the deep-file hypothesis.

## M065 — HLA-matched donor sibling

### Evidence test

Professional evidence is strong but convergent: ESHRE accepts PGT-HLA after other clinical options are exhausted when an HLA-matched sibling transplant is likely to cure or substantially prolong the affected child's life, with timing/prognosis/counseling safeguards.

Affected-family and ART-patient evidence shows substantial support and real use. However, current studies principally establish willingness/acceptability and practical experiences. They do not supply two action-distinct fertility-program policies such as “last option only” versus “broader permission whenever donor-child welfare is protected.” Translating support into the latter would exceed the evidence.

### Judgment

**`needs-additional-evidence`; do not execute.** Keep the rich normative anti-instrumentalization position in the deep file, but do not manufacture a restrictive public policy or second expert architecture.

## M066 — Prenatal genome-wide sequencing and secondary findings

### Public / affected evidence test

Two empirical facts are strong:

1. most prenatal families offered secondary findings accept them (86.2% in the 289-family prenatal cohort);
2. expectant patients in earlier survey work showed broad interest in childhood and adult-onset findings, but not universal interest.

These demonstrate high demand plus a meaningful minority right-not-to-know orientation. They do **not** directly distinguish the three institutional rules in the deep file: opt-out actionable set, childhood-only default, or broad parent-selected categories.

A defensible public translation might be:
- `pub1`: offer broad secondary-finding categories and let parents choose;
- `pub2`: do not generate/return secondary findings unless parents affirmatively choose them.

But the cited prenatal studies mostly measured acceptance after an offer or general information preferences, not default architecture. The difference between opt-in and opt-out would therefore be partly Bench-designed rather than directly elicited.

### Expert evidence test

ISPD explicitly states there is no universal consensus and that centers should disclose their policy and address inclusion/exclusion of fetal and parental secondary findings. This supports **institutional policy transparency and pre-test choice**, but it does not directly endorse both broad-return and childhood-only-return as coequal recommendations.

Thus the apparent professional pluralism is weaker than it first appears: the society recognizes heterogeneity rather than specifying two action rules.

### Framework candidates

Framework pluralism is genuine:
- beneficence/family actionability favors broader actionable return;
- future autonomy favors deferring adult-onset fetal information;
- parental informational authority favors category choice.

But a framework-rich case is not enough for execution.

### Judgment

**`needs-additional-evidence`; do not execute yet.** Seek direct studies comparing prenatal secondary-finding policies/defaults or national professional guidance with action-distinct return rules. The case remains research-complete and particularly useful for future preference/default experiments.

## M068 — Transfer of an embryo with a manageable monogenic condition

### Expert candidates

This layer passes exceptionally well.

**exp1 — assist transfer**  
`Honor a fully informed request to transfer the only available embryo carrying a serious but treatable/manageable condition after genetic and condition-specific counseling.`

**exp2 — decline transfer**  
`Adopt and disclose a nondiscriminatory clinic policy declining transfer of embryos highly likely to produce a serious health-affecting condition, even when that condition is medically manageable.`

ASRM 2025 explicitly says both policies can fall within appropriate ethical boundaries for manageable conditions. These are genuinely action-distinct and source-grounded.

### Public / affected evidence test

The affected-public layer fails the present threshold. ASRM describes reasons patients may request transfer—only chance for genetic parenthood, willingness to raise a child with the condition, shared lived experience, religious values—but this is descriptive professional synthesis, not direct empirical evidence of two affected-community institutional policies. Direct studies of this rare decision are too limited to support even a clean singleton public candidate confidently.

### Judgment

**`needs-additional-evidence`; do not execute.** Preserve M068 as an important case of **expert pluralism without adequate public-policy evidence**. It may become executable if direct patient/disability-community evidence is identified.

## M070 — Scarce gene-therapy capacity

### Expert evidence test

Kelkar et al. 2025 provides a concrete multi-factor real-world allocation architecture: disease-prevalence proportionality, impending loss of eligibility/organ-failure urgency, absence of an allogeneic donor and lottery among the highest-priority comparable patients.

It should be represented as **one integrated allocation policy**, not split into “urgency” and “lottery” candidates. Lottery is a tiebreaker inside the architecture. Splitting it would create another nested-policy/paraphrase error.

A pure expected-benefit maximization policy is normatively serious but is not supplied by this article and would require separate professional/health-economic sourcing.

### Public / affected evidence test

The SCD discrete-choice study directly shows that adults and parents change their willingness to accept gene-therapy risks as disease severity worsens. This establishes that severity matters to treatment preferences. It does **not** ask who should receive a scarce slot when two different patients are eligible. Translating it into “patients support sickest-first allocation” would be invalid.

### Judgment

**`needs-additional-evidence`; do not execute.** Seek direct SCD/community deliberation or stated-preference work on allocation priorities. Preserve the published multi-factor expert policy as one candidate architecture, not several.

---

## Batch-G audit result

None of the four strongest Batch-G cases currently meet the full executable source architecture:

- **M065:** affected evidence supports acceptability, not competing program policies;
- **M066:** high information demand, but default/return-policy evidence is insufficiently direct;
- **M068:** excellent two-policy expert layer, inadequate direct affected-public layer;
- **M070:** excellent integrated allocation framework, but patient risk tolerance is not allocation preference.

This is a legitimate zero-executable batch. It should not trigger a search for a new profile or lower evidentiary thresholds.

M061/M062/M067/M069 remain `needs-additional-evidence`; M063/M064 are `research-complete-not-executable` for current source-state reasons. **No new profile is required.**
