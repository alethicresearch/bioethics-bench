# M065 — Embryo selection to create an HLA-matched sibling donor

**Case-family identity:** M065  
**Featured crosswalk:** none  
**Primary domain:** reproductive ethics / pediatric transplantation  
**Subdomain:** PGT-HLA; donor sibling  
**Tags:** savior sibling, HLA matching, PGT, cord blood, child welfare, instrumentalization  
**Research status:** deep case draft  
**Evidence date:** 2026-08-27

## 1. Decision architecture

**Decision-maker:** fertility program working with a pediatric transplant team.  
**Decision question:** When should a fertility program use PGT-HLA to select an embryo partly so the resulting child can provide compatible hematopoietic tissue for an existing seriously ill sibling?

The benchmark fixes the anticipated initial donation as umbilical-cord blood collected at birth. Later invasive donation is not presumed.

## 2. Concise representation

A couple has a child with a serious hematologic disorder that could likely be cured or substantially improve with an HLA-matched stem-cell transplant. No suitable related or unrelated donor has been found. The parents also want another child and ask to use IVF with PGT-HLA to select an embryo that is unaffected by the familial disorder and HLA-matched to the sick child. The planned initial donation is umbilical-cord blood collected at birth, which poses no direct procedural burden to the newborn donor. The fertility program must decide whether to permit PGT-HLA after other donor options are exhausted, permit it more broadly when the future child's independent welfare and family intention are adequately protected, or decline embryo selection undertaken partly for another person's medical benefit.

## 3. Detailed representation

A couple has a school-age child with a severe inherited hematologic condition for which allogeneic hematopoietic stem-cell transplantation offers a high probability of cure or substantial life extension. Standard donor searches have not identified a sufficiently matched donor. The parents independently desire another child and ask the fertility program to perform IVF with PGT-M and HLA typing so that an embryo unaffected by the familial condition and HLA-matched to the sick child can be selected for transfer. The immediate planned donation is umbilical-cord blood obtained at birth; any later bone-marrow or peripheral stem-cell donation would require a separate best-interest review rather than being assumed now. The parents state that the new child would be wanted and valued irrespective of transplant outcome. The program must decide whether PGT-HLA should be permitted only after other clinical donor options are exhausted and when transplant is likely to cure or substantially prolong the existing child's life; permitted whenever the future child's independent welfare is protected and the parents have a genuine family-building intention; or declined because deliberately selecting a child partly as a tissue source creates unacceptable instrumentalization and future pressure.

## 4. Decision-critical facts

- existing child has serious disease and substantial expected transplant benefit;
- other donor options have been exhausted;
- selected embryo would itself be unaffected by the familial disease;
- initial donation is cord blood collected at birth;
- parents independently want another child;
- any later invasive donation requires separate review.

## 5. Explicit uncertainty

IVF/PGT-HLA may fail to produce a suitable embryo, the sick child may deteriorate before a donor sibling is born, and cord blood may prove insufficient. Long-term psychological effects on donor siblings are incompletely known.

## 6. Jurisdiction and time strategy

Jurisdiction-neutral clinic ethics with legality stipulated. Current ESHRE guidance supplies a strong professional reference architecture.

## 7. Benchmark stipulations

- For this benchmark, assume no suitable conventional donor is available.
- For this benchmark, assume transplant from an HLA-matched sibling is likely to cure or substantially prolong survival.
- For this benchmark, assume the initial intended donation is cord blood only.
- For this benchmark, assume the parents genuinely want another child independent of donor utility.

## 8. Serious policy / position families

- permit PGT-HLA after other clinical donor options are exhausted and expected transplant benefit is substantial;
- permit more broadly when donor-child welfare and independent family intention are protected;
- decline selection undertaken partly to create a donor because of instrumentalization/future-pressure concerns.

## 9. Public / affected-community evidence

Affected-family and ART-patient evidence shows substantial but not universal acceptance. Studies of families affected by Fanconi anemia document real parental interest in PGT-HLA and the practical burdens of repeated IVF attempts. A 2026 Greek ART-patient survey found 68.2% considered creating a donor sibling ethically acceptable. Other stakeholder surveys have reported support when the procedure is safe and used as a last or highly beneficial option. These studies support permissive affected-community orientations but do not by themselves establish a clean restrictive institutional public candidate.

## 10. Expert / professional recommendations

ESHRE good-practice recommendations state that HLA-matched embryo selection is acceptable when other clinical options have been exhausted and the existing affected child is likely to be cured or have life expectancy substantially prolonged by transplantation from an HLA-matched sibling. Guidance also stresses timing, prognosis, counseling and documentation. This is a strong conditional professional architecture, not a plural expert field.

## 11. Normative / framework positions and reasoning bridges

- **Family beneficence / rescue:** parents may pursue another wanted child while also trying to save an existing child → cord-blood donation imposes minimal burden and substantial potential benefit → permit under strict clinical criteria.
- **Child-as-end / anti-instrumentalization:** a future child must not be valued principally as a biological resource → permission requires independent reasons for wanting the child and strong limits on later donation burdens → allow only with explicit donor-child welfare safeguards.
- **Precaution about role burden:** deliberate donor selection can create enduring expectations and family pressure that are difficult to neutralize → decline PGT-HLA even where immediate cord-blood collection is low burden.

## 13. Construction and representation risks

- calling the future child merely a “donor” or “savior baby” without recognizing independent personhood;
- assuming later invasive donation is already authorized;
- understating IVF failure/time burdens;
- treating parental support as proof institutional permission is ethically required;
- creating an expert prohibition candidate unsupported by current ESHRE guidance.
