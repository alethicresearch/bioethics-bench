## Why the final forty add zero executable cases

The zero yield is a result of applying the construction standard consistently rather than a failure of the cases.

Repeated reasons include:

1. **personal preference is not institutional policy** — e.g. M189 mammography and M196 prognosis communication contain rich preference heterogeneity, but the evidence does not directly specify universal clinic rules;
2. **behavior under a policy is not endorsement of that policy** — e.g. plant-based defaults can change meal choices without establishing that participants endorse default architecture;
3. **process branches are not competing policies** — child-abuse reporting, IPV response, opioid benefit–risk management, surrogate disagreement and clinician termination contain multiple conditional actions inside one professional process;
4. **professional governance can already integrate the values at issue** — e.g. HPAI control, climate adaptation finance, documentation ethics and managed-care stewardship;
5. **human public opinion cannot automatically represent nonhuman interests** — animal research, invasive-species control, wild-animal disease, xenotransplant source-animal welfare and animal-product procurement expose a source-architecture limitation rather than an invitation to substitute human attitudes for animal welfare;
6. **frontier concern is not an authorization rule** — public concern/support for geoengineering or genetically modified mosquitoes does not automatically yield two bounded field-release policies.

## High-value non-executable cases

Non-executable status is not a ranking of philosophical importance. Several central cases remain deliberately non-executable:

- **M129 — QALYs and disability:** real institutions use different measurement architectures, but direct disability-community evidence does not yet justify competing institutional measurement rules on the community's behalf;
- **M156–M164 — animal/One Health cases:** expose the unresolved problem of representing interests of beings who cannot provide survey or deliberative testimony;
- **M169 — solar geoengineering field research:** mature professional governance and substantial public-attitude evidence, but insufficient direct public evidence for the represented authorization alternatives;
- **M189 — mammography guideline disagreement:** genuine USPSTF/ACS professional divergence plus personal preference heterogeneity, but not policy-level affected evidence under the strict rule;
- **M196 — prognostic uncertainty:** rich patient communication preferences are best represented as preference-sensitive communication rather than fabricated universal rules;
- **M200 — community living versus higher public cost:** strong disability-rights/Olmstead architecture, but insufficient direct affected evidence for a specific funding threshold.

These cases are important research outputs because they reveal where the Bench's source architecture can and cannot responsibly create an executable policy comparison.

## Asymmetric-profile interpretation

Mean aggregation remains required for asymmetric profiles to remove deterministic pool-size advantage. A further interpretation issue is now explicit: binary `2×1×2` candidate sets can contain a denser cluster of same-orientation cross-pool comparisons. Mean corrects partner-count bias but does not erase the composition of the represented policy set. Observed convergence in asymmetric cases must therefore be interpreted as conditional on candidate composition and profile shape.

This belongs in later validation/P3 analysis. It is not a reason to fabricate candidates or modify QCCS v1.
