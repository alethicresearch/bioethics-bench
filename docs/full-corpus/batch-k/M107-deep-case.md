# M107 — Normothermic regional perfusion after circulatory death

**Case-family identity:** M107  
**Primary domain:** transplantation / deceased donation  
**Subdomain:** normothermic regional perfusion; death determination  
**Tags:** NRP, DCD, dead donor rule, informed consent, organ procurement  
**Research status:** deep case draft  
**Evidence date:** 2026-08-27

## 1. Decision architecture

**Decision-maker:** transplant system / donor hospital policy authority.  
**Decision question:** Should a donor hospital permit normothermic regional perfusion after declaration of death by circulatory criteria, and if so under what safeguards?

## 2. Concise representation

A hospital participates in donation after circulatory death. After death is declared following permanent cessation of circulation, an organ-procurement team proposes normothermic regional perfusion to restore warm oxygenated blood to abdominal or thoracoabdominal organs while surgically preventing blood flow to the brain. The technique can improve organ viability and increase transplantation, but clinicians and professional organizations disagree about whether restoring circulation is compatible with death determination and the dead-donor rule. The hospital must decide whether to permit NRP under transparent consent and brain-isolation safety protocols, prohibit NRP because of unresolved death/dead-donor-rule concerns, or permit only specified NRP configurations after independent ethics/safety review.

## 3. Detailed representation

A donor hospital is revising policy for donation after circulatory death (DCD). After withdrawal of life-sustaining treatment, death is declared by clinicians independent of the donation team following permanent cessation of circulation and the required observation period. An organ-procurement organization proposes NRP: extracorporeal circulation would restore warm oxygenated blood to selected organs to improve viability, while vessels are clamped or vented to prevent cerebral reperfusion. NRP can increase the number and quality of organs recovered, and its use is growing, but ethical controversy persists over whether restoring regional circulation after a circulatory declaration of death is conceptually compatible with death determination and the dead-donor rule, and whether families understand the procedure. The hospital must choose whether to permit NRP with standardized brain-isolation safeguards, monitoring and explicit authorization; prohibit it while the conceptual and safety dispute remains unresolved; or permit only defined abdominal or thoracoabdominal configurations after independent ethics/safety review and institutional readiness criteria.

## 4. Decision-critical facts

- death is declared before NRP by clinicians separate from procurement;
- cerebral circulation is intended to remain excluded;
- NRP improves organ viability/supply;
- professional controversy over death determination is real and current;
- protocol variation and safety standardization remain active policy issues.

## 5. Explicit uncertainty

Uncertainty concerns brain reperfusion risk, consistency of death definitions, family understanding, institutional readiness and public trust. Ethical disagreement is not reducible to a single technical safety question.

## 6. Jurisdiction and time strategy

Evidence-dated 2026 US-facing governance case. HRSA/OPTN is actively developing safety guidelines, so the case should be re-versioned as policy stabilizes.

## 7. Benchmark stipulations

- For this benchmark, assume death is declared independently before NRP begins.
- For this benchmark, assume the proposed protocol includes measures intended to prevent cerebral reperfusion.
- For this benchmark, assume NRP offers a meaningful organ-viability benefit.

## 8. Serious policy / position families

- permit NRP under explicit consent and standardized safety/brain-isolation safeguards;
- prohibit NRP because restoring circulation is incompatible with an acceptable death/dead-donor-rule framework;
- permit only specified NRP configurations under independent institutional review.

## 9. Public / affected-community evidence

Direct informed-public or donor-family evidence comparing these NRP policies remains limited. Family experience and public trust are recognized by HRSA/OPTN as central, but that recognition cannot be translated into a public candidate pool.

## 10. Expert / professional recommendations

Professional disagreement is genuine. OPTN's 2024 ethical analysis supports transparent protocols and adequate authorization while HRSA/OPTN continues safety-standard development. Other professional organizations have issued both supportive and critical statements. Contemporary 2025–2026 ethics literature continues to defend and challenge NRP on death/dead-donor-rule grounds. This is real expert pluralism, but no public layer yet supports execution under the Full Corpus architecture.

## 11. Normative / framework positions and reasoning bridges

- **Respect-for-donor + utility:** if death has been validly declared and the brain remains excluded, restoring organ circulation honors donation while increasing lifesaving organs → permit with transparent safeguards.
- **Dead-donor-rule integrity:** circulation restoration after circulatory death can undermine the conceptual basis of the declaration and public trust → prohibit until a defensible death framework is established.
- **Procedural precaution:** ethical acceptability may vary by technique and institutional capacity → permit only configurations that satisfy independent safety and ethics criteria.
