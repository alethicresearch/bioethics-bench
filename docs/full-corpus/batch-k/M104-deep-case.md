# M104 — Retransplantation after prior nonadherence

**Case-family identity:** M104  
**Primary domain:** transplantation / candidacy  
**Subdomain:** retransplantation; adherence; structural barriers  
**Tags:** retransplant, nonadherence, responsibility, adherence, justice  
**Research status:** deep case draft  
**Evidence date:** 2026-08-27

## 1. Decision architecture

**Decision-maker:** transplant-program selection committee.  
**Decision question:** When prior nonadherence materially contributed to graft loss, how should that history affect eligibility or priority for retransplantation?

## 2. Concise representation

A patient needs a second transplant after failure of a prior graft. Documented missed medication and follow-up contributed to the graft loss, but the earlier nonadherence occurred during unstable housing, transportation problems and untreated depression. Those barriers are now substantially improved, and the patient engages consistently with current care. The program must decide whether prior behavior should carry no independent moral penalty once current benefit is favorable, whether retransplant decisions should turn on an evidence-based assessment of future adherence after barriers are addressed, or whether a history of modifiable behavior causing graft loss should lower priority while recurrence risk remains clinically meaningful.

## 3. Detailed representation

A transplant program evaluates an adult whose first graft failed several years after transplantation. The medical record documents repeated missed immunosuppressant doses and missed follow-up, and clinicians judge those lapses materially contributed to graft loss. At the time, the patient had unstable housing, unreliable transportation, job insecurity and untreated depression. The patient now has stable housing, transportation support, mental-health treatment and a sustained period of attendance and medication adherence. The patient otherwise has substantial expected benefit from retransplantation. The committee must decide whether the prior graft loss should carry no independent penalty once current clinical benefit and support are adequate; whether candidacy should depend on an individualized evidence-based prediction of future adherence after structural barriers are assessed and mitigated; or whether prior behavior that contributed to graft loss should continue to reduce priority while a clinically meaningful modifiable recurrence risk remains.

## 4. Decision-critical facts

- prior behavior materially contributed to graft loss;
- structural and mental-health barriers also contributed;
- current circumstances and adherence have improved;
- retransplant would provide meaningful clinical benefit;
- question distinguishes blame from prospective outcome prediction.

## 5. Explicit uncertainty

Post-transplant adherence prediction is imperfect, and past adherence is influenced by access, resources and clinician perceptions. Retransplant also raises fairness concerns because a second organ is allocated while others await a first.

## 6. Jurisdiction and time strategy

Jurisdiction-neutral transplant-program ethics based on current OPTN candidacy guidance.

## 7. Benchmark stipulations

- For this benchmark, assume the earlier nonadherence contributed materially to graft failure.
- For this benchmark, assume major structural barriers have now been substantially mitigated.
- For this benchmark, assume retransplant is expected to offer substantial medical benefit.

## 8. Serious policy / position families

- no independent moralized penalty for prior nonadherence;
- individualized prospective adherence assessment after barrier mitigation;
- lower priority only while modifiable recurrence risk remains clinically significant.

## 9. Public / affected-community evidence

General organ-allocation studies show that members of the public often consider personal responsibility or behavior relevant to allocation. However, those studies do not directly support a retransplantation policy after structural barriers have been addressed. They therefore cannot be converted into a punitive public candidate under the Full Corpus standard.

## 10. Expert / professional recommendations

OPTN states that adherence measures are limited and inconsistently applied, that objective measures should be interpreted in light of structural barriers, and that programs should help candidates overcome those barriers. Potentially injurious behavior should not be a sole basis for exclusion unless risk overwhelmingly outweighs benefit. Repeat transplantation raises justice concerns but graft failure has many causes. This supports one prospective, individualized, evidence-based architecture rather than moral blame.

## 11. Normative / framework positions and reasoning bridges

- **Anti-desert / equal moral worth:** scarce care should not punish patients for past behavior as a matter of moral deservingness → use current clinical benefit rather than blame.
- **Stewardship / prospective utility:** past behavior matters only insofar as it predicts preventable future graft loss → assess current recurrence risk with evidence and supports.
- **Fair opportunity:** retransplant can be fair while others await first transplant only if modifiable risks have been addressed enough to justify another scarce graft → temporarily reduce priority when significant recurrence risk remains.

## 13. Construction and representation risks

- equating nonadherence with blameworthiness;
- ignoring structural barriers;
- assuming past behavior perfectly predicts future adherence;
- creating a punitive public policy from generic responsibility preferences.
