## 1. Decision architecture

**Decision-maker:** treating neurorehabilitation/medical team with surrogate.  
**Decision question:** When a patient with a prolonged disorder of consciousness has no reliable prior instruction about clinically assisted nutrition and hydration (CANH), what policy should govern continuation or withdrawal?

The decision is specific to long-term CANH. It does not assume that a disorder-of-consciousness diagnosis establishes a uniformly poor quality of life and it does not treat diagnostic labels as perfectly certain.

## 2. Concise representation

An adult has remained in a prolonged disorder of consciousness for 14 months after severe nontraumatic brain injury and receives nutrition and hydration through a feeding tube. Serial standardized assessments by an experienced team support a diagnosis of chronic unresponsive wakefulness syndrome, with no reliable evidence of command following, but clinicians explain that diagnostic and prognostic uncertainty is not zero. The patient left no advance directive and family members disagree about whether the patient would regard continued biological survival as worthwhile. For this benchmark, assume CANH can maintain life for years and has manageable but real burdens, while withdrawal would predictably lead to death with palliative symptom management. The team must decide what policy should govern continuation or withdrawal.

## 3. Detailed representation

An adult suffered severe nontraumatic brain injury 14 months ago and remains dependent on clinically assisted nutrition and hydration through a gastrostomy tube. The patient is medically stable and no longer requires ventilatory or vasopressor support. Multiple serial standardized assessments by clinicians experienced in disorders of consciousness support chronic unresponsive wakefulness syndrome; there is no reproducible command following or functional communication. For this benchmark, assume clinicians have appropriately investigated confounders and explain that diagnostic error and later recovery are unlikely but cannot be reduced to zero. The patient left no treatment-specific advance directive. One close family member believes the patient strongly valued independence and would reject years of noninteractive life; another believes food and water should continue unless there is certainty the patient cannot recover. CANH can maintain life for years but carries ongoing tube-related care, infection/procedure risks and institutional burden; withdrawal would predictably lead to death with mouth care, symptom treatment and palliative support. The team must decide what policy should govern continued CANH or withdrawal in the absence of a reliable prior instruction.

## 4. Decision-critical facts

- prolonged DoC after 14 months of nontraumatic injury;
- serial standardized expert assessment, not one bedside impression;
- residual diagnostic/prognostic uncertainty remains;
- no reliable treatment-specific advance directive;
- family disagreement concerns patient values and meaning of feeding;
- CANH is medically sustaining but non-restorative;
- withdrawal would cause death and palliative care is available;
- no immediate resource crisis or other competing patient.

## 5. Explicit uncertainty

AAN guidance emphasizes diagnostic accuracy and that prognosis is not universally poor. The benchmark therefore does not call the state “permanent” or stipulate absolute impossibility of recovery. The ethical decision must tolerate residual uncertainty without letting uncertainty do all the normative work.

## 6. Jurisdiction and time strategy

Jurisdiction-neutral ethical policy. The benchmark assumes CANH is legally considered a medical intervention that may in principle be continued or withdrawn through appropriate surrogate/best-interest decision-making; local court requirements are excluded.

## 7. Benchmark stipulations

- **Assessment stipulation:** repeated standardized expert evaluations have been completed.
- **Uncertainty stipulation:** meaningful recovery is unlikely but not impossible.
- **Directive stipulation:** no reliable prior CANH instruction exists.
- **Treatment stipulation:** CANH can sustain life long term but does not restore consciousness and has manageable burdens.
- **Palliation stipulation:** withdrawal would be accompanied by appropriate symptom care.

## 8. Serious policy / position families

- continue CANH absent clear evidence that the patient would refuse it, because survival is possible and uncertainty remains;
- permit withdrawal when a structured best-interests process finds continued non-restorative treatment inconsistent with the patient's values and interests;
- use a time-limited reassessment period before any withdrawal where prognosis or diagnosis remains materially uncertain;
- require independent review when family members disagree about patient values or the moral status of CANH.

## 9. Public / affected-community evidence

Family interviews show genuine heterogeneity about life-prolonging treatment in prolonged DoC. Families often give central weight to what they believe the patient would have wanted, but they differ about whether ongoing treatment serves the patient's interests (PMID 26539603). Interview research on CANH withdrawal found that some relatives who believed the patient would prefer death nevertheless found death following withdrawal of food/fluids emotionally or morally disturbing and associated it with starvation/dehydration (PMID 24425753). This creates two potentially distinct affected orientations: preserve CANH absent clear refusal versus treat CANH as withdrawable medical treatment when inconsistent with patient values.

Candidate audit must ensure these are translated as policies, not simply emotional reactions.

## 10. Expert / professional recommendations

The AAN/ACRM/NIDILRR guideline on prolonged disorders of consciousness, reaffirmed in 2024, prioritizes accurate diagnosis, serial assessment, treatment of confounders and careful prognostic counseling. ASPEN's ethics position treats artificially administered nutrition and hydration as a clinical intervention subject to autonomy, beneficence, nonmaleficence and justice through collaborative interdisciplinary decision-making (PMID 33616284). ESPEN likewise emphasizes autonomy, cultural/religious values and ethically permissible withholding/withdrawal.

The professional layer therefore appears to support one broad individualized/shared-decision architecture rather than two opposing expert rules.

## 11. Normative / framework positions and reasoning bridges

- **Preservation of life under uncertainty:** life-sustaining care should not be stopped on uncertain prognostic or quality-of-life judgments without clear patient refusal → residual uncertainty and lack of directive favor continued CANH.
- **Autonomy/substituted values and proportionality:** CANH is a medical intervention whose justification depends on the patient's values and benefits/burdens → continued biological survival alone need not control → permit withdrawal when a rigorous process finds treatment inconsistent with the patient's likely values/interests.
- **Disability justice / epistemic humility:** clinicians and families can underestimate consciousness and quality of life after severe brain injury → diagnostic safeguards and avoidance of disability-based assumptions are essential → require reassessment and independent review before irreversible withdrawal.

## 13. Construction and representation risks

- using “persistent/permanent vegetative state” as if diagnosis/prognosis were infallible;
- describing disability or noncommunication as a life not worth living;
- treating CANH as either “ordinary food” or “ordinary medical treatment” without acknowledging the contested meaning;
- making withdrawal sound like abandonment or continuation sound like neutral nonaction;
- changing diagnostic confidence or family disagreement across companion representations.
