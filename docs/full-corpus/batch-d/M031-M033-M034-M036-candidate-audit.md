# Batch D candidate audit — M031, M033, M034, M036

**Evidence date:** 2026-08-27  
**Purpose:** test the four M031–M040 cases with the strongest apparent executable source architecture before any machine-readable record is authored.

The audit asks whether each source class supports **distinct actionable policies of the same kind**. Survey heterogeneity is not enough by itself. Legal permission is labeled as policy architecture, not professional moral consensus.

## M031 — medical aid in dying for terminal illness

### Audit result: provisional `2×2×2`

This case survives because public attitudes are directly plural and there are two real institutional/professional policy architectures in a legally permissive setting.

### Public candidates

**pub1 — safeguarded access orientation**

> Permit qualified terminally ill adults to access medical aid in dying under capacity, voluntariness and informed-choice safeguards alongside meaningful hospice and palliative-care access.

**Provenance:** adapted from national US evidence showing substantial willingness to consider MAID (44% in PMID 39992651) and the 2025 Pew finding that 63% considered physician-assisted death morally acceptable or not a moral issue. This is a Bench institutional translation, not a claim that 63% endorsed this exact safeguard package.

**pub2 — institutional nonparticipation orientation**

> Do not make clinician-assisted death an institutional service; provide intensive palliative and end-of-life care and preserve patients' ability to seek lawful options outside the system.

**Provenance:** adapted from the substantial public minority describing physician-assisted death as morally wrong (35% in the 2025 Pew survey). The continuity/nonabandonment clause is Bench policy completion and should be labeled as such.

These are genuinely different institutional rules rather than two descriptions of personal willingness.

### Expert / policy candidates

**exp1 — safeguard-based legal access architecture**

> Permit clinician participation only for legally eligible, decisionally capable terminally ill adults after required assessments, informed consent, voluntariness safeguards and documented opportunity to withdraw.

**Provenance:** Oregon's mature Death with Dignity statutory/regulatory architecture, used explicitly as a **policy** source rather than professional ethical consensus. Oregon's 2025 report documents continued operation of the framework.

**exp2 — professional nonparticipation architecture**

> Do not authorize physician prescribing intended to enable death; instead require nonabandonment, communication, comfort care and adequate relief of suffering at the end of life.

**Provenance:** AMA Opinion 5.7 and ACP opposition. AMA's separate conscience language should remain metadata rather than softening this candidate into the same policy as exp1.

AAHPM's studied neutrality belongs in expert metadata because it helps explain why the professional field cannot simply be described as unanimous opposition, but it is not necessary as a third candidate.

### Framework candidates

**fw1 — autonomy/relief of suffering**

> Permit safeguarded medical aid in dying for a capable terminally ill adult because control over an approaching death can be a central autonomous interest when suffering or loss of function is unacceptable to that person.

**fw2 — professional-role/nonmaleficence**

> Prohibit clinician participation in intentionally enabling death because medicine's duties of care, palliation and nonabandonment should not be extended to providing the means of death.

### Profile

Symmetric `2×2×2`; existing symmetric profile shape is sufficient. The record would be Full Corpus, not Featured.

### Main risks before transcription

- do not conflate self-administered MAID with clinician-administered euthanasia;
- keep legal permission stipulated in both representations;
- do not describe Oregon policy as medical consensus;
- public percentages support orientations, not exact wording;
- individual clinician conscience is a separate implementation issue from institutional participation.

## M033 — advance request for euthanasia after loss of capacity in dementia

### Audit result: provisional `2 public × 1 expert × 2 framework`

The deep file initially allowed for a possible 2×2×2 structure. Candidate audit shows that the second “expert” position is currently **physician attitude evidence**, not a comparably authoritative professional policy. It should not be promoted into a second expert candidate.

### Public candidates

**pub1 — advance-request eligibility orientation**

> Permit euthanasia in advanced dementia when a clear competent advance directive applies and the remaining legal safeguards can be independently established despite loss of current communicative capacity.

**Provenance:** adapted from Dutch public support for advanced-dementia euthanasia on a prior request (60% in PMID 32652560; 77% in older stakeholder data, PMID 22695742). Safeguard wording reflects the represented Dutch policy context rather than the public survey itself.

**pub2 — contemporaneous-confirmation orientation**

> Do not perform euthanasia after the person can no longer communicate a current request, even when a prior directive exists; use the directive instead to guide ordinary treatment and comfort decisions.

**Provenance:** adapted from the substantial public minority not supporting advance-request euthanasia and from qualitative public concerns about uncertainty. This is a policy translation; the survey did not prescribe the ordinary-treatment clause.

### Expert / policy candidate

**exp1 — Dutch advance-directive due-care pathway**

> Allow a competent advance directive to replace the oral request after capacity is lost, but require the physician to establish all other statutory due-care criteria and use exceptional scrutiny before performing euthanasia.

**Provenance:** current Regional Euthanasia Review Committees guidance, latest EuthanasiaCode 1 January 2026.

Dutch GP surveys show substantial professional reluctance, including 42.2% disagreement that an advance directive can replace an oral request (PMID 34789166). That is important professional-attitude evidence but does not yet amount to a second authoritative clinical policy architecture. Do not fabricate `exp2` from a survey attitude.

### Framework candidates

**fw1 — precedent autonomy**

> Honor a specific, repeatedly affirmed advance request when its stated conditions are met because loss of capacity should not erase the person's competent prior authority over how life ends.

**fw2 — current welfare/irreversibility**

> Require contemporaneous communicative confirmation before assisted death because the current person still has experiential interests and an irreversible intervention should not proceed when present wishes and suffering cannot be reliably established.

### Profile

Existing `full-corpus-2x1x2-mean-v1`, if independent review approves. Mean required; no new profile.

### Main risks before transcription

- explicitly Netherlands/2026;
- directive does not alone satisfy due-care criteria;
- current calm/agitation cannot be made dispositive;
- public opposition should not be attributed to physicians and vice versa;
- distinguish M033 from M027 treatment refusal.

## M034 — CANH in prolonged disorder of consciousness

### Audit result: provisional `2 public × 1 expert × 2 framework`

Affected-family evidence is genuinely plural at the treatment-policy level, while current professional guidance converges on individualized diagnosis/prognosis and shared ethical decision-making.

### Public / affected candidates

**pub1 — continue absent clear refusal**

> Continue clinically assisted nutrition and hydration when prior wishes are uncertain and meaningful diagnostic or prognostic uncertainty remains, while maintaining serial specialist reassessment.

**Provenance:** adapted from family accounts favoring continued life-prolonging treatment, uncertainty concerns and symbolic/moral concern about withdrawing food and fluids (PMID 26539603; 24425753).

**pub2 — permit values-based withdrawal**

> Permit withdrawal of clinically assisted nutrition and hydration when a careful surrogate/best-interests process finds that continued non-restorative treatment is inconsistent with the patient's likely values and interests, with full palliative care.

**Provenance:** adapted from family accounts in which relatives believed the patient would not have wanted prolonged treatment and from diverse family views about proper treatment (PMID 26539603; 24425753). The formal best-interests process is a Bench policy translation.

These are not merely emotional reactions: they yield different treatment rules under the same uncertain prior-wish scenario.

### Expert candidate

**exp1 — diagnostic rigor + individualized AANH decision**

> Use serial standardized disorder-of-consciousness assessment and an interdisciplinary benefit/burden and patient-preference process before continuing or withdrawing clinically assisted nutrition and hydration.

**Provenance:** AAN/ACRM/NIDILRR DoC guideline (reaffirmed 2024), ASPEN AANH ethics position (PMID 33616284) and ESPEN ethics guidance. Current professional sources do not justify a second expert endpoint.

### Framework candidates

**fw1 — life preservation/epistemic humility**

> Continue nutrition and hydration when prior wishes are unknown because irreversible withdrawal should not rest on uncertain prognosis or assumptions about the value of life with profound disability.

**fw2 — autonomy/proportionality**

> Permit withdrawal when rigorous review finds continued CANH inconsistent with the person's values and unable to provide a benefit they would regard as worthwhile, while ensuring comfort care.

### Profile

Existing `full-corpus-2x1x2-mean-v1`, if independent review approves.

### Main risks before transcription

- both representations must preserve serial standardized assessment and residual uncertainty;
- no disability-as-harm assumption;
- avoid describing tube feeding simply as ordinary oral feeding;
- public candidate wording should not imply families are legally decisive everywhere.

## M036 — withholding versus withdrawing equivalent treatment

### Audit result: **do not transcribe yet**

The public evidence clearly demonstrates a withholding/withdrawing asymmetry, but candidate translation does not yet yield two independent public policies.

A defensible public-derived candidate is:

> Require a stronger communication or review process before withdrawing an established life-sustaining treatment than before declining to initiate the same treatment.

This follows the experimentally observed greater reluctance toward withdrawal (PMID 38912645) and cross-cultural physician/public attitude gaps.

What would `pub2` be? “Treat them identically” is not directly supported as a separate public orientation by the current studies merely because some participants accept withdrawal. The strongest **equivalence** source is professional (AMA), not public. Splitting the sample around mean responses would manufacture a second policy not established by the data.

The expert layer likewise has one canonical architecture:

> Treat withholding and withdrawal as ethically equivalent when prognosis, goals and consequences are materially identical.

Normative scholarship supplies genuine framework disagreement, but that cannot substitute for a missing public candidate.

A `1 public × 1 expert × 2 framework` record would require a new profile. There is no substantive reason to introduce that profile merely to make M036 executable.

**Classification:** `needs-additional-evidence`.

The case remains methodologically valuable and should stay in the Full Corpus. Further public work might eventually support a second policy orientation, but no new profile or infrastructure change is justified now.
