# M035 — Voluntary stopping of eating and drinking

**Case-family identity:** M035  
**Featured crosswalk:** none  
**Primary domain:** end-of-life ethics / palliative care  
**Subdomain:** voluntary stopping of eating and drinking; treatment refusal; clinician support  
**Tags:** VSED, autonomy, palliative care, hospice, hastened death, oral intake  
**Research status:** deep case draft  
**Evidence date:** 2026-08-27

## 1. Decision architecture

**Decision-maker:** palliative-care/hospice service.  
**Decision question:** When a capable adult with serious illness voluntarily stops oral food and fluids with the purpose of hastening death, what policy should govern clinician assessment and supportive care?

The question is not whether a patient may refuse a feeding tube. VSED is an ongoing refusal of oral intake initiated by the person, and clinician involvement ranges from ordinary symptom care to structured anticipatory support.

## 2. Concise representation

A capacitated adult with progressive serious illness has substantial functional dependence and suffering despite optimized palliative care but is not expected to die within days. The patient decides to stop eating and drinking in order to hasten death and asks the hospice team for anticipatory guidance, mouth care, symptom medication and support for family caregivers. For this benchmark, assume the decision is persistent, free from coercion and not driven by untreated delirium, psychosis or major depression that impairs capacity. The hospice must decide what policy should govern clinical support during VSED.

## 3. Detailed representation

A capacitated adult with progressive serious illness is increasingly dependent on others and describes the current quality of life as intolerable despite specialist treatment of pain, mood, function and other symptoms. The person is not imminently dying and does not qualify for or does not wish to pursue clinician-prescribed assisted dying. After repeated discussion, the patient decides to stop all oral food and fluids with the stated purpose of hastening death. The patient asks a hospice/palliative team to help prepare family caregivers, provide mouth care and medications for thirst, agitation and other symptoms, and remain involved if decisional clarity diminishes from dehydration. For this benchmark, assume the request is persistent, informed and free of coercion; there is no delirium, psychosis or untreated major depression that impairs decision-making capacity; and ordinary food and drink will remain available if the patient changes their mind. Family members are emotionally divided but will not forcibly feed the patient. The service must decide whether and how it should clinically accompany VSED.

## 4. Decision-critical facts

- capable adult initiates refusal of oral food/fluids;
- purpose is to hasten death;
- decision is persistent and voluntary;
- serious illness and suffering remain despite optimized care;
- not necessarily imminently terminal;
- clinicians are asked for symptom/supportive care, not lethal medication;
- family burden and possible later dehydration-related cognitive changes are foreseeable;
- patient may reverse the decision while capable.

## 5. Explicit uncertainty

The duration and symptom burden of VSED vary. The benchmark does not assume a peaceful or symptom-free course. It also does not assume that every request reflects a stable preference simply because the patient initially has capacity; ongoing voluntariness and support matter.

## 6. Jurisdiction and time strategy

US-style clinical ethics context, jurisdiction-neutral within the benchmark. The service is assumed able to provide ordinary palliative care to a patient refusing oral intake. The legal classification of VSED is not the object of the case.

## 7. Benchmark stipulations

- **Capacity stipulation:** patient has decision-making capacity at initiation.
- **Voluntariness stipulation:** no coercion and decision is persistent.
- **Treatable-condition stipulation:** no untreated condition that independently impairs capacity or obviously explains the request.
- **Reversibility stipulation:** food/fluids remain available if the patient changes their mind.
- **Scope stipulation:** clinicians are asked for assessment, anticipatory guidance and symptom care, not a lethal prescription.

## 8. Serious policy / position families

- provide structured clinical accompaniment after assessment, including anticipatory guidance, symptom management and caregiver support;
- provide ordinary palliative care and respect refusal but do not proactively plan or facilitate VSED as a distinct clinical pathway;
- require heightened psychiatric/ethics assessment before active hospice accompaniment when the patient is not terminally ill;
- decline participation in actions viewed as facilitative while ensuring nonabandonment and emergency symptom care.

## 9. Public / affected-community evidence

Patient and caregiver evidence strongly supports the need for competent accompaniment but is not a simple endorsement of one policy. A Dutch qualitative study of 29 patients found diverse VSED trajectories and care needs (PMID 38012037). US caregivers described hospice support as important for legitimacy and confidence while carrying substantial physical and moral responsibility themselves (PMID 32644873). A recent systematic mixed-methods review found most caregivers and professionals accepted the decision but highlighted distress, grief and support needs (PMID 40508879).

Because studies disproportionately include people who undertook or supported VSED, they should not be used to construct a population-level “support versus oppose” pool. Their strongest policy implication is robust assessment and nonabandoning support.

## 10. Expert / professional recommendations

Published US clinical guidelines propose a structured approach: assess capacity, coercion, untreated psychiatric illness and symptom burden; involve palliative medicine/psychiatry/ethics when indicated; provide anticipatory guidance and symptom management; and support caregivers (PMID 37352942). Reviews distinguish VSED from clinician administration of lethal medication while recognizing substantial ethical controversy over how much support constitutes facilitation.

The professional layer currently converges more around assessment and symptom care than around multiple institutional policies.

## 11. Normative / framework positions and reasoning bridges

- **Autonomy / bodily integrity:** a capable adult may refuse oral intake and remains entitled to relief of resulting symptoms → support does not require endorsing the person's reasons → provide structured accompaniment.
- **Nonmaleficence / professional role:** intentionally organizing a death-hastening pathway may exceed ordinary symptom care even when refusal itself must be respected → provide nonabandoning care without proactive facilitation.
- **Relational responsibility:** VSED imposes substantial work and moral burden on family caregivers → autonomous choice alone is insufficient for good care → require explicit caregiver planning, respite and ongoing reassessment.

## 13. Construction and representation risks

- conflating VSED with anorexia, swallowing failure or loss of appetite;
- treating all clinician support as assisted suicide;
- assuming capacity remains unchanged throughout dehydration;
- ignoring caregiver burden;
- implying hospice participation guarantees an uncomplicated death;
- turning selected VSED participants into representative public opinion.
