## Professional convergence persists

The n=50 and n=100 pattern remains pronounced. Professional/governance bodies often synthesize competing values into a single conditional practice rule while public/affected evidence or normative frameworks retain multiple orientations.

Repeated reasons for non-execution through M150 include:

- one integrated professional rule with multiple nested conditions;
- public support/concern that does not specify an institutional action;
- affected evidence supporting one consent/control orientation but not a competing policy;
- legal variation that cannot be treated as expert ethical pluralism;
- frontier technology whose future policy would require extrapolation beyond current professional sources;
- direct professional disagreement without a valid public/affected pool;
- measurement/governance alternatives without direct affected-community evidence selecting among them.

## Measurement cases are first-class even when non-executable

M129 (QALYs and disability) is retained as a major corpus case despite current non-executable status. NICE's use of QALYs with severity/other modifiers and ICER's reporting of equal-value life years show that real institutions already use different measurement architectures. The missing element is sufficiently direct disability-community evidence supporting competing institutional measurement rules. The corpus records that evidentiary boundary rather than constructing a replacement metric on the community's behalf.

This distinction is methodologically important for the broader normative-computation program: a disagreement about what a measure represents can be a central ethical problem even when the source architecture does not yet support an executable SACRE candidate set.

## AI/data block lessons

M141–M150 demonstrate that a rapidly developing domain does not automatically yield more executable cases.

- M141 commercial data partnerships survives because public evidence directly changes with commercial recipient and consent/benefit conditions.
- M144 AI disclosure survives because patients directly distinguish broad disclosure from context/materiality-sensitive disclosure.
- M146 ambient AI documentation does **not** survive: high patient acceptability is not evidence for a default-use policy when the strongest consent studies recommend permission before use.
- M148 prior authorization does not survive because professional guidance converges on physician review and direct public policy evidence is absent.
- M149 model updating does not survive despite FDA's mature PCCP architecture because the affected/public source pool is missing.

These refusals are examples of the strict construction standard working as intended.
