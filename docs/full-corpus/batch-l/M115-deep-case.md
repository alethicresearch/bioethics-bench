# M115 — International recruitment of health workers from shortage countries

**Case-family identity:** M115  
**Primary domain:** global health / workforce ethics  
**Subdomain:** international recruitment; health-system vulnerability  
**Tags:** health workers, migration, recruitment, WHO Global Code, brain drain, co-investment  
**Research status:** deep case draft  
**Evidence date:** 2026-08-27

## 1. Decision architecture

**Decision-maker:** high-income health system/employer.  
**Decision question:** Should a health system actively recruit nurses or physicians from a country identified as having severe health-workforce vulnerability?

## 2. Concise representation

A high-income health system has major staffing vacancies and is considering a targeted recruitment campaign in a country on WHO's 2026 health-workforce support and safeguards list. Individual clinicians from that country remain free to apply for jobs abroad. The employer must decide whether to refrain from active recruitment, recruit only through a bilateral agreement that provides proportional workforce/health-system investment to the source country, or recruit openly while relying on individual workers' right to migrate.

## 3. Detailed representation

A hospital network in a high-income country faces persistent shortages of nurses and physicians. A recruitment agency proposes a large targeted campaign in a lower-income country whose health-worker density and service coverage place it on WHO's 2026 Support and Safeguards List. The source government has not requested exemption from the safeguards. Individual health workers retain the right to seek jobs abroad and some actively want migration opportunities. WHO's amended Global Code distinguishes that individual freedom from systematic active recruitment and encourages government-to-government arrangements and proportional co-investment when recruitment occurs. The employer must decide whether to avoid active recruitment from the listed country; recruit only through a bilateral agreement that supports source-country workforce priorities; or conduct ordinary active recruitment on the view that limiting employer outreach unfairly constrains workers' opportunities.

## 4. Decision-critical facts

- source country is on the current WHO safeguards list;
- source government has not opted out of safeguards;
- individual workers remain free to apply independently;
- proposed action is active systematic recruitment by employer/agency;
- bilateral co-investment is feasible.

## 5. Explicit uncertainty

Migration can benefit workers through income, training and remittances while harming source-system capacity. Net effects vary by occupation, return migration and policy design.

## 6. Jurisdiction and time strategy

Evidence-dated 2026 global-health governance case reflecting the amended WHO Global Code and 2026 safeguards framework.

## 7. Benchmark stipulations

- For this benchmark, assume the source country is on the final 2026 WHO support/safeguards list and has not requested exemption.
- For this benchmark, assume individual health workers may still independently seek employment abroad.

## 8. Serious policy / position families

- no active recruitment from vulnerable source countries;
- recruitment only through bilateral co-investment agreement;
- ordinary recruitment based on worker freedom and destination need.

## 9. Public / affected-community evidence

Health-worker migration research documents mixed worker experiences and motivations, but direct affected-worker comparisons of these employer policy architectures are insufficient for the current executable standard. Individual desire to migrate is not evidence for unrestricted employer targeting.

## 10. Expert / professional recommendations

WHO's 2026 framework is explicit: refrain from active international recruitment from listed vulnerable countries, while preserving individual freedom to migrate; where recruitment occurs, government-to-government agreements and proportional support for source health systems are encouraged. This is one integrated conditional professional architecture.

## 11. Normative / framework positions and reasoning bridges

- **Global nonmaleficence:** destination systems should not solve their shortages by materially worsening access in vulnerable source systems → avoid active recruitment.
- **Fair benefit sharing:** migration can be legitimate if destination gains are matched by source-country workforce investment → use bilateral co-investment.
- **Worker autonomy:** health professionals are not state resources and should be able to pursue career opportunities → recruitment restrictions require care not to become migration restrictions.

## 13. Construction and representation risks

- confusing active employer recruitment with individual migration;
- treating health workers as property of source countries;
- assuming remittances offset service loss automatically;
- splitting bilateral safeguards from the WHO no-active-recruitment architecture as though unrelated.
