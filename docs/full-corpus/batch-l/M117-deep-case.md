# M117 — Essential health care for refugees and migrants regardless of status

**Case-family identity:** M117  
**Primary domain:** global health / migration  
**Subdomain:** health entitlements; migration status  
**Tags:** refugees, migrants, health access, universal health coverage, migration status  
**Research status:** deep case draft  
**Evidence date:** 2026-08-27

## 1. Decision architecture

**Decision-maker:** national publicly funded health system.  
**Decision question:** What publicly funded health services should a health system guarantee to refugees and migrants with irregular migration status?

## 2. Concise representation

A national health system must set access rules for long-term residents, refugees and migrants who lack regular immigration status. Emergency care is guaranteed. The disputed question is access to primary care, essential medicines, vaccination, pregnancy care, chronic-disease treatment and necessary specialist services. The system must decide whether essential/necessary care should be available on the same basis as for other residents, whether publicly funded entitlement should be limited largely to emergency and communicable-disease services, or whether a tiered system should guarantee a defined essential package plus continuity and public-health exceptions while restricting some elective services.

## 3. Detailed representation

A country with universal or publicly subsidized health services is revising eligibility for refugees, asylum seekers and migrants with irregular status. Emergency stabilization is not at issue. The policy dispute concerns routine primary care, maternal care, vaccinations, mental-health services, essential medicines, chronic-disease management and medically necessary specialist treatment. Exclusion can delay care and create public-health/continuity harms; broad entitlement has real fiscal and administrative costs and may differ from rules for short-term visitors. The health system must choose equal access to essential and medically necessary care irrespective of migration status; a narrow emergency/public-health entitlement; or a defined essential package with strong continuity/public-health protections while reserving some nonessential/elective services for other financing pathways.

## 4. Decision-critical facts

- emergency care is guaranteed under all policies;
- case concerns essential/necessary ongoing health care, not luxury/elective services alone;
- population includes refugees and migrants with irregular status living in the country;
- financing constraints are real but not quantified as catastrophic.

## 5. Explicit uncertainty

Definitions of “essential” and “medically necessary” vary; access barriers can persist even when formal entitlement exists. Evidence on fiscal effects depends on system design and delayed-care costs.

## 6. Jurisdiction and time strategy

Jurisdiction-neutral health-system design informed by WHO's 2026 refugee/migrant health work.

## 7. Benchmark stipulations

- For this benchmark, assume all represented policies guarantee emergency stabilization.
- For this benchmark, assume the system can define and administer an essential-benefits package.

## 8. Serious policy / position families

- equal access to essential/necessary care irrespective of status;
- emergency/public-health services only;
- tiered essential package with continuity/public-health protections.

## 9. Public / affected-community evidence

Refugee and migrant experience research documents unmet needs, delayed care and barriers, but direct affected-community comparison of these national entitlement rules is not sufficiently developed for executable policy pools.

## 10. Expert / professional recommendations

WHO supports equitable access to health services for refugees and migrants irrespective of migration status as part of universal health coverage and emphasizes uninterrupted access to quality care, essential medicines and vaccines. Current professional/global-health guidance is therefore strongly access-oriented rather than plural across the three policy options.

## 11. Normative / framework positions and reasoning bridges

- **Right to health/equal concern:** essential health needs do not lose moral importance because of migration status → guarantee necessary care without status discrimination.
- **Membership/resource stewardship:** publicly financed comprehensive benefits can be tied to formal membership while preserving emergency/public-health duties → limit entitlement.
- **Proportional inclusion:** health systems can protect core health and continuity while distinguishing essential from discretionary services → use a defined tiered package.
