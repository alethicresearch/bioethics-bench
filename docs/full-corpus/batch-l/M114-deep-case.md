# M114 — Global allocation of a scarce vaccine or therapeutic

**Case-family identity:** M114  
**Primary domain:** global health / scarcity  
**Subdomain:** international countermeasure allocation  
**Tags:** vaccines, therapeutics, global allocation, equity, pandemic, reciprocity  
**Research status:** deep case draft  
**Evidence date:** 2026-08-27

## 1. Decision architecture

**Decision-maker:** international allocation mechanism distributing a scarce pandemic countermeasure among countries.  
**Decision question:** During severe global scarcity, how should available doses be divided among countries that differ in population, outbreak burden, health-system vulnerability and manufacturing contribution?

## 2. Concise representation

An effective pandemic vaccine is available, but global supply is enough for only a fraction of people during the first six months. Countries differ in population size, current mortality, health-system strain and manufacturing contribution. The international mechanism must decide whether to begin with equal population-proportional shares, allocate primarily according to current epidemiologic need and expected health benefit, or reserve a limited reciprocity tranche for countries that contributed manufacturing or frontline global-response capacity while distributing the remainder by need and equity.

## 3. Detailed representation

An international financing and procurement mechanism controls a large but insufficient supply of a highly effective vaccine during a severe pandemic. All participating countries have accepted common reporting and delivery requirements. Some countries have much larger populations; some face rapidly rising mortality and fragile health systems; several invested heavily in manufacturing or supplied globally scarce inputs and health workers. Supply cannot cover all high-risk groups immediately. The mechanism must choose a transparent first-phase allocation rule: equal per-capita or population-proportional access so every country can protect a comparable initial share; dynamic need/benefit allocation weighted toward current outbreak burden, vulnerable populations and health-system collapse risk; or a multiprinciple rule that reserves a bounded reciprocity contribution for countries providing critical global-response resources while directing most doses according to need and equity.

## 4. Decision-critical facts

- decision-maker is international rather than national;
- supply is severely scarce for a defined initial period;
- countries differ in outbreak burden, population and contribution;
- wealthy purchasing power is not allowed to decide allocation directly;
- delivery capacity exists in all represented countries.

## 5. Explicit uncertainty

Outbreak burden changes over time, data quality differs across countries, and production contributions can reflect historical wealth rather than moral sacrifice.

## 6. Jurisdiction and time strategy

Future-pandemic but evidence-grounded global allocation case using WHO/COVAX and 2025 pandemic-countermeasure guidance.

## 7. Benchmark stipulations

- For this benchmark, assume participating countries can deliver the allocated product effectively.
- For this benchmark, assume supply is insufficient for all priority populations during the first phase.
- For this benchmark, assume allocation is not contractually predetermined by purchasing power.

## 8. Serious policy / position families

- equal initial population-proportional access;
- dynamic need/epidemiologic-benefit allocation;
- multiprinciple need/equity allocation with bounded reciprocity recognition.

## 9. Public / affected-community evidence

Cross-national public research after COVID-19 documents values concerning global solidarity, national priority, vulnerability and reciprocity, but much of it asks about principles or willingness to share rather than the exact country-level rules above. Under the Full Corpus standard, that evidence should not be converted into institutional candidates without a direct policy mapping.

## 10. Expert / professional recommendations

WHO's global COVID allocation framework used an initial broadly population-proportional phase followed by risk-sensitive allocation, while later WHO ethics guidance emphasizes equity, efficiency, reciprocity and procedural fairness as jointly relevant. These are stages/components of integrated global allocation architectures rather than clean competing professional policies.

## 11. Normative / framework positions and reasoning bridges

- **Equal moral standing of countries/persons:** initial access should not depend on wealth or negotiating power → allocate a comparable population share first.
- **Need/benefit:** scarce doses should prevent the most death and system collapse → direct more supply toward current high-risk settings.
- **Fair reciprocity within equity:** contributors to global response can receive limited recognition when doing so sustains cooperative production, provided reciprocity does not displace urgent need.
