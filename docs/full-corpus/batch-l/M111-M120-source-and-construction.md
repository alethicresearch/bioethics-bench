# Bioethics Bench — Batch L source and construction packet (M111–M120)

**Evidence date:** 2026-08-27  
**Scope:** directed donation discrimination, paired exchange, multi-organ allocation, global countermeasure allocation, health-worker recruitment, pharmaceutical IP/access, migrant health, clinical neutrality, technology transfer and global gene-therapy financing.

## M111 — Directed donation with discriminatory conditions

OPTN ethical principles permit directed donation to a named individual as an autonomy exception but explicitly oppose donations directed to a social group based on race, religion, gender or sexual orientation. The case should distinguish naming a loved one from excluding or preferring a protected class. Direct affected/public evidence comparing program policies is limited. Likely research-complete-not-executable.

## M112 — Paired kidney exchange and chain obligations

Current living-donor consent guidance requires that donors remain free to withdraw up to surgery; OPTN ethics also warns programs against pressuring donors not to back out of paired exchanges/chains. The ethically serious question is how programs can reduce reliance and communicate commitment without coercively eliminating withdrawal. Professional guidance is strongly convergent; likely non-executable.

## M113 — Multi-organ transplantation versus separate recipients

HRSA/OPTN recognizes the direct opportunity cost of giving multiple organs to one candidate rather than separate single-organ candidates and is actively standardizing multi-organ allocation. Eligibility criteria and safety nets aim to identify candidates who benefit most while protecting access for single-organ candidates. Public/affected policy evidence is limited. Strong research case, likely non-executable.

## M114 — Global allocation of scarce vaccine or therapeutic

WHO global allocation frameworks combine equal initial access, epidemiologic risk/need, health-system protection, equity, reciprocity, efficiency and transparent procedural fairness. The case should use a fixed international decision-maker and explicit outbreak burden/manufacturing contributions. Public cross-national values evidence exists but often concerns principles rather than concrete country-allocation rules. Candidate audit only if direct allocation-policy evidence is found.

## M115 — International recruitment of health workers from shortage countries

WHO amended the Global Code in May 2026. The 2026 Support and Safeguards List recommends refraining from active recruitment from vulnerable countries, while explicitly preserving individual workers' freedom to pursue migration. Recruitment can occur through government-to-government agreements with proportional support/co-investment for source health systems. This creates a strong integrated professional architecture, not an individual migration ban. Direct affected-worker policy evidence should be sought before execution.

## M116 — Pharmaceutical IP versus access during major health need

Policy families include preserving exclusivity with subsidized procurement, compulsory licensing/technology sharing, and negotiated tiered licensing/manufacturing transfer. WHO, WTO/TRIPS public-health flexibilities and technology-access initiatives provide governance anchors, but this case can easily collapse innovation, manufacturing capacity and price into one slogan. Direct patient/public policy evidence is likely insufficient for execution.

## M117 — Essential health care for refugees and migrants regardless of status

WHO's 2026 refugee/migrant health work and standing guidance support equitable access to essential health care irrespective of migration status as part of universal health coverage. A defensible case should focus on scope/financing and continuity, not construct denial of emergency care as a peer professional position. Likely professional convergence and non-executable status.

## M118 — Clinical neutrality in armed conflict

WMA guidance is explicit: physicians must provide impartial care without discrimination; no distinction between civilian or combatant patients is permitted except based on clinical facts/need. Security separation can occur outside bedside triage. The corpus should retain this as a consensus/control case rather than manufacture a professional civilian-priority rule.

## M119 — Technology transfer versus immediate procurement

WHO supports sustainable local production and technology transfer to improve access and health security; its mRNA Technology Transfer Programme explicitly aims to build regional production capacity and future pandemic resilience. A donor-policy case can compare immediate lowest-cost procurement with capacity-building or a staged hybrid, but affected-country/donor evidence must directly support these institutional choices before execution.

## M120 — Global allocation of a curative but ultra-expensive gene therapy

A fixed marginal international health budget could fund a small number of curative treatments or high-impact primary/public-health services. Serious policy families include total-health maximization, severity/rescue priority and a reserved portfolio. Current HTA and global-access frameworks illuminate the tradeoff, but there is no mature global professional allocation rule for this exact comparison. Likely research-complete-not-executable unless direct priority-setting evidence supports actionable policies.

## Batch-L working hypothesis

No case is presumptively executable. M114 and M119 may merit candidate audit if current direct policy-preference evidence is identified; otherwise this batch should remain a high-value consensus/structural-governance block. No new benchmark profile should be introduced to rescue execution.
