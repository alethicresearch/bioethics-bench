# M113 — Multi-organ transplantation versus separate recipients

**Case-family identity:** M113  
**Primary domain:** transplantation / allocation  
**Subdomain:** multi-organ allocation; opportunity cost  
**Tags:** multi-organ transplant, allocation, opportunity cost, utility, fairness  
**Research status:** deep case draft  
**Evidence date:** 2026-08-27

## 1. Decision architecture

**Decision-maker:** national organ-allocation authority.  
**Decision question:** When one candidate could receive two organs from the same donor while two other candidates could each receive one, how should the system weigh combined-transplant benefit against opportunities for separate recipients?

## 2. Concise representation

A deceased donor has two organs that could be transplanted together into one critically ill candidate or separately into two single-organ candidates. For this benchmark, assume all three are medically compatible and the expected outcomes for each option are explicitly estimated. The authority must decide whether a multi-organ candidate should receive both organs whenever a clinical eligibility threshold is met, whether separate transplantation should be preferred when it benefits more patients overall, or whether a multiprinciple rule should reserve multi-organ allocation for cases where urgency and expected combined benefit cross a high threshold.

## 3. Detailed representation

A national transplant system must allocate two organs recovered from one deceased donor. Candidate A has failure of both organs and could receive a simultaneous combined transplant. Candidates B and C each need one of the organs. For this benchmark, assume compatibility and expected short-term surgical outcomes are acceptable for all three, and the allocation authority has reliable estimates of survival benefit and urgency. Giving both organs to A may offer substantial integrated benefit and avoid future organ failure, but it also means two single-organ candidates lose an opportunity. The authority must choose whether satisfying a defined medical eligibility criterion for multi-organ transplantation is sufficient for priority, whether two single-organ transplants should be preferred when aggregate expected benefit is higher, or whether multi-organ allocation should require a combined urgency/benefit threshold with safety-net protection for candidates initially receiving only one organ.

## 4. Decision-critical facts

- one donor can serve one multi-organ or two single-organ candidates;
- outcomes and urgency are explicitly represented;
- opportunity cost is direct and visible;
- safety-net mechanisms can reduce risk of initially choosing one organ.

## 5. Explicit uncertainty

Long-term outcomes may be correlated across organs, and sequential single-organ strategies can fail. Equity effects vary by organ and candidate subgroup.

## 6. Jurisdiction and time strategy

Current US-facing allocation governance informed by OPTN multi-organ policy development, with policy authority stipulated.

## 7. Benchmark stipulations

- For this benchmark, assume all candidates are medically eligible and compatible.
- For this benchmark, assume expected outcome differences are available to the authority.

## 8. Serious policy / position families

- multi-organ priority once defined medical eligibility is met;
- prefer separate recipients when aggregate expected benefit is higher;
- multiprinciple threshold plus safety net.

## 9. Public / affected-community evidence

Direct public or patient evidence comparing multi-organ and single-organ allocation policies is limited. General allocation preferences for saving more lives cannot be translated into a specific multi-organ rule.

## 10. Expert / professional recommendations

OPTN explicitly recognizes the opportunity cost of multi-organ allocation and has developed eligibility criteria and safety nets while continuing policy work to improve equity for both multi-organ and single-organ candidates. Current policy is therefore a conditional, multiprinciple architecture rather than a simple maximize-number-transplanted rule.

## 11. Normative / framework positions and reasoning bridges

- **Integrated clinical benefit:** some candidates need multiple organs to obtain meaningful benefit at all → eligibility for combined transplantation can justify allocating both organs.
- **Aggregate benefit:** using two organs for two recipients can produce more total health/lives saved → prefer separate allocation when benefit differences are substantial.
- **Fair multiprinciple allocation:** urgency, expected benefit and opportunity cost all matter → use a threshold rule with safety nets rather than one principle alone.
