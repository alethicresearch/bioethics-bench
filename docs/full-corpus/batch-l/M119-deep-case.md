# M119 — Technology transfer versus immediate procurement

**Case-family identity:** M119  
**Primary domain:** global health / technology access  
**Subdomain:** local manufacturing; donor policy  
**Tags:** technology transfer, local manufacturing, vaccines, resilience, procurement  
**Research status:** deep case draft  
**Evidence date:** 2026-08-27

## 1. Decision architecture

**Decision-maker:** international donor financing a vaccine/diagnostic program in a lower-resource country.  
**Decision question:** Should funding prioritize the fastest near-term procurement or invest materially in local manufacturing and technology transfer even when initial delivery is slower?

## 2. Concise representation

A donor has a fixed five-year budget for vaccines and diagnostics in a lower-resource region. Purchasing finished products from established international manufacturers would deliver more doses in the first two years. Funding a local manufacturing and technology-transfer partnership would deliver fewer products initially but could create regional production capacity, trained staff and faster future emergency response. The donor must choose immediate lowest-cost procurement, local-capacity priority, or a staged hybrid that guarantees current supply while tying additional investment to manufacturing milestones.

## 3. Detailed representation

An international donor supports a lower-resource country's vaccine and diagnostic program. A fixed five-year budget can either purchase large volumes from established foreign manufacturers immediately or finance a technology-transfer partnership involving equipment, regulatory strengthening, workforce training and local production. For this benchmark, assume the local project is technically credible but will produce fewer usable doses during the first two years; if successful, it will provide substantial regional capacity by years three to five and improve preparedness for future outbreaks. The donor must decide whether to maximize immediate procurement, prioritize long-term local production and technical autonomy despite near-term opportunity cost, or split funding through a staged hybrid that secures current essential products while expanding local capacity against transparent performance milestones.

## 4. Decision-critical facts

- fixed budget creates a real near-term opportunity cost;
- local production is technically credible but slower initially;
- technology transfer includes know-how, workforce and regulatory support, not equipment alone;
- long-term capacity has regional resilience value.

## 5. Explicit uncertainty

Local manufacturing projects can fail commercially or become unsustainable if demand is insufficient. Immediate procurement can save more lives now but leave persistent dependency.

## 6. Jurisdiction and time strategy

Jurisdiction-neutral donor-policy case grounded in WHO local-production and mRNA technology-transfer initiatives.

## 7. Benchmark stipulations

- For this benchmark, assume local manufacturing can meet quality standards if the planned capacity investment succeeds.
- For this benchmark, assume immediate procurement delivers more doses during the first two years but does not create local production capacity.

## 8. Serious policy / position families

- immediate lowest-cost procurement;
- priority investment in local capacity/technology transfer;
- staged hybrid with current supply plus milestone-based capacity investment.

## 9. Public / affected-community evidence

Countries and regional institutions frequently advocate local production and health sovereignty, but direct affected-community comparisons of these donor budget policies are limited. General support for self-sufficiency does not establish the amount of near-term health benefit that should be sacrificed.

## 10. Expert / professional recommendations

WHO supports sustainable local production and technology transfer to improve equitable access and preparedness, including the mRNA Technology Transfer Programme. These programs also emphasize workforce, regulation, quality and sustainability. They demonstrate a strong capacity-building orientation but do not constitute a professional rule that capacity should always override immediate procurement.

## 11. Normative / framework positions and reasoning bridges

- **Immediate beneficence:** current preventable illness has moral urgency → maximize products delivered now when the budget is fixed.
- **Structural justice/capability:** durable technical capacity reduces dependency and improves future self-determination → accept some near-term opportunity cost for local production.
- **Intertemporal proportionality:** present lives and future resilience both matter → guarantee current supply while building capacity in staged milestones.
