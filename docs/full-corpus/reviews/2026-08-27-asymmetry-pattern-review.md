## The interpretation is the part to be careful about

The checkpoint reads the pattern as a property of the source ecology:

> professional organizations often synthesize contested considerations into one practical
> guidance architecture even when affected publics and normative frameworks remain plural.

That may well be true. But the same observation is equally consistent with a property of the
**construction method**, and the two cannot be separated by counting.

Candidates must be actionable institutional policies of the same kind. Professional guidance
already arrives in that form — a guideline *is* a policy architecture, so one document yields
one candidate more or less directly. Public evidence does not: survey heterogeneity is visible
as a distribution, and turning it into candidates requires a translation step that naturally
yields two, because the distribution's two tails are what make it heterogeneous.

So the pipeline has an asymmetry built into it before any case is looked at. The public layer
is translated *from* heterogeneity, and the expert layer is read *from* documents. A method
that searches distributions for spread and guidelines for content will find spread in the
first and coherence in the second — whether or not the world is arranged that way.

Two further contributors, both benign in themselves:

- **Recency.** Current guidance is usually the *latest* statement from each body, and later
  statements tend to have absorbed earlier disagreement. Professional disagreement is often
  visible across time rather than across bodies at one moment.
- **Selection of the source.** Where two societies address the same question, taking the more
  specific or more recent one is good scholarship and also removes a candidate.

## A falsifiable check, before this is load-bearing

The claim is worth making in the paper if it survives an attempt to break it, and worth
dropping if it does not. Proposed, cheap, and does not need the whole corpus:

Take a sample of the 11 `2×1×2` families — six would do — and for each run a **deliberate
adversarial search for a second professional position**: a different national body, a
dissenting society statement, a formal minority position, or an earlier guideline whose
operational rule differs and was not superseded on evidential grounds. Search for professional
disagreement as hard as the public layer is searched for heterogeneity.

- If a real second professional rule is found in **most** of the sample, the asymmetry is
  substantially a search artifact, and the corpus should say so and re-derive those cases.
- If **few or none** turn up after a genuine search, the property is supported, and the paper
  can state it with the evidence of having tried to refute it — which is much stronger than
  stating it from a count.

Either outcome is publishable and the second is a real finding. What should not happen is the
claim entering the paper on the strength of the tally alone, because the tally is exactly what
both explanations predict.
