**Gates applied from scratch**, not presuming the old classification: source-to-policy fidelity,

**Result: 2 clear, 8 do not.** The two that clear are the two nominated as positive controls.

## Batch B fails uniformly, and says so itself

All four Batch-B cases describe their own public layer as construction, in their own audits:

- M012 — *"inferred policy families from qualitative themes rather than direct survey policies"*
- M018 — *"an inferred policy family, not a professional recommendation"*, with the warning that
  `pub1` *"must not be mislabeled as evidence-based prescribing guidance"*
- M019 — *"Bench policy translations from those repeated preference patterns"*
- M020 — *"inferred policy families from those workforce attitudes rather than literal votes on one
  hospital rule"*

Under the current gate the core action must be source-supported before completion is added. In each
of these the *core action itself* is the constructed part: patients' orientations toward seeking a
stem-cell intervention are not a view about what clinicians should do; wanting antibiotics is not a
prescribing policy; preferring video is not a triage rule; willingness to serve is not a position on
whether service should be mandatory.

These audits are not careless. They were written before the standard tightened and were honest
about what they were doing — which is why the disposition can be reached from their own text.

Two secondary findings, not needed for the verdict. In M018 `pub2` (delayed prescription with
activation criteria) and `exp2` (delayed prescription with defined triggers) are the same rule in
two pools. In M019 `pub1` ("allow a video-first option") transfers from preference evidence better
than `pub2` ("**require** in-person"), so even a partial rescue would be one-sided.

## M025 fails the new gate, and is repairable

The scenario states the parents *"decline implantation for now because they value Deaf identity."*
`pub2` reads: *"Favor timely cochlear implantation… **when the family prioritizes spoken-language
and auditory opportunities**."*

The antecedent is false on the represented facts, so `pub2` issues no contrary instruction here.
The public pool therefore does not span the axis in practice, whatever it looks like on paper. This
is the failure mode M144 was cleared against, arriving from the opposite direction — there the
scenario sat where candidates agreed; here the candidate is conditioned on a fact the scenario
denies.

`fw2` is not conditioned that way and does produce the opposing action, so the framework layer
spans. **The fix is a wording change, not a demotion:** an unconditional `pub2` — favour timely
implantation for an eligible child while maintaining sign-language access — is what the
auditory-opportunity evidence supports anyway. Re-audit and it can return.

## M030 has a gap rather than a verdict

`pub1` carries a provenance line; `pub2` carries none. Gate 1 cannot be applied to a candidate with
no stated source support, so this is *unassessable* rather than failed. Separately, `pub1` is
*"adapted directly from preference evidence favouring chemotherapy+hospice"*, and a personal
preference for concurrent care is not a position on a benefit rule that requires other patients to
waive treatment — the concern raised in the brief, which stands.

## Why M028 and M033 clear

Both asked the institutional question rather than a personal one.

**M028** surveyed 1,386 people after a brain-death explainer: 41.9% agreed *a hospital should be
required* to continue treatment. The audit then records the distinction itself — only 24.4% would
request continued treatment for themselves — which is the preference/policy gap measured inside a
single study. The `while transfer or resolution is pursued` clause is declared completion on a
supported core. That is the standard met exactly.

**M033** rests on support for advance-request euthanasia in advanced dementia (60%, 77% in older
stakeholder data). *Whether someone should remain eligible on a prior directive* is an eligibility
rule, not a moral attitude, and `pub2` is the same question's minority rather than a different
construct. The audit also declines to build `exp2` from 42.2% GP disagreement, correctly treating
professional attitude as attitude.

The contrast with M031 is the whole finding in one pair. M031's public layer is 63% "morally
acceptable" and 35% "morally wrong" — an attitude toward the act. M033's is support for an
eligibility rule. Both concern assisted dying; only one asked a policy question.

## What this implies

Eight of ten earlier cases do not survive gates applied after they were written, and the two that
do are the two whose surveys asked institutional questions. That is not a failure of the earlier
work — it is the strongest available evidence that the strict standard is doing something, since
it was applied here by someone who did not build these cases and reached the answer the corpus
author predicted case by case.

It also means the canonical count moves again, and materially. The remaining eleven —
M041, M042, M045, M050, M054, M056, M060, M075, M080, M094, M097 — should be expected to fall
closer to this rate than to the five-of-six seen in the recent batches, since they were audited
under the same earlier conventions.
