Apply the four current hard gates from scratch: source-to-policy fidelity; action distinctness; axis spanning; scenario/action divergence. Do not preserve a case merely because an older audit called it executable.

## Strong positive controls

### M056 — extension of the 14-day embryo-research limit

**Prediction: likely clear.** The public survey directly asks whether embryos should be cultured beyond 14 days. `pub1` retains the boundary and `pub2` permits extension; those are policy-level answers to the represented axis. The specialized-review safeguards in `pub2` are completion on a source-supported core action and should remain explicitly labeled as Bench-authored completion. Check that the scenario is genuinely beyond day 14 and that the expert candidate is one current ISSCR conditional architecture.

### M075 — broad versus study-by-study biobank consent

**Prediction: likely clear.** The representative survey directly compares broad consent with study-by-study consent (52%/48%). Both public candidates correspond to the consent models actually elicited. Check only that `pub2` does not overstate `each materially distinct future study` beyond the source's study-by-study construct and that the represented future use falls within broad-consent scope so the candidates actually diverge.

### M080 — post-trial access

**Prediction: likely clear, but inspect the completion boundary.** Participant research explicitly asks about post-trial obligations and contains both an ongoing-access orientation and a no-special-drug/care-obligation orientation. `pub2`'s responsible-transition language is completion, not the core action. Verify that the represented participant has benefited, still needs the intervention, and lacks an immediately equivalent ordinary source so the two policies diverge.

## Highest-risk cases

### M042 — prenatal screening/disability justice

**Prediction: likely fail gate 1.** The audit itself says `pub1` is an adapted policy translation from support for screening availability and `pub2` converts affected-family stigma/routinization concerns into an explicit opt-in mechanism. The core institutional default/opt-in action appears Bench-supplied rather than directly elicited. Do not let the normative importance of disability-informed counseling substitute for policy evidence.

### M045 — posthumous use of reproductive material

**Prediction: likely fail gate 1.** `pub1` translates general weight on prior consent into an explicit-written-authorization-only rule; `pub2` translates fertility/sperm-banking attitudes and shared family plans into a strong-evidence surviving-partner rule. Check whether the underlying studies actually ask those authorization rules. If not, both core actions are constructed.

### M050 — donor anonymity / identity access

**Prediction: likely fail gate 1 despite strong real-world policy comparators.** Donor-conceived adults' interest in identity does not itself establish a rule of unilateral identity release at 18; the audit explicitly says HFEA supplies the action structure, not the public source. Donor/recipient privacy preferences likewise may not establish a mutual-consent identity-release rule. Keep HFEA/ASRM policy evidence separate from affected-public evidence.

### M054 — genomic secondary findings

**Prediction: pub1 fails gate 1 decisively; likely case failure unless the public pool can be rebuilt without inventing a default.** The audit says the studies show very high uptake of actionable findings, **not preference for an opt-out default**. Uptake under an offered architecture cannot establish the default architecture. For `pub2`, distinguish actual preference for prior category choice from a laboratory's use of opt-in; implementation is not necessarily endorsement.

### M097 — supervised consumption services

**Prediction: public pool vulnerable.** `pub1` relies heavily on willingness of people who use drugs to use an SCS and perceived benefits; willingness to use a service is not necessarily an institutional position that government should establish a permanent service. `pub2` is closer to an implementation-policy source because community stakeholders explicitly discussed local acceptability/evidence before implementation, but one-sided policy grounding is insufficient. Expert pilot-versus-expand pluralism appears genuine and should not rescue a weak public pool.

## Borderline cases

### M041 — publicly funded IVF allocation

**Prediction: genuinely uncertain.** `pub2` is comparatively strong because a representative discrete-choice experiment directly elicits allocation priorities. `pub1` says 62.7% preferred maximizing access rather than maximizing live births, which may support an equal-access rule, but `lottery or uniform queue` is completion and must not become the core. Also scrutinize `exp1`: NICE supports eligibility/effectiveness principles, while applying them as the ranking rule for an oversubscribed fixed queue is explicitly Bench completion. If the expert core action is not itself source-supported, demote rather than treating eligibility as allocation.

### M060 — non-medical sex selection

**Prediction: public layer may pass; expert layer needs strict source review.** Public sources include support/opposition to sex selection and, in some studies, legal prohibition, which may be policy-level enough. But `exp1`'s family-balancing limitation is explicitly described as a synthesis across ASRM and ESHRE rather than a direct recommendation. Determine whether those sources themselves support that bounded clinic policy or whether the core action is assembled by the Bench. `exp2` is more directly contemplated by ASRM.

### M094 — workforce vaccination mandate

**Prediction: direct policy question but contextual-transfer risk may bind.** The Gallup-panel study directly asks whether employer-enforced vaccination mandates are acceptable, so this is not the Batch-B preference→policy problem. However, the benchmark narrows the case to a serious outbreak, patient-facing workers, strong transmission benefit, free access and failed voluntary measures. Decide whether transferring a generic employer mandate attitude into that more mandate-favorable fact pattern preserves source-to-policy fidelity. `pub2`'s no-mandate core is supported by mandate opposition; testing/education/alternatives are completion. Scenario divergence must also be checked against the expert necessity threshold because the scenario already stipulates failed voluntary measures.

## Expected interpretation

The goal is not a target yield. If the three positive controls clear and the explicit translation cases fail, that would further support the methodological distinction between **evidence about a policy** and **evidence about values or preferences from which an author can imagine a policy**. If the predictions are wrong, record why; falsifying the author's expectation is useful review evidence.
