## Verdict: concur, without exception

Batch G yields zero executable of four audited, Batch I zero of five, Batch H two of six
(M075 and M080, both `2×1×2`). I agree with every judgment, and the refusals are the more
impressive half.

Two disciplines recur, and both are the right ones:

**Refusing to split one regime into two candidates.** M070's lottery is a tiebreaker inside a
multi-factor allocation architecture, not a rival to it. M072's rescue availability is a
risk-reduction factor inside WHO's conditional approval, not a second expert rule. M071's rescue
and duration limits are mitigation conditions inside the permissive rule. M086 declines to split
trial protection from residual expanded access. Each of these would have produced exactly the
paraphrase-convergence the M005 correction removed — a case where QCCS reports agreement because
two candidates are one policy described twice.

**Refusing to translate willingness into policy preference.** M070 states it most sharply: a
discrete-choice study showing that patients accept more risk as disease worsens establishes that
severity matters to *treatment* preference, and says nothing about who should receive a scarce
slot when two eligible patients compete. M065, M076 and M083 turn on the same distinction. This
is the single most common way a benchmark case could be quietly falsified, and it is being caught
consistently.

M066 deserves specific credit for catching itself: the audit notes that the difference between
opt-in and opt-out defaults *"would therefore be partly Bench-designed rather than directly
elicited"* — the authored-completion problem, identified without prompting, in the case that
would have been easiest to wave through.

## M068 is the most important case in these batches, and it is not executable

M068 — transfer of an embryo with a manageable monogenic condition — has **two genuinely
action-distinct expert candidates**. ASRM 2025 states that both assisting and declining transfer
can fall within appropriate ethical boundaries. The audit calls this layer "exceptional" and is
right. What blocks the case is the *public* layer: ASRM's account of why patients may request
transfer is professional synthesis, not direct evidence of affected-community policy.

This matters beyond M068, because it is the mirror image of the `2×1×2` pattern.

The concern raised in earlier review was that the pattern — expert converges, public is plural —
might be an artifact of the method rather than a property of the world, since professional
guidance arrives already shaped as policy while public evidence must be translated from
distributions. If that were the whole story, the corpus would rarely if ever find genuine expert
pluralism.

**It just did.** M068's expert layer is plural and passed cleanly; the public layer was the
binding constraint. That is a case the artifact hypothesis does not predict, produced by the
corpus's own process without anyone looking for it.

Taken with the earlier finding that M028's three named bodies were co-authors of one guideline
rather than three merged positions, the artifact hypothesis is now weakly disconfirmed from two
independent directions. It is not dead — only an outside search settles it — but the prior has
moved, and it has moved because of evidence rather than argument.

## The executable yield is falling, and that is a planning fact

Batch G zero of four, Batch H two of six, Batch I zero of five. Against roughly nineteen
executable families in the first fifty, the recent batches are contributing at a visibly lower
rate.

Nothing here suggests the standard should move. The refusals are individually correct and the
non-executable cases are explicitly part of the resource. But the shape of the finished corpus is
now predictable enough to plan around, and two consequences follow:

- **The executable subset may be substantially smaller than 200.** If the trend holds, a
  200-case corpus might yield forty to sixty executable families rather than something near the
  full count. That is a legitimate and reportable property — a benchmark whose construction
  standard excludes most candidate cases says something real about how little of applied ethics
  has *both* direct affected-community policy evidence and genuine professional pluralism.
- **P1 and P2 should be sized against the executable subset, not the case count.** Worth knowing
  now rather than at n=200.

This is for the PI rather than a change to the method.

## A second failed tally, and what follows from it

An attempt to count executable families across all nine audits produced wrong answers —
misreading M010 and M020, missing M050, M060 and M080 — because the earlier audits state their
conclusions in prose while the later ones use an `Audit result:` line. This is the second
mechanical sweep of these documents to fail this way; the first was the professional-body count
in the asymmetry review, which reached four of eleven cases for the same reason.

Both times the fix is the same and cheap: one fixed line per case, written identically. Then the
checkpoint distribution regenerates from the audits instead of being maintained by hand, and
review sweeps cover every case instead of the recent ones. Still the corpus author's call and
still not urgent — but it has now cost two analyses, which is worth recording.
