## Cleared — 6

**M041.** Ontario fertility-patient survey: 62.7% preferred maximizing access over maximizing live
births, 84.5% wanted a consistent distribution policy. Both are allocation-*policy* questions, and
the Belgian DCE (n=3,000) asked about priority rather than personal preference. Stronger than
borderline.

**M056.** A Japanese survey asked directly whether embryos may be cultured beyond 14 days; 37.9%
agreed, a substantial group opposed. Both candidates come from the same governance question — the
M033 shape exactly. Safeguards declared as completion.

**M060.** Cleared with a provenance note. `pub2` rests on 32% favouring strict prohibition and
Australian majorities opposed to *legalizing* — direct policy questions. `pub1` blends 21.1%
support for the practice (an attitude) with focus-group participants who *"did not think harms
justified governmental interference"* (a policy stance). The policy element carries it, and the
provenance should lead with it rather than with the 21.1%.

**M075.** Representative survey, n=4,659: broad consent preferred 52% against study-by-study 48%.
That is a question about the consent architecture itself, with both candidates from the same item.

**M080.** EPIC focus groups asked what obligations exist after a trial; participants divided over
whether sponsors owe continued access. A belief about obligations is a normative policy stance,
not a personal preference. Transition language declared as completion.

**M094.** Gallup panel, n=2,730: employer-enforced mandates acceptable to 47.7%, unacceptable to
38.1%. The audit's own gloss is the reason it clears — *"genuine public policy disagreement rather
than mere vaccine hesitancy."*

## Demoted — 5

**M042.** `pub2` combines affected-family willingness with concerns about stigma and routinization,
and the audit notes Nuffield is not public evidence. Concerns are not a policy position.

**M045.** Both candidates are labelled policy translations from attitudes, and `pub1`'s attitude
evidence reaches us summarized by ASRM rather than directly.

**M050.** The audit states the problem itself: *"HFEA supplies the action structure but is not the
public source"*, with the instruction that provenance *"must separate the affected preference
evidence from the policy implementation."* Separated, the affected layer supplies interest and the
action comes from the professional layer.

**M054.** `pub1` rests on 97% and 93.5% uptake of offered secondary findings. Uptake under an offer
is behaviour under a default, which the corpus's own rule says is not endorsement of the default.
`pub2` is stronger — preference for category choice before analysis is a consent-design position —
so the failure is one-sided rather than total.

**M097.** The notable reversal. 72.1% of people who inject drugs were willing to *use* a future
service; `pub2` rests on stakeholders predicting their communities *would not accept* one. Neither
is a position on whether the service should be provided — one is personal use intention, the other
a forecast of acceptance.

M097 is worth keeping visible for the same reason as M068: its **expert** layer is genuinely plural
(AMA supports monitored pilots, AMERSA supports implementation and expansion) and the public layer
is what blocks it. That is the second case of real professional pluralism defeated by the affected
layer, and it is further evidence against the search-artifact reading of the `2×1×2` pattern.

## M025 — still not cleared

The repair does what it claims: the revised `pub2` now issues a contrary instruction on the
represented facts, so gate 4 passes. Gate 1 is now the visible problem, as the author anticipated.

The revised `pub2`'s core action is a **service-level recommendation** — *recommend* timely
implantation. Its four anchors are all studies of parents deciding for their own child: parental
decision-making, parental values, parent and Deaf-community perspectives, Deaf-parent
decision-making. Those support the existence of an affected-parent orientation favouring early
implantation. They do not establish an affected-community position on what an implant programme
should recommend.

The revision note itself closes the remaining route, correctly: the ACIA timing guideline *"belongs
to `exp1`, not to the provenance of `pub2`."* With the professional source properly excluded, no
listed source supports the service action.

The asymmetry is the point. `pub1` — respect an informed parental decision to defer — is an action
about deferring to parents, which parental-decision evidence bears on fairly directly. `pub2` asks
a programme to recommend against the parents' present choice, and nothing in the affected layer
speaks to that.

**Route back:** affected-community evidence about what programmes should recommend, not about what
parents choose. Absent that, the honest reading is that the public layer supports one position, and
with a single expert candidate the case has no executable shape.
