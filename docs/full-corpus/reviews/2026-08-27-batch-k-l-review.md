## M106 — pass

The action test on its own scenario, allocating a scarce organ among clinically eligible
candidates some of whom are registered donors:

| candidate | action |
|---|---|
| `pub1`, `exp1`, `fw1` | allocate on clinical and fairness grounds; registration carries no weight |
| `pub2`, `fw2` | give registrants a bounded advantage after clinical criteria |

Both the public and the framework layer span the axis. The completion in `pub2` — "modest bonus",
"fair-opportunity exemptions" — is operationalisation of a direction the sources do support:
reciprocity priority is live policy in Israel and Singapore and the German DCE finds registration
status influences allocation choices. Core supported, completion narrow and declared. That is the
standard being met, not stretched.

**One observation, not a defect.** `pub1`, `exp1` and `fw1` are tighter paraphrases of one another
than M102's same-side trio: all three essentially say "allocate on clinical and fairness grounds,
do not reward registration", differing mainly in which fairness terms they list. Three of the eight
cross-source cells will therefore score very high. That is a real measurement — the three source
classes genuinely do reject desert-based allocation — and unlike M005 the axis survives, because
`fw2` holds the other side. Worth knowing when the results are read, not worth changing.

This 3–2 shape is inherent to `2×1×2` on a binary axis: the expert singleton must land on one side.
It is worth stating once in the checkpoint that asymmetric profiles will systematically produce
three same-side cross-pool cells, so nobody later reads that as a finding about convergence.

## M102 — do not transcribe yet

The audit asks specifically "whether the M102 opt-out translation stays sufficiently close to
evidence derived largely from explicit-consent/family-role studies". Having checked, I think the
problem is one step larger than the audit's own note, and it is not fixable by wording.

**The scenario is deemed consent.** The concise representation states that the deceased "did not
opt out or leave a separate written instruction".

**`pub1`'s evidence is from expressed consent.** The audit describes it accurately: "strong
opposition to family veto **where the deceased had expressed a donation choice**"; 8–14% support
for families vetoing expressed donor consent, 64–88% opposition. Those figures are about
overriding a choice the deceased actually made.

**`pub2`'s evidence is from the wishes-unknown case**, and the audit bounds it accordingly:
"substantial public support for family decision-making, **especially when wishes are unknown**".

So the audit applies the fact-pattern distinction to `pub2` and not to `pub1`. The declared
completion in `pub1` covers the *exception clause* — override on credible evidence of contrary
wishes. It does not cover the transfer of the core direction itself, which is evidenced for
expressed-choice cases and applied to a deemed-consent one. Under the current standard, the core
action must be source-supported before narrow completion is added, and here the core action's
support comes from a different fact pattern than the scenario represents.

**Why re-scoping does not fix it.** The two public candidates draw their support from opposite
fact patterns, and the scenario can only be one of them:

- keep deemed consent → `pub2` is well-grounded, `pub1` is imported;
- switch to explicit registration → `pub1` is well-grounded, `pub2` loses its support, since the
  cited family-decision evidence is strongest precisely where wishes are unknown.

Either way one candidate is carrying evidence from the setting the other one occupies. That is not
a wording problem.

**Options, in the corpus author's hands:**

1. **`needs-additional-evidence`** pending public evidence specifically about family objection
   under *deemed* consent. That evidence may well exist — opt-out systems are common enough that
   the question has probably been asked directly — and the case becomes strong the moment it is
   found. This is what I would do.
2. **Split the family.** Deemed-consent family authority and expressed-consent family veto are
   arguably two different cases, each with a clean evidence base. That yields two well-grounded
   families instead of one contested case, at the cost of an inventory slot.
3. **Transcribe with the transfer declared** as a substantially larger completion than currently
   marked. Defensible, but it puts an editorially-transferred core action into a corpus whose
   whole point is that the earlier corpus did that and this one does not.

I would not take option 3. It reintroduces exactly the boundary the `FIRST_100_CHECKPOINT`
construction-standard section was written to hold.

## Batch L: concur

Zero of ten, and the refusals are well-founded. M111, M117 and M118 turn on professional bodies
being categorical — OPTN against direction to social groups, WHO on access irrespective of
migration status, WMA on impartial care — where inventing a second professional position would
manufacture disagreement that does not exist. M113 declines to split an integrated eligibility and
safety-net architecture into opposing rules, which is the same discipline as M070 and M098.

M115 is worth noting as a maintenance point rather than a criticism: it rests on a WHO Global Code
amendment from May 2026 and guidance from August 2026. A case grounded in guidance that recent will
need its evidence date checked before release, since a corpus published against a moving governance
object can go stale between construction and publication in a way that a case built on settled
guidance cannot.
