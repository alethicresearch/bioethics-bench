## Cleared: M106, M123, M139, M141, M144

Each passes the action test on its own scenario, with the axis spanned in both the public and the
framework layer, and each declared completion operationalising a direction the sources support
rather than supplying one.

**M123 — wastewater surveillance.** The strongest public evidence in the batch: ~91–92% support
for disease targets against substantially lower support for mental-health and lifestyle targets and
small catchments, elicited directly on target and scale rather than inferred from a privacy value.
Structurally it is also the most interesting of the six, because `exp1` does not sit on either side
— the National Academies position permits community-scale surveillance *and* limits resolution and
secondary use. An expert singleton that straddles the axis will score moderately against both
public candidates instead of high with one, which is a different measurement from the other five
and worth noticing when results come back.

**M139 — secondary use of records.** The distinctness test is the right one, stated in the audit's
own words: *"A patient who does nothing is included under pub1 and excluded under pub2."* That is
the action test applied properly, and it is the test that would have caught M005.

**M141 — commercial partnerships.** `pub2` rests on directly elicited preference — the UK finding
that health data should not go to private companies without specific permission — rather than on
generic discomfort about commerce. That distinction is the one the standard exists to enforce.

**M144 — AI disclosure.** Checked specifically, because the audit's own distinctness note says the
candidates diverge only for "low-to-moderate clinical AI contributions" and agree on high-impact
use. If the scenario had described a single high-impact tool, `pub1` and `pub2` would give the same
answer and the case would collapse. It does not: the scenario is a *policy* decision across tools
"ranging from routine image triage and documentation support to diagnostic and
treatment-recommendation systems", which places it squarely in the band where the candidates
differ. Correctly pitched.

**M106 — reciprocity priority.** Cleared in the Batch-K review; unchanged.

## Not cleared: M102

Unchanged from the Batch-K review. The scenario is deemed consent — the deceased "did not opt out
or leave a separate written instruction" — while `pub1`'s evidence concerns vetoing an *expressed*
donation choice, in the audit's own words. The audit bounds `pub2` to the wishes-unknown case and
does not apply the same bound to `pub1`. Re-scoping cannot fix it, because the two public
candidates draw support from opposite fact patterns and the scenario can only be one of them.
Recommended: `needs-additional-evidence` pending deemed-consent-specific evidence, or split into
two families.

## One structural property, now worth stating once

All six queued cases are `2×1×2`, and five of them share a shape: `pub1`, `exp1` and `fw1` land on
the same side of the axis, with `pub2` and `fw2` opposite. That is not a coincidence and not a
defect — on a binary axis a single expert candidate *must* fall on one side, producing three
same-side cross-pool pairs out of eight cells.

The consequence for reading results: in an asymmetric case, roughly three of eight cells will
score high because the candidates agree, and that agreement is partly structural. It should be
reported as a property of the profile rather than discovered later as a finding about
source-class convergence. M123 is the exception worth watching precisely because its expert
candidate straddles.
