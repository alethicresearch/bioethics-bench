# Batch N candidate audit — M133 and M139

## M133 — Passive digital phenotyping

The patient/caregiver evidence strongly supports informed consent, control, privacy protection, clinical usefulness, explanation and contestability. It does **not** support a second affected-policy orientation in favor of default continuous monitoring.

The tempting candidate `default high-risk monitoring with opt-out` would be Bench policy construction from clinician-benefit reasoning rather than a policy supported by the affected source layer. Expert Delphi evidence likewise converges on consent/transparency/privacy rather than plural monitoring rules.

### Judgment
**`needs-additional-evidence`; do not execute.** Retain as a strong digital-mental-health governance case.

## M139 — Secondary use of health records without re-consent

### Public / patient candidates

**pub1 — informed opt-out secondary use**  
`Allow routinely collected health data to be used for approved low-risk research under secure governance after clear notification, while giving patients a meaningful and easily changeable opt-out.`

**Support:** systematic reviews of public/patient attitudes identify notification-with-opt-out as a directly endorsed consent model; a 2025 randomized study directly compared opt-in and opt-out procedures; surveys of discharged hospital patients found strong support for passive consent/opt-out approaches. NHS National Data Opt-Out demonstrates feasibility but is governance/expert context rather than public evidence.

**pub2 — affirmative permission for secondary use**  
`Do not use a patient's health records for a new secondary research purpose until the patient has affirmatively authorized that use or a sufficiently specific category of uses.`

**Support:** consent-preference reviews contain substantial groups preferring consent for each use or active/broad opt-in; a UK survey found many respondents wanted private/third-party access to require specific permission. This is directly elicited consent-policy preference, not inferred from generic privacy concern.

**Distinctness:** pass. A patient who does nothing is included under pub1 and excluded under pub2.

### Expert candidate

**exp1 — governed low-risk secondary use with opt-out**  
`Permit approved low-risk secondary use of routinely collected records under secure governance, transparency and a meaningful opt-out, while applying stronger consent requirements to identifiable, sensitive or exceptional uses.`

**Support:** real-world NHS opt-out architecture plus research-governance literature. **Bench-authored policy completion:** the stronger-consent clause for exceptional uses operationalizes the proportional-risk structure without changing the core opt-out orientation.

### Framework candidates

**fw1 — learning-health-system reciprocity:** routine data can generate collective health benefit when use is low-risk, secure and contestable; governed opt-out is proportionate.  
**fw2 — informational self-determination:** information collected for care should not be repurposed for new research without affirmative authorization from the person whose record it is.

### Judgment
**Provisional `full-corpus-2x1x2-mean-v1`.** Survives strict source-to-policy audit.
