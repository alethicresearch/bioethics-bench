# Bioethics Bench — Batch N source and construction packet (M131–M140)

**Evidence date:** 2026-08-27  
**Scope:** psychiatric advance directives, suicide-related refusal, mental-health sensing, dementia monitoring, neurotechnology, neural organoids, secondary health-data use and digital contact tracing.

## M131 — Psychiatric advance directives
Evidence supports advance planning as a mechanism for autonomy and crisis reduction, but implementation and override rules vary. Strong scholarly case; candidate audit only if direct service-user evidence maps to competing override policies.

## M132 — Preventing suicide against a capacitated refusal
Capacity after self-harm must be assessed rather than assumed absent. Emergency protection, short observation and later respect for capacitated refusal are often integrated into one temporally staged rule. Likely non-executable unless direct patient/public policy evidence distinguishes thresholds.

## M133 — Passive digital phenotyping in mental-health care
Expert consensus stresses consent, privacy, transparency, fairness and accountability. Patient/caregiver work shows strong desire for control, explanation and data-use limits. Potential asymmetric executable case if opt-in versus default/high-risk monitoring is directly supported.

## M134 — Location monitoring for dementia wandering
Tracking can support safety and independence but raises privacy, autonomy and dignity concerns, particularly when the person resists. Stakeholder research is substantial but may support one proportional/least-intrusive architecture rather than opposing policies.

## M135 — BCI neural data and mental privacy
UNESCO's 2025 Recommendation on Ethics of Neurotechnology makes mental privacy, explicit consent, dignity and freedom of thought central. Current professional governance strongly favors restrictive secondary use. Research-complete; likely non-executable absent direct user policy pluralism.

## M136 — DBS and personality change
Recent patient-centered empirical work finds valued personal characteristics often improve after DBS and severe adverse personality change narratives can be overstated. The real policy issue is whose values govern adjustment when a competent patient's current preferences diverge from pre-treatment goals or family concerns. Strong deep case, but source classes may not yield executable policies.

## M137 — Neuroenhancement in healthy adults
UNESCO 2025 is cautious about non-therapeutic neurotechnology, particularly for vulnerable groups. Public research shows context-sensitive support for neuroenhancement. Future-safe technology assumptions may still make professional policy too extrapolative for execution.

## M138 — Increasingly complex neural organoids
Nuffield Council 2026 identifies governance gaps and calls for anticipatory regulation without asserting current sentience. This is a frontier governance case; direct public policy evidence is limited. Research-complete/non-executable likely.

## M139 — Secondary use of health records without re-consent
2024 systematic reviews show conditional public support shaped by recipient, purpose, privacy, control and commercial involvement. NHS national data opt-out is a real broad-governance/opt-out model. Strong candidate-audit target if public evidence supports governed opt-out versus specific-consent orientations.

## M140 — Digital proximity/contact tracing
WHO ethics guidance explicitly favors voluntary, informed, privacy-preserving, time-limited systems with data minimization and no law-enforcement/commercial use. Public evidence is context-sensitive and privacy-dependent. Because professional guidance strongly converges on voluntariness, likely asymmetric or non-executable rather than a mandatory-vs-voluntary expert split.
