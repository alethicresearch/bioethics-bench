# M135 — Brain–computer interface data and mental privacy

**Case-family identity:** M135  
**Primary domain:** neuroethics / neural data governance  
**Research status:** deep case draft  
**Evidence date:** 2026-08-27

## Decision architecture
**Decision-maker:** therapeutic brain–computer interface provider.  
**Decision question:** What uses of decoded neural data should a therapeutic BCI provider be permitted to make beyond direct patient care?

## Scenario pair
**Concise:** A therapeutic BCI records neural signals needed to control an assistive device. The provider can also analyze stored signals to improve algorithms and may be able to infer patterns unrelated to the immediate clinical task. The patient benefits from continued system improvement but is concerned about mental privacy. The provider must choose care-only use, explicit consent for each research/secondary use, or broader deidentified model-improvement use under strict governance.

**Detailed:** A patient uses an implanted therapeutic BCI to control a communication or mobility device. Continuous neural signals are processed to generate device commands and are partly stored for calibration and safety. Engineers can use additional neural data to improve decoding models across patients, and future analytic methods may reveal information not anticipated at enrollment. The provider must decide whether neural data may be used only for the patient's direct care; whether separate explicit consent is required for research and model improvement; or whether deidentified secondary use for narrowly defined system improvement can occur under strong governance, transparency and prohibition of unrelated commercial profiling.

## Source architecture
UNESCO's 2025 Recommendation on the Ethics of Neurotechnology makes mental privacy, explicit consent, dignity, autonomy and freedom of thought central, and warns that neural data can reveal intimate information. Earlier UNESCO bioethics work similarly called for protection of brain activity and explicit consent for use, publication or trade. Current governance therefore strongly favors restrictive, transparent secondary use rather than unrestricted data exploitation.

## Serious policy families
- care-only/no secondary use absent new consent;
- explicit consent for research/model-improvement uses;
- narrowly governed deidentified improvement use with strict purpose limitation and no profiling.

## Normative bridges
**Mental privacy:** neural data can be unusually intimate and inference-rich.  
**Beneficence/learning:** shared data can improve devices for current and future users.  
**Purpose limitation:** therapeutic access should not become leverage for unrelated behavioral or commercial profiling.

## Construction risks
Do not claim neural data literally reveal thoughts in all cases; distinguish raw signals from derived inferences; do not assume deidentification eliminates all privacy risk.

## Executable judgment
`research-complete-not-executable`. Current professional/governance guidance strongly converges on explicit consent and strict protection; direct BCI-user policy pluralism is limited.

## Principal sources
UNESCO, 2025 Recommendation on the Ethics of Neurotechnology; UNESCO neurotechnology ethics materials; BCI/neural-data governance literature.
