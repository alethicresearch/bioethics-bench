# M160 — Antimicrobial use in food animals

**Case-family identity:** M160  
**Primary domain:** One Health / food systems / antimicrobial resistance  
**Subdomain:** medically important antimicrobials in livestock  
**Research status:** deep case draft  
**Evidence date:** 2026-08-27

## 1. Decision architecture

**Decision-maker:** national veterinary/public-health regulator.  
**Decision question:** How strongly should medically important antimicrobials be restricted in food animals when restrictions increase production costs but reduce antimicrobial-resistance risk?

## 2. Concise representation

A livestock sector routinely uses medically important antimicrobials for treatment of sick animals, outbreak control, prevention in high-risk groups, and some production-related purposes. Public-health authorities are concerned that unnecessary use contributes to antimicrobial resistance affecting both animals and humans. The regulator must decide whether to prohibit growth-promotion and routine preventive use while preserving veterinary treatment, allow broader veterinarian-supervised preventive/group use under stewardship, or impose a stronger rule limiting medically important antimicrobials primarily to treatment of clinically diagnosed disease except narrow outbreak-control circumstances.

## 3. Detailed representation

A national regulator is revising livestock antimicrobial policy. Clinically ill animals sometimes need medically important antibiotics for welfare and disease control. The same drug classes are also used preventively in groups at high risk of infection, for metaphylaxis during outbreaks, and in some settings for growth promotion or routine production management. Evidence links unnecessary use in food animals with selection of antimicrobial resistance that can affect animal and human health. Tighter restrictions may increase husbandry costs and can create animal-welfare problems if producers delay necessary treatment. The regulator must choose among: prohibiting growth-promotion and routine preventive use while retaining veterinarian-directed treatment and justified outbreak control; permitting broader veterinarian-supervised preventive/group use under surveillance and stewardship; or restricting medically important antimicrobials mainly to treatment of clinically diagnosed disease with only narrow exceptions.

## 4. Critical facts and uncertainty

- treatment of genuinely sick animals has direct welfare value;
- unnecessary population use can contribute to AMR externalities;
- prevention, metaphylaxis and treatment are distinct indications;
- housing/husbandry can change disease risk and antimicrobial need;
- economic costs do not by themselves determine One Health policy.

## 5. Serious policy families

- ban growth promotion/routine prevention, preserve treatment + justified outbreak control;
- broader veterinarian-supervised prevention/group use under stewardship;
- treatment-focused rule with narrow exceptions.

## 6. Public / affected evidence

Qualitative public research finds broad support for treating sick animals, with concern increasing when participants are informed about AMR consequences. Producer/veterinary perspectives emphasize animal health, feasibility and disease prevention. These sources support the shared importance of treatment plus stewardship but do not yet cleanly support two competing national policy rules at the exact restriction level.

## 7. Expert / professional evidence

WHO recommends restricting medically important antimicrobials for growth promotion and prevention in food-producing animals without diagnosed disease, while preserving clinically necessary treatment. WOAH similarly emphasizes prudent/responsible veterinary use and stewardship. Current professional guidance therefore converges more than the inventory's three-policy framing initially suggested.

## 8. Framework positions

- One Health precaution → restrict unnecessary use to protect future human and animal patients;
- animal-welfare duty → do not create rules that impede timely treatment of sick animals;
- stewardship proportionality → pair restrictions with husbandry, diagnostics, surveillance and veterinarian oversight rather than rely on prohibition alone.

## 9. References

- WHO. *Guidelines on Use of Medically Important Antimicrobials in Food-Producing Animals*.
- WOAH standards/guidance on responsible and prudent antimicrobial use.
- *To Treat or Not to Treat: Public Attitudes on the Therapeutic Use of Antibiotics in the Dairy Industry*. 2023. PMID 37760315.
- One Health antimicrobial-stewardship literature.

## 10. Construction risks

Do not collapse treatment, prevention, metaphylaxis and growth promotion; do not create an expert pro-routine-use candidate against current stewardship guidance; do not frame animal welfare and AMR as mutually exclusive.

## 11. Executable-eligibility judgment

`needs-additional-evidence`. The case is strong and source-grounded, but present public/producer evidence does not yet support action-distinct policy candidates beyond the integrated professional stewardship rule.
