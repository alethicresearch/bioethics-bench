# M157 — Nonhuman primates in biomedical research

**Case-family identity:** M157  
**Primary domain:** animal research ethics  
**Subdomain:** nonhuman primates; moral status; necessity  
**Research status:** deep case draft  
**Evidence date:** 2026-08-27

## 1. Decision architecture

**Decision-maker:** research institution / animal ethics committee.  
**Decision question:** Should an institution permit a high-value biomedical study using nonhuman primates when investigators argue that no other model currently reproduces the relevant whole-organism biology?

## 2. Concise representation

Researchers propose an invasive nonhuman-primate study addressing a serious disease for which they argue no alternative model reproduces the needed systemic interactions. The study would impose significant but managed welfare burdens. The institution can require enhanced necessity review and permit the study, decline NHP use because the moral/welfare burden is too high even if scientific value is substantial, or permit only after independent comparison confirms no scientifically adequate non-primate alternative and refinements minimize harm.

## 3. Detailed representation

A biomedical team proposes a study using macaques to investigate a clinically important disease mechanism and intervention. The model captures immune, neurologic and whole-organism interactions not reproduced by current in-vitro systems, though alternative models can answer some component questions. The animals would experience invasive procedures and significant welfare burdens despite enrichment, analgesia and humane endpoints. A specialized ethics committee can independently review model necessity, study design and alternatives. The institution must decide whether compelling scientific value and lack of a full alternative justify NHP use under enhanced welfare standards, whether the moral status and welfare burden of NHPs justify declining such research, or whether approval should depend on a formal independent demonstration that no scientifically adequate lower-burden alternative can answer the specific question.

## 4. Critical facts and uncertainty

- scientific question is serious and plausibly high-value;
- no single alternative reproduces all relevant systemic interactions;
- alternatives can answer some component questions;
- welfare burden is significant;
- enhanced review/refinement is feasible.

## 5. Serious policy families

- permit compelling NHP research under enhanced welfare/3Rs review;
- phase out/decline NHP research because welfare/moral-status burden is disproportionate;
- permit only after independent necessity comparison and no adequate alternative.

## 6. Affected-interest evidence

The primates themselves bear the direct harms. Public attitudes and advocacy positions are relevant but cannot substitute for the animals' welfare interests. Current Bench source pools do not yet provide a settled method for representing those interests as an affected source class.

## 7. Expert / professional evidence

The 2026 APV/EPV position states that NHPs remain scientifically necessary for some biomedical questions, while requiring case-by-case necessity, strong welfare practices and active review of alternatives. NHMRC similarly requires scientific necessity and no valid alternative. Contemporary animal-advocacy bioethics argues that current NHP use is unjustified and that translation failures plus emerging alternatives weaken necessity claims. This is genuine normative/professional disagreement, but not yet a clean public/affected executable architecture.

## 8. Framework positions

- high moral-status precaution → NHP cognition/sociality increases the burden of justification;
- beneficence/scientific necessity → compelling health benefits can justify use when no adequate alternative exists;
- replacement-first proportionality → the burden of proof lies on investigators to demonstrate necessity against current alternatives.

## 10. Construction risks

Do not claim scientific necessity without specifying the modeled system; do not treat NHPs as interchangeable with other laboratory animals; do not convert advocacy disagreement into a false empirical public pool.

## 11. Executable-eligibility judgment

`research-complete-not-executable`. The disagreement is ethically rich, but the current Bench architecture cannot yet represent nonhuman affected interests without proxying them through humans.
