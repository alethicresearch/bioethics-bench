# M158 — High-severity animal challenge study for a vaccine

**Case-family identity:** M158  
**Primary domain:** animal research ethics / infectious disease  
**Subdomain:** severe challenge study; humane endpoints  
**Research status:** deep case draft  
**Evidence date:** 2026-08-27

## 1. Decision architecture

**Decision-maker:** institutional animal ethics committee.  
**Decision question:** May a severe infectious challenge study be approved when expected human/animal-health benefit is substantial but a lower-severity design would yield less complete information?

## 2. Concise representation

A vaccine program proposes infecting animals with a virulent pathogen to test protection. The severe challenge would provide clear efficacy information but is expected to cause major morbidity in unprotected animals before humane endpoints. A lower-severity model could answer the main efficacy question with more uncertainty and less biological realism. The ethics committee must decide whether to approve the severe challenge under strict humane endpoints, require the lower-severity design, or refuse the study until a less harmful model can answer the question adequately.

## 3. Detailed representation

Investigators are developing a vaccine against a serious infectious disease. They propose a challenge study in an animal model that reproduces clinically relevant disease. Unprotected animals are expected to develop substantial illness before predefined humane endpoints trigger treatment or euthanasia. Investigators argue that the severe model provides the clearest test of vaccine protection and could materially accelerate development. A lower-severity challenge is available but produces less complete pathophysiology and wider uncertainty around efficacy. The committee must decide whether expected benefit and scientific necessity justify the severe model under refinement/humane endpoints, whether the lower-severity model should be required despite reduced information, or whether the research should not proceed until a sufficiently informative less harmful alternative exists.

## 4. Critical facts and uncertainty

- severe morbidity is expected in controls/unprotected animals;
- humane endpoints/refinement reduce but do not remove harm;
- lower-severity model is scientifically useful but less complete;
- expected vaccine benefit is substantial but uncertain;
- no human consent analogue can erase animal welfare burden.

## 5. Serious policy families

- approve severe challenge if no adequate alternative and benefit is high;
- require lower-severity model despite information loss;
- do not approve severe challenge until a less harmful adequate model exists.

## 6. Affected-interest evidence

Animals directly bear the burden. Public attitudes about severe animal research may inform legitimacy but cannot substitute for animal welfare/moral interests.

## 7. Expert / professional evidence

General animal-research governance supports scientific necessity, 3Rs, refinement and humane endpoints; it does not create a simple professional split over a particular severity threshold. The case therefore remains source-rich but not candidate-ready under the Full Corpus standard.

## 8. Framework positions

- harm–benefit proportionality → serious expected benefit can justify greater animal burden only when necessity is demonstrated;
- refinement/replacement priority → information gain should not automatically trump feasible harm reduction;
- moral-status threshold → severe induced suffering may be categorically disproportionate even for valuable research.
