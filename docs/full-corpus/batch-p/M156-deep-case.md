# M156 — Biomedical animal research: replacement threshold

**Case-family identity:** M156  
**Primary domain:** animal research ethics  
**Subdomain:** 3Rs; replacement; NAMs  
**Research status:** deep case draft  
**Evidence date:** 2026-08-27

## 1. Decision architecture

**Decision-maker:** institutional animal care and use committee / research institution.  
**Decision question:** When a scientifically valuable study would impose moderate harm on animals, when should the existence of a plausible nonanimal method require replacement?

## 2. Concise representation

Investigators propose a moderate-harm animal study addressing a clinically important question. A human-cell/microphysiological alternative can answer much of the same question but is slower, more expensive and does not reproduce every whole-organism interaction. The animal model is also imperfectly predictive of humans. The institution must decide whether to approve the animal study under ordinary 3Rs/harm–benefit review, require the nonanimal method whenever it can answer the core question even at added cost/time, or prohibit the animal study only when the alternative is scientifically adequate for the specific decision-relevant endpoint.

## 3. Detailed representation

A research team proposes an animal experiment expected to cause moderate, time-limited pain/distress despite analgesia and refinement. The study addresses a significant biomedical question. A validated human-cell/microphysiological system can reproduce the primary endpoint and several mechanistic interactions, but not all systemic responses; it would also add cost and delay. The animal model can answer the full preclinical package but has known limits in human translation. The institution must decide whether 3Rs review permits the animal study because the alternative is not a complete replacement, whether ethical replacement is required whenever the nonanimal method can answer the core research question adequately, or whether animal use is permissible only after a documented fit-for-purpose comparison shows that the alternative would materially fail the specific scientific aim.

## 4. Critical facts and uncertainty

- animal harm is moderate, not trivial;
- nonanimal method is scientifically meaningful but not identical;
- both models have limitations;
- cost/time differences are real but not themselves scientific invalidity;
- replacement must be assessed against the actual research question.

## 5. Serious policy families

- conventional 3Rs/harm–benefit approval;
- replacement whenever a nonanimal method adequately answers the core question;
- explicit fit-for-purpose comparison before animal approval.

## 6. Affected-interest evidence

Animals bear the direct welfare burden. Human public opinion is relevant to institutional legitimacy but cannot substitute for representation of animal suffering/interests. The current Bench public/expert/framework architecture does not yet cleanly encode nonhuman affected interests without a human proxy.

## 7. Expert / professional evidence

NIH/OLAW states the 3Rs are foundational and that alternatives/NAMs should be considered when scientifically appropriate. NIH's 2025–2026 human-based science initiatives actively aim to reduce animal use while explicitly recognizing that animal models remain necessary for some questions. This supports fit-for-purpose replacement rather than categorical abolition or routine animal preference.

## 8. Framework positions

- 3Rs proportionality → use animals only when justified by scientific need and minimized harm;
- replacement priority → if the core question can be answered without sentient harm, extra cost/time is insufficient reason to use animals;
- epistemic responsibility → choose the model with the best relevant validity, not the historically familiar model.

## 10. Construction risks

Do not equate “not identical” with “not an alternative”; do not assume NAMs always outperform animals; do not proxy animal welfare solely through human attitudes.
