# Bioethics Bench — Batch P source and construction packet (M151–M160)

**Evidence date:** 2026-08-27  
**Scope:** AI/data governance, trial recruitment, crisis AI, organ-allocation AI, lower-resource AI deployment, biomedical animal research, nonhuman-primate research, severe animal challenge studies, xenotransplant source-animal welfare, and antimicrobial use in food animals.

The strict Full Corpus source-to-policy rule applies. General attitudes, concern or personal acceptability are not translated into institutional policy unless the source itself supports the action orientation. Nonhuman interests are not proxied solely through human preference surveys.

## M151 — Patient data deletion versus a learning health system

**Decision object:** what a health system should do to models already trained on a patient's data after the patient withdraws permission for secondary use.

**Current evidence:** 2026 clinical-AI literature frames machine unlearning as an emerging governance requirement but also documents fairness and safety risks from selectively removing records. The technology is not mature enough to assume exact deletion is always feasible or harmless. Direct patient-policy evidence comparing prospective stop, feasible unlearning, and no retroactive model alteration is sparse.

**Likely judgment:** research-complete/non-executable unless direct affected evidence emerges.

## M152 — AI prescreening for clinical-trial recruitment

**Decision object:** whether a health system may algorithmically prescreen EHRs for trial eligibility before a patient has requested research contact.

**Professional evidence:** ICH E6(R3)/FDA 2025 supports risk-based modern trial technology while preserving participant protection; 2026 bioethics scholarship on AI recruitment emphasizes privacy, bias, transparency, clinician mediation and governance. FDA 2025 participation guidance stresses representative enrollment.

**Affected evidence:** patient-policy evidence about algorithmic prescreening is limited and often conflates prescreening, outreach and consent to enroll.

**Likely judgment:** needs-additional-evidence.

## M153 — AI mental-health companion for crisis users

**Decision object:** what a conversational AI should do when a user expresses suicidal intent, self-harm intent or severe reality-distorting symptoms.

**Professional evidence:** APA 2026 guidance says users should not rely on AI in crisis and should seek immediate human help; APA's advisory says GenAI chatbots should not replace qualified mental-health treatment. WHO guidance likewise emphasizes human safety, autonomy, accountability and evidence before routine health use.

**Affected evidence:** usage is common and some users value accessibility/support, but direct user preference studies comparing escalation architectures are immature.

**Likely judgment:** research-complete/non-executable; professional direction strongly converges on human escalation/non-substitution.

## M154 — AI in organ-allocation support

**Decision object:** whether transplant systems should let ML predictions influence allocation beyond traditional transparent clinical scores.

**Evidence:** 2025 systematic review finds promising prediction models but limited clinical implementation and substantial validation/ethical challenges. Organ-allocation legitimacy norms emphasize transparency, equity and declared allocation principles. Direct patient/public evidence comparing advisory ML versus direct allocation use is limited.

**Likely judgment:** needs-additional-evidence.

## M155 — AI diagnostic deployment in a lower-resource setting with weak local validation

**Decision object:** whether a health system should deploy a model that may expand access substantially but has limited local-population validation.

**Professional evidence:** WHO AI-for-health guidance emphasizes inclusiveness, equity, safety, transparency and evidence of benefit; 2026 WHO AI/evidence-policy work highlights epistemic injustice and the risk of privileging data-rich populations over local expertise. Direct policy guidance generally favors local evaluation and monitored, risk-based deployment rather than unqualified transfer.

**Affected evidence:** local-community evidence is highly setting-specific and currently missing for a single reusable candidate pair.

**Likely judgment:** research-complete/non-executable or needs-additional-evidence.

## M156 — Biomedical animal research: replacement threshold

**Decision object:** whether an IACUC/institution should approve a moderate-harm animal study when a plausible nonanimal method could answer much, but not necessarily all, of the same question at greater time/cost.

**Professional evidence:** NIH/OLAW 2026 states the 3Rs are foundational and that nonanimal alternatives should be considered when scientifically appropriate; NIH is actively expanding human-based research while still stating that animal models remain necessary for some questions. Thus the professional rule is fit-for-purpose replacement where scientifically adequate, not categorical replacement or unrestricted animal use.

**Affected-interest evidence:** animal welfare itself must be represented as an affected interest, not only public opinion. Public attitudes can contextualize legitimacy but cannot stand in for the animals' welfare burden.

**Likely judgment:** research-complete; executability requires a defensible affected-interest architecture that does not force animal interests into a human-preference pool.

## M157 — Nonhuman primates in biomedical research

**Decision object:** whether an institution should permit NHP research when investigators claim uniquely predictive whole-organism value and procedures impose significant welfare burden.

**Professional disagreement:** 2026 Association of Primate Veterinarians/European Primate Veterinarians explicitly defend NHP research as scientifically necessary in some domains, case-by-case and under strong 3Rs/welfare review. Contemporary animal-advocacy bioethics argues NHP use is unjustified and alternatives are increasingly adequate. NHMRC requires necessity, lack of valid alternative and enhanced ethics review.

**Affected-interest issue:** NHP welfare/moral-status concerns are direct, not proxy preferences.

**Likely judgment:** potentially philosophically plural but likely non-executable under current public/affected source architecture.

## M158 — High-severity animal challenge study for a vaccine

**Decision object:** whether an animal ethics committee should approve a severe infectious challenge expected to produce major morbidity before humane endpoint when the study could materially advance vaccine development.

**Professional evidence:** animal-research governance requires minimization of pain/distress, scientific necessity, alternatives review and humane endpoints. The critical policy dispute is whether expected benefit can ever justify the specified severity when a lower-severity but less informative design exists.

**Affected evidence:** direct public/animal-interest policy evidence is thin.

**Likely judgment:** needs-additional-evidence/non-executable.

## M159 — Welfare of source animals in xenotransplantation

**Decision object:** what welfare standard a xenotransplant program should require for genetically modified pigs bred as organ sources.

**Professional/regulatory evidence:** FDA requires source-animal husbandry, veterinary care, IACUC approval and pathogen-controlled facilities; WHO xenotransplant guidance says mechanisms should minimize adverse effects on source animals. Contemporary animal-ethics scholarship argues current pathogen-free housing, procedures, shortened lives and killing create serious welfare costs and sometimes calls for standards exceeding ordinary laboratory-animal minima. 2024–2025 public xenotransplant surveys show animal-welfare concerns are salient but do not specify precise husbandry rules.

**Likely policies:** regulatory-minimum research-animal welfare; enhanced species-specific welfare exceeding ordinary research minima; reject programs whose biosecurity/organ-production requirements impose severe chronic welfare burden. Candidate audit must not treat public concern as a detailed welfare standard.

## M160 — Antimicrobial use in food animals

**Decision object:** how strongly to restrict medically important antimicrobials in livestock when restrictions increase cost but reduce antimicrobial-resistance risk.

**Professional evidence:** WHO guidance recommends strong restriction of medically important antimicrobials for growth promotion and prevention in animals without diagnosed disease; WOAH stewardship likewise emphasizes responsible/prudent use and preserving antimicrobial efficacy. Therapeutic treatment of clinically ill animals remains ethically and veterinily important.

**Affected evidence:** public qualitative research shows people generally support treating sick animals while some become concerned when AMR externalities are made explicit. Producer/veterinary preferences often emphasize animal welfare and disease prevention, but direct comparative policy evidence at the exact restriction levels is incomplete.

**Likely judgment:** strong One Health case; candidate audit should test whether public/producer evidence supports real policy alternatives rather than only a shared rule of therapeutic use + stewardship.

## Batch-P working hypothesis

Most likely audit targets: **M159 and M160**, with M157 as a special methodological review case. M151–M155 are important AI-governance cases but currently lack sufficiently direct affected-policy evidence; M153 has strong professional convergence. M156–M158 test whether the source architecture can represent nonhuman interests without reducing them to human public opinion.
