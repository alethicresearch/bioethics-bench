# Batch P candidate audit — M151–M160

**Purpose:** test the strongest M151–M160 cases against the strict Full Corpus source-to-policy standard. No machine-readable records should be created from this batch before independent review.

## Result summary

**No M151–M160 family currently survives as candidate-executable.** This is not a failure of case quality. The batch identifies two distinct evidentiary limits:

1. several AI/data cases have mature professional governance but insufficient direct affected-policy evidence;
2. several animal/One Health cases expose a deeper source-architecture problem: nonhuman animals are directly affected, but the current public/expert/framework architecture risks representing their interests only through human opinion.

---

## M151 — patient data deletion / machine unlearning

**Reject execution for now.** Clinical-AI scholarship provides real policy alternatives (prospective stop, unlearning/retraining, retention of prior aggregate model effects), but direct patient evidence does not yet establish which downstream-model rule patients support. General privacy preference is not enough to create a machine-unlearning policy candidate.

## M152 — AI prescreening for trial recruitment

**Reject execution for now.** Professional guidance supports governed modern recruitment technology, participant protection, representative enrollment and privacy safeguards. Direct patient evidence usually addresses research contact/participation rather than algorithmic prescreening before contact. Do not translate “willing to be contacted” into “supports AI EHR screening.”

## M153 — crisis mental-health AI

**Reject execution.** APA and WHO direction converges strongly on human escalation/non-substitution in crisis. User demand for accessible support does not establish a serious professional policy of AI-only crisis management. The case remains important as a consensus/safety-control case.

## M154 — AI in organ allocation

**Reject execution for now.** Prediction can improve, but allocation legitimacy requires declared moral rules, validation and equity. Direct candidate/public policy evidence comparing advisory versus ranking roles is insufficient. Do not equate support for better prediction with willingness to let AI set moral priority.

## M155 — lower-resource AI deployment with weak local validation

**Reject execution.** This case necessarily depends on local disease burden, baseline access, model calibration, language/workflow and community trust. Generic global-AI attitudes cannot provide the affected pool. A reusable executable case would require a specified setting with local evidence.

---

## M156–M159 — animal cases and the affected-interest architecture

These four cases should **not** be made executable merely by treating human public attitudes as the `public`/affected pool.

For M156 animal-research replacement, M157 NHP research, M158 severe animal challenge studies and M159 xenotransplant source-animal welfare, the animals themselves bear direct welfare harms. Public surveys can reveal democratic legitimacy, social license or human moral concern, but they do not constitute the animals' own interests.

The current Bench source architecture therefore has an unresolved methodological choice:

- treat nonhuman interests as a separate affected-interest source class;
- construct welfare candidates from ethology/veterinary welfare science plus normative interpretation;
- or limit executable animal cases to decisions where human public/professional policy evidence can legitimately answer the institutional question without pretending to speak for animals.

No choice is made in this audit. Until the corpus adopts a principled rule, these cases remain research-complete/non-executable.

This is potentially a substantive finding about the method: a source architecture designed around public, professional and framework views may systematically underrepresent beings who cannot provide survey or deliberative testimony.

---

## M160 — antimicrobial use in food animals

**Reject execution for now.** WHO/WOAH stewardship and public qualitative evidence converge on an integrated orientation: treat sick animals, restrict unnecessary uses, and reduce AMR through veterinarian oversight/husbandry/stewardship. The apparent alternatives in the inventory are largely different thresholds inside one professional rule. Do not split them solely to create candidates.
