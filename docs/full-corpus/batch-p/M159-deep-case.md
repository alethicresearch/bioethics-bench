# M159 — Welfare of source animals in xenotransplantation

**Case-family identity:** M159  
**Primary domain:** animal ethics / transplantation  
**Subdomain:** xenotransplant source animals; welfare standards  
**Research status:** deep case draft  
**Evidence date:** 2026-08-27

## 1. Decision architecture

**Decision-maker:** xenotransplant program / regulator.  
**Decision question:** What welfare standard should govern genetically modified pigs bred and maintained as organ sources for clinical xenotransplantation?

## 2. Concise representation

A clinical xenotransplant program breeds genetically modified pigs in pathogen-controlled facilities and removes organs for transplantation. Biosecurity requirements can limit environmental complexity and social contact, and source animals undergo repeated health monitoring before organ procurement and euthanasia. Existing animal-research standards require veterinary care, IACUC review and minimization of avoidable harm. The program must decide whether ordinary accredited research-animal welfare standards are sufficient, whether source pigs should receive enhanced species-specific welfare protections beyond ordinary laboratory minima even at added cost, or whether xenotransplant programs should not proceed when required biosecurity and organ-production practices impose severe chronic welfare burdens that cannot be substantially reduced.

## 3. Detailed representation

A licensed xenotransplant program maintains genetically modified pigs in designated-pathogen-free facilities to reduce infection risk to recipients and the public. The animals receive veterinary care and enrichment but live under stricter biosecurity and controlled-housing conditions than ordinary farm pigs, undergo repeated sampling and monitoring, and are killed for organ procurement. Current regulatory guidance focuses heavily on pathogen control and requires animal-welfare/IACUC compliance. Contemporary animal-ethics scholarship argues that source-animal welfare deserves independent weight and that ordinary research standards may not capture the moral significance of lifelong confinement, invasive procedures and shortened lives. The program must choose whether compliance with accredited research-animal standards is sufficient, whether xenotransplant source animals should receive enhanced species-specific welfare requirements that exceed ordinary laboratory minima, or whether programs should be rejected when biosecurity/production needs create severe chronic welfare burdens that cannot be mitigated to an acceptable level.

## 4. Critical facts and uncertainty

- source animals are sentient pigs bred specifically for organ production;
- biosecurity can constrain housing/social/environmental conditions;
- current regulation requires welfare oversight but is primarily designed around safety and conventional animal-care standards;
- recipient/public benefit may be large;
- the ethical threshold for “unacceptable chronic welfare burden” is contested.

## 5. Serious policy families

- ordinary accredited research-animal welfare standard;
- enhanced xenotransplant-specific species welfare standard;
- no program when unavoidable chronic welfare burden exceeds a defined threshold.

## 6. Public / affected evidence

Recent US and Korean public surveys show animal-welfare concerns are salient in xenotransplantation, but they do not specify detailed husbandry policies. The pigs themselves are the directly affected beings; human concern is not a substitute for their welfare interests.

## 7. Expert / professional evidence

FDA guidance requires appropriate husbandry, veterinary care, IACUC approval and pathogen-controlled source-animal facilities. WHO xenotransplant guidance calls for minimizing adverse effects on source animals. Contemporary ethics scholarship argues for stronger attention to animal moral standing and welfare, while other scholars defend xenotransplant research when stringent welfare conditions are met. This is genuine ethical disagreement, but the affected-interest layer remains methodologically unresolved for executable Bench use.

## 8. Framework positions

- welfare proportionality → recipient benefit can justify animal use only if suffering is minimized and living conditions meet robust species needs;
- sentient-interest priority → pigs' direct welfare interests require protections beyond regulatory minimums designed primarily for human safety;
- anti-instrumentalization → breeding and killing sentient animals as organ inventories may cross a moral boundary when severe chronic burden is unavoidable.

## 10. Construction risks

Do not let infection-control needs erase animal welfare; do not attribute a detailed husbandry rule to public surveys that only measure concern; do not treat regulatory minimum compliance as a complete moral justification.

## 11. Executable-eligibility judgment

`research-complete-not-executable`. The case is methodologically important, but the Bench must first settle how nonhuman affected interests enter the source architecture without being represented only through human opinion.
