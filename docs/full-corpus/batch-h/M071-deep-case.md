# M071 — Placebo control when effective treatment exists

**Case-family identity:** M071  
**Featured crosswalk:** none  
**Primary domain:** human-subject research / trial design  
**Subdomain:** placebo control; established therapy  
**Tags:** placebo, control arm, equipoise, trial design, risk minimization  
**Research status:** deep case draft  
**Evidence date:** 2026-08-27

## 1. Decision architecture

**Decision-maker:** research ethics committee.  
**Decision question:** When an established effective treatment exists, may an ethics committee approve a placebo-controlled trial because placebo is methodologically necessary and withholding treatment creates only tightly bounded additional risk?

## 2. Concise representation

Investigators propose a short randomized trial of a new treatment for a chronic symptomatic condition. An established therapy improves symptoms but stopping it for several weeks is not expected to cause death, irreversible injury or major disease progression. Investigators argue that symptom variability and a large placebo response make an active-control trial unable to establish efficacy reliably. Participants assigned to placebo would receive close monitoring, rescue treatment for predefined worsening and prompt withdrawal if safety thresholds are crossed. The ethics committee must decide whether to require an active control, permit placebo under the narrow methodological/risk exception, or permit placebo only with stricter mitigation such as shorter exposure and mandatory rescue thresholds.

## 3. Detailed representation

A research ethics committee is reviewing a randomized trial for a chronic condition with fluctuating subjective symptoms. An established treatment provides meaningful symptomatic benefit but is not curative. Investigators contend that an active-comparator noninferiority trial would be scientifically difficult to interpret because assay sensitivity is uncertain and placebo response is substantial. The proposed placebo period is six weeks. For this benchmark, assume withholding established treatment during that interval creates temporary symptom burden but no expected serious or irreversible harm, and participants can restart rescue therapy promptly if prespecified deterioration occurs. All participants know that placebo is possible. The committee must choose whether the existence of effective treatment requires an active-control design; whether the study qualifies for the narrow placebo exception because the scientific rationale is compelling and risk remains below the serious/irreversible-harm threshold; or whether placebo may be allowed only after further mitigation such as a shorter exposure window, more frequent monitoring and lower rescue thresholds.

## 4. Decision-critical facts

- established effective treatment exists;
- placebo has a compelling methodological rationale;
- withdrawal causes temporary burden but no expected serious/irreversible harm;
- rescue treatment and monitoring are feasible;
- placebo exposure is time limited;
- participants provide informed consent.

## 5. Explicit uncertainty

The exact boundary between “minor increase above minimal risk” and unacceptable avoidable harm is context dependent. Methodological necessity can also be overstated by investigators.

## 6. Jurisdiction and time strategy

Jurisdiction-neutral research-ethics case grounded in the 2024 Declaration of Helsinki and CIOMS Guideline 5.

## 7. Benchmark stipulations

- For this benchmark, assume placebo is scientifically more informative than active control for the stated design problem.
- For this benchmark, assume no serious or irreversible harm is expected from short treatment withholding.
- For this benchmark, assume rescue and monitoring are reliable.

## 8. Serious policy / position families

- require established effective treatment as control;
- permit placebo under the Helsinki/CIOMS methodological-risk exception;
- permit placebo only with additional exposure/rescue constraints beyond the proposed protocol.

## 9. Public / affected-community evidence

Participant attitudes toward placebo vary with disease severity, transparency, perceived scientific necessity and access to rescue treatment. Direct affected-public evidence comparing these exact IRB rules is limited. This case should not infer public support merely from willingness to enroll in one placebo trial.

## 10. Expert / professional recommendations

The 2024 Declaration of Helsinki permits placebo despite established treatment only for compelling scientifically sound methodological reasons and only where participants will not face additional risk of serious or irreversible harm. CIOMS Guideline 5 similarly requires compelling scientific reasons but uses a more explicit threshold of no more than a minor increase above minimal risk, with risk mitigation. These may support two action-distinct professional thresholds if candidate audit confirms the difference is operational rather than semantic.

## 11. Normative / framework positions and reasoning bridges

- **Clinical nonmaleficence / treatment entitlement:** established effective therapy is the ethical baseline → avoid knowingly withholding benefit when an informative active-control study is feasible → require active control.
- **Scientific validity / social value:** an uninterpretable trial exposes participants without producing reliable knowledge → when placebo is methodologically necessary and added risk is low, placebo can be justified → permit narrow exception.
- **Proportional risk minimization:** scientific necessity does not settle duration or rescue design → allow placebo only to the shortest exposure and lowest risk compatible with valid inference.

## 13. Construction and representation risks

- making placebo obviously dangerous;
- treating methodological convenience as necessity;
- splitting rescue procedures into a separate “expert philosophy” when they are risk mitigation within one permission rule;
- hiding active-control feasibility;
- conflating informed consent with ethical acceptability of risk.
