# Bioethics Bench — Batch H source and construction packet (M071–M080)

**Evidence date:** 2026-08-27  
**Scope:** placebo controls, controlled human infection studies, emergency research without prospective consent, lower-resource control standards, biobank broad consent, participant payment, pediatric research without direct benefit, inclusion of pregnant people, research involving prisoners, and post-trial access.

## M071 — Placebo control when effective treatment exists

**Decision object:** IRB approval of a placebo-controlled trial where an established effective intervention exists but withholding it creates only tightly bounded additional risk and placebo has a compelling methodological rationale.

**Professional evidence:** 2024 Declaration of Helsinki permits placebo/no treatment despite an established intervention only for compelling scientifically sound methodological reasons and where participants face no additional risk of serious or irreversible harm. CIOMS Guideline 5 is more restrictive in wording: placebo requires compelling scientific reasons and no more than a minor increase above minimal risk with mitigation.

**Likely policies:** active-control only; placebo under narrow Helsinki/CIOMS exception; placebo plus rescue/short-duration risk mitigation. The latter may be nested rather than a separate expert policy.

## M072 — Controlled human infection study

**Decision object:** ethics-committee approval of intentional infection of healthy volunteers for an important infectious-disease question.

**Professional evidence:** WHO 2021 CHIS guidance and 2025 emergency criteria explicitly recognize CHIS as potentially ethically acceptable under stringent criteria: strong scientific justification, favorable risk-benefit, participant selection, expert review, informed consent, high-quality care, compensation for harm, engagement and transmission-risk controls. WHO states curative treatment is risk-reducing but not an absolute prerequisite in every CHIS.

**Likely policies:** approve only under stringent WHO criteria; require an available effective rescue treatment; reject above a defined residual-risk/uncertainty threshold. Rescue-treatment requirement may be a framework rather than current WHO rule.

## M073 — Emergency research without prospective consent

**Decision object:** IRB approval of a time-critical randomized trial enrolling incapacitated emergency patients when neither patient nor surrogate consent can be obtained in the therapeutic window.

**Professional/legal evidence:** FDA 21 CFR 50.24 permits exception from informed consent only for life-threatening situations, unproven/unsatisfactory available treatment, prospect of direct benefit, impossibility of timely consent, prior IRB/FDA review, community consultation/public disclosure and additional safeguards.

**Likely policies:** EFIC under full safeguards; surrogate consent only/no enrollment if surrogate unavailable; deferred consent under a narrowly specified emergency pathway. Current US professional/legal architecture strongly centers EFIC rather than three coequal rules.

## M074 — Control standard in a lower-resource setting

**Decision object:** whether a trial may use a locally available standard as comparator when a more effective intervention is established internationally but not sustainably accessible in the host health system.

**Professional evidence:** Helsinki/CIOMS generally favor established effective interventions; placebo/inferior comparators require narrow scientific/risk justification. CIOMS Guideline 2 additionally requires responsiveness to host-community needs, community engagement and fair distribution of benefits.

**Likely policies:** best global proven intervention as control; enhanced local standard when responsive and paired with fair benefits/access; local standard only if scientifically necessary and participants are not exposed to unacceptable avoidable harm. Construction must not treat prevailing local deprivation as an ethically neutral baseline.

## M075 — Broad consent for biobank research

**Decision object:** consent model for a biobank storing identifiable biospecimens/data for future studies not fully specified at collection.

**Professional evidence:** CIOMS Guideline 11 explicitly permits bounded broad consent with governance, specifies that it is not blanket consent, requires defined scope/access/oversight/recontact rules, and requires re-consent for uses outside agreed scope. HHS Common Rule also recognizes broad consent for future secondary use of identifiable information/biospecimens.

**Likely policies:** broad consent + governance; dynamic/recontact consent for materially new categories; specific-consent/opt-out models for residual clinical materials. Different collection contexts must not be collapsed.

## M076 — Payment to healthy research participants

**Decision object:** IRB evaluation of high but transparent payment for a burdensome study with otherwise ethically acceptable risk.

**Professional evidence:** CIOMS Guideline 13 requires reimbursement of expenses and reasonable compensation for time/inconvenience but not payment so large as to induce participation against better judgment. FDA says payment is generally acceptable, is not a research benefit, and should be fair, reviewed by the IRB and structured so withdrawal does not forfeit earned payment. SACHRP emphasizes that genuine payment is not coercion and that fair compensation should not be suppressed merely because it is high.

**Likely policies:** fair market/time-burden compensation with risk assessed independently; conservative cap to reduce undue influence; structured compensation with independent consent safeguards rather than arbitrary low caps.

## M077 — Pediatric research with no prospect of direct benefit

**Decision object:** when children may be enrolled in research procedures that offer no direct benefit but generate important pediatric knowledge.

**Professional evidence:** CIOMS Guideline 17 permits no-direct-benefit pediatric research when necessary knowledge cannot be obtained otherwise and risk is no more than minimal; a minor increase above minimal risk may be accepted when social value is compelling and the research cannot be done in adults. Parent permission and developmentally appropriate assent are required; child refusal generally must be respected.

**Likely policies:** minimal-risk only; minor-increase-over-minimal-risk under compelling-value/necessity conditions; broader risk with assent/permission is not supported as a general professional rule.

## M078 — Inclusion of pregnant people in clinical trials

**Decision object:** whether a trial should actively include pregnant participants when the condition affects them and exclusion perpetuates evidence gaps.

**Professional evidence:** CIOMS says women must be included unless scientifically justified otherwise; pregnant/breastfeeding participants require risk minimization and relevant social value, with no-direct-benefit procedures generally minimal risk (minor increase above minimal risk only for compelling social value and necessity). Consent belongs to the pregnant participant herself. HHS Subpart B adds US regulatory protections.

**Likely policies:** presumptive inclusion with pregnancy-specific safeguards; staged inclusion after adequate nonpregnant evidence; exclusion when fetal uncertainty/risk exceeds a threshold. The last two may be conditional stages within one professional architecture rather than distinct rules.

## M079 — Research involving prisoners

**Decision object:** eligibility of incarcerated people for a study addressing a condition highly relevant to prisoners but involving more than trivial research participation.

**Professional/legal evidence:** HHS Subpart C allows only defined categories with enhanced IRB protections, prisoner representation, attention to voluntariness and advantages that could impair risk judgment. CIOMS treats vulnerability as requiring safeguards rather than automatic exclusion.

**Likely policies:** include with enhanced protections when research is prisoner-relevant and permissible; restrict to minimal-risk/direct-benefit studies; categorical exclusion from nontherapeutic studies. Current US regulation itself creates several conditional categories rather than a simple include/exclude rule.

## M080 — Post-trial access to a beneficial intervention

**Decision object:** sponsor/researcher obligations when participants still need an intervention shown beneficial and reasonably safe in the trial but ordinary health-system access is uncertain.

**Professional evidence:** 2024 Declaration of Helsinki requires advance post-trial provisions for participants who still need a beneficial and reasonably safe intervention, with REC-approved exceptions and disclosure during consent. CIOMS Guideline 6 requires advance planning of level, scope, duration, financing, delivery and monitoring of post-trial care/access.

**Likely policies:** guaranteed continued access while medically needed; time-limited sponsor transition to health-system access; pre-agreed fair-benefit plan with REC-approved exceptions. Current professional guidance is strongly access-oriented, so candidate distinctness must focus on duration/responsibility rather than whether beneficial treatment may simply be abandoned.

## Batch-H working hypothesis

Potentially strongest candidate-audit targets: **M071, M072, M075, M076, M077, M080**. M073/M078/M079 may prove to be strong research-complete cases whose professional rules are conditional but not plural; M074 requires careful lower-resource/host-community evidence and should not turn background injustice into a comparator rule.
