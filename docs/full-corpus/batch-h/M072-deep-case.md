# M072 — Controlled human infection study

**Case-family identity:** M072  
**Featured crosswalk:** none  
**Primary domain:** human-subject research / infectious disease  
**Subdomain:** controlled human infection studies  
**Tags:** human challenge, CHIS, infectious disease, healthy volunteers, risk, consent  
**Research status:** deep case draft  
**Evidence date:** 2026-08-27

## 1. Decision architecture

**Decision-maker:** research ethics committee.  
**Decision question:** Under what conditions, if any, should an ethics committee approve deliberate infection of healthy volunteers for an important infectious-disease question?

## 2. Concise representation

Investigators propose a controlled human infection study in healthy adults to answer an important vaccine and pathogenesis question that conventional field studies would answer much more slowly. The challenge strain and dose are standardized, transmission to third parties can be tightly controlled, participants would be intensively monitored and compensated for research-related harm, and informed consent includes a demanding comprehension assessment. The pathogen can cause substantial illness, but severe outcomes are expected to be rare in the selected population. An effective rescue treatment reduces risk but does not eliminate it. The ethics committee must decide whether to approve under stringent WHO-style criteria, require an even lower residual-risk threshold or highly effective rescue before approval, or reject deliberate infection because the remaining nontherapeutic risk to healthy volunteers is too high.

## 3. Detailed representation

A research ethics committee is reviewing a controlled human infection study designed to accelerate evaluation of an important vaccine and improve understanding of infection dynamics. Healthy adults would be intentionally exposed to a carefully characterized challenge strain in a specialized isolation unit. Participant selection excludes known high-risk conditions; transmission outside the unit is tightly controlled; continuous clinical monitoring, rapid diagnostics, an effective rescue treatment and intensive supportive care are available; participants receive long-term follow-up and compensation for research-related injury. Investigators argue that field trials would take much longer and may be scientifically less informative. The challenge infection nevertheless carries nonzero risk of severe disease and uncertain long-term effects. The committee must decide whether the study meets stringent international criteria for ethical CHIS; whether approval should require a still lower quantified residual risk or near-certain rescue efficacy; or whether intentional infection of healthy volunteers is impermissible above a conservative risk threshold even with consent and social value.

## 4. Decision-critical facts

- healthy adult volunteers;
- intentional infection is scientifically important, not merely convenient;
- specialized containment and monitoring exist;
- effective rescue reduces but does not eliminate risk;
- severe outcomes are uncommon but possible;
- informed consent/comprehension and harm compensation are robust.

## 5. Explicit uncertainty

Long-term sequelae and rare severe outcomes cannot be completely ruled out. The value of accelerated knowledge is uncertain and may depend on whether other research routes remain feasible.

## 6. Jurisdiction and time strategy

Jurisdiction-neutral research ethics grounded in WHO's 2021 CHIS guidance and 2025 emergency criteria. The case is not pathogen-specific and contains no operationally harmful procedural detail.

## 7. Benchmark stipulations

- For this benchmark, assume the study has high social/scientific value.
- For this benchmark, assume transmission to third parties can be tightly controlled.
- For this benchmark, assume effective rescue/supportive care is available but not risk eliminating.

## 8. Serious policy / position families

- approve under stringent WHO-style criteria;
- approve only if an even lower residual-risk/rescue threshold is met;
- reject deliberate infection above a conservative healthy-volunteer risk threshold.

## 9. Public / affected-community evidence

Public attitudes toward challenge studies vary with disease severity, perceived social value, transparency and participant risk. COVID-19 challenge-study debates produced meaningful public engagement, but willingness to volunteer or general approval is not yet equivalent to two stable ethics-committee policies. Participant-specific evidence may support risk tolerance but requires careful policy translation.

## 10. Expert / professional recommendations

WHO's general CHIS guidance and 2025 emergency criteria explicitly recognize CHIS as potentially ethically acceptable under stringent conditions including strong scientific justification, favorable risk-benefit assessment, expert review, participant selection, consent, community engagement, high-quality clinical care and risk minimization. WHO does not make availability of a specific curative therapy an absolute universal prerequisite, though treatment is an important risk-reduction measure. This is one conditional professional architecture rather than a simple permissive rule.

## 11. Normative / framework positions and reasoning bridges

- **Autonomous altruism/social value:** competent adults can voluntarily accept bounded nontherapeutic risk to generate major public-health knowledge → permit when risks are minimized and consent is unusually robust.
- **Healthy-volunteer nonmaleficence:** researchers deliberately cause disease in people who do not need treatment → consent does not license high residual risk → impose a stricter ceiling than ordinary research.
- **Precaution under uncertainty:** rare harms and long-term sequelae may be poorly characterized → if uncertainty is too large, defer CHIS until safer challenge models or rescue measures exist.
