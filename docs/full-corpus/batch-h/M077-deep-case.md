# M077 — Pediatric research with no prospect of direct benefit

**Case-family identity:** M077  
**Featured crosswalk:** none  
**Primary domain:** human-subject research / pediatrics  
**Subdomain:** no-direct-benefit research; minimal risk  
**Tags:** pediatric research, assent, minimal risk, social value, no direct benefit  
**Research status:** deep case draft  
**Evidence date:** 2026-08-27

## Decision architecture

**Decision-maker:** research ethics committee.  
**Decision question:** When may children be enrolled in research procedures that offer no prospect of direct clinical benefit but are necessary to answer an important pediatric health question?

## Concise representation

Investigators propose a study requiring children with a rare disease to undergo research-only blood draws and one imaging procedure that offers no direct benefit. The knowledge cannot be obtained from adults because the biological process is specific to childhood. Parents would give permission and children capable of assent would be asked to agree. The committee must decide whether no-direct-benefit pediatric research should be limited to minimal risk, whether a minor increase above minimal risk may be accepted when social value is compelling and the question cannot be answered otherwise, or whether the study should be rejected whenever procedures exceed minimal risk even with assent and parental permission.

## Detailed representation

A research ethics committee reviews a natural-history study of a rare pediatric disorder. The study includes repeated blood sampling and one imaging procedure that is not clinically indicated and offers no prospect of direct benefit to participants. The information is important for designing future treatments and cannot be obtained from adults because the disease process changes with development. For this benchmark, assume most procedures are minimal risk but the imaging procedure creates a small additional burden that the committee could reasonably classify as a minor increase above minimal risk. Parents would give permission; school-age children and adolescents would receive developmentally tailored information and be asked for assent; refusal would ordinarily be respected. The committee must decide whether to approve only if all nonbeneficial procedures remain minimal risk, permit a minor increase above minimal risk because the social value is compelling and the research cannot be done otherwise, or reject the study unless the potentially burdensome imaging component is removed.

## Decision-critical facts

- no direct clinical benefit;
- knowledge is specifically pediatric and cannot be obtained from adults;
- most procedures are minimal risk;
- one procedure may be a minor increase above minimal risk;
- parent permission and age-appropriate assent are feasible;
- child refusal is respected.

## Explicit uncertainty

“Minimal risk” and “minor increase” are interpretive categories and may vary by comparator population and procedure. Social value is important but difficult to quantify.

## Jurisdiction and time strategy

Jurisdiction-neutral research-ethics case grounded in CIOMS Guideline 17 and compatible pediatric research regulations.

## Serious policy / position families

- minimal-risk ceiling for no-direct-benefit research;
- minor increase above minimal risk when social value/necessity are compelling;
- remove the higher-burden procedure and preserve only minimal-risk components.

## Public / affected-community evidence

Parents and young people often support pediatric research when burdens are low and the disease-specific purpose is clear, but willingness varies with procedure burden and perceived benefit to future children. This deep file needs direct studies of risk thresholds before executable public candidates can be claimed.

## Expert / professional recommendations

CIOMS Guideline 17 explicitly allows no-direct-benefit pediatric research at minimal risk and permits a minor increase above minimal risk when social value is compelling and the research cannot be conducted in adults. Parent permission and assent are required and child refusal generally should be respected. This supplies a genuine conditional professional boundary, but “minimal only” is not itself the CIOMS rule.

## Normative / framework positions

- **Protective constraint:** children cannot fully authorize altruistic research burdens → no-direct-benefit procedures should remain minimal risk.
- **Fair inclusion/social value:** excluding children can leave pediatric care evidence-poor → a small additional burden can be justified for indispensable, high-value pediatric knowledge.
- **Burden minimization:** when a higher-burden component is scientifically useful but not essential, remove it rather than expand permissible risk.

## Key sources

- CIOMS Guideline 17, 2016.
- HHS pediatric research protections/Subpart D as contextual regulatory architecture.

## Construction risks

Do not let parent permission substitute for assent; do not call all imaging or blood sampling minimal risk without scenario stipulation; separate “minor increase” from substantial risk; do not use social value to justify unlimited nonbeneficial risk.

## Executable-eligibility judgment

`needs-additional-evidence`. Expert conditional rules are strong, but affected-family/child policy evidence requires targeted research.

## Review requirements

Pediatric research ethics; pediatric IRB expertise; child/parent perspective; risk-classification review.
