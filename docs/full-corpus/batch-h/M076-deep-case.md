# M076 — High payment to healthy research participants

**Case-family identity:** M076  
**Featured crosswalk:** none  
**Primary domain:** human-subject research / participant payment  
**Subdomain:** compensation; undue influence  
**Tags:** payment, healthy volunteers, undue influence, fair compensation, IRB  
**Research status:** deep case draft  
**Evidence date:** 2026-08-27

## Decision architecture

**Decision-maker:** research ethics committee.  
**Decision question:** How should an ethics committee evaluate a high payment offer for a burdensome study whose underlying research risks are independently ethically acceptable?

The case explicitly separates payment from risk permissibility: payment cannot make an otherwise excessive-risk study acceptable.

## Concise representation

A healthy-volunteer pharmacology study requires ten inpatient days, repeated blood sampling and major time disruption but has already passed independent scientific and risk review. Investigators propose payment substantially above local minimum-wage compensation because recruitment is difficult and the burden is high. Payment accrues as participation proceeds, reimbursement of expenses is separate, and participants keep earned compensation if they withdraw. The ethics committee must decide whether to allow fair time/burden-based compensation even when the total is high, impose a conservative cap to reduce possible undue influence, or approve higher payment only with strengthened consent/comprehension safeguards and independent confirmation that research risk remains acceptable without considering the payment.

## Detailed representation

A phase-1 healthy-volunteer study requires participants to remain in a clinical research unit for ten days, undergo frequent blood sampling, follow dietary and activity restrictions and miss work or caregiving responsibilities. An independent IRB risk assessment has concluded that the protocol's physical risks are ethically acceptable regardless of payment. Investigators propose reimbursement of all direct expenses plus compensation for time, inconvenience and study burden at a rate that produces a high total payment relative to many participants' ordinary disposable income. Payments accrue proportionally rather than being withheld until completion; withdrawing participants keep what they have earned. Investigators argue that low payment would unfairly externalize research burdens and worsen recruitment. The committee must decide whether a high but fair time/burden-based payment should be approved; whether total payment should be capped because large offers may distort judgment; or whether high payment should be permitted only with strengthened consent/comprehension processes and explicit confirmation that risk evaluation has been conducted independently of compensation.

## Decision-critical facts

- protocol risk is independently acceptable;
- payment is for time/inconvenience, not a “benefit” offsetting risk;
- reimbursement is separate;
- payment accrues over time and is retained upon withdrawal;
- total amount is high relative to some participants' income;
- recruitment difficulty is present but not itself an ethical justification.

## Explicit uncertainty

There is no universally validated monetary threshold for undue influence. High payment can attract people with fewer economic options, but low payment can itself exploit participants' time and burden.

## Jurisdiction and time strategy

US-like IRB context generalized from FDA, CIOMS and SACHRP guidance.

## Serious policy / position families

- fair time/burden compensation without arbitrary low cap;
- conservative total cap to reduce undue influence;
- higher payment with strengthened consent and independent risk review.

## Public / affected-community evidence

Healthy-volunteer research shows participants value compensation and often treat payment as an important reason for enrollment. Empirical work on undue influence generally does not show that higher payment automatically destroys understanding or voluntariness. However, this deep file has not yet identified participant studies that directly compare cap versus fair-market compensation policies.

## Expert / professional recommendations

CIOMS requires reasonable reimbursement and compensation and warns against amounts that induce participation against better judgment. FDA says participant payment is generally acceptable, is not a research benefit, should be fair, reviewed by the IRB and paid on a schedule that does not penalize withdrawal. SACHRP further emphasizes that genuine payment is not coercion, that reimbursement/fair compensation do not themselves create undue influence, and that excessive payment concern should not lead to systematic underpayment. These sources support fair-compensation and safeguard-focused policies more strongly than arbitrary caps.

## Normative / framework positions

- **Fairness/non-exploitation:** research depends on participants' labor, time and inconvenience → fair compensation should not be suppressed merely because the total is large.
- **Autonomy protection:** unusually attractive payment can focus attention on money and reduce careful deliberation → cap or scrutinize offers where decision distortion is credible.
- **Procedural separation:** payment and risk are distinct ethical questions → allow high compensation only after independent risk review and robust comprehension/withdrawal protections.

## Construction risks

Do not call payment coercion; do not use payment as a benefit offsetting study risk; avoid inventing a universal dollar threshold; preserve pro-rata payment and withdrawal rights.
