# M078 — Inclusion of pregnant people in clinical trials

**Case-family identity:** M078  
**Featured crosswalk:** none  
**Primary domain:** human-subject research / pregnancy  
**Subdomain:** fair inclusion; fetal uncertainty  
**Tags:** pregnancy, clinical trials, inclusion, fetal risk, evidence gaps, consent  
**Research status:** deep case draft  
**Evidence date:** 2026-08-27

## Decision architecture

**Decision-maker:** trial sponsor and research ethics committee.  
**Decision question:** When a condition materially affects pregnant patients and evidence gaps are clinically important, under what circumstances should a therapeutic trial actively include pregnant participants?

## Concise representation

A medication is already used off-label during pregnancy for a serious maternal condition, but pregnant patients were excluded from pivotal trials and dosing/safety evidence is poor. Nonpregnant adult data are substantial and animal reproductive studies do not show a strong fetal safety signal, but fetal uncertainty remains. The proposed trial offers a prospect of maternal benefit and includes pregnancy-specific monitoring and long-term infant follow-up. The sponsor and ethics committee must decide whether pregnant patients should be presumptively eligible under pregnancy-specific safeguards, included only after additional nonpregnant safety data or a staged sentinel cohort, or excluded until fetal uncertainty falls below a more conservative threshold.

## Detailed representation

A sponsor is designing a therapeutic trial for a serious chronic condition that commonly persists during pregnancy. Clinicians already use the drug class during pregnancy because withholding treatment can harm maternal health, yet dosing, maternal efficacy and fetal safety evidence remain limited because pregnant people were historically excluded from trials. The investigational drug has substantial nonpregnant adult safety data and no major reproductive toxicity signal in available preclinical evidence. Participation could directly benefit the pregnant participant, but fetal risks remain uncertain. The protocol includes pregnancy-specific pharmacokinetic monitoring, fetal surveillance, obstetric expertise and infant follow-up. The sponsor and ethics committee must choose whether pregnant patients should be presumptively eligible when general risk-benefit criteria and pregnancy-specific safeguards are satisfied; whether enrollment should begin only after a staged sentinel cohort and additional nonpregnant evidence; or whether pregnancy should remain an exclusion criterion until fetal uncertainty is substantially lower.

## Decision-critical facts

- condition affects pregnant patients and untreated disease carries maternal risk;
- drug already has substantial nonpregnant evidence;
- no strong fetal safety signal is known, but uncertainty remains;
- trial offers potential maternal benefit;
- pregnancy-specific monitoring/follow-up are feasible;
- consent belongs to the pregnant participant.

## Explicit uncertainty

Rare fetal harms may not appear before broader use. Exclusion itself can shift risk into uncontrolled off-label care and leave future pregnant patients without evidence.

## Jurisdiction and time strategy

Jurisdiction-neutral ethics grounded in CIOMS pregnancy guidance, with HHS Subpart B as regulatory context.

## Serious policy / position families

- presumptive inclusion with pregnancy-specific safeguards;
- staged inclusion after additional nonpregnant/sentinel evidence;
- temporary exclusion until fetal-risk uncertainty is lower.

## Public / affected-community evidence

Pregnant patients and advocacy groups increasingly criticize routine exclusion from research, especially when medicines are used clinically without pregnancy evidence. Direct empirical comparisons of the three trial-entry rules remain limited and require targeted review before public candidates are constructed.

## Expert / professional recommendations

CIOMS treats women as presumptively includable unless there is a good scientific reason for exclusion and provides specific risk rules for pregnant participants. For interventions with potential individual benefit, risks should be minimized and outweighed by prospect of benefit; for no-direct-benefit procedures, risk thresholds are stricter. The pregnant participant's own consent cannot be replaced by another person's permission. This supports inclusion under proportionate safeguards rather than blanket exclusion, while allowing risk-based staged design.

## Normative / framework positions

- **Justice/fair inclusion:** systematic exclusion transfers uncertainty into ordinary care and denies pregnant patients evidence-based treatment → presume eligibility with tailored safeguards.
- **Precaution for fetus:** fetal harms can be irreversible and difficult to detect early → stage exposure until enough safety evidence accumulates.
- **Maternal autonomy / proportionality:** pregnancy does not erase a competent adult's authority to accept research risk when potential maternal benefit exists → evaluate risk-benefit rather than categorical status.

## Construction risks

Do not describe pregnant people as inherently vulnerable/incapable; do not require partner/father permission; distinguish direct-benefit trials from nonbeneficial fetal-risk studies; do not assume exclusion is risk-free.

`needs-additional-evidence`. Professional architecture supports fair inclusion but direct affected-policy evidence and action-distinct expert policies need deeper sourcing.
