## M181 — Recurrent nonadherence to chronic-disease treatment
**Scenario:** PASS; structural barriers, treatment burden, capacity and patient desire for treatment are explicit.  
**Universe (7):** redesign care around cost/housing/work barriers and simplify access (✓ affected evidence/professional); simplify regimen and accept less aggressive targets when treatment burden itself undermines achievable benefit (✓ patient-centered chronic-care reasoning); intensive adherence support with pharmacy/social-work/case-management follow-up before labeling treatment failure (✓ professional/source-informed); require demonstrated ability to follow selected unusually high-risk or scarce therapies only when adherence is materially necessary to safety/effectiveness (constructed bounded comparator); never condition ordinary necessary care on moralized “compliance” (✓ justice/framework); structural-justice rule treating adherence failure partly as a system-design problem (✓ framework); proportional stewardship rule matching resource-intensive supports to expected benefit while preserving baseline care (constructed/framework).  
**Source-grounded:** strong for barriers and responsive care, weak for multiple universal institutional rules. **Expanded:** YES. **Demo:** VERY HIGH. **Action:** EXPAND-UNIVERSE.

## M182 — Suspected elder abuse when a capacitated patient resists intervention
**Scenario:** PASS; capacity, legal reporting threshold, safety risk and refusal are fixed without assuming incapacity from age.  
**Universe (6):** honor the competent refusal while using confidential safety planning, resources and close follow-up (✓ safeguarding/autonomy); intensify multidisciplinary assessment of coercion, capacity and immediate danger before discharge when evidence is concerning but threshold is uncertain (✓ professional); offer voluntary temporary separation/respite or alternative safe placement without making it a condition of future autonomy (constructed/supportive comparator); report or override refusal only when the stipulated statutory/imminent-danger threshold is crossed (✓ professional/legal); least-restrictive safeguarding rule requiring escalation only as risk/constraint becomes necessary and proportionate (✓ framework); relational-autonomy rule supporting trusted-person/community involvement chosen by the patient while screening for coercion (✓ framework).  
**Source-grounded:** one integrated conditional safeguarding architecture. **Expanded:** YES. **Demo:** HIGH. **Action:** EXPAND-UNIVERSE.

## M183 — Suspected child abuse with uncertain evidence
**Scenario:** PASS; law uses reasonable suspicion rather than diagnostic proof and specialist evaluation can occur promptly.  
**Universe (6):** report once reasonable-suspicion threshold is met while continuing medical/forensic evaluation (✓ AAP/professional); if current facts genuinely remain below reasonable suspicion, complete rapid specialist assessment before reporting provided delay does not increase danger (✓ threshold-based professional); use immediate protective hospitalization/observation only when medical or safety facts independently justify it (constructed bounded comparator); communicate the report and next steps to caregivers when doing so does not increase risk (✓ trauma-informed/professional); child-protection framework favors a low evidentiary threshold because waiting for certainty can expose the child to preventable harm (✓ framework); procedural-fairness/nonmaleficence rule minimizes unnecessary coercive investigation while never raising the legal reporting threshold (✓ framework).  
**Source-grounded:** strong threshold architecture; no serious “wait for proof” candidate. **Expanded:** YES. **Demo:** HIGH. **Action:** EXPAND-UNIVERSE.

## M184 — Intimate-partner violence disclosure and patient autonomy
**Scenario:** PASS; competent adult, high-risk history, no mandatory-reporting trigger and no endangered child/dependent are explicit.  
**Universe (6):** confidential patient-led safety planning and resource offer without forced police/shelter involvement (✓ ACOG/professional); assertive multidisciplinary advocacy/outreach that remains opt-in and preserves the patient’s right to refuse (✓ source-informed professional); schedule proactive confidential follow-up and repeat danger assessment rather than treating one refusal as the end of care (✓ professional/source-informed); breach confidentiality only when a defined emergency/legal threshold is met (✓ professional/legal); autonomy/trauma-informed framework rejects coercive “rescue” below that threshold (✓ framework); relational-safety framework supports stronger clinician effort to expand feasible exit/safety options while leaving final action with the competent patient (✓ framework).  
**Source-grounded:** strong integrated autonomy-plus-safety process. **Expanded:** YES. **Demo:** VERY HIGH. **Action:** EXPAND-UNIVERSE.

## M185 — Chronic pain and opioid prescribing under emerging misuse risk
**Scenario:** PASS; meaningful functional benefit, rising risk indicators, no immediate overdose and continued care obligations are explicit.  
**Universe (7):** continue opioids with intensified monitoring/risk mitigation while benefits still exceed risks (✓ CDC); collaborative gradual taper/transition when risks now exceed benefits (✓ CDC); discontinue controlled prescribing only when risk becomes unmanageable while continuing pain/OUD care and nonabandonment (✓ professional); evaluate and treat possible OUD without treating one toxicology result or early refill as moral proof (✓ professional); time-limited continuation with predefined reassessment goals when benefit-risk balance is genuinely uncertain (constructed/proportional comparator); nonmaleficence/stewardship framework gives overdose/diversion risk increasing weight as evidence accumulates (✓ framework); relational-autonomy framework opposes abrupt forced taper and emphasizes shared goals, function and withdrawal safety (✓ framework).  
**Source-grounded:** strong conditional professional architecture. **Expanded:** YES. **Demo:** VERY HIGH. **Action:** EXPAND-UNIVERSE.

## M186 — Treating acute pain in a patient with opioid use disorder
**Scenario:** PASS; severe surgical pain, stable buprenorphine treatment and relapse/undertreatment concerns are fixed.  
**Universe (6):** continue effective OUD medication and provide full multimodal analgesia including additional opioids when indicated (✓ contemporary addiction/pain guidance); use an opioid-sparing multimodal plan while retaining rescue opioid access and specialist input (✓ professional); temporarily adjust buprenorphine/OUD regimen only for a specific perioperative indication under addiction/pain expertise (✓ professional); use individualized shared planning that explicitly addresses relapse risk, tolerance and the patient’s prior pain-treatment experience (✓ source-informed professional); equal-treatment/non-stigma framework rejects undertreatment because of OUD history (✓ framework); relapse-prevention/nonmaleficence framework favors the lowest opioid exposure compatible with adequate analgesia, never untreated severe pain (✓ framework).  
**Source-grounded:** professional convergence around adequate analgesia + OUD continuity; implementation differences remain analytically useful. **Expanded:** YES. **Demo:** HIGH. **Action:** EXPAND-UNIVERSE.

## M187 — Adolescent EHR and patient-portal confidentiality
**Scenario:** PASS after identity replacement; bedside confidentiality is already settled and the decision object is EHR/proxy-access architecture.  
**Universe (7):** granular segmentation of sensitive diagnoses, labs, prescriptions, problem-list items and notes with proxy blocking while ordinary proxy access remains (✓ AAP 2026 direct); confidential adolescent encounter/sub-portal architecture that automatically protects legally sensitive categories while preserving adolescent access (source-informed implementation); broad proxy access with clinician item-by-item privacy marking plus prompts/auditing (constructed implementation comparator); adolescent-controlled proxy permissions from a defined age with safety/legal exceptions (constructed autonomy comparator); system-wide suppression of confidential billing/EOB and exchanged-data leakage as a mandatory safeguard (✓ AAP direct, cross-cutting rather than a complete portal architecture); confidentiality/developing-autonomy framework favors robust technical control because leakage can deter sensitive-care disclosure (✓ framework); parental-responsibility/care-coordination framework favors broad nonconfidential proxy access with reliable protected carve-outs (✓ framework).  
**Source-grounded:** strong Expert architecture; direct stakeholder preference over technical defaults remains limited. **Expanded:** YES. **Demo:** VERY HIGH. **Action:** RECONSTRUCTED/EXPAND-UNIVERSE. Historical M187 is superseded as a duplicate of M004.

## M188 — Predictive genetic testing of a child for an adult-onset condition
**Scenario:** PASS; no childhood surveillance/treatment benefit, child is 12 and not asking to know, and parental anxiety is explicit but not treated as a medical indication.  
**Universe (6):** defer predictive testing until the young person can decide because no childhood medical action follows (✓ AAP/ACMG); permit a narrow exception where psychosocial burden is substantial and a sufficiently mature adolescent concurs (✓ professional exception); individualized ethics/genetics review where penetrance, age of onset, family circumstances or near-term management depart from the canonical case (✓ professional); parental-authority comparator permitting testing after informed permission even without childhood intervention (constructed serious comparator); future-autonomy/right-to-an-open-future framework favors deferral (✓ framework); family-welfare/relational framework permits carefully bounded earlier knowledge when substantial family/child psychosocial benefit is credibly expected (✓ framework).  
**Source-grounded:** strong professional deferral architecture with narrow exceptions. **Expanded:** YES. **Demo:** HIGH. **Action:** EXPAND-UNIVERSE.

## M189 — Mammography screening when current guidelines disagree
**Scenario:** PASS; average-risk women in their 40s, harms/benefits and guideline dates are explicit.  
**Universe (7):** public/source-informed orientation toward screening now/at shorter intervals (✓ survey-inferred); public/source-informed orientation toward delaying or reducing frequency after balanced decision information (✓ survey-inferred); USPSTF biennial screening age 40–74 (✓ direct Expert policy); ACS annual screening in the 40s under its current schedule (✓ direct Expert policy); mortality-benefit framework favoring more frequent early screening (✓ framework); iatrogenic-burden framework favoring less frequent screening to reduce false positives/overdiagnosis (✓ framework); preference-sensitive framework favoring standardized counseling without one standing frequency recommendation where multiple guideline-supported options exist (✓ framework).  
**Source-grounded:** YES; existing audited geometry 2 Public × 2 Expert × 3 Framework, 16 cross-source pairs, Mean aggregation. **Expanded:** NOT NEEDED for present universe. **Demo:** VERY HIGH. **Action:** RETAIN. The older Batch-S “0 executable” summary is stale and superseded by the later deep-case audit/records.

## M190 — Incidental 4-mm pulmonary nodule after low-value imaging
**Scenario:** PASS; low-risk adult, small solid nodule, prior low-value imaging and patient anxiety are all explicit.  
**Universe (6):** no routine follow-up below the applicable risk/size threshold (✓ Fleischner/professional); optional surveillance only when individualized risk or informed preference justifies it within guideline discretion (✓ professional/source-informed); escalate to more intensive work-up only when additional clinical/radiographic risk changes the category (✓ professional); shared-decision comparator permitting one low-burden surveillance scan for persistent informed anxiety despite low malignancy risk (constructed bounded comparator); nonmaleficence/diagnostic-stewardship framework opposes cascades generated by incidental findings (✓ framework); precaution/relational-autonomy framework permits proportionate surveillance when residual uncertainty and patient values justify the downstream burden (✓ framework).  
**Source-grounded:** one risk-stratified professional architecture. **Expanded:** YES. **Demo:** HIGH. **Action:** EXPAND-UNIVERSE.
