# Bioethics Bench — Rich Candidate Audit M111–M120

**Date:** 2026-08-30

## M111 — Directed donation with discriminatory conditions
**Scenario:** PASS only if donor is otherwise eligible and the requested condition is clearly distinguished from naming a loved one.  
**Universe (6):** permit donation to a named individual (✓ direct governance/autonomy exception); reject conditions directing organs to or excluding recipients by race/religion/gender/sexual orientation (✓ direct OPTN ethics); accept the donation but remove the discriminatory condition before allocation (constructed policy comparator); refuse the donation entirely when the condition itself threatens public trust or dignitary equality (constructed/framework); donor-autonomy framework favoring wide control over a gratuitous gift (✓ framework); equal-status/anti-discrimination framework limiting donor control once an organ enters the allocation system (✓ framework).  
**Source-grounded:** professional/governance strong; Public role sparse. **Expanded:** YES. **Demo:** HIGH. **Action:** EXPAND-UNIVERSE.

## M112 — Paired kidney exchange and chain obligations
**Scenario:** PASS; donor remains competent and legally/ethically free to withdraw before surgery, but withdrawal can disrupt a chain.  
**Universe (6):** preserve unconditional right to withdraw without penalty up to surgery (✓ direct professional); use scheduling/sequence design to reduce reliance on non-simultaneous promises (✓ professional/governance); require explicit advance counseling about chain consequences while prohibiting pressure to continue (✓ professional); allow confidential withdrawal reasons so donor need not bear interpersonal blame (✓ professional/constructed); autonomy framework treating withdrawal as nonwaivable (✓ framework); reliance/fairness framework supporting stronger moral—though not coercive—commitment once others have acted in reliance (✓ framework).  
**Source-grounded:** strong consensus on noncoercive withdrawal. **Expanded:** YES. **Demo:** HIGH. **Action:** EXPAND-UNIVERSE.

## M113 — Multi-organ transplantation versus separate recipients
**Scenario:** PASS if one candidate has a genuine clinical indication for multiple organs and two single-organ candidates are also eligible.  
**Universe (7):** prioritize multi-organ candidate when combined transplant provides substantially greater survival benefit and organs are jointly necessary (✓ governance-derived); protect single-organ access with explicit multi-organ eligibility/safety-net rules (✓ HRSA/OPTN policy); allocate organs separately when expected aggregate benefit materially exceeds the combined transplant (✓ framework/constructed); use a threshold rule requiring minimum incremental benefit for multi-organ allocation (constructed policy); urgency/rescue framework favoring multi-organ candidate (✓ framework); aggregate-benefit framework favoring separate recipients (✓ framework); procedural justice rule requiring transparent nationally consistent criteria and audit of effects on single-organ waiting lists (✓ governance/framework).  
**Source-grounded:** governance strong; affected Public role thin. **Expanded:** YES. **Demo:** VERY HIGH. **Action:** EXPAND-UNIVERSE.

## M114 — Global allocation of scarce vaccine or therapeutic
**Scenario:** PASS only with a named international allocator, fixed supply, country epidemiology, health-system burden, and manufacturing contribution explicit.  
**Universe (7):** equal initial allocation then need/risk-based redistribution (✓ WHO-type governance); prioritize countries with greatest epidemiologic burden and marginal health benefit (✓ framework/governance); prioritize health-system/frontline protection to preserve response capacity (✓ governance); give reciprocity weight to countries contributing manufacturing/research resources (✓ framework/governance); allocate proportionally by population after minimum need threshold (constructed equality comparator); reserve a share for low-resource/worst-off countries independent of purchasing power (✓ equity framework); multiprinciple transparent allocation combining need, benefit, equity, reciprocity, and procedural fairness (✓ WHO-derived).  
**Source-grounded:** governance yes; cross-national public policy role needs case-specific evidence. **Expanded:** YES. **Demo:** VERY HIGH. **Action:** EXPAND-UNIVERSE.

## M115 — International recruitment of health workers from shortage countries
**Scenario:** PASS; distinguish individual worker migration from active recruitment by destination institutions.  
**Universe (7):** refrain from active recruitment from safeguard-list countries while preserving individual freedom to migrate (✓ direct WHO); allow recruitment only through government-to-government agreements with proportional support/co-investment for source systems (✓ WHO); unrestricted recruitment where workers independently apply and contracts are fair (constructed/source-informed); destination system compensates source-country training loss through financing/training partnerships (✓ framework/governance); worker-autonomy framework rejecting restrictions on individual mobility (✓ framework); global-health-equity framework limiting institutional recruitment that worsens severe shortages (✓ framework); ethical-recruitment certification with transparency, no recruitment fees, portability, and retention support (✓ governance-derived).  
**Source-grounded:** professional/governance strong; worker-policy preferences need evidence. **Expanded:** YES. **Demo:** VERY HIGH. **Action:** EXPAND-UNIVERSE.

## M116 — Pharmaceutical IP versus access during major health need
**Scenario:** PASS if product, manufacturing bottleneck, price, demand, and whether licensing would actually increase supply are fixed.  
**Universe (7):** preserve exclusivity with subsidized procurement/donation (✓ governance/practice); negotiated tiered voluntary licensing and technology transfer (✓ governance); compulsory licensing/TRIPS public-health flexibility when voluntary access fails (✓ direct policy option); public/private patent pool or shared manufacturing platform (✓ governance); innovation-incentive framework favoring time-limited exclusivity with access obligations (✓ framework); right-to-health/global-justice framework favoring broad licensing/technology sharing during severe need (✓ framework); staged trigger: voluntary agreement first, compulsory mechanisms if price/supply targets unmet (constructed proportionality).  
**Source-grounded:** policy/governance rich. **Expanded:** YES. **Demo:** VERY HIGH. **Action:** EXPAND-UNIVERSE.

## M117 — Essential health care for refugees/migrants regardless status
**Scenario:** PASS if the dispute is scope/financing beyond emergency care, not whether acutely lifesaving care may be denied.  
**Universe (6):** equal access to essential services irrespective status (✓ WHO governance); universal emergency/communicable/maternal-child core plus staged access to broader services (constructed policy comparator); residence-based enrollment in ordinary primary/chronic care with firewall from immigration enforcement (✓ framework/governance); restrict publicly funded nonurgent services to a defined essential package where resources are constrained (constructed); equal moral status/public-health framework favoring broad inclusion (✓ framework); stewardship framework allowing transparent scope limits but rejecting status as a proxy for worth (✓ framework).  
**Source-grounded:** professional inclusionary consensus. **Expanded:** YES. **Demo:** HIGH. **Action:** EXPAND-UNIVERSE.

## M118 — Clinical neutrality in armed conflict
**Scenario:** PASS as a consensus/control case; security risk may affect logistics but bedside clinical priority is by need.  
**Universe (5):** impartial clinical triage based solely on medical need/urgency (✓ direct WMA); security separation outside bedside triage without discriminatory clinical priority (✓ governance); prioritize those most likely to benefit when resources are scarce, irrespective combatant status (✓ professional/framework); civilian-priority rule (constructed comparator, explicitly contrary to clinical-neutrality guidance); medical-neutrality/equal-status framework (✓ framework).  
**Source-grounded:** very strong convergence. **Expanded:** YES but intentionally limited. **Demo:** MEDIUM as a consensus/control case. **Action:** RETAIN/EXPAND-MINIMALLY.

## M119 — Technology transfer versus immediate procurement
**Scenario:** PASS if donor/funder has a fixed budget and must choose short-run procurement, local capacity building, or a hybrid.  
**Universe (7):** lowest-cost immediate procurement to maximize near-term doses/treatments (constructed efficiency comparator); invest in local production/technology transfer despite higher short-run cost (✓ WHO governance); staged hybrid: procure now while financing regional capacity for future supply (✓ governance-derived); require licensing/training/quality systems as condition of donor procurement contracts (constructed policy); near-term lives-saved framework favoring procurement (✓ framework); resilience/self-determination framework favoring technology transfer (✓ framework); equity rule prioritizing capacity in regions structurally excluded from manufacturing (✓ framework/WHO).  
**Source-grounded:** governance strong; affected/donor preference evidence limited. **Expanded:** YES. **Demo:** VERY HIGH. **Action:** EXPAND-UNIVERSE.

## M120 — Global allocation of ultra-expensive curative gene therapy
**Scenario:** PASS if fixed marginal global-health budget and displaced alternatives are explicit.  
**Universe (7):** maximize total health gain across all interventions (✓ framework/HTA-derived); severity/rescue priority funding some cures despite lower cost-effectiveness (✓ framework); reserve portfolio share for rare severe curative therapies while spending remainder on high-impact population services (constructed multiprinciple); threshold with severity modifier (✓ HTA/governance analogy); conditional funding tied to price reduction/evidence generation (✓ governance); equal-life-chance/anti-abandonment framework favoring access for small severe-disease groups (✓ framework); opportunity-cost/global-justice framework prioritizing scalable interventions for the worst-off populations (✓ framework).  
**Source-grounded:** no mature global professional rule; candidate universe remains excellent. **Expanded:** YES. **Demo:** VERY HIGH. **Action:** EXPAND-UNIVERSE.
