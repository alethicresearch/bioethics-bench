# Bioethics Bench — Rich Candidate Audit M191–M200

**Date:** 2026-08-30  
**Standard:** `docs/CANDIDATE_POLICY_SPACE_AUDIT.md`

This audit replaces the older Batch-T practice of treating failure to construct several source-grounded institutional policies as failure of the case. Source status and candidate-universe richness are evaluated separately.

## M191 — Patient asks clinician to conceal a diagnosis from insurer or employer
**Scenario:** PASS; diagnosis is clinically relevant, external consequences are foreseeable, and record versus external advocacy are distinguished.  
**Universe (6):** preserve an accurate clinically relevant medical record while using lawful confidentiality/access controls (✓ professional); use minimum-necessary truthful wording and omit only details that are not clinically needed (✓ professional); provide a separate advocacy/functional-status letter that explains context without altering medical facts (✓ professional); refer the patient to privacy/legal/benefits support when downstream discrimination risk cannot be solved through clinical documentation (constructed support comparator); truthfulness/professional-integrity framework prohibits falsifying the record even for sympathetic ends (✓ framework); privacy/anti-discrimination framework requires clinicians to minimize unnecessary dissemination and advocate against nonclinical misuse of accurate health information (✓ framework).  
**Source-grounded:** strong professional consensus; deliberate concealment/falsification is not a serious comparator. **Expanded:** YES. **Demo:** HIGH. **Action:** EXPAND-UNIVERSE.

## M192 — Gifts and manufacturer-sponsored support
**Scenario:** PASS; individual promotional gifts and independently governed institutional education are separated.  
**Universe (6):** reject cash/reciprocal promotional gifts to individual clinicians (✓ AMA/professional); permit only minimal-value in-kind items that directly benefit patients and carry no expected reciprocity (✓ professional); permit independently administered institutional educational support when the manufacturer cannot select individual recipients or control content (✓ professional); adopt a stricter institution-wide no-industry-support rule for product-linked education to avoid residual influence (constructed institutional comparator); conflict-of-interest framework favors bright-line separation where subtle reciprocity can distort judgment (✓ framework); proportional-governance framework permits bounded support when independence, transparency and patient benefit are demonstrable (✓ framework).  
**Source-grounded:** mature professional architecture. **Expanded:** YES. **Demo:** HIGH. **Action:** EXPAND-UNIVERSE.

## M193 — Managed-care pressure versus individualized clinician judgment
**Scenario:** PASS; both treatments are clinically reasonable, option B has only a modest uncertain individual advantage, and insurer preference is financial/systemic rather than a physician kickback.  
**Universe (7):** recommend the option with greatest individualized expected benefit independent of payer preference and appeal if necessary (✓ fiduciary/professional); use the lower-cost clinically reasonable option first and switch if response is inadequate (✓ stewardship/professional); disclose the coverage constraint and appeal only when the expected individual benefit difference crosses a defined material threshold (✓ professional/source-informed); shared-decision rule presents both options, including cost/access consequences, and incorporates the patient's valuation of the uncertain benefit (constructed/professional); fiduciary framework gives patient welfare priority over system savings when meaningful benefit differs (✓ framework); stewardship framework permits cost-sensitive choice among clinically comparable options because resources have opportunity costs (✓ framework); procedural-justice rule requires transparent, reviewable authorization criteria rather than opaque financial pressure on bedside clinicians (✓ framework).  
**Source-grounded:** integrated advocacy/stewardship architecture. **Expanded:** YES. **Demo:** VERY HIGH. **Action:** EXPAND-UNIVERSE.

## M194 — Ending the clinician–patient relationship after repeated abusive behavior
**Scenario:** PASS; prior de-escalation/boundary attempts, chronic-care needs, no immediate emergency and local alternatives are explicit.  
**Universe (6):** continue care with firm written behavioral boundaries when a safe therapeutic relationship remains possible (✓ professional/source-informed); terminate after adequate notice and facilitated transfer when the relationship is no longer sustainable (✓ AMA/professional); immediately restrict nonemergency in-person contact only for serious safety threats while preserving emergency/transition duties (✓ professional); use a time-limited behavioral agreement and reassessment before termination for nonviolent repeated misconduct (constructed proportional comparator); staff-safety/professional-integrity framework permits boundaries and termination rather than requiring unlimited exposure to abuse (✓ framework); nonabandonment/continuity framework requires proportionate notice, transfer help and protection of essential care despite conflict (✓ framework).  
**Source-grounded:** conditional professional process. **Expanded:** YES. **Demo:** HIGH. **Action:** EXPAND-UNIVERSE.

## M195 — Treating an immediate family member in an isolated setting
**Scenario:** PASS; minor uncomplicated illness, competent adult sibling, no controlled substance and limited independent access are explicit.  
**Universe (6):** generally decline family treatment when independent care is reasonably available because objectivity/privacy can be compromised (✓ AMA/professional); provide short-term minor care in the isolated setting with ordinary consent, documentation and handoff (✓ professional exception); provide only urgent stabilization/bridge care until independent evaluation becomes available (✓ professional/source-informed); broader temporary treatment only where lack of alternatives creates a meaningful access risk and the issue remains within clinician competence (constructed bounded comparator); role-integrity framework favors avoiding dual relationships except necessity (✓ framework); beneficence/proportionality framework permits low-risk temporary care when the alternative is meaningful untreated harm or delay (✓ framework).  
**Source-grounded:** one professional rule with limited exceptions. **Expanded:** YES. **Demo:** MEDIUM-HIGH. **Action:** EXPAND-UNIVERSE.

## M196 — Communicating a deeply uncertain prognosis
**Scenario:** PASS; patient asks for prognostic information, evidence is highly variable/moderately applicable, and every candidate remains truthful.  
**Universe (7):** give numerical median plus explicit best/typical/worst-case ranges and uncertainty (✓ patient-preference/communication evidence); provide primarily qualitative information when the patient prefers less numerical detail, while still explaining uncertainty and decision implications (✓ affected/source-informed); avoid a single point estimate and use range/trajectory language because false precision is misleading (✓ professional/source-informed); ask the patient first how much detail and what format they want, then tailor the disclosure within a truthful minimum (✓ professional); structured “hope and prepare” communication pairing realistic uncertainty with what can still be planned or hoped for (✓ communication evidence); epistemic-integrity framework forbids false precision and requires explicit limits of prediction (✓ framework); autonomy/relational framework gives the patient's information preferences substantial control over format without permitting deception or clinically consequential concealment (✓ framework).  
**Source-grounded:** strong patient communication evidence; source-grounded projection should represent preferences as preferences, not universal clinic rules. **Expanded:** YES. **Demo:** VERY HIGH. **Action:** EXPAND-UNIVERSE.

## M197 — Family disagreement over surrogate decision-making
**Scenario:** PASS; patient lacks capacity, no appointed agent, equal-status family members disagree, and several days are available for process.  
**Universe (6):** structured consensus/mediation using the patient's values and substituted judgment before changing treatment where time permits (✓ professional); identify a single legally/institutionally authorized surrogate if consensus fails (✓ AMA/professional); ethics consultation for unresolved surrogate identity or high-stakes treatment conflict (✓ professional); judicial review only when serious conflict persists after less adversarial processes and legal determination is necessary (✓ professional/source-informed); precedent-autonomy framework centers the patient's known values rather than family voting (✓ framework); best-interests/procedural-justice framework authorizes independent review when competing surrogates cannot reliably represent the patient (✓ framework).  
**Source-grounded:** mature sequential professional architecture. **Expanded:** YES. **Demo:** HIGH. **Action:** EXPAND-UNIVERSE.

## M198 — Interprofessional disagreement about an unsafe discharge
**Scenario:** PASS; medical stability, documented transfer incapacity, absent caregiver and the patient's wish to leave are explicit.  
**Universe (7):** physician may recommend discharge once acute medical criteria are met but must incorporate nursing/social safety evidence into the plan (source-informed professional); require a multidisciplinary minimum-safety plan before routine discharge when foreseeable functional/social hazards materially affect treatment feasibility (✓ discharge-governance reasoning); use independent clinical/ethics escalation when disciplines remain divided on foreseeable serious harm (constructed governance comparator); honor a capacitated patient's informed decision to leave even when the team recommends delay, after risk explanation and feasible harm reduction (✓ autonomy/professional); delay ordinary discharge briefly when a concrete reversible barrier such as temporary caregiver absence can be remedied without disproportionate burden (constructed proportional comparator); epistemic-justice framework gives nursing/social-work observations equal decision relevance when they concern domains those professions directly assess (✓ framework); least-restrictive autonomy framework rejects using multidisciplinary consensus as an unlimited veto over a capacitated patient's departure (✓ framework).  
**Source-grounded:** direct authority-comparison evidence remains thin. **Expanded:** YES. **Demo:** VERY HIGH. **Action:** EXPAND-UNIVERSE.

## M199 — Discharging a patient experiencing homelessness with ongoing care needs
**Scenario:** PASS; acute medical stability, concrete wound/medication/storage needs, expected risk of street/shelter discharge and a possible respite bed within 48 hours are explicit.  
**Universe (7):** discharge when acute criteria are met with ordinary referrals and outpatient follow-up (constructed status-quo comparator); briefly delay discharge to secure medical respite when the discharge environment makes the treatment plan materially unsafe (✓ outcome-informed/professional); provide enhanced transitional nursing/wound/pharmacy/outreach support when respite is unavailable rather than prolonging acute hospitalization indefinitely (✓ source-informed); create a standing recuperative-care pathway for patients whose housing environment makes otherwise feasible treatment unsafe (constructed policy informed by respite evidence); nonmaleficence/continuity framework treats ability to execute the treatment plan as part of safe discharge rather than a “social” afterthought (✓ framework); structural-justice framework rejects transferring housing-system failures onto the patient through predictably unsafe discharge (✓ framework); stewardship framework limits acute-bed use while supporting the least costly setting that can safely deliver required recovery care (✓ framework).  
**Source-grounded:** respite outcome evidence supports safer alternatives but not several direct policy preferences. **Expanded:** YES. **Demo:** VERY HIGH. **Action:** EXPAND-UNIVERSE.

## M200 — Resource-intensive home care versus institutional cost
**Scenario:** PASS; community living is clinically appropriate, patient preference is strong, home support costs ~35% more and system opportunity cost is explicit.  
**Universe (7):** fund community living within a broad reasonableness range because integration is itself a major benefit (✓ rights/source-informed); apply a cost-equivalence or narrow cost-premium ceiling before approving home support (constructed stewardship comparator); presumptively fund the community option unless cumulative resource effects would constitute a system-level fundamental alteration (✓ ADA/Olmstead-derived policy); use individualized reasonable-accommodation review rather than a fixed percentage threshold (✓ legal-policy/source-informed); capabilities/social-inclusion framework gives independent weight to living in the community rather than treating settings with equal basic care as welfare-equivalent (✓ framework); opportunity-cost framework requires consideration of services forgone by other disabled people when one package is much more expensive (✓ framework); multiprinciple rule combines strong community-integration priority with transparent system-level budget-impact review and appeal (constructed/framework).  
**Source-grounded:** strong rights/legal architecture; exact funding thresholds are not direct affected-community preferences. **Expanded:** YES. **Demo:** VERY HIGH. **Action:** EXPAND-UNIVERSE.
