3. Recommend transition to comfort care but preserve parental decision authority within a medically reasonable range — ✓ professional/shared-decision.
4. After second opinion, ethics review, transfer opportunity, and due process, permit forgoing treatment judged nonbeneficial despite persistent parental request — ✓ direct professional.
5. Give parents strong presumptive authority because they bear special responsibility for the newborn under uncertainty — ✓ framework-derived.
6. Make the child's benefit/burden balance controlling once treatment mainly prolongs suffering or dying — ✓ framework-derived best interests/nonmaleficence.

**Candidate universe (7):**

2. Presume active resuscitation when survival remains materially possible unless informed parents choose comfort care — constructed-comparator / survival-priority framework.

4. Begin a time-limited trial of resuscitation at birth and redirect according to actual condition/response — ✓ framework-derived proportionality; professionally compatible.

6. Give survival/vitality greater weight when parents prioritize the possibility of life despite disability/uncertainty — ✓ framework-derived; grounded in affected-family values, not a public institutional policy.
7. Give avoidance of invasive suffering greater weight when parents prioritize comfort and burdens — ✓ framework-derived; grounded in affected-family values.

## M027 — Advance directive versus current contentment in dementia

**Scenario:** PASS; this is a paradigmatic conflict between precedent autonomy and present interests.

**Candidate universe (7):**
1. Follow the applicable prior directive even when the person now appears content, because the capable person's precedent autonomous choice governs — ✓ framework-derived precedent autonomy; supported by directive literature.
2. Give current experiential welfare and expressed assent/dissent priority when the person can no longer endorse the earlier directive — ✓ framework-derived present-interest/minimal autonomy.
3. Use a hybrid rule: prior directive has strong presumptive weight but can be overridden when current wellbeing is substantial and treatment burden/benefit differs materially from what was anticipated — ✓ framework-derived hybrid.
4. Interpret the directive through the person's broader values rather than literal wording, using substituted judgment where possible — ✓ framework-derived/source-informed surrogate ethics.
5. Honor present resistance to burdensome treatment even if the old directive would permit it; do not use precedent autonomy to compel unwanted present intervention — ✓ framework-derived current agency.
6. Use a time-limited treatment trial when prognosis and current quality of life are uncertain, then reassess against both prior values and present welfare — constructed-comparator / proportionality.
7. Require an independent review when literal directive and current apparent interests sharply conflict, documenting why one temporal self's interests are given priority — constructed/procedural comparator.

**Source-grounded canonical Public/Expert:** limited; **expanded philosophical projection:** excellent. **Demo:** VERY HIGH. **Action:** EXPAND-UNIVERSE; retain even without canonical tripartite source-grounded projection.

## M029 — Palliative sedation for refractory suffering

**Scenario:** PASS if suffering is genuinely refractory after specialist palliative care and death is expected from underlying illness rather than sedation.

**Candidate universe (7):**
1. Use proportional sedation at the lowest depth that relieves refractory suffering, with reassessment and titration — ✓ direct professional.
2. Permit continuous deep sedation when lesser sedation cannot adequately relieve refractory suffering and the patient gives informed consent — ✓ direct professional.
3. Begin intermittent/time-limited sedation before continuous deep sedation when symptom pattern and patient goals make a reversible trial feasible — ✓ source-informed professional/proportionality.
4. Honor a capable dying patient's informed preference for deeper continuous sedation once suffering is refractory, even if a lighter level might technically reduce symptoms — ✓ framework-derived autonomy.
5. Require the minimum effective depth because preserving interaction/consciousness has intrinsic and relational value when symptom relief can still be achieved — ✓ framework-derived proportionality/relational value.
6. Decline sedation intended primarily to hasten death, while distinguishing that intention from proportionate symptom relief that may foreseeably affect consciousness or survival — ✓ direct professional / doctrine-of-double-effect-type framework.
7. Use shared decision with family involvement only as the patient wishes; family distress alone should not determine depth — ✓ source-informed affected-family/professional.

**Source-grounded:** professional strong, empirical Public-policy plurality limited. **Expanded:** YES. **Demo:** HIGH. **Action:** EXPAND-UNIVERSE.
