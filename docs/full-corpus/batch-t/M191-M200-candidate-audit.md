## M196 — communicating deeply uncertain prognosis

M196 has unusually direct patient evidence about communication preferences. Patients differ in how much numerical information they want; many prefer ranges, best/typical/worst-case framing, explicit uncertainty, and language that combines preparation with realistic hope.

The strict-source problem is that these are **individual communication preferences**, not evidence for two competing institutional communication policies. The professionally defensible response is itself preference-sensitive: be truthful, elicit how much/detail the patient wants, avoid false precision, and communicate uncertainty in a decision-supportive form. Turning “some patients prefer numbers, others less numerical detail” into two universal clinic rules would flatten the evidence rather than represent it.

**Disposition:** `needs-additional-evidence`. Retain as a major communication/epistemic-uncertainty case, but do not execute it merely because the patient-preference literature is rich.

## M200 — resource-intensive home care versus institutional cost

M200 also has a mature source architecture. Disability rights and the ADA/Olmstead integration mandate establish a strong orientation toward community-based services when appropriate, desired and reasonably accommodated, while the “fundamental alteration” limit explicitly recognizes wider resource constraints.

What is missing is direct affected-community evidence supporting **action-distinct funding thresholds** such as:

1. fund home/community care within a broad cost premium;
2. enforce cost equivalence with institutional care; versus
3. presumptively fund community integration until a system-level fundamental-alteration threshold is crossed.

A general preference for community living or opposition to unnecessary institutionalization does not itself specify which public funding rule should apply at a 35% cost differential.

**Disposition:** `needs-additional-evidence`. High-priority future re-review target; do not convert a rights orientation into a specific cost rule on behalf of disabled people.
