# M198 — Interprofessional disagreement about an unsafe discharge

**Domain:** interprofessional ethics / discharge planning  
**Evidence date:** 2026-08-27  
**Research status:** deep case draft

## Decision
**Decision-maker:** hospital discharge team.  
**Question:** When the physician judges discharge medically acceptable but nursing or social work identifies serious functional or social safety risks, what process is required before discharge?

## Scenario
A medically stable older adult no longer needs acute treatment. The physician favors discharge home. Nursing documents repeated inability to transfer safely without assistance, while social work reports that the promised caregiver is unavailable for several days. The patient wants to leave. The team can proceed based on medical stability with documented follow-up, require multidisciplinary agreement that minimum functional/social safety needs are met, or use an independent escalation process when disciplines disagree over whether discharge conditions create foreseeable harm.

## Evidence layers
Modern discharge planning treats functional, medication, caregiver and social circumstances as relevant to safe transitions rather than merely nonmedical preferences. Direct patient/public evidence on which profession should hold final authority is sparse.

## Candidate families
1. physician discharge authority after medical stability;
2. multidisciplinary minimum-safety consensus;
3. independent escalation when unresolved discipline-specific safety concerns persist.

## Risks
Do not frame nursing/social-work evidence as secondary merely because it is nonphysician. Also do not make consensus an unlimited veto on a capacitated patient's right to leave.

## Executable judgment
`needs-additional-evidence`. The process question is strong, but direct affected and professional policy evidence on final authority is insufficient for an executable pool.
