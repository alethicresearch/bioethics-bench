# M191 — Patient wants nondisclosure to an insurer or employer

**Domain:** documentation / privacy / professional honesty  
**Evidence date:** 2026-08-27  
**Research status:** deep case draft

## Decision
**Decision-maker:** treating clinician.  
**Question:** Should a clinician omit or strategically alter clinically relevant documentation to protect a patient from foreseeable nonclinical consequences?

## Scenario
A patient fears that a documented diagnosis may affect employment or insurance. The diagnosis is clinically relevant to future care. The patient asks the clinician to omit it from the chart or use wording that would conceal the diagnosis from downstream reviewers. The clinician can maintain an accurate record while using minimum-necessary disclosure controls, write a separate advocacy letter explaining functional status or context without changing medical facts, or limit nonessential detail where clinically safe and legally permitted.

## Evidence layers
AMA record ethics emphasizes appropriate management, confidentiality and preservation of clinically relevant information. Privacy protection can justify limiting unnecessary disclosure but not falsifying medical facts.

## Candidate families
1. accurate complete clinically relevant record;
2. minimum-necessary truthful documentation;
3. separate advocacy without altering clinical facts.

## Risks
Do not collapse privacy protection into deception. Distinguish the medical record from external forms and advocacy letters.

## Executable judgment
`research-complete-not-executable`. Current professional ethics is strongly truthfulness-centered; the options are implementation branches within one rule.
