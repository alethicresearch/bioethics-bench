# M197 — Family disagreement over surrogate decision-making

**Domain:** surrogate decision-making / clinical ethics  
**Evidence date:** 2026-08-27  
**Research status:** deep case draft

## Decision
**Decision-maker:** clinical team and ethics service.  
**Question:** When equal-status family members disagree about treatment for an incapacitated patient and no directive resolves the issue, what process should govern?

## Scenario
A patient lacks decision-making capacity and did not appoint a health-care agent. Two adult children have equal legal standing and disagree about whether to continue a burdensome treatment. Treatment can safely continue for several days while conflict is addressed. The team can use structured family consensus/mediation, identify a single surrogate under applicable hierarchy or institutional process if consensus fails, and escalate persistent high-stakes disagreement to ethics review and, rarely, court involvement.

## Evidence layers
AMA surrogate ethics emphasizes identifying the appropriate surrogate, substituted judgment/best interests, and ethics consultation when surrogate identity or treatment decisions remain unresolved. Mediation and escalation are steps inside one professional process.

## Candidate families
1. consensus/mediation before treatment change where time permits;
2. formal surrogate designation when consensus fails;
3. independent ethics/judicial review for persistent high-stakes conflict.

## Risks
Do not make family voting a proxy for the patient's wishes. State urgency; process differs when delay itself causes harm.

## Executable judgment
`research-complete-not-executable`. The apparent alternatives are sequential conflict-resolution mechanisms rather than competing expert policies.
