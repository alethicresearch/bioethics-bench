# M195 — Treating a family member or close colleague

**Domain:** professional boundaries  
**Evidence date:** 2026-08-27  
**Research status:** deep case draft

## Decision
**Decision-maker:** clinician.  
**Question:** When is it ethically acceptable to diagnose, prescribe for or treat an immediate family member?

## Scenario
A physician's adult sibling develops an uncomplicated minor infection while traveling in a remote area where no other clinician is immediately available. The physician can evaluate the sibling adequately, the care is within competence and no controlled substance is involved. The decision is whether to provide short-term treatment and document/communicate it to the sibling's regular clinician, refuse because family treatment should generally be avoided, or provide only emergency/minor temporary care until independent care becomes available.

## Evidence layers
AMA ethics states physicians generally should not treat immediate family because objectivity, privacy and consent can be compromised, but recognizes limited exceptions for emergencies, isolated settings and short-term minor problems, with documentation and communication duties.

## Candidate families
1. avoid family treatment except emergency/isolation;
2. permit short-term minor care with documentation and handoff;
3. broader treatment only where independent care is unavailable and risks are low.

## Risks
Do not generalize from a minor infection to sensitive, chronic or controlled-substance care. Keep the family member free to decline.

## Executable judgment
`research-complete-not-executable`. Current professional guidance already defines the limited exceptions as one conditional rule.
