# M200 — Resource-intensive home care requested by patient and family

**Domain:** disability / long-term care / public resource allocation  
**Evidence date:** 2026-08-27  
**Research status:** deep case draft

## Decision
**Decision-maker:** public long-term-services funding authority.  
**Question:** When a person with severe disability can live safely at home only with substantially more publicly funded support than institutional care, how should the system weigh community-living preference against opportunity cost?

## Scenario
An adult with severe physical disability and intact decision-making capacity strongly prefers to remain in an accessible apartment near family and community. Clinicians agree community living is medically appropriate if 24-hour attendant support is funded. The home package would cost about 35% more than a high-quality institutional placement. Other people also need publicly funded long-term services. The authority must choose whether to honor community living within a broad reasonableness range, apply a cost-equivalence/resource ceiling before approving home services, or give substantial priority to community-living capabilities and integration unless the added cost would fundamentally alter the wider service system.

## Evidence layers
**Affected/right-holders:** disability-rights and community-integration principles treat unnecessary institutional segregation as a significant harm; people receiving services must be offered meaningful choice and information about community options.

**Professional/legal-policy:** the ADA/Olmstead integration mandate requires community-based services when appropriate, desired and reasonably accommodated, while recognizing a resource-sensitive 'fundamental alteration' defense. This is one mature conditional architecture rather than an unlimited right to any requested expenditure.

**Frameworks:** autonomy/capabilities and social inclusion; fair opportunity-cost stewardship; reasonable-accommodation/fundamental-alteration compromise.

## Candidate families
1. honor home/community preference within a broad reasonable cost range;
2. cost-equivalent ceiling before public funding exceeds institutional alternative;
3. strong community-integration priority unless additional cost would fundamentally alter services for others.

## Risks
Do not assume institutional placement and community living are welfare-equivalent because both provide basic care. Do not treat cost as irrelevant to a public system serving many people.

## References
ADA.gov current Community Integration guidance; DOJ Olmstead integration-mandate guidance.

## Executable judgment
`needs-additional-evidence`, but high-priority audit target. The rights/policy architecture is strong; direct affected-community evidence needs to support action-distinct funding rules rather than only a general preference for home/community living.
