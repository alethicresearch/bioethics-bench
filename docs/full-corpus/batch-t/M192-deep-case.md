# M192 — Gifts or industry-sponsored incentives

**Domain:** conflicts of interest / professionalism  
**Evidence date:** 2026-08-27  
**Research status:** deep case draft

## Decision
**Decision-maker:** medical practice or training program.  
**Question:** What relationships with manufacturers should clinicians accept when the company sells products they prescribe or use?

## Scenario
A device company offers clinicians meals and branded gifts tied to product education and separately offers an unrestricted educational grant to the institution. The clinicians use competing products and have discretion over purchasing/prescribing. The practice must choose whether to reject individual gifts/incentives while allowing independently administered institutional educational support, allow only minimal patient-benefiting in-kind items without reciprocity, or prohibit all manufacturer support connected to product education.

## Evidence layers
AMA ethics rejects cash gifts and gifts with expected/implied reciprocity, permits only minimal-value in-kind gifts that directly benefit patients, and allows some institutional trainee/educational support when recipient selection is independent.

## Risks
Do not use obsolete norms about pens/meals as though all current professional bodies treat them alike. Separate individual promotional gifts from independently governed institutional relationships.

`research-complete-not-executable`. Professional guidance is mature and strongly convergent around conflict mitigation.
