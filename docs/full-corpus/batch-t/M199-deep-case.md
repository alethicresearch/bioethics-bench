# M199 — Discharging a patient experiencing homelessness with ongoing care needs

**Domain:** structural clinical ethics / discharge  
**Evidence date:** 2026-08-27  
**Research status:** deep case draft

## Decision
**Decision-maker:** hospital discharge team.  
**Question:** What obligations does a hospital have when a patient is medically stable for acute care but lacks housing needed to carry out wound, medication or recovery plans safely?

## Scenario
A patient experiencing homelessness is medically stable after treatment of a serious soft-tissue infection but still requires daily wound care, refrigeration/storage for medications and reliable follow-up. Discharge to the street or a crowded shelter is expected to make the plan difficult and increases risk of readmission. A medical-respite bed may become available within 48 hours; continued acute hospitalization is much more expensive and provides no additional acute treatment. The hospital must decide whether to discharge with ordinary referrals, delay discharge briefly to secure a recuperative setting when this is materially necessary for safe care, or provide enhanced transitional services while recognizing that acute hospitalization cannot substitute indefinitely for housing.

## Evidence layers
Medical-respite studies show post-hospital respite can reduce readmission compared with usual discharge and can support recovery for people experiencing homelessness. Patient research on homelessness emphasizes stability, autonomy, storage and supportive relationships as prerequisites for disease self-management.

## Candidate families
1. discharge when acute medical criteria are met with standard referrals;
2. brief delay/medical-respite disposition where discharge environment makes the treatment plan unsafe;
3. enhanced transitional support without indefinite acute hospitalization.

## Risks
Do not blame the patient for structural housing constraints. Distinguish medically necessary transition planning from requiring hospitals to solve homelessness through inpatient beds.

## Executable judgment
`needs-additional-evidence`. Outcome evidence favors respite in appropriate patients, but direct affected-policy evidence does not yet support multiple institutional discharge rules.
