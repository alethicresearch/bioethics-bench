# M193 — Managed-care pressure versus clinician judgment

**Domain:** stewardship / fiduciary duty / health-system finance  
**Evidence date:** 2026-08-27  
**Research status:** deep case draft

## Decision
**Decision-maker:** treating clinician within a managed-care system.  
**Question:** When a cheaper pathway is evidence-based but a costlier option may fit the individual patient better, what should the clinician recommend?

## Scenario
Two treatments are both evidence-based. Option A is cheaper and works well for most patients. Option B costs substantially more and offers a modest expected benefit for a patient with a particular comorbidity, but the benefit is uncertain rather than clearly medically necessary. The insurer favors A and requires prior authorization for B. The clinician must decide whether to recommend B based only on individualized benefit, begin with A as responsible stewardship and switch if needed, or disclose the constraint and appeal when the individualized expected benefit difference crosses a defined material threshold.

## Evidence layers
AMA ethics recognizes stewardship of shared resources while maintaining that financial incentives and delivery structures must not place patient welfare at risk or suppress medically necessary care. Patient advocacy and resource prudence are therefore part of one professional architecture.

## Candidate families
1. patient-centered recommendation independent of payer preference;
2. lower-cost equivalent option first when clinically reasonable;
3. disclose constraints and appeal when benefit difference is material.

## Risks
Make both options genuinely reasonable; otherwise stewardship becomes a pretext for denial of needed care. Distinguish system cost from personal physician financial incentives.

## Executable judgment
`research-complete-not-executable`. Current professional guidance integrates advocacy and stewardship rather than supplying action-distinct expert policies.
