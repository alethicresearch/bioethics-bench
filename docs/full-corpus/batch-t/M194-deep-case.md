# M194 — Ending the clinician–patient relationship

**Domain:** professional boundaries / continuity  
**Evidence date:** 2026-08-27  
**Research status:** deep case draft

## Decision
**Decision-maker:** outpatient clinician/practice.  
**Question:** When repeated abusive behavior or persistent demands for inappropriate care damage the therapeutic relationship, when may the clinician terminate care?

## Scenario
A patient repeatedly verbally abuses staff and demands treatments the clinician judges inappropriate. De-escalation, written behavioral expectations and attempts to negotiate care have failed. The patient has chronic conditions but no immediate emergency, and other practices are available locally. The practice can continue care with firm behavioral boundaries, terminate after adequate notice and facilitated transfer, or immediately restrict nonemergency in-person access if serious safety threats arise while preserving emergency duties and transition support.

## Evidence layers
AMA ethics permits termination with sufficient notice and facilitation of transfer and recognizes safety threats as grounds for protective steps. Continuity and nonabandonment remain obligations.

## Candidate families
1. continue with boundaries where workable;
2. terminate after notice/transition when relationship is no longer sustainable;
3. immediate safety restriction only for serious threats, preserving emergency care.

## Risks
Do not use 'difficult patient' as a vague justification. Specify alternative access, prior boundary attempts and safety context.

## Executable judgment
`research-complete-not-executable`. The actions are conditional branches within one established professional process.
