# Bioethics Bench — Batch T source and construction packet (M191–M200)

**Evidence date:** 2026-08-27  
**Scope:** truthful documentation, industry gifts, managed-care pressure, relationship termination, treating family, prognostic uncertainty, surrogate conflict, interprofessional discharge, homelessness discharge, and high-cost home/community care.

## Source architecture

- **M191:** professional record ethics requires accurate clinically relevant documentation and confidentiality/minimum-necessary handling; advocacy can occur without falsifying records.
- **M192:** AMA current ethics rejects cash/reciprocal gifts and permits only limited patient-benefiting minimal-value in-kind support; institutional educational support can be structured independently. This is a relatively settled professional norm.
- **M193:** AMA ethics combines patient advocacy with stewardship: financial incentives must not cause withholding of medically necessary care, but clinicians should use resources prudently where alternatives are clinically reasonable.
- **M194:** AMA permits termination with adequate notice and facilitated transfer; safety threats can justify stronger immediate boundaries while emergency duties remain.
- **M195:** AMA says physicians generally should not treat immediate family, with limited exceptions for emergencies, isolated settings and short-term minor problems, plus documentation/communication duties.
- **M196:** prognosis evidence directly shows heterogeneous patient information preferences but substantial preference for explicit ranges, uncertainty and best/typical/worst-case framing among many patients. Professional communication should remain truthful while tailoring amount/form to patient preference.
- **M197:** AMA surrogate ethics identifies a surrogate and uses substituted judgment/best interests; unresolved disagreement should move to ethics/institutional review before court where feasible. Consensus/mediation and formal surrogate designation are steps within one process.
- **M198:** safe discharge is inherently multidisciplinary because functional, nursing and social risks may be decision-critical. Direct affected-policy evidence on 'who decides' is sparse.
- **M199:** medical respite evidence shows safer post-hospital options can reduce readmission for people experiencing homelessness. A discharge decision should represent actual wound/medication/recovery needs rather than treat housing status as moral failure.
- **M200:** ADA/Olmstead integration mandate strongly favors community-based services when appropriate, desired and reasonably accommodated, while recognizing a 'fundamental alteration' resource limit. This creates a mature rights-plus-resource conditional architecture; direct disability-community evidence should inform any executable policy.

## Key sources

- AMA Code: Management of Medical Records; Gifts to Physicians from Industry; Professionalism in Health Care Systems/Stewardship; Terminating a Patient-Physician Relationship; Treating Self or Family; Decisions for Adult Patients Who Lack Capacity.
- Prognosis communication studies: PMID 30782978, PMID 37232458, PMID 12046799, PMID 31904887, and recent uncertainty/hope studies.
- Homeless medical respite outcome literature including PMID 19363773 and PMID 24212162.
- ADA.gov current Community Integration guidance and Olmstead integration-mandate guidance.

## Working hypothesis

**M196** and **M200** are the strongest audit targets. M196 has direct patient preference heterogeneity and potentially distinct communication policies; M200 has unusually mature affected-rights and public-resource architecture. M191–M195 and M197 are likely professionally convergent; M198/M199 may lack sufficiently direct affected-policy comparison.
