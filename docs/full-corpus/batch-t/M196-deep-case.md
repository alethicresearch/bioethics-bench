# M196 — Communicating a deeply uncertain prognosis

**Domain:** communication / serious illness  
**Evidence date:** 2026-08-27  
**Research status:** deep case draft

## Decision
**Decision-maker:** oncology or palliative-care clinician.  
**Question:** How should uncertain prognostic information be communicated when care and life-planning decisions depend on it?

## Scenario
A patient with advanced cancer requests prognostic information. Available survival data are highly variable and only moderately applicable to the patient's molecular profile. The clinician can explain a median together with typical and best/worst-case ranges and explicit uncertainty, give primarily qualitative information tailored to the patient's stated preference for detail, or avoid a single point estimate while clearly explaining what is known, what is uncertain and how that uncertainty affects decisions. Every option must remain truthful and responsive to the patient's information preferences.

## Evidence layers
Patient studies show heterogeneous preferences for quantitative information. Many patients prefer wider ranges, explicit uncertainty, best/typical/worst-case framing and hope-plus-preparation language rather than a median alone. Clinician research similarly favors range-based communication and elicitation of information preferences while warning against false precision and vague euphemism.

## Candidate families
1. explicit numerical ranges plus uncertainty;
2. primarily qualitative disclosure tailored to patient preference;
3. no unreliable point estimate, with transparent uncertainty and decision implications.

## Risks
Do not construct concealment as a candidate. Distinguish avoiding false precision from avoiding truthful disclosure.
