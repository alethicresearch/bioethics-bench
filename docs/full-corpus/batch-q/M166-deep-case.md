# M166 — Allocating climate-health adaptation resources

**Domain:** population ethics / climate adaptation  
**Evidence date:** 2026-08-27  
**Research status:** deep case draft

## Decision
**Decision-maker:** national health ministry.  
**Question:** How should a fixed climate-health adaptation budget be allocated across interventions with different expected health gains and vulnerability profiles?

## Scenario
The ministry has a fixed five-year adaptation budget insufficient to fund all proposed programs. Option A expands heat-warning and cooling systems in densely populated cities and yields the largest projected aggregate mortality reduction. Option B strengthens vector-borne-disease control in lower-income rural regions with smaller projected aggregate benefit but substantially higher baseline vulnerability. Option C creates a minimum resilience package for every region and spends the remaining funds according to expected health gain. The ministry must choose a standing allocation rule.

## Critical facts
- projected effects are independently modeled with uncertainty ranges;
- vulnerability includes poverty, baseline disease burden and limited adaptive capacity;
- all regions receive ordinary health services; this is incremental adaptation funding;
- no option dominates on both total benefit and equity.

## Evidence layers
WHO climate-resilience guidance emphasizes vulnerability assessment, equity, health-system resilience and context-specific prioritization. Direct public policy evidence comparing these exact national allocation rules is sparse.

## Candidate families
1. maximize expected health gain;
2. prioritize the most climate-vulnerable/worst-off regions;
3. guarantee a geographic minimum floor, then maximize benefit with remaining funds.

## Risks
Do not use projected health gains as certain outcomes; do not equate geographic equality with equity.

## Executable judgment
`needs-additional-evidence`. The normative alternatives are clear but the affected/public source pool is not yet policy-specific enough for execution.
