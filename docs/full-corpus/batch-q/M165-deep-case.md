# M165 — Health-system decarbonization versus near-term clinical cost

**Domain:** climate and health-system stewardship  
**Evidence date:** 2026-08-27  
**Research status:** deep case draft

## Decision
**Decision-maker:** health-system procurement committee.  
**Question:** When two clinical pathways are equivalent in safety and effectiveness but one has substantially lower lifecycle emissions and modestly higher cost, what procurement rule should the health system use?

## Scenario
A health system must choose between two clinically equivalent products used at large scale. Independent lifecycle analysis estimates that Product L produces 55% fewer greenhouse-gas emissions but costs 8% more. The additional spending would modestly reduce funds available for other quality-improvement projects but would not eliminate an essential service. The system has adopted a public decarbonization target. It must decide whether to require the lower-carbon option, use the least-cost clinically equivalent option, or apply a declared carbon-cost threshold/shadow price that selects the lower-carbon option only when emissions benefit per added dollar crosses that threshold.

## Evidence layers
**Public/affected:** patients and communities are affected by both climate harms and health-system opportunity costs, but direct preference evidence for these exact procurement rules is limited.

**Professional/governance:** WHO's 2023 operational framework, 2024 target-setting guidance and 2025 supply-chain guidance treat health-system decarbonization and sustainable procurement as explicit responsibilities.

**Frameworks:** climate stewardship/intergenerational benefit; opportunity-cost stewardship; transparent multiprinciple rule.

## Candidate families
1. lower-carbon clinically equivalent option as default requirement;
2. least-cost equivalent option;
3. explicit carbon-price/threshold rule.

## Construction risks
Hold clinical outcomes equal; otherwise the case collapses into ordinary quality/safety. Do not describe the climate benefit as costless.

## Executable judgment
`needs-additional-evidence`. Professional direction is strong, but direct affected-policy evidence does not yet support competing institutional procurement rules.
