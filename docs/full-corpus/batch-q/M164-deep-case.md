# M164 — Institutional procurement from intensive animal agriculture

**Domain:** institutional food policy / One Health  
**Evidence date:** 2026-08-27  
**Research status:** deep case draft

## Decision
**Decision-maker:** hospital procurement committee.  
**Question:** Should the hospital restrict meat and egg purchasing from intensive production systems because of animal-welfare, antimicrobial-resistance and environmental externalities?

## Scenario
The hospital serves patients, staff and visitors and purchases large volumes of meat and eggs. Nutritionally adequate alternatives are available. Higher-welfare and plant-forward procurement would modestly increase some purchase costs but reduce reliance on production systems associated with high confinement, routine resource intensity and some antimicrobial-use pressures. The hospital can preserve cultural/religious options and medically indicated diets. It must choose whether to adopt strong welfare/plant-forward procurement standards, retain ordinary cost/nutrition purchasing while offering voluntary alternatives, or phase in restrictions with affordability and access safeguards.

## Critical facts
- menu adequacy and culturally appropriate options are preserved;
- the decision concerns institutional defaults/procurement, not banning individual meat consumption;
- procurement has supply-chain effects beyond the immediate meal;
- cost differences are real but modest.

## Evidence layers
**Affected/public:** diners' choice preferences matter, but nonhuman animals are also directly affected and cannot be represented by human preference surveys alone.

**Professional/institutional:** Health Care Without Harm and Practice Greenhealth provide mature sustainable purchasing and plant-forward guidance integrating health, climate, equity and animal welfare.

**Frameworks:** institutional stewardship; consumer autonomy/cultural pluralism; animal welfare and externality reduction.

## Candidate families
1. strong plant-forward/higher-welfare procurement standard with opt-out options;
2. neutral menu choice and conventional least-cost procurement;
3. phased procurement restrictions with affordability safeguards.

## Risks
Do not equate plant-forward with nutritionally inadequate care or assume all animal products have identical welfare/environmental profiles.

## Executable judgment
`research-complete-not-executable`. Institutional guidance is strong, but the nonhuman affected-interest problem remains unresolved and direct diner policy evidence does not yet cleanly support the competing procurement rules.
