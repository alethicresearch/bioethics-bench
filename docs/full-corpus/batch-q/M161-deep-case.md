# M161 — Culling versus vaccination during an animal-borne outbreak

**Domain:** One Health / veterinary public health  
**Evidence date:** 2026-08-27  
**Research status:** deep case draft

## Decision
**Decision-maker:** national veterinary/public-health authority.  
**Question:** During a severe HPAI outbreak affecting commercial poultry, what mix of culling and vaccination should the authority use to control transmission while limiting animal-welfare, livelihood and public-health harms?

## Concise representation
A country is experiencing recurrent H5N1 outbreaks in poultry with documented farm-to-farm spread and occasional mammalian spillover. Rapid depopulation of infected flocks can suppress transmission but kills very large numbers of birds and imposes major losses on producers. A matched poultry vaccine is available; it can reduce disease and viral circulation but requires surveillance, biosecurity, monitoring of vaccine performance and an exit strategy, and does not eliminate the need to depopulate infected flocks. The authority must choose whether to rely primarily on rapid culling of infected/exposed flocks, add risk-targeted vaccination plus surveillance to reduce the scale of future culling, or use targeted depopulation only for infected/high-risk groups while emphasizing vaccination, movement controls and compensation.

## Detailed representation
For this benchmark, assume the outbreak is serious enough that inaction would cause sustained poultry transmission and increase animal, economic and human-exposure risks. The authority can finance surveillance, compensation and vaccination. Vaccination is effective enough to reduce clinical disease and transmission but is not sterilizing. WOAH guidance treats vaccination as a possible component of a broader control strategy, not a stand-alone substitute for biosecurity, surveillance and disease-control measures. The ethical dispute therefore concerns how aggressively to use mass depopulation when a viable vaccination strategy can reduce future infections but carries implementation uncertainty and trade costs.

## Critical facts and uncertainty
- HPAI control is a One Health problem involving animal welfare, livelihoods, wildlife spillover and human exposure.
- Vaccination is feasible but must be paired with surveillance and other controls.
- Some culling of infected flocks remains necessary under all serious strategies.
- The scale of preventive depopulation is the policy variable.

## Evidence layers
**Affected/public:** farmers and communities bear livelihood and food-system consequences; direct comparative affected-policy evidence remains fragmented. Birds themselves are directly affected, invoking the unresolved nonhuman-interest representation problem.

**Professional:** WOAH 2024–2026 guidance supports context-sensitive integrated strategies; vaccination should be considered where scientifically justified, while culling, surveillance, biosecurity and movement controls remain core measures.

**Frameworks:** precaution/public-health control; animal-welfare proportionality; livelihood/equity and least-harm disease control.

## Candidate orientation families
1. culling-dominant rapid suppression;
2. integrated vaccination plus targeted depopulation;
3. minimum-necessary culling with compensation and vaccination emphasis.

## Risks
Do not portray vaccination as a complete replacement for outbreak control or mass culling as the only professionally supported approach. Do not treat producer preference as equivalent to animal welfare.
