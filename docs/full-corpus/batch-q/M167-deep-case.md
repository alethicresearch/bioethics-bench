# M167 — Heatwave triage for scarce cooling resources

**Domain:** climate emergency ethics  
**Evidence date:** 2026-08-27  
**Research status:** deep case draft

## Decision
**Decision-maker:** city public-health emergency authority.  
**Question:** During an extreme heat emergency, who should receive priority for scarce cooled shelter and transport?

## Scenario
A prolonged heatwave overwhelms municipal cooling capacity. The city can provide cooled transport and shelter to only 60% of people requesting assistance during the peak 48 hours. Some residents have severe cardiopulmonary disease or take medications increasing heat risk; others have fewer medical vulnerabilities but no air-conditioning, unstable housing, or outdoor work exposure. The city must choose whether to prioritize predicted medical mortality risk, use a vulnerability rule incorporating both clinical and social lack-of-alternative factors, or create risk tiers and use lottery/first-available placement within each tier.

## Evidence layers
Professional heat-health guidance consistently identifies older adults, people with chronic illness, socially isolated people, people without cooling access and outdoor workers as high-risk groups. Direct public comparative preference evidence among these allocation algorithms is limited.

## Candidate families
1. highest clinical risk first;
2. combined medical/social vulnerability priority;
3. broad risk tiers with equal chance within tier.

## Risks
Do not make social vulnerability a vague proxy; specify lack of alternatives and exposure. Do not assume first-come access is neutral when transport and information are unequal.

## Executable judgment
`needs-additional-evidence`. Strong professional vulnerability guidance, insufficient direct affected-policy pluralism.
