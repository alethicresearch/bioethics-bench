# M170 — Release of genetically modified mosquitoes

**Domain:** vector-borne disease / emerging public-health technology  
**Evidence date:** 2026-08-27  
**Research status:** deep case draft

## Decision
**Decision-maker:** national vector-control authority.  
**Question:** Under what governance conditions should the authority authorize a field release of genetically modified mosquitoes to reduce malaria transmission?

## Scenario
A mosquito-control program has completed laboratory and contained studies of a genetically modified mosquito intended to reduce malaria transmission. For this benchmark, assume the intervention has a plausible efficacy signal and no known major ecological toxicity, but environmental effects and public trust cannot be reduced to zero uncertainty. The proposed release would occur in several communities with high malaria burden. Local engagement identifies substantial support but also concerns about reversibility, institutional trust and who controls the program. The authority must decide whether to proceed with a staged field release plus intensive monitoring/community governance, limit work to contained/semi-field studies until additional ecological evidence is available, or proceed once health/environment thresholds are met even without unanimous local support.

## Evidence layers
**Affected/public:** recent Northern Uganda research found broad support for gene-drive mosquitoes but acceptance depended on attitudes, social norms and trust; California engagement likewise found pragmatic concerns about control, targeting and institutional cooperation rather than blanket rejection.

**Professional/governance:** WHO/TDR guidance requires staged testing, risk-benefit assessment, regulatory review, social/ethical analysis and meaningful community engagement. It does not require unanimity.

**Frameworks:** precaution/reversibility; public-health benefit under legitimate governance; community authority/social license.

## Candidate families
1. staged field release with monitoring and community governance;
2. contained/semi-field research only pending stronger evidence;
3. authorize after predefined safety/efficacy thresholds despite residual local dissent.

## Risks
Do not treat community engagement as a public-relations exercise or imply support equals informed collective authorization. Do not conflate self-limiting modified mosquitoes with all gene-drive systems.

## References
WHO/TDR GMM Guidance Framework, 2nd ed.; WHO position on GMMs; PMID 41114908; PMID 35360388.

## Executable judgment
`needs-additional-evidence`. Current community studies support conditional acceptance and concern but do not yet cleanly establish two action-distinct field-release policies under the strict source-to-policy standard.
