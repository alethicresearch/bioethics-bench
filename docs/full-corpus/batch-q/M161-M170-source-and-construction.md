# Bioethics Bench — Batch Q source and construction packet (M161–M170)

**Evidence date:** 2026-08-27  
**Scope:** animal-borne outbreak control, invasive/wild-animal intervention, institutional food procurement, climate-health decarbonization/adaptation, heat/displacement, solar geoengineering research, and genetically modified mosquitoes.

## Source architecture

- **M161 — zoonotic outbreak culling/vaccination:** WOAH's current HPAI guidance treats culling, movement controls, surveillance, biosecurity and—where risk analysis supports it—vaccination as components of one context-sensitive control strategy. WOAH 2026 explicitly argues vaccination should no longer be excluded as a complementary tool. The policy question must therefore compare genuinely different outbreak strategies rather than split one integrated WOAH rule.
- **M162 — invasive/feral-animal control:** conservation governance supports lethal and nonlethal population-control tools depending on ecological effectiveness and welfare. Public attitudes are species/context dependent. The nonhuman affected-interest problem identified in M156–M159 remains active.
- **M163 — wild-animal disease intervention:** wildlife vaccination programs such as oral rabies vaccination show intervention can be operationally legitimate for public/conservation purposes; evidence directly supporting intervention purely to reduce wild-animal suffering is much thinner.
- **M164 — institutional animal-product procurement:** Health Care Without Harm/Practice Greenhealth support procurement standards that integrate health, environmental, workforce and animal-welfare considerations. Direct patient/student policy preferences are less mature than the institutional guidance.
- **M165 — health-system decarbonization:** WHO's 2023 operational framework, 2024 target-setting guidance and 2025 supply-chain guidance explicitly treat low-carbon health systems and sustainable procurement as health-system responsibilities. The case should hold clinical outcomes equivalent to isolate cost versus climate responsibility.
- **M166 — climate-health adaptation allocation:** WHO guidance prioritizes vulnerability assessment, resilience and health equity but does not prescribe one universal allocation formula across expected health gain, worst-off priority and geographic floors.
- **M167 — heatwave cooling-resource triage:** professional guidance prioritizes medically/socially vulnerable populations, but direct public preference evidence among competing cooling-allocation rules is limited.
- **M168 — climate displacement/continuity:** WHO 2025–2026 guidance strongly favors migrant-inclusive health systems and continuity/equitable access. Do not manufacture a professional 'host first' alternative from political controversy.
- **M169 — solar geoengineering field research:** National Academies recommends a governed research program, permitting, impact assessment and stringent limits for outdoor substance-release experiments; nationally representative cross-country research shows public risk/benefit and governance preferences remain heterogeneous. This is a strong audit target.
- **M170 — genetically modified mosquitoes:** WHO's GMM framework requires staged risk/benefit assessment, regulation, ethics and meaningful community engagement. Recent Northern Uganda and California studies show support can be substantial but conditional on trust, control and local process. Potential audit target, though direct 'contained trials only' versus 'field deployment' preferences must not be invented.

## Working hypothesis

Audit M169 and M170 most closely. M161 likely collapses into one integrated professional strategy; M162–M164 remain constrained by the unresolved nonhuman affected-interest architecture; M165–M168 have strong governance but may lack direct affected-policy pluralism.
