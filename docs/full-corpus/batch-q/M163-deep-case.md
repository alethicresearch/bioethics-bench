# M163 — Intervening in wild-animal disease

**Domain:** wildlife health / animal ethics  
**Evidence date:** 2026-08-27  
**Research status:** deep case draft

## Decision
**Decision-maker:** wildlife agency.  
**Question:** Should the agency vaccinate a free-living wildlife population against a naturally occurring disease when vaccination reduces suffering but is costly and alters ecological dynamics?

## Concise representation
A free-living carnivore population experiences recurrent rabies outbreaks causing severe neurologic disease and death. An oral vaccination campaign could substantially reduce infection and spillover but would require repeated bait distribution and ecological monitoring. The disease is naturally circulating rather than introduced by a recent human action. The agency must decide whether to vaccinate primarily to reduce wildlife suffering, vaccinate only where conservation or human/animal public-health goals also justify intervention, or leave the natural disease process largely unmanaged except for targeted spillover control.

## Detailed representation
For this benchmark, assume oral vaccination is technically effective and does not create a major direct toxicity risk. The main uncertainty concerns ecological effects, recurring costs and the scope of public responsibility for naturally occurring wild-animal suffering. Real programs such as wildlife oral rabies vaccination demonstrate that large-scale intervention is operationally possible, especially for public-health and conservation purposes; they do not establish a professional norm to intervene whenever a natural disease causes animal suffering.

## Evidence layers
**Affected/public:** nonhuman animals directly bear disease and intervention effects. Human attitudes can inform legitimacy but not substitute for animal interests.

**Professional:** wildlife-health programs support intervention for rabies control and spillover reduction; no mature consensus exists on a general welfare-based duty to treat natural wild-animal disease.

**Frameworks:** welfare-reduction; conservation/public-health mandate; natural-process/nonintervention.

## Candidate families
1. intervene to reduce severe wild-animal disease and suffering;
2. intervene only when public-health/conservation benefits independently justify it;
3. nonintervention absent anthropogenic cause, apart from spillover control.

## Risks
Do not infer that 'natural' means harmless or that welfare improvement necessarily overrides ecosystem effects.

USDA APHIS wildlife oral rabies vaccination program; wildlife disease ethics literature.
