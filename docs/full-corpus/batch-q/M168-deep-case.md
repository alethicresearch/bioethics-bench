# M168 — Climate displacement and continuity of health care

**Domain:** migration / climate-health equity  
**Evidence date:** 2026-08-27  
**Research status:** deep case draft

## Decision
**Decision-maker:** regional health authority receiving a large climate-displaced population.  
**Question:** How should newly added health-system capacity be allocated when displaced people and host communities both face strained services?

## Scenario
After repeated flooding, 40,000 displaced residents arrive in a neighboring region. The receiving health system obtains a temporary funding increment but cannot immediately eliminate all waiting times. Displaced patients disproportionately need medication continuity, maternal care and chronic-disease follow-up; host communities already have long primary-care waits. The authority must decide whether to allocate added capacity by equal clinical need regardless of displacement status, reserve some capacity for continuity needs created by displacement while protecting a host-community service floor, or prioritize reducing existing host-system backlogs before expanding newcomer-specific services.

## Evidence layers
WHO's 2025–2026 climate-migration work and current refugee/migrant health guidance emphasize inclusive, equitable, people-centered systems, continuity of care and meaningful engagement of affected communities. They do not support a status-based 'host first' exclusion rule.

## Candidate families
1. status-neutral equal clinical need;
2. vulnerability/continuity rule with protected host floor;
3. host-backlog-first allocation.

## Risks
Do not frame displaced people as a disease threat or resource burden. Distinguish an incremental allocation question from denying ordinary care.

## Executable judgment
`research-complete-not-executable`. Professional guidance strongly favors inclusion and continuity; direct affected-policy evidence does not support a valid competing expert/public pool.
