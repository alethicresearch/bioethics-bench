# M162 — Control of invasive or feral animals

**Domain:** conservation ethics / animal welfare  
**Evidence date:** 2026-08-27  
**Research status:** deep case draft

## Decision
**Decision-maker:** regional conservation authority.  
**Question:** When an introduced mammal population is causing measurable loss of endemic wildlife, what population-control policy should the authority adopt?

## Concise representation
An introduced feral mammal population is reproducing rapidly on an island and predating endemic ground-nesting birds. Ecologists project substantial additional losses if the population is not reduced. A lethal control program can reduce the population quickly but causes direct animal deaths; fertility control and exclusion are less harmful to individual animals but would reduce the population more slowly and may not protect the most vulnerable bird colonies in time. The authority must choose among rapid lethal control with humane methods, a primarily nonlethal fertility/exclusion program, or targeted lethal control only in the highest-risk areas while expanding nonlethal measures elsewhere.

## Detailed representation
For this benchmark, assume the ecological harm is well documented and attributable to the introduced population rather than uncertain correlation. The authority has access to humane-method standards, fertility-control technology and monitoring. Complete eradication is not assumed necessary; the decision is about the acceptable balance between welfare harms to individual introduced animals and foreseeable harms to endemic animals and ecosystems. Public attitudes vary strongly by species, perceived invasiveness and whether nonlethal alternatives are credible.

## Critical facts and uncertainty
- ecological damage is substantial and independently documented;
- lethal control would be effective faster than nonlethal control;
- fertility control is feasible but slower and imperfect;
- both introduced and endemic animals have welfare interests;
- long-term ecosystem response remains uncertain.

## Evidence layers
**Affected/public:** human public opinion can describe democratic legitimacy but cannot stand in for either introduced or endemic animals' interests.

**Professional:** conservation and animal-welfare guidance typically favors effectiveness, proportionality, humane methods and monitoring rather than a categorical lethal/nonlethal rule.

**Frameworks:** individual-animal welfare; ecological integrity/species protection; least-harm proportionality.

## Candidate orientation families
1. rapid humane lethal control;
2. nonlethal fertility/exclusion first;
3. targeted lethal control plus broader nonlethal management.

## Risks
Do not assume an animal is morally less important because it is labeled invasive. Do not assume nonlethal methods are automatically effective or lethal methods automatically ecologically sufficient.

## Executable judgment
`research-complete-not-executable`. The case is important, but the Bench has not yet adopted a principled affected-interest source rule for nonhuman animals.
