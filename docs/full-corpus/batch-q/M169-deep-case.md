# M169 — Field testing solar geoengineering with health consequences

**Domain:** planetary health / research governance  
**Evidence date:** 2026-08-27  
**Research status:** deep case draft

## Decision
**Decision-maker:** national research regulator participating in an international consortium.  
**Question:** Should the regulator authorize a small outdoor solar-radiation-modification experiment with possible transboundary implications?

## Scenario
Researchers propose a tightly bounded outdoor experiment releasing a quantity of material far below levels expected to measurably alter global temperature. Modeling and laboratory work cannot answer a key atmospheric-chemistry question. The study would be publicly registered, independently reviewed, environmentally monitored and reversible in the ordinary sense that the release is brief and non-deployment scale. Neighboring countries and affected communities have been consulted but not all groups support proceeding. The regulator must choose whether to authorize under stringent national/international governance, defer outdoor release until a stronger international authorization mechanism exists, or permit only after affected-region consent reaches a specified collective threshold.

## Critical facts
- this is research, not climate deployment;
- the release is deliberately small relative to any climate-altering scale;
- a scientifically important question cannot be answered by modeling/lab work alone;
- transboundary governance and public legitimacy remain unresolved.

## Evidence layers
**Public:** nationally representative research in Mexico, the UK and US shows heterogeneous risk/benefit perceptions and policy preferences; 22-country engagement research shows strong concern for meaningful, context-sensitive public participation and legitimacy.

**Professional/governance:** National Academies recommends robust governance, permitting, impact assessment, public registry, stakeholder engagement and case-by-case outdoor experiments only when observations cannot otherwise be obtained.

**Frameworks:** precaution/moratorium; responsible research under governance; affected-community/transboundary consent.

## Candidate families
1. authorize a bounded outdoor experiment under robust governance;
2. defer substance-release experiments pending stronger international governance;
3. authorize only after a stronger affected-region consent/legitimacy threshold.

## Risks
Do not conflate field research with deployment. Do not invent a globally agreed consent rule.

## Executable judgment
Provisional `full-corpus-2x1x2-mean-v1`, subject to candidate audit. Public evidence plausibly supports proceed-with-governance versus defer/stronger-legitimacy orientations; professional guidance provides one bounded-governance architecture.
