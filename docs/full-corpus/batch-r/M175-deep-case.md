# M175 — Institutional food policy: health, climate and animal welfare

**Domain:** institutional food ethics / climate / animal welfare  
**Evidence date:** 2026-08-27  
**Research status:** deep case draft

## Decision
**Decision-maker:** public university or hospital food-service authority.  
**Question:** Should the institution make nutritionally adequate plant-based meals the default while retaining easy opt-out access to meat-containing meals?

## Scenario
The institution serves thousands of meals each day. A plant-forward default menu would reduce procurement emissions and reliance on intensive animal agriculture, is nutritionally adequate and can retain culturally/religiously appropriate alternatives. Meat-containing meals remain available by simple request. Randomized studies at university events show defaults substantially change meal selection and environmental impact. The authority must choose a plant-based default with opt-out, neutral parallel choice with price parity, or procurement standards focused on welfare/emissions without changing meal defaults.

## Evidence layers
Institutional sustainable-food guidance supports plant-forward procurement and menu design. Randomized default studies show behavioral effect, not endorsement of the policy. Nonhuman animal interests also cannot be reduced to diner preferences.

## Candidate families
1. plant-forward default with easy opt-out;
2. neutral choice architecture;
3. sustainability/welfare procurement standards without default change.

## Risks
Do not infer policy legitimacy from default effectiveness. Preserve nutritional adequacy and cultural access. Do not treat nonhuman welfare as identical to environmental impact.

## References
Health Care Without Harm / Practice Greenhealth; plant-based default RCTs, PMID 38908405.

## Executable judgment
`research-complete-not-executable`. The behavioral evidence is not policy-preference evidence, and the nonhuman affected-interest issue persists.
