# M172 — One high-cost rescue treatment versus population primary care

**Domain:** health resource allocation  
**Evidence date:** 2026-08-27  
**Research status:** deep case draft

## Decision
**Decision-maker:** public health-system funding committee.  
**Question:** Should a fixed exceptional-treatment budget fund one identifiable patient's very expensive therapy or a package of essential primary-care services benefiting many patients?

## Scenario
A patient with a severe, otherwise progressive disease is eligible for a treatment expected to add several good-quality life-years but costs the same as expanding hypertension, diabetes and vaccination services to thousands of patients. Neither option is trivial: the rescue treatment has meaningful expected benefit, while the primary-care package is projected to avert more total morbidity and premature mortality. The committee must decide whether to prioritize severe identifiable need, maximize aggregate health benefit, or reserve a capped exceptional-care fund while using the remainder for population services.

## Evidence layers
Public priority-setting studies show that total health gain matters but is modified by age, baseline health, severity and other distributional concerns. These studies do not establish a general public rule that identifiable patients should override larger population gains.

## Candidate families
1. rescue/severity priority;
2. maximize expected total health;
3. protected exceptional-care allocation plus population-health budget.

## Risks
Avoid numbers that make one option overwhelmingly dominant. Distinguish severity priority from mere identifiability and do not imply primary care benefits are speculative.

## Executable judgment
`needs-additional-evidence`. Normative options are mature; direct affected/public evidence for this specific rescue-versus-primary-care policy rule remains insufficient.
