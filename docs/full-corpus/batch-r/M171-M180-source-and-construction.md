# Bioethics Bench — Batch R source and construction packet (M171–M180)

**Evidence date:** 2026-08-27  
**Scope:** global health spending, rescue versus population benefit, research-community benefit sharing, climate-adaptation finance, institutional food defaults, low-value testing, medically inappropriate treatment, unsupported certification, complementary therapy, and refusal of effective treatment.

## Source architecture

- **M171:** global-health assistance versus domestic marginal health spending is normatively canonical, but direct public evidence generally measures support for aid rather than willingness to trade a specified domestic health benefit for larger foreign gains. Treat as research-complete unless direct policy-choice evidence is found.
- **M172:** contemporary public priority-setting studies show people weight total health gain, severity, age and baseline health in complex ways. A general 'rule of rescue' should not be inferred from salience of an identifiable patient.
- **M173:** international research ethics strongly recognizes community engagement, responsiveness and fair benefit distribution. LMIC stakeholder reviews and recent post-trial-access studies support community benefits, but do not cleanly divide between guaranteed product access and broader health-system benefits.
- **M174:** Green Climate Fund uses a multiprinciple investment framework—impact, paradigm shift, sustainable development, recipient need, country ownership, efficiency/effectiveness—and reserves a substantial adaptation floor for particularly vulnerable countries. The real architecture is already multi-criteria rather than one pure justice rule.
- **M175:** institutional sustainable-food guidance supports plant-forward procurement. Randomized university-event studies show strong default effects on actual meal choice, but behavior under a default is not evidence that participants endorse the default as policy.
- **M176:** Choosing Wisely/low-value-care literature supports avoiding tests with low expected benefit and foreseeable downstream harms; patient requests often arise from reassurance, expectations and uncertainty. Direct evidence on order-versus-decline institutional rules is limited.
- **M177:** AMA ethics explicitly states physicians need not provide scientifically invalid or medically ineffective interventions and should negotiate medically appropriate plans rather than equate autonomy with entitlement.
- **M178:** certification/documentation must remain truthful; clinicians may document actual limitations and advocate within genuine ambiguity but should not make false medical claims.
- **M179:** NCCIH encourages disclosure and coordination of complementary approaches and distinguishes complementary use from substituting unproven treatment for effective care.
- **M180:** capacitated patients may refuse proven treatment, but clinicians need not provide an unproven alternative. Ongoing relationship/harm reduction is distinct from endorsing the requested therapy.
