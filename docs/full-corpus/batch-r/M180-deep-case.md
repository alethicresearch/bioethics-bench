# M180 — Alternative therapy replacing effective treatment

**Domain:** informed refusal / professional integrity  
**Evidence date:** 2026-08-27  
**Research status:** deep case draft

## Decision
**Decision-maker:** treating clinician.  
**Question:** When a capacitated patient wants to stop proven treatment in favor of an unproven alternative, what should the clinician do?

## Scenario
A patient with a highly treatable cancer wants to stop recommended therapy and pursue an alternative regimen lacking credible evidence. The patient understands that stopping standard treatment substantially reduces the chance of cure and does not ask the oncologist to administer the alternative regimen. The clinician must decide how to respect refusal while maintaining professional integrity and continuity: accept the informed refusal and remain available for monitoring/support, intensify counseling and harm-reduction follow-up while preserving the relationship, or transfer non-emergency care if the patient demands a treatment plan the clinician cannot ethically endorse while ensuring no abandonment.

## Evidence layers
NCCIH advises against substituting unproven approaches for effective conventional treatment. General consent ethics recognizes a capacitated patient's right to refuse treatment. Professional integrity does not require a clinician to provide an unproven intervention.

## Candidate families
1. respect refusal and continue relationship/support;
2. continued relationship with structured harm reduction and repeated informed discussion;
3. transfer when demands make a professionally acceptable treatment relationship impossible, preserving emergency/transition duties.

## Risks
Do not conflate refusing standard treatment with loss of capacity. Separate the patient's right to refuse from any supposed right to clinician provision of the alternative.

## Executable judgment
`research-complete-not-executable`. The professional architecture is conditional but convergent; there is no serious expert policy requiring provision of the unproven substitute.
