# M178 — Request for unsupported medical exemption or certification

**Domain:** professional honesty / advocacy  
**Evidence date:** 2026-08-27  
**Research status:** deep case draft

## Decision
**Decision-maker:** primary-care clinician.  
**Question:** Should a clinician sign a work or school exemption that the patient strongly wants when the medical criteria are not met?

## Scenario
A patient asks the clinician to certify that a chronic condition medically prevents participation in a workplace requirement. The patient reports genuine discomfort and worries that compliance will worsen symptoms, but examination and current criteria do not support the requested categorical exemption. The clinician could decline inaccurate certification while documenting symptoms and limitations, provide a narrower accommodation letter that truthfully describes functional restrictions without claiming an unsupported diagnosis/exemption, or sign the broader exemption only if the clinical criteria genuinely remain ambiguous after further assessment.

## Evidence layers
Professional documentation duties require accuracy and truthfulness. Advocacy may include explaining limitations and requesting reasonable accommodations, but does not justify false clinical statements.

## Candidate families
1. decline unsupported certification;
2. provide minimum-necessary truthful functional documentation/advocacy;
3. broader exemption only where evidence truly leaves a reasonable professional range.

## Risks
Do not turn social hardship into malingering. Separate advocacy from deception and keep the criteria sufficiently defined that the case is not simply legal paperwork.

## Executable judgment
`research-complete-not-executable`. Professional ethics converges on truthful documentation; the remaining dispute concerns application in genuine ambiguity, not competing source-grounded policies.
