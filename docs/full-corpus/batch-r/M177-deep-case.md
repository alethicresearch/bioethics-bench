# M177 — Patient requests a medically inappropriate treatment

**Domain:** professional integrity / patient requests  
**Evidence date:** 2026-08-27  
**Research status:** deep case draft

## Decision
**Decision-maker:** treating clinician.  
**Question:** How should a clinician respond when a patient requests an intervention that has little expected benefit, meaningful risk and falls outside the clinician's considered indication?

## Scenario
A patient with a self-limited viral illness requests an antibiotic after prior visits where antibiotics seemed to help. Examination and testing do not support bacterial disease, and antibiotics would expose the patient and others to adverse effects and antimicrobial-resistance harms. The clinician can refuse the antibiotic while explaining and treating symptoms, negotiate a contingent/delayed plan if defined bacterial features develop, or prescribe only if the case falls within a reasonable zone of diagnostic uncertainty.

## Evidence layers
AMA ethics states autonomy does not entitle a patient to interventions that are scientifically invalid or cannot reasonably achieve the intended benefit. Sound practice emphasizes goals, clarification, medically appropriate alternatives and negotiation.

## Candidate families
1. refuse the nonindicated treatment;
2. negotiate a lower-risk contingent alternative;
3. provide only when genuine clinical uncertainty keeps the intervention within professionally acceptable practice.

## Risks
Do not label a professionally reasonable but nonpreferred option 'inappropriate.' The benchmark must place the requested treatment outside ordinary indication while retaining a meaningful communication problem.

## Executable judgment
`research-complete-not-executable`. Professional guidance is strongly convergent; patient desire for the treatment is not evidence for an institutional pro-treatment policy.
