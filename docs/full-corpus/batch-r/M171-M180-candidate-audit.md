## Why the canonical global-justice cases do not automatically execute

M171 and M172 are philosophically central allocation cases. Their source problem is not lack of normative theory; it is lack of direct affected/public evidence supporting the represented institutional rules. General support for foreign aid does not itself support allocating a specified marginal health dollar abroad rather than domestically. Likewise, concern for an identifiable severely ill patient does not establish a public rule that one rescue treatment should outrank a larger primary-care benefit.

M173 has strong international ethics support for community engagement and fair benefit sharing, and host-community stakeholders consistently value meaningful benefits. The evidence does not cleanly divide into guaranteed product access versus broader health-system benefit packages as competing community policies.

M174 is not usefully split into single-principle professional candidates because current climate-finance governance is already explicitly multiprinciple. M175's randomized plant-based-default evidence establishes behavioral response to a default, not participant endorsement of the default as institutional policy; the nonhuman affected-interest issue also persists.

## Everyday practice cases

- **M176:** low-value-care guidance supports avoiding routine imaging without indication. Patient reassurance motives do not establish a policy to order low-value testing.
- **M177:** professional ethics does not equate patient autonomy with entitlement to medically ineffective/inappropriate treatment.
- **M178:** truthful certification/documentation is a convergent professional requirement; advocacy can occur without false claims.
- **M179:** widespread complementary-therapy use and patient interest do not establish competing clinician policies. Current guidance favors open communication, safety review and non-substitution for effective treatment.
- **M180:** informed refusal and professional nonabandonment form one conditional architecture; the patient may refuse proven care without creating a clinician duty to endorse the unproven substitute.
