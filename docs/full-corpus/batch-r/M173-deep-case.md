# M173 — Benefit sharing after externally sponsored research

**Domain:** global research ethics  
**Evidence date:** 2026-08-27  
**Research status:** deep case draft

## Decision
**Decision-maker:** multinational trial sponsor and host-country ethics authority.  
**Question:** What community-level benefits should be guaranteed after a successful trial conducted in a low-resource community that otherwise cannot afford the resulting intervention?

## Scenario
A sponsor conducts a multi-year trial in a region with limited health infrastructure. The intervention proves effective and is expected to be marketed internationally at a price beyond the host health system's near-term budget. Participants have a separate post-trial-access plan. The unresolved question concerns the broader host community. The sponsor can guarantee a time-limited supply of the successful product, fund broader health-system capacity chosen with the community, or rely on fair participation, national registration and a general global-access strategy without a special host-community entitlement.

## Evidence layers
International research ethics supports responsiveness, community engagement and fair distribution of benefits. LMIC stakeholder research repeatedly identifies healthcare, capacity building and collateral community benefit as important. Recent Ethiopian stakeholders similarly endorsed benefits for participants and communities but varied on feasible mechanisms. These sources do not cleanly divide affected stakeholders into product-only versus system-benefit policies.

## Candidate families
1. guaranteed product access for the host community;
2. negotiated broader community/health-system benefit package;
3. no distinct community entitlement beyond fair research conditions and global access planning.

## Risks
Keep participant post-trial access separate from community benefit sharing. Do not present one sponsor-funded clinic as equivalent to structural access.

## Executable judgment
`needs-additional-evidence`. Strong benefit-sharing orientation, but the affected evidence does not support two action-distinct community policies at required granularity.
