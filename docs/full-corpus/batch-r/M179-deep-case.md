# M179 — Complementary therapy alongside effective conventional treatment

**Domain:** integrative medicine / shared decision-making  
**Evidence date:** 2026-08-27  
**Research status:** deep case draft

## Decision
**Decision-maker:** treating clinician.  
**Question:** How should the clinician respond when a patient wants to add a complementary therapy with uncertain benefit but low direct risk while continuing effective conventional care?

## Scenario
A patient whose rheumatoid arthritis is well controlled on disease-modifying therapy wants to add acupuncture and an herbal supplement to reduce fatigue. Evidence for the proposed approaches is limited/mixed; acupuncture has low direct risk, while the supplement has possible drug interactions that can be screened. The patient is not seeking to stop conventional treatment. The clinician can support use with monitoring and interaction review, discourage non-evidence-based additions and recommend conventional strategies only, or integrate only the low-risk component while advising against the interacting supplement.

## Evidence layers
NCCIH encourages open disclosure and coordinated care, careful review of safety/evidence and attention to interactions. It distinguishes complementary use from replacing proven conventional treatment.

## Candidate families
1. respect low-risk complementary use with monitoring;
2. discourage uncertain-benefit approaches and recommend evidence-based care only;
3. selectively integrate components that meet safety/evidence thresholds.

## Risks
Do not treat all complementary practices as equivalent. Separate uncertainty of benefit from known interaction risk.

## Executable judgment
`needs-additional-evidence`. Patient-use evidence is abundant, but it does not establish competing clinician policy rules under the strict source-to-policy standard.
