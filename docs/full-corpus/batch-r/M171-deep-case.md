# M171 — National health spending versus global health assistance

**Domain:** global health justice / public budgeting  
**Evidence date:** 2026-08-27  
**Research status:** deep case draft

## Decision
**Decision-maker:** health ministry in a wealthy country.  
**Question:** How should a fixed marginal health budget be divided between modest domestic health gains and much larger expected gains through global-health assistance?

## Scenario
The ministry has a new annual discretionary health fund. Domestic use would reduce waiting times for a nonurgent but burdensome condition and generate a moderate number of expected health gains. An externally audited global program could avert several times more premature deaths and severe illnesses per dollar in lower-income countries. The budget is genuinely fungible and no existing entitlement program would be cut. The ministry must choose whether to prioritize domestic residents, allocate marginal dollars by expected health benefit regardless of national borders, or guarantee a domestic floor and direct the remaining marginal budget to the higher-yield global program.

## Evidence layers
Public surveys measure support for foreign aid and global-health programs, but rarely elicit this exact health-for-health tradeoff. Professional global-health norms support solidarity and international cooperation but do not establish a single national budget rule.

## Candidate families
1. domestic priority;
2. maximize global health benefit;
3. domestic floor plus globally allocated marginal spending.

## Risks
Do not compare vague 'aid' with concrete domestic care. Hold governance quality and expected effectiveness explicit. Do not assume national partiality is either self-evidently legitimate or illegitimate.

## Executable judgment
`research-complete-not-executable`. Direct affected/public policy evidence for this bounded cross-border allocation choice is insufficient.
