## Batch-level source findings

### M021 — parental refusal/continuation of life-sustaining treatment for a severely ill newborn

**Decision:** what policy should a NICU use when parents request continued intensive life-sustaining treatment for a newborn whose expected benefit is very small and treatment burdens are substantial?

**Affected evidence.** Parent studies show heterogeneous preferences about decisional control and a strong need for sensitive communication, time, professional guidance and role flexibility. Parents do not uniformly want either full decisional control or clinician control. Recent NICU work includes Morillo Palomo et al. (2026; PMID 42324645), showing most bereaved parents valued shared decision-making and 76.9% preferred professional guidance, and qualitative work showing some parents value being protected from the burden of a final decision while others value active parental responsibility (PMID 36670597; PMID 38229540; PMID 27665039).

**Professional layer.** AAP guidance on forgoing life-sustaining medical treatment, reaffirmed in 2023, centers the child's best interests, benefit/burden assessment, collaborative decision-making and comfort care; the 2026 AAP clinical/technical reports on potentially nonbeneficial treatment add a stepwise conflict-resolution process, second opinion, institutional review, transfer/appeal and, in rare cases, forgoing treatment after due process.

**Initial judgment.** Strong research case; public evidence is about decision process more directly than about two institutional policies. Do not convert role heterogeneity into a fabricated pro/anti-treatment split.

### M022 — resuscitation at the threshold of viability

**Decision:** for an anticipated delivery in the periviable range, when outcomes are seriously uncertain, should the neonatal plan be active resuscitation, comfort-focused care, or parent-guided assessment within a gray zone?

**Affected evidence.** Parents facing periviable delivery balance survival/vitality against suffering; treatment information alone does not determine choices (PMID 39521888). Qualitative work shows that comfort care is often poorly understood or framed as “doing nothing,” creating a communication hazard (PMID 32332448).

**Professional layer.** ACOG's Periviable Birth consensus recommends individualized assessment and explicitly asks clinicians to discuss whether parents prioritize survival or minimizing suffering. It treats resuscitation decisions as preference-sensitive and allows a stepwise approach based on gestational age, fetal/neonatal condition and parental wishes rather than a single gestational-age cutoff.

**Initial judgment.** Potential asymmetric executable case: affected evidence supports genuinely different parental orientations, while professional guidance largely converges on shared, individualized gray-zone decision-making.

### M023 — parents seek highly experimental treatment for a critically ill child

**Decision:** when standard options are exhausted and parents request an investigational intervention with uncertain and probably low benefit, what should the hospital do?

**Affected evidence.** Pediatric oncology literature documents the difficulty of choosing between phase-I/experimental treatment and no further disease-directed treatment; parents often give substantial weight to clinician recommendations (PMID 9348593; PMID 2484648). These studies support the seriousness of the choice but do not yet cleanly map to multiple institutional access policies.

**Professional/regulatory layer.** FDA expanded-access criteria require serious/life-threatening disease, lack of satisfactory alternatives, inability to enroll in a trial, a favorable potential-benefit/risk judgment, and no interference with clinical development. FDA's April 2026 guidance clarifies implementation of expanded access under 21 CFR Part 312.

**Initial judgment.** Strong full-corpus case, but likely `needs-additional-evidence` for a multi-candidate public pool unless more direct affected-family evidence on access policy is found.

### M024 — adolescent gender-affirming medical treatment under contested evidence

**Decision:** what policy should a multidisciplinary service use for an adolescent requesting a puberty-related medical intervention when developmental capacity, expected benefits/harms and the evidence base remain contested?

**Affected evidence.** Qualitative studies show adolescents and parents undertake extended information-seeking and decision processes; youth and parents can differ in timing, concerns and perceived barriers, with supportive relationships and adequate time important to shared decision-making (PMID 31972534; PMID 32596730; PMID 36115898).

**Professional/evidence layer.** Current guidance is materially divergent across systems. The Endocrine Society continues to recommend multidisciplinary assessment and permits puberty suppression/hormonal treatment for appropriately assessed adolescents; NHS England does not routinely commission puberty-suppressing hormones for gender incongruence/dysphoria and in 2026 was separately consulting on policy for masculinising/feminising hormones. Systematic reviews published in 2024–2025 describe important limitations and low/very-low certainty in parts of the evidence base (PMID 38594047; PMID 38594053; PMID 39855724).

**Initial judgment.** `research-complete-not-executable` for now. The professional-policy object is moving and jurisdiction-dependent. Do not freeze a synthetic “consensus” or convert one country's commissioning rule into a universal expert candidate.

### M025 — cochlear implantation for a Deaf child

**Decision:** when Deaf parents decline cochlear implantation for a young Deaf child who is clinically eligible, what policy should the service use regarding parental choice, language access and preservation of future options?

**Affected evidence.** Parent studies show genuine value pluralism: Deaf identity, sign language, communication goals, mainstream/bilingual outcomes and medical risks matter differently across families (PMID 21941879; PMID 15236889; PMID 11006449). Deaf-community opposition to pediatric implantation has also evolved rather than being uniform (PMID 15148196).

**Professional layer.** The 2022 American Cochlear Implant Alliance pediatric candidacy guideline, endorsed by the American Academy of Audiology, supports team-based, whole-child/family-centered evaluation and early intervention where indicated while integrating family goals and preferences.

**Initial judgment.** Potential asymmetric executable case: affected evidence is genuinely plural; professional guidance may support one broad family-centered candidacy architecture rather than two distinct expert policies.

### M026 — withdrawal of life-sustaining treatment over family objection

**Decision:** when ongoing ICU treatment has very low expected benefit relative to burden and the surrogate objects to withdrawal, what fair process should govern continued treatment or withdrawal?

**Affected evidence.** Families who resist withdrawal cite faith, need for more time, differing interpretations of the patient's condition and moral concern about causing death (PMID 38661968). Historical family interviews identify communication failures and perceived disrespect as major sources of conflict (PMID 11176185).

**Professional layer.** The ATS/AACN/ACCP/ESICM/SCCM statement recommends a fair-process response to requests for potentially inappropriate treatment rather than bedside unilateralism. Later empirical work on hospital conflict procedures shows they are feasible but burdensome and can escalate distress (PMID 37380828).

**Initial judgment.** Research-complete process case; affected evidence supports reasons for continued treatment better than distinct institutional procedures. Likely `needs-additional-evidence` unless candidate audit finds a defensible asymmetric profile.

### M027 — advance directive versus current contentment in dementia

**Decision:** should a prior directive refusing a specified life-prolonging treatment control when a person with advanced dementia now appears comfortable, engaged and unable to understand the earlier directive?

**Affected evidence.** Preferences for life-sustaining treatment vary sharply by treatment burden and hypothetical dementia state (PMID 15859720). Surrogates often mispredict or override stated preferences, and dementia changes surrogate recommendations in ways that can depart from prior wishes (PMID 38358920; PMID 27410259).

**Normative layer.** The core disagreement is precedent autonomy versus present experiential interests/minimal autonomy, with hybrid accounts seeking to integrate prior values and current wellbeing (e.g., Dworkin; Jaworska; PMID 35015243).

**Initial judgment.** Philosophically excellent and research-complete, but current empirical evidence does not directly yield multiple institutional policy candidates. Preserve as non-executable unless later evidence supports a source-grounded profile.

### M028 — continued organ support after death by neurologic criteria

**Decision:** after a valid BD/DNC determination, what accommodation policy should a hospital use when the family rejects the determination and asks that organ support continue?

**Affected/public evidence.** Public understanding of brain death is often incomplete; only a minority of families in one large study could fully define brain death after being informed (PMID 14558637). A recent nationwide survey specifically examined informed public attitudes toward family requests for continued support after brain death (PMID 39810074). Hospital chaplains also report frequent religious objections, while their own views vary on whether support should continue (PMID 34195896).

**Professional layer.** The 2023 AAN/AAP/CNS/SCCM consensus guideline recommends a reasonable but limited period for families after death and requires institutional processes for disagreements. A 2025 AAN position statement further addresses objections, communication and public trust. These documents treat BD/DNC as death while recognizing limited accommodation rather than indefinite physiologic support.

**Initial judgment.** Strong candidate for later asymmetric executable construction, but the public survey should be read at candidate level before committing to two public policies.

### M029 — palliative sedation for refractory suffering

**Decision:** when a dying patient has severe refractory suffering despite specialist palliative care, what sedation policy should govern depth, timing and reassessment?

**Affected evidence.** Patients and relatives with experience of continuous deep sedation largely value relief of suffering and agency but report communication and continuity concerns (PMID 37822095). Bereaved-family comparisons of proportional versus continuous deep sedation did not show worse overall experience with deep sedation and identified some quality-of-care advantages (PMID 35039967).

**Professional layer.** The revised 2024 EAPC framework defines palliative sedation around refractory suffering, autonomy and proportionality, recommends the lowest effective depth, and permits deeper/continuous sedation when proportionate; it deliberately avoids a single required life-expectancy threshold.

**Initial judgment.** Research-complete. Professional guidance is strongly convergent around proportionality; affected evidence does not yet justify multiple policy candidates at the same granularity. Likely non-executable/needs additional evidence.

### M030 — concurrent disease-directed treatment and hospice support

**Decision:** should a payer or integrated health system permit hospice-level supportive services while a terminally ill adult continues selected disease-directed cancer treatment?

**Affected evidence.** Patients and caregivers can disagree about tradeoffs between treatment burden, quality of life and hospice; illness understanding is associated with less preference for aggressive care, but this does not imply that patients want an all-or-nothing choice (PMID 20137849; PMID 31768736). Qualitative palliative-care work shows many patients prefer shared decisions with family rather than a single fixed orientation (PMID 38756049).

**Professional/policy evidence.** Medicare hospice generally requires election of hospice and waiver of Medicare payment for treatment of the terminal illness and related conditions. In contrast, the Veterans Health Administration permits concurrent hospice and cancer-directed therapy. VA observational studies show concurrent care can coexist with hospice use and is not necessarily associated with more aggressive end-of-life care; higher hospice availability with concurrent treatment was associated with lower aggressive-care use and lower costs in advanced lung cancer (PMID 32119800; PMID 30920603; PMID 37846638).

**Initial judgment.** Very promising for executable construction because two real policy architectures already exist (traditional hospice election versus concurrent care) and affected preferences plausibly vary. Candidate-level audit required before any JSON.
