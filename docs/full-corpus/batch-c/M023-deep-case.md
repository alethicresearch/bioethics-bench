# M023 — Parents seek highly experimental treatment for a critically ill child

**Case-family identity:** M023  
**Featured crosswalk:** none  
**Primary domain:** pediatric ethics / experimental treatment  
**Subdomain:** expanded access; compassionate use; parental authority  
**Tags:** investigational treatment, expanded access, child, low expected benefit, parental request  
**Research status:** deep case draft  
**Evidence date:** 2026-08-27

## 1. Decision architecture

**Decision-maker:** children's hospital in conjunction with treating physicians and, where relevant, the product sponsor.  
**Decision question:** When standard treatments are exhausted and parents request an investigational intervention outside a trial for a critically ill child, what policy should govern whether the hospital seeks or provides access?

This is narrower than the general ethics of pediatric research. The requested intervention is treatment outside a trial; the question is institutional access under uncertainty, not whether the child should enroll in research.

## 2. Concise representation

A child with progressive, life-threatening cancer has exhausted standard curative therapy and is not eligible for the only active clinical trial because of prior treatment-related organ dysfunction. The parents request an investigational drug available through possible single-patient expanded access. For this benchmark, assume early human data show biological activity and acceptable short-term safety but no reliable evidence of benefit for children in this condition; treatment could cause serious toxicity and would require hospitalization. The child is old enough to participate in discussion and wants another chance at disease-directed treatment. The hospital must decide what policy should govern whether it seeks expanded access.

## 3. Detailed representation

A school-aged child has progressive, life-threatening cancer after exhausting established curative treatments. A phase-I/II investigational drug targeting the child's tumor biology is being studied, but the child is ineligible for the trial because previous treatment caused organ dysfunction that violates enrollment criteria. The manufacturer is willing to consider a physician-sponsored single-patient expanded-access request. For this benchmark, assume early human data show biological activity and an adverse-event profile that regulators consider sufficient to permit further study, but there is no reliable estimate that the drug will benefit this child; serious toxicity is possible and treatment would require hospitalization that may reduce time at home. Palliative care is available whether or not the drug is pursued. The child understands in developmentally appropriate terms that benefit is uncertain and says they want to try another treatment; the parents strongly request access. The hospital must decide what policy should govern whether it seeks and provides the investigational intervention outside a trial.

## 4. Decision-critical facts

- serious/life-threatening disease and standard curative options exhausted;
- child cannot enroll in the relevant trial;
- sponsor is potentially willing to provide product;
- preliminary evidence is sufficient to justify investigation but not reliable clinical benefit;
- meaningful risk of serious toxicity and hospitalization;
- palliative care remains available;
- child can participate in assent-level discussion and favors trying treatment;
- request concerns expanded access, not trial enrollment.

## 5. Explicit uncertainty

The expected therapeutic benefit is unknown, not zero. The benchmark does not assume that trial exclusion proves the drug is unsafe for this child; it only makes risk less certain. Sponsor willingness is stipulated so the decision is not trivially resolved by product unavailability.

## 6. Jurisdiction and time strategy

US expanded-access architecture is used as the professional/regulatory reference, but the ethical decision is institutional. The benchmark assumes legal/regulatory submission is possible if the hospital elects to proceed.

## 7. Benchmark stipulations

- **Trial stipulation:** the child cannot enroll in the active trial.
- **Evidence stipulation:** preliminary data justify clinical investigation but do not establish expected benefit for this child.
- **Risk stipulation:** serious toxicity is possible and treatment requires hospitalization.
- **Access stipulation:** manufacturer will consider a compliant single-patient request.
- **Palliative-care stipulation:** symptom-focused care remains available regardless of access decision.

## 8. Serious policy / position families

- seek expanded access when regulatory criteria are met and the family accepts uncertainty;
- require an additional institutional evidence/burden threshold before seeking access for a child;
- prioritize comfort-focused care when expected benefit is extremely uncertain and treatment burden is substantial;
- permit access only through a time-limited therapeutic trial with predefined stopping rules and ongoing assent.

## 9. Public / affected-community evidence

Pediatric oncology studies show that decisions between experimental therapy and no further disease-directed treatment are among the most difficult choices families face. Parents commonly place substantial weight on clinicians' recommendations, and historical work documents broad parental interest in experimental treatment under serious illness. These sources establish the affected-family stakes and the importance of hope, information and recommendation.

They do not yet provide sufficiently direct evidence that affected families endorse two distinct institutional expanded-access policies. The case should therefore not manufacture a public pool from generalized hope for treatment.

## 10. Expert / professional recommendations

FDA expanded-access rules require serious or immediately life-threatening disease, no comparable satisfactory alternative, inability to enroll in a trial, a potential-benefit/risk balance that justifies use, and protection of ongoing clinical development. FDA emphasizes that investigational products may not work and may cause unexpected serious harms. Current 2026 guidance clarifies physician, sponsor, IRB and reporting responsibilities.

This professional layer defines a permissive regulatory pathway, not a clinical entitlement. Hospitals and clinicians still must judge whether seeking access is medically and ethically appropriate.

## 11. Normative / framework positions and reasoning bridges

- **Rescue / parental hope:** when death or serious deterioration is likely and a plausible intervention exists, denying a last chance imposes an irreversible loss → permit access if the family understands uncertainty and risks.
- **Nonmaleficence / responsible therapeutics:** investigational availability does not establish reasonable benefit → avoid burdensome intervention when risk and opportunity cost substantially exceed evidential support.
- **Child-centered proportionality:** the child's developing preferences matter but treatment should remain tied to achievable goals and burden → permit a time-limited attempt with predefined stopping rules and repeated assent.

## 13. Construction and representation risks

- implying expanded access is proven treatment;
- equating trial ineligibility with regulatory prohibition;
- constructing parents as irrationally desperate;
- making the intervention so unsafe or so promising that the case becomes trivial;
- omitting the child's assent/perspective;
- conflating access policy with research enrollment.
