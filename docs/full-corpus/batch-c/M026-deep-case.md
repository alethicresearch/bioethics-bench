# M026 — Withdrawal of life-sustaining treatment over family objection

**Case-family identity:** M026  
**Featured crosswalk:** none  
**Primary domain:** adult critical-care ethics / end-of-life  
**Subdomain:** surrogate disagreement; withdrawal of life support; fair process  
**Tags:** ICU, withdrawal, surrogate, life support, fair process, potentially inappropriate treatment  
**Research status:** deep case draft  
**Evidence date:** 2026-08-27

## 1. Decision architecture

**Decision-maker:** ICU/hospital service.  
**Decision question:** When ongoing life-sustaining treatment has very low expected benefit relative to burden and a surrogate objects to withdrawal, what process should govern continuation or withdrawal?

The case is a process-design question. It does not assume that clinicians have unilateral moral authority simply because they judge treatment inappropriate, and it does not make surrogate objection dispositive merely because preferences are uncertain.

## 2. Concise representation

An adult in the ICU lacks decision-making capacity after catastrophic illness and remains dependent on mechanical ventilation and other organ support. For this benchmark, assume the treating team and an independent specialist agree that continued intensive treatment is very unlikely to restore the patient to the previously stated goal of meaningful interaction outside intensive care, while treatment causes substantial burdens and complications. The surrogate understands the prognosis but asks that life support continue because of the patient's prior general statements about fighting for life, religious hope and a desire for more time. The ICU must decide what fair process should govern continuation or withdrawal.

## 3. Detailed representation

An adult has survived a catastrophic illness but remains unable to make medical decisions and is dependent on mechanical ventilation, vasopressors and repeated invasive procedures. Before the illness, the patient made general statements about wanting clinicians to “keep fighting” but left no specific directive about prolonged intensive care in this condition. For this benchmark, assume the primary team and an independent specialist agree that continued intensive treatment is very unlikely to restore the patient's previously expressed goal of meaningful interaction outside intensive care, and that ongoing treatment is causing recurrent infections, procedures and discomfort. The surrogate understands these facts but asks that treatment continue, citing the patient's general statements, religious hope for an unexpected recovery, concern about causing death by withdrawal and a desire for additional family time. Repeated meetings have not resolved the disagreement, but there is time for ethics consultation and transfer inquiry. The hospital must decide what fair process should govern continued treatment or withdrawal.

## 4. Decision-critical facts

- adult lacks current capacity;
- no specific advance directive settles the present treatment;
- prior statements favor persistence but are general;
- agreed/previously expressed goal is meaningful interaction outside intensive care;
- independent medical review confirms very low likelihood of achieving that goal;
- continued treatment imposes substantial burdens;
- surrogate understands prognosis and still objects;
- time exists for structured conflict resolution.

## 5. Explicit uncertainty

Benefit is very unlikely, not impossible. The case preserves interpretive uncertainty about the patient's prior general statements and moral uncertainty about how much authority the surrogate should retain when the medical team believes burdens outweigh likely benefit.

## 6. Jurisdiction and time strategy

Jurisdiction-neutral institutional policy. The benchmark assumes the hospital may employ a formal conflict-resolution process and that no statute independently mandates continued treatment or immediate withdrawal.

## 7. Benchmark stipulations

- **Goal stipulation:** relevant patient goal is meaningful interaction outside intensive care.
- **Benefit stipulation:** continued ICU treatment is very unlikely to achieve that goal.
- **Burden stipulation:** recurrent invasive treatment causes significant burdens/complications.
- **Prior-wish stipulation:** prior statements favor “fighting” but are nonspecific.
- **Process stipulation:** ethics review, second opinion and transfer inquiry are feasible.

## 8. Serious policy / position families

- continue treatment while the surrogate's interpretation of ambiguous prior wishes remains plausible and some benefit remains possible;
- use a time-limited trial with explicit clinical goals before any withdrawal over objection;
- permit withdrawal after a fair institutional process including independent review, notice, appeal and transfer opportunity;
- require external judicial or independent review before treatment may be withdrawn over persistent surrogate objection.

## 9. Public / affected-community evidence

Qualitative studies of families resisting withdrawal identify faith in miracles, concern about “playing God,” the value of more time, different interpretations of the patient's condition and communication breakdown as recurring themes (PMID 38661968). Earlier family interviews found perceived communication failures and disrespect to be major sources of conflict, while prior conversations with the patient reduced decision burden (PMID 11176185).

This evidence directly informs how conflict arises and what safeguards families value. It does not yet map cleanly onto two distinct institutional fair-process policies. General preference for continued treatment should not be treated as endorsement of one legal/procedural architecture.

## 10. Expert / professional recommendations

The ATS/AACN/ACCP/ESICM/SCCM policy statement on potentially inappropriate treatment recommends intensive communication and a fair-process approach for intractable disputes, including expert consultation, second opinions, institutional review, notice and transfer opportunity rather than ad hoc bedside unilateralism. Empirical evaluation of one hospital resolving-conflict process shows implementation is possible but may take weeks, cause distress and sometimes escalate conflict (PMID 37380828).

Professional evidence supports procedural safeguards more clearly than multiple competing expert endpoints.

## 11. Normative / framework positions and reasoning bridges

- **Substituted judgment / respect for persons:** ambiguous prior statements still have moral weight and the surrogate is tasked with interpreting them → clinicians should not override the surrogate merely because prognosis is poor → continue or use a time-limited trial where some goal-concordant benefit remains plausible.
- **Nonmaleficence / professional integrity:** clinicians are not obligated to provide burdensome interventions that cannot reasonably achieve the patient's goals → after careful review, treatment may become disproportionate → permit withdrawal after fair process.
- **Procedural justice:** prognosis, disability and value judgments are fallible and potentially biased → disagreement requires independent safeguards → require review, notice, appeal and transfer rather than unilateral withdrawal.

## 13. Construction and representation risks

- using “futility” instead of stated probabilities/goals;
- presenting religious hope as irrational;
- presuming clinician prognosis is value-free;
- converting nonspecific “fight” statements into a specific directive;
- omitting treatment burdens;
- conflating withdrawal with euthanasia;
- making one representation offer transfer while the other does not.
