# M030 — Concurrent disease-directed treatment and hospice support

**Case-family identity:** M030  
**Featured crosswalk:** none  
**Primary domain:** end-of-life care / health-system design  
**Subdomain:** hospice eligibility; concurrent care; cancer treatment  
**Tags:** hospice, concurrent care, cancer, palliative care, reimbursement, treatment choice  
**Research status:** deep case draft  
**Evidence date:** 2026-08-27

## 1. Decision architecture

**Decision-maker:** payer or integrated health system designing hospice eligibility and payment policy.  
**Decision question:** Should hospice-level supportive services be available to a terminally ill adult who wishes to continue selected disease-directed treatment for the terminal illness?

The case is a program-design decision, not a bedside decision about whether a particular patient should stop chemotherapy. It asks whether access to hospice support should require an all-or-nothing election away from disease-directed treatment.

## 2. Concise representation

An adult with metastatic lung cancer has an expected prognosis under six months if the disease follows its usual course and wants hospice-level home support, symptom management and caregiver services. The patient also wants to continue a low-frequency cancer-directed therapy that may modestly slow progression but is not expected to cure the disease. For this benchmark, assume the therapy's burdens are acceptable to the patient and the health system could finance either a traditional hospice benefit that excludes payment for treatment of the terminal illness or a concurrent-care model that permits selected disease-directed treatment alongside hospice. The payer must decide which policy to adopt.

## 3. Detailed representation

An adult with metastatic lung cancer has progressive disease despite prior therapy and is certified as having an expected prognosis of six months or less if the illness follows its ordinary course. The patient wants the home nursing, symptom management, psychosocial care and caregiver support available through hospice but also wants to continue a selected cancer-directed therapy given intermittently. For this benchmark, assume the therapy is not curative, may modestly slow progression in some patients, has burdens the patient considers acceptable and does not require ICU-level rescue or experimental access. The patient understands the prognosis and explicitly values both quality-focused hospice support and the possibility of some disease control. The payer or integrated health system can choose a traditional hospice election model in which coverage for treatment of the terminal illness is waived, a concurrent model allowing selected disease-directed therapy while hospice continues, or a narrower time-limited concurrent pathway. The decision is what program rule should govern eligibility.

## 4. Decision-critical facts

- terminal illness with hospice-level prognosis;
- disease-directed treatment is noncurative but may have modest benefit;
- patient understands prognosis and values both symptom support and continued therapy;
- treatment burden is acceptable to the patient and not an emergency intervention;
- hospice services would otherwise be appropriate;
- decision is at payer/program level;
- both traditional election and concurrent-care architectures are operationally feasible.

## 5. Explicit uncertainty

The magnitude of disease-directed benefit is uncertain but not assumed zero. The case also does not stipulate that concurrent care necessarily saves money or reduces aggressive care for every patient. Existing observational evidence informs feasibility and system effects but does not determine the normative policy by itself.

## 6. Jurisdiction and time strategy

US program-design context dated to 2026 because real policy architectures differ. Traditional Medicare hospice serves as one policy model; Veterans Health Administration concurrent hospice/cancer treatment serves as another. The benchmark asks what a payer or integrated system should adopt, rather than what current Medicare law already requires.

## 7. Benchmark stipulations

- **Eligibility stipulation:** patient meets clinical hospice prognosis criteria.
- **Treatment stipulation:** selected disease-directed therapy is noncurative, plausibly beneficial and acceptable in burden.
- **Preference stipulation:** patient understands prognosis and wants both hospice support and continued therapy.
- **Feasibility stipulation:** payer can operationalize either traditional or concurrent coverage.
- **Scope stipulation:** treatment is related to the terminal illness, so the policy choice genuinely matters.

## 8. Serious policy / position families

- traditional hospice election: hospice benefits require forgoing payer coverage of disease-directed treatment for the terminal illness;
- full concurrent care: allow hospice services and selected disease-directed treatment simultaneously when the patient remains hospice eligible;
- conditional concurrent care: allow specified treatments when goal-concordant and proportionate, with utilization review or time-limited reassessment;
- carve-out model: preserve hospice core services while separately financing predefined palliative disease-directed interventions.

## 9. Public / affected-community evidence

Patients and caregivers with advanced cancer can differ in how they trade off treatment burden, quality of life and hospice, and more accurate illness understanding is associated with less preference for aggressive care without implying that people want a binary cure-versus-comfort choice. Qualitative palliative-care research shows many patients value shared decision-making with family and accommodate relational concerns rather than following a single fixed treatment orientation.

This evidence supports at least two plausible affected orientations: avoid forced abandonment of desired disease-directed treatment to access hospice versus favor a clear comfort-focused transition that reduces treatment burden and ambiguity. Candidate audit must ensure these are grounded in actual patient/caregiver evidence rather than inferred from the existence of two payment systems.

## 10. Expert / professional recommendations

Current US policy supplies unusually concrete competing institutional architectures. CMS describes the Medicare hospice benefit as comfort-focused and requires beneficiaries who elect hospice to waive Medicare payment for the terminal illness and related conditions except care arranged by hospice. In contrast, the Veterans Health Administration permits concurrent hospice and cancer-directed therapy.

Observational VA studies show concurrent care is feasible. Concurrent hospice use increased over time; facilities with greater hospice availability could provide hospice while some patients continued cancer therapy. Studies found no clear increase in aggressive end-of-life care attributable to concurrent care, and one analysis associated higher hospice availability/concurrent treatment with less aggressive care and lower costs without a survival difference. Another VA cohort found that only a minority of concurrent-care patients actually continued intensive cancer-directed treatments and use declined near death.

These data support genuine expert/policy pluralism rather than hypothetical alternatives.

## 11. Normative / framework positions and reasoning bridges

- **Autonomy / nonabandonment:** patients may reasonably value both symptom support and limited disease control → requiring an artificial either/or election can deter hospice and frustrate goals → permit concurrent care.
- **Stewardship / program integrity:** hospice is designed around comfort-focused interdisciplinary care and finite resources → open-ended disease-directed treatment may blur goals and raise cost/coordination burdens → retain a clear hospice election boundary.
- **Proportionality / value-based care:** some disease-directed treatments are low-burden and goal-concordant while others are burdensome and marginal → categorical prohibition and unlimited concurrency are both crude → permit conditional concurrent care using explicit benefit/burden criteria.

## 13. Construction and representation risks

- treating Medicare's current rule as ethical proof rather than one policy architecture;
- treating VA observational outcomes as randomized causal evidence;
- making continued treatment clearly futile or clearly curative;
- conflating palliative disease-directed treatment with “aggressive care”;
- assuming all hospice patients want to stop disease-directed therapy;
- ignoring program cost and coordination constraints;
- making the detailed version more treatment-beneficial than the concise version.
