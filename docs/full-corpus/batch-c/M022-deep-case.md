# M022 — Resuscitation at the threshold of viability

**Case-family identity:** M022  
**Featured crosswalk:** none  
**Primary domain:** neonatal ethics  
**Subdomain:** periviable birth; resuscitation; parental decision-making  
**Tags:** periviability, resuscitation, comfort care, prematurity, parental values  
**Research status:** deep case draft  
**Evidence date:** 2026-08-27

## 1. Decision architecture

**Decision-maker:** perinatal/neonatal service in consultation with the pregnant patient and family.  
**Decision question:** For an anticipated periviable birth with substantial uncertainty in survival and disability, what policy should govern whether neonatal resuscitation is initiated?

The case is not “what gestational age is viable?” It fixes a gray-zone clinical context in which more than one ethically defensible plan exists and asks how decisional authority and treatment defaults should be structured.

## 2. Concise representation

A pregnant patient is expected to deliver at 22 weeks 6 days at a tertiary center with full neonatal intensive-care capability. For this benchmark, assume the fetus has no major anomaly, estimated size is appropriate for gestation, and individualized outcome estimates leave a meaningful but highly uncertain chance of survival with a substantial risk of serious morbidity. The pregnant patient and partner understand that active resuscitation may lead either to survival, prolonged intensive care with major impairment, or death after treatment, while comfort-focused care would avoid invasive resuscitation. They ask what options are ethically available. The service must decide what policy should govern resuscitation planning in this gray zone.

## 3. Detailed representation

A pregnant patient presents with threatened delivery at 22 weeks 6 days at a tertiary perinatal center. Accurate dating is available, antenatal steroids have been discussed, the fetus is appropriately grown for gestational age and no major congenital anomaly has been identified. For this benchmark, assume individualized prognostic estimates support a meaningful but highly uncertain chance of survival if active neonatal resuscitation is attempted, with a substantial risk of serious short- and long-term morbidity among survivors. The clinical team explains that active treatment may result in survival after a prolonged NICU course, death despite invasive treatment, or survival with significant impairment; comfort-focused care would avoid intubation and other invasive resuscitation while providing warmth, analgesia and family contact. The pregnant patient and partner understand both options and differ somewhat in how they weigh survival against suffering, although they can continue deliberating. The center must decide what policy should govern whether resuscitation is initiated in this periviable gray zone.

## 4. Decision-critical facts

- gestational age fixed at 22+6 weeks with reliable dating;
- tertiary center with relevant neonatal capability;
- no major fetal anomaly and appropriate estimated size;
- survival is possible but highly uncertain;
- serious morbidity risk remains substantial;
- active resuscitation and comfort-focused care are both clinically available;
- parents receive balanced information and can deliberate;
- decision concerns default/authority in the gray zone, not emergency treatment after an unexpectedly vigorous birth.

## 5. Explicit uncertainty

Outcome estimates at the threshold of viability vary by center, infant characteristics and treatment practice. The benchmark therefore avoids a single universal survival percentage. Uncertainty is load-bearing: if survival were effectively impossible or highly likely without severe burden, the policy space would narrow.

## 6. Jurisdiction and time strategy

Jurisdiction-neutral perinatal policy. The service is assumed legally able to offer either resuscitation or comfort-focused care within the represented gray zone.

## 7. Benchmark stipulations

- **Gray-zone stipulation:** both active resuscitation and comfort-focused care are professionally defensible options in this clinical setting.
- **Clinical-capability stipulation:** full neonatal intensive care is available.
- **Information stipulation:** the pregnant patient/family have received balanced counseling about survival, morbidity and comfort care.
- **No-anomaly stipulation:** no separate lethal or major congenital anomaly determines the decision.

## 8. Serious policy / position families

- presumptive active resuscitation unless parents decline after informed counseling;
- parent-guided shared decision-making with no default between active treatment and comfort care;
- comfort-focused default unless parents affirmatively request active resuscitation;
- stepwise assessment at birth, allowing initial stabilization with rapid reassessment rather than treating the prenatal choice as irrevocable.

## 9. Public / affected-community evidence

Recent interviews with birthing parents after periviable consultation show that decisions reflect a balance between survival/vitality and suffering rather than a simple response to numerical prognosis (PMID 39521888). Qualitative work also shows that comfort care is often poorly understood or described as “doing nothing,” which can distort informed choice (PMID 32332448). These findings support genuine plural orientations toward active treatment versus comfort-focused care and strongly support neutral explanation of both.

Affected evidence therefore plausibly supports at least two public-derived policy orientations, but later candidate audit should ensure they are policies about the decision rule rather than merely personal treatment preferences.

## 10. Expert / professional recommendations

ACOG's Periviable Birth consensus treats these decisions as preference-sensitive. It recommends discussion of whether the parents' goal is optimizing survival or minimizing suffering, individualized assessment rather than gestational age alone, a stepwise approach to care, and ongoing reevaluation. Provider research similarly describes shared decision-making as optimal in the periviable range.

The professional layer currently appears more convergent around individualized shared decision-making than around two competing expert policies.

## 11. Normative / framework positions and reasoning bridges

- **Presumption for life / rescue:** when survival remains meaningful and the future child's preferences are unknown, irreversible non-treatment carries special moral weight → use active resuscitation as the default while allowing informed parental refusal.
- **Parental authority under uncertainty:** outcomes are deeply uncertain and values about acceptable burdens legitimately differ → parents should choose among medically reasonable active and comfort-focused options without a clinician-imposed default.
- **Nonmaleficence / proportionality:** invasive resuscitation can impose prolonged burdens with uncertain benefit → where prognosis lies at the lowest edge of viability, comfort-focused care may be the default unless parents affirmatively choose treatment.

## 13. Construction and representation risks

- treating gestational age as the sole determinant;
- embedding outdated survival percentages as universal facts;
- presenting comfort care as no care;
- making one option linguistically more compassionate or courageous;
- changing fetal condition, hospital capability or prognostic uncertainty across representations;
- confusing parental preference evidence with institutional-policy endorsement.
