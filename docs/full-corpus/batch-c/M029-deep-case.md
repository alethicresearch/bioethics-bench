# M029 — Palliative sedation for refractory suffering

**Case-family identity:** M029  
**Featured crosswalk:** none  
**Primary domain:** palliative care / end-of-life  
**Subdomain:** palliative sedation; refractory symptoms; proportionality  
**Tags:** palliative sedation, refractory suffering, continuous deep sedation, proportionality, end of life  
**Research status:** deep case draft  
**Evidence date:** 2026-08-27

## 1. Decision architecture

**Decision-maker:** specialist palliative-care team with the patient.  
**Decision question:** When a dying patient has severe suffering that remains refractory despite specialist palliative care, what policy should govern the depth, timing and reassessment of palliative sedation?

The decision is about the sedation policy, not whether symptom relief is permissible. It distinguishes proportional sedation from an automatic move to continuous deep unconsciousness and keeps intention, refractoriness and patient choice explicit.

## 2. Concise representation

A capacitated adult with advanced cancer is near the end of life and has severe breathlessness and panic that remain intolerable despite specialist palliative treatment. The patient asks for sedation even if meaningful conversation becomes impossible. For this benchmark, assume the palliative team agrees that the symptoms are refractory, the patient understands the likely effects of lighter, intermittent and continuous deep sedation, and there is no intention to hasten death. The service must decide what policy should govern sedation depth, continuity and reassessment.

## 3. Detailed representation

A capacitated adult with advanced metastatic cancer is approaching the end of life and experiences severe breathlessness and panic. Multiple evidence-based symptom treatments, including specialist palliative interventions, have been tried without adequate relief. The patient describes the suffering as intolerable and asks to be sedated even if this prevents further meaningful conversation with family. The patient and family have discussed the loss of interaction, hydration decisions and the possibility that sedation may be lighter or intermittent before becoming continuous and deep. For this benchmark, assume the specialist palliative team agrees that the suffering is refractory, sedation medication will be titrated for symptom relief rather than to cause death, and no separate request for euthanasia or assisted dying is being made. The service must decide what policy should govern the depth and continuity of sedation and whether planned reassessment should be required.

## 4. Decision-critical facts

- patient has decision-making capacity;
- life-limiting advanced illness and end-of-life context;
- severe suffering is both intolerable to the patient and judged refractory after specialist review;
- sedation is intended for symptom relief, not to hasten death;
- patient accepts potential loss of communication;
- family is informed but patient remains decision-maker;
- lighter/intermittent and continuous-deep sedation are clinically possible;
- hydration is a separate decision and should not be smuggled into the sedation choice.

## 5. Explicit uncertainty

The exact remaining life expectancy is uncertain and is not used as a fixed eligibility threshold. The degree of sedation required for adequate relief may only become clear through titration. The case therefore asks about a policy of proportional escalation rather than prespecifying one clinically necessary depth.

## 6. Jurisdiction and time strategy

Jurisdiction-neutral palliative-care policy. The benchmark assumes palliative sedation is lawful and professionally available and excludes euthanasia/assisted-dying law from the decision.

## 7. Benchmark stipulations

- **Refractoriness stipulation:** specialist palliative team agrees further conventional symptom treatment is unlikely to provide acceptable relief in time.
- **Capacity stipulation:** patient has capacity and requests sedation after informed discussion.
- **Intention stipulation:** medication is titrated to relieve suffering, not to cause death.
- **Options stipulation:** proportional/light/intermittent and continuous deep sedation are technically available.
- **Hydration stipulation:** hydration decisions are handled independently.

## 8. Serious policy / position families

- titrate sedation proportionally to the minimum depth needed for relief, with reassessment and intermittent approaches where feasible;
- permit continuous deep sedation when suffering is refractory and the informed patient prefers reliable unconsciousness rather than repeated awakening;
- reserve continuous deep sedation for the final stage of dying or after a more stringent independent review;
- require planned attempts at reduction/interruption unless the patient's condition or preferences make reassessment inappropriate.

## 9. Public / affected-community evidence

Interview research with patients and relatives who experienced or considered continuous deep sedation found broad acceptance of sedation as a palliative option, a strong priority on avoiding suffering and a desire for agency, alongside concerns about insufficient communication and continuity (PMID 37822095). Bereaved-family comparisons of proportional and continuous deep sedation found no worse overall family experience with deep sedation and some more favorable care assessments, though the study was not designed to establish a preferred institutional policy (PMID 35039967).

Affected evidence therefore supports autonomy, suffering relief and communication but does not clearly create two distinct public policy architectures at the same level of abstraction.

## 10. Expert / professional recommendations

The revised 2024 European Association for Palliative Care framework reached high consensus across 42 statements. It defines sedation through refractoriness, intolerability and proportionality; emphasizes patient autonomy; recommends using the lowest sedation depth that adequately relieves suffering and maintaining interaction where possible; recognizes intermittent/respite sedation earlier in illness; and allows continuous deep sedation when proportionate. It deliberately does not impose one specific remaining-life-expectancy threshold and separates hydration decision-making.

This is a strongly convergent professional architecture rather than a plural expert pool.

## 11. Normative / framework positions and reasoning bridges

- **Beneficence/nonmaleficence with proportionality:** refractory suffering creates a strong duty of relief, but consciousness and interaction also have value → reduce consciousness only as much as required → use proportional titration and reassessment.
- **Autonomy:** a capable patient may reasonably prioritize reliable relief over continued interaction → when risks and alternatives are understood, patient preferences should shape depth and continuity → permit continuous deep sedation when clinically proportionate and requested.
- **Precaution / moral distinguishability:** deep continuous sedation can be difficult to distinguish socially from practices intended to hasten death → rigorous indication, documentation and independent review protect trust → apply heightened safeguards before continuous deep sedation.

## 13. Construction and representation risks

- equating palliative sedation with euthanasia;
- assuming deep sedation necessarily shortens life;
- using a fixed prognosis threshold unsupported by the current EAPC framework;
- ignoring the patient's own intolerability judgment;
- treating family preference as overriding a capacitated patient;
- conflating hydration with sedation;
- constructing “proportional” and “deep” policies as opposites when deep sedation can itself be proportionate.
