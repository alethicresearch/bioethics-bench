# Bioethics Bench — Batch J source and construction packet (M091–M100)

**Evidence date:** 2026-08-27  
**Scope:** environmental sustainability of clinical trials; dual-use life-science research/publication; vaccination mandates; isolation/quarantine; newborn screening; supervised consumption services; catastrophic ventilator triage; high-cost therapy reimbursement; ordinary ICU scarcity.

## M091 — Environmental footprint of a clinical trial

**Decision object:** whether a funder/REC should require a lower-carbon trial design when scientific validity and participant safety are preserved but implementation is less convenient or modestly more costly.

**Professional evidence:** WHO's February 2026 report positions environmental sustainability as part of trial design, conduct and oversight and recommends mitigation/adaptation strategies involving greener design, decentralized/digital approaches, sustainable procurement and governance without compromising trial integrity, with attention to social justice and LMIC capacity.

**Likely policies:** require lower-carbon design when validity/safety preserved; proportional sustainability requirement tied to avoidable emissions/cost; scientific/participant priorities first with voluntary mitigation. Current WHO guidance may largely support one proportional architecture rather than plural professional rules.

## M092 — Dual-use pathogen/life-science research

**Decision object:** institutional authorization of valuable life-science research that could also increase capabilities for accidental or deliberate biological harm.

**Safety boundary:** Bench scenarios remain at governance level and do not include experimental methods, engineering steps, target characteristics, or other enabling technical detail.

**Professional evidence:** WHO 2022 global framework treats dual-use governance as shared responsibility across the research lifecycle and calls for biorisk identification, assessment, mitigation, oversight, biosafety/biosecurity, stakeholder engagement and One Health governance. WHO's TAG continues operationalization through 2024–2026.

**Likely policies:** proceed with layered risk mitigation; modify aims/methods/information handling to reduce biorisk; do not conduct above a defined unacceptable-risk threshold. These are likely parts of one risk-management architecture.

## M093 — Publication of dual-use methods

**Decision object:** journal/editorial policy when legitimate research yields information whose detailed dissemination could materially increase misuse risk.

**Professional evidence:** WHO dual-use framework explicitly includes publishers/editors in shared governance. WHO held a 2025 publishers/editors meeting on dual-use governance, reflecting active policy development.

**Likely policies:** full publication when risk is low; publish conclusions with redacted/controlled-access enabling details when necessary; decline dissemination of information above an unacceptable misuse threshold. No enabling material belongs in the Bench.

## M094 — Vaccination mandate during a serious outbreak

**Decision object:** whether an institution/government should require vaccination for a defined high-transmission-risk group after voluntary measures fail to achieve sufficient uptake.

**Professional evidence:** WHO mandatory-vaccination ethics guidance treats mandates as potentially justifiable only when necessary/proportionate to a significant public-health goal, supported by evidence of vaccine safety/effectiveness and likely impact, and mindful of liberty, trust, equity, accessibility and exemptions/alternatives.

**Likely policies:** mandate with medical exemptions after necessity threshold; conditional mandate only after voluntary/access measures prove insufficient; remain voluntary with intensive access/education. Need direct public policy evidence specific to fixed scenario.

## M095 — Isolation of a contagious person

**Decision object:** compulsory isolation of a person known to be contagious who refuses effective voluntary isolation and poses a substantial transmission risk.

**Professional evidence:** AMA ethics and WHO TB ethics guidance support voluntary adherence first, mandatory isolation as a last resort, least-restrictive means, reciprocity/support, scientific necessity, non-discrimination, due process and transparency.

**Likely policies:** supported voluntary isolation first with legal backstop; immediate compulsory isolation in high/imminent transmission risk; no coercion absent an extreme threshold. The first two may be threshold variants within one professional rule.

## M096 — Expansion of newborn screening

**Decision object:** whether a state/program should add a condition to universal newborn screening when a reliable screening test exists and early diagnosis may benefit some children but treatment-effect evidence remains uncertain.

**Professional evidence:** HRSA RUSP process is evidence-based and considers screening benefit, feasibility and treatment availability. States may add conditions beyond RUSP, but national recommendation is a formal evidence/governance process rather than enthusiasm alone.

**Likely policies:** add to universal panel; pilot under research/data collection before universal expansion; defer until evidence of effective early intervention is stronger.

## M097 — Supervised consumption service

**Decision object:** whether a city should establish supervised consumption services for people at high overdose risk.

**Empirical evidence:** supervised consumption sites prevent onsite overdose deaths and connect users to services; 2025/2026 systematic review finds population-level overdose-mortality effects remain uncertain. Other outcomes and neighborhood effects are context-specific.

**Likely policies:** establish as harm reduction; do not establish and prioritize other treatment/prevention strategies; time-limited evaluated pilot with neighborhood/community safeguards. Strong direct community/policy-attitude evidence needed before execution.

## M098 — Ventilator triage during catastrophic scarcity

**Decision object:** allocation when demand exceeds ventilator capacity so not all clinically eligible patients can be treated.

**Professional evidence:** major crisis standards frameworks prioritize short-term survival/likelihood of benefit while avoiding categorical exclusions; disability-rights critiques caution against long-term life expectancy and structural-bias criteria. Lottery/first-come-only rules may increase deaths and can reproduce access inequity. Multiprinciple frameworks combine benefit, urgency, equity and lottery among close cases.

**Likely policies:** maximize near-term survival; multiprinciple benefit/equity rule; lottery among patients not meaningfully distinguishable after clinical criteria.

## M099 — Public reimbursement of a very high-cost therapy

**Decision object:** whether a public health system should fund a therapy with substantial benefit for a small severe/rare-disease population when doing so displaces other effective care.

**Professional/governance evidence:** NICE explicitly incorporates opportunity cost, standard cost-effectiveness thresholds and severity modifiers; highly specialized technologies use a substantially higher threshold for ultra-rare severe conditions, with explicit recognition that this displaces greater aggregate health gains elsewhere. CMS coverage-with-evidence-development provides a separate pathway where evidence uncertainty is material.

**Likely policies:** ordinary cost-effectiveness threshold; severity/rarity-weighted higher threshold; conditional coverage/evidence development under high uncertainty. Need affected/public preference evidence for executable construction.

## M100 — Routine ICU admission under ordinary scarcity

**Decision object:** prioritization when staffed ICU beds are scarce in ordinary operations rather than declared disaster conditions.

**Professional evidence:** AMA allocation ethics applies to chronically scarce resources including ICU beds and recommends medically relevant criteria including urgency, likelihood and anticipated duration of benefit, while rejecting social worth. Fair process and reassessment are important.

**Likely policies:** expected near-term benefit; urgency/worst-off priority; time-limited trial with reassessment when prognosis is uncertain; lottery among similarly eligible patients. Many are components of a multiprinciple rule rather than independent expert policies.

## Batch-J working hypothesis

Likely strongest audit targets: **M094, M097, M098, M099, M100**. M091–M093 are important frontier governance cases but direct affected-policy evidence is likely limited; M095 professional consensus favors a least-restrictive stepped architecture; M096 may need parent/public screening-priority evidence.
