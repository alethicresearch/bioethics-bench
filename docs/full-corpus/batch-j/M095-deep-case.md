# M095 — Compulsory isolation for substantial infectious risk

**Case-family identity:** M095  
**Primary domain:** public-health ethics  
**Tags:** isolation, infectious disease, liberty, least restrictive means, reciprocity, due process  
**Research status:** deep case draft  
**Evidence date:** 2026-08-27

## Decision architecture

**Decision-maker:** public-health authority.  
**Decision question:** When may authorities compel isolation of a person known to be contagious who refuses effective voluntary isolation and poses a substantial transmission risk?

## Scenario pair

**Concise:** An adult is confirmed to have a highly transmissible infection that can cause serious disease in others. The person remains contagious, cannot reliably avoid close community contact and repeatedly refuses supported home isolation despite clear counseling, paid leave, food/housing assistance and practical help. A time-limited isolation order with appeal rights and humane medical care is available. The authority must decide whether to impose compulsory isolation now, continue supported voluntary isolation with a legal order only if specific further noncompliance occurs, or avoid coercion unless the transmission risk becomes extreme/imminent.

**Detailed:** A public-health authority is managing an adult with laboratory-confirmed contagious disease. Epidemiologic evidence indicates a substantial risk of person-to-person transmission and meaningful harm to susceptible contacts. The person understands the diagnosis but repeatedly leaves the agreed isolation setting for nonessential close-contact activities. Authorities have provided paid-leave documentation, food delivery, suitable housing, communication support and clinical follow-up, and have attempted negotiated voluntary adherence. A compulsory order would be time limited to contagiousness, reviewed regularly, allow legal challenge and provide safe/humane accommodation. The authority must choose whether the demonstrated substantial risk and failed voluntary measures justify compulsory isolation; whether one further supported warning/conditional legal backstop is ethically required before compulsion; or whether coercive detention should be reserved for only an extreme or immediately catastrophic transmission threat.

## Evidence architecture

AMA ethics supports voluntary adherence first and mandatory isolation when a contagious patient fails to comply, using the least restrictive scientifically sound approach. WHO ethics guidance on involuntary isolation/detention similarly treats coercion as exceptional, after reasonable adherence measures fail, and requires legality, necessity, least restriction, non-discrimination, due process and reciprocity/support.

This professional layer is highly convergent on a stepped least-restrictive architecture; the alternatives differ mainly in threshold/timing. Direct affected/public policy evidence is limited and context-dependent.

## Frameworks

- **Harm principle:** substantial preventable risk to others can justify liberty restriction after voluntary measures fail.
- **Least restrictive means:** coercion is justified only after serious supportive alternatives have been attempted and must last no longer than necessary.
- **High-threshold civil liberty:** involuntary confinement is a grave state power and should require unusually imminent/severe risk beyond ordinary infectiousness.

## Construction risks

Distinguish isolation of contagious people from quarantine of exposed asymptomatic contacts. Provide reciprocity/support before compulsion. Include due process and finite duration. Do not use isolation as punishment.

## Executable judgment

`needs-additional-evidence`. Professional guidance largely converges on supported voluntary measures followed by necessary least-restrictive compulsion; public evidence does not yet support two institution-level rules.

## Sources
