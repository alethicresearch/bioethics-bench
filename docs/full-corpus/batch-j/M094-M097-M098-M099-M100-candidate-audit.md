# Batch J candidate audit — M094, M097, M098, M099, M100

## M094 — Vaccination mandate for a high-risk workforce during a serious outbreak

### Public candidates

**pub1 — require vaccination for the defined workforce**  
`Require vaccination for patient-facing workers during the serious outbreak, with medical exemptions and proportionate alternatives where vaccination is medically contraindicated.`

**Support:** a nationally representative US Gallup-panel study (n=2,730) found employer-enforced employee mandates acceptable to 47.7% and unacceptable to 38.1%, demonstrating genuine public policy disagreement rather than mere vaccine hesitancy. The fixed Bench scenario is narrower and more mandate-favorable than that 2020 generic employer question because it specifies a serious outbreak, patient-facing exposure, strong vaccine safety/effectiveness, transmission reduction, free access and failed voluntary measures. The exact healthcare-workforce application is therefore a Bench policy translation, not a claim that 47.7% specifically endorsed this scenario.

**pub2 — retain a voluntary/alternative-risk-reduction policy unless necessity becomes stronger**  
`Continue voluntary vaccination with intensive access, education, testing and infection-control alternatives unless evidence shows those less restrictive measures cannot adequately protect patients.`

**Support:** the same public survey found substantial opposition to employer mandates. Later worker surveys also show meaningful preference splits around employer vaccination requirements. The alternative-measures clause is **Bench-authored policy completion** to convert mandate opposition into a defensible institution-level policy without constructing a blanket anti-vaccine rule.

**Distinctness:** pass. Under the fixed scenario pub1 mandates now; pub2 requires a stronger failure-of-alternatives threshold before compulsion.

### Expert candidate

**exp1 — conditional necessity/proportionality mandate architecture**  
`Use a vaccination mandate only when it is necessary and proportionate to a significant public-health goal, the vaccine is sufficiently safe and effective, access is equitable, and less restrictive measures are inadequate; include appropriate exemptions and alternatives.`

**Support:** WHO mandatory-vaccination ethics guidance. One professional architecture rather than mandate-versus-no-mandate professional pluralism.

### Framework candidates

**fw1 — harm prevention / reciprocity**  
`Require vaccination once avoidable transmission from patient-facing workers creates substantial preventable risk to vulnerable patients and workers have been given free, accessible vaccination.`

**fw2 — least restrictive liberty**  
`Use compulsory vaccination only after voluntary access, education and less restrictive infection-control alternatives demonstrably fail to achieve the necessary level of patient protection.`

### Judgment

**Provisional profile: `full-corpus-2x1x2-mean-v1`.** Survives candidate audit, but independent review should scrutinize temporal/representational transfer from early COVID mandate polling to the benchmark's deliberately generic serious-outbreak scenario. This case must remain evidence-dated and must not be represented as measuring current attitudes toward an unspecified vaccine.

## M097 — Supervised consumption services

### Public / affected candidates

**pub1 — establish a supervised consumption service**  
`Establish a supervised consumption service that provides overdose response, sterile supplies and voluntary links to health and social care, with people who use drugs involved in service design.`

**Support:** direct affected-community evidence is strong. In a 2025 Stockholm study of 219 people who inject drugs, 72.1% were willing to use a future SCS. A 2012 two-city stakeholder study found people who use drugs perceived meaningful health and safety benefits. A 2019 systematic review found stakeholder perceptions repeatedly differed by lived experience, with PWUD/staff often more positive than non-user community stakeholders. A 2026 Canadian national survey also found generally positive public perceptions of client-level effects.

**pub2 — do not establish a permanent service without local evidence/acceptance; use other services or a limited evaluation first**  
`Do not establish a permanent supervised consumption service at this time; continue other overdose-prevention and treatment services while building local evidence and community acceptance, with a pilot remaining available if those conditions can be met.`

**Support:** qualitative work in four heavily affected US counties found stakeholders recognized potential benefits but generally believed their communities would not currently accept SCSs because of cultural, resource and practical barriers; stakeholders emphasized evidence and community education before implementation. The 2019 stakeholder systematic review documents recurring concerns about location, rules and community impacts. The “pilot remains available” clause is **Bench-authored policy completion** that translates caution into an actionable policy rather than an absolute moral prohibition.

**Distinctness:** pass. One establishes an ongoing service; the other declines permanent implementation now and requires a local-evidence/acceptance pathway first.

### Expert candidates

**exp1 — monitored pilot implementation**  
`Implement a monitored and evaluated supervised consumption pilot designed to generate local evidence on feasibility, effectiveness, community effects and health-system impact before broader adoption.`

**Support:** AMA Policy H-95.925, reaffirmed 2023, explicitly supports development and implementation of pilot supervised injection facilities designed, monitored and evaluated to inform policymakers.

**exp2 — implement and expand overdose prevention centers**  
`Establish and expand overdose prevention centers as an evidence-based harm-reduction service, integrate health and social supports, and involve people who use drugs in design and operation.`

**Support:** AMERSA's 2024 position statement endorses overdose prevention centers and calls for increased access and policy change facilitating urgent expansion, with community-specific design and PWUD involvement.

**Distinctness:** pass. exp1 treats a pilot/evidence-generation phase as the present policy; exp2 treats implementation/expansion as warranted now.

### Framework candidates

**fw1 — harm reduction / respect**  
`Establish supervised consumption because preventing immediate overdose death and creating voluntary pathways to care are worthwhile benefits even when drug use continues.`

**fw2 — learning / community proportionality**  
`Use a time-limited evaluated pilot before permanent implementation because direct client benefits must be weighed alongside uncertain population-level and local community effects.`

### Judgment

**Provisional profile: `featured-core-2x2x2-v1` by shape only; Full Corpus collection.** Survives candidate audit and is the strongest Batch-J symmetric case. Independent review should verify that pub2 remains a community-evidence translation rather than a disguised abstinence-policy candidate, and that geographical heterogeneity is transparent in provenance.

## M098 — Ventilator triage during catastrophic scarcity

A nationally representative US discrete-choice experiment (n=2,000) found survival probability was the most important attribute for allocation of the last ICU bed. Other US/UK work similarly found strong preference to allocate to the patient with meaningfully higher survival probability and to use chance when survival prospects are equal. This does **not** support two public candidates “maximize survival” and “lottery.” It supports one nested public rule: use prognosis when it meaningfully distinguishes patients; use lottery when it does not.

Cross-national experimental evidence also finds lower public support for lotteries than expert-committee allocation overall, with substantial country variation. That variation is important but does not justify a universal public lottery candidate.

Professional crisis frameworks likewise commonly combine clinical benefit with nondiscrimination/equity constraints and chance among close cases. Splitting these components would recreate the integrated-policy error.

**Judgment: `needs-additional-evidence`; do not execute.** Keep M098 as a priority RE-Iteration case because the represented clinical/equity criteria can be challenged and revised even without forcing a 2×2×2 source profile.

## M099 — Public reimbursement of a very high-cost therapy

The public evidence strongly rejects **rarity alone** as a sufficient reason for special funding. A representative UK study of 3,950 adults found 54% favored equal allocation between rare/common disease at equal cost, 32% favored rare disease, and support for rare disease fell when its treatment became much more expensive. Systematic reviews find severity, magnitude of benefit and lack of alternatives matter more than rarity itself.

This can support an ordinary opportunity-cost orientation and a severity-sensitive orientation, but it does not cleanly support the deep file's three policies as separate public rules.

At the institutional layer, NICE's ordinary appraisal/severity modifier and Highly Specialised Technologies pathway are genuine policy architectures. CMS Coverage with Evidence Development is also real, but it addresses **evidence uncertainty** rather than the same distributive question as a severity premium. Treating NICE-HST and CMS-CED as two expert answers to one dimension would mix axes.

**Judgment: `needs-additional-evidence`; do not execute.** A later version could isolate either (a) severity premium versus ordinary threshold with matched public evidence, or (b) immediate versus evidence-development coverage under uncertainty, but the present case deliberately contains both cost and durability uncertainty and should not conflate them.

## M100 — Routine ICU admission under ordinary scarcity

Professional guidance supports medically relevant criteria including urgency, likelihood and duration of benefit, fair process and reassessment, while rejecting social worth. These are components of a multiprinciple ordinary-allocation architecture.

Most direct public evidence retrieved concerns pandemic/disaster triage. Importing catastrophic-scarcity public preferences into an ordinary winter-occupancy case would violate the Bench's contextual-fidelity rule.

**Judgment: `needs-additional-evidence`; do not execute.** Seek ordinary-care ICU patient/public priority evidence if available; otherwise retain as a complete non-executable allocation case.
