# M100 — Routine ICU admission under ordinary scarcity

**Case-family identity:** M100  
**Primary domain:** clinical allocation / intensive care  
**Tags:** ICU beds, ordinary scarcity, urgency, benefit, time-limited trial, lottery  
**Research status:** deep case draft  
**Evidence date:** 2026-08-27

## Decision architecture

**Decision-maker:** hospital ICU triage service under ordinary high occupancy, not disaster crisis standards.  
**Decision question:** When only one staffed ICU bed is immediately available for several patients who could benefit, how should ordinary-care triage prioritize admission?

## Scenario pair

**Concise:** A hospital has one staffed ICU bed and three patients who may need it. One is most critically ill and needs immediate organ support but has a moderate chance of recovery; one is less unstable but has a higher predicted probability of benefit from ICU care; the third has substantial uncertainty and might improve with a short ICU trial followed by reassessment. No disaster declaration is in effect, and transfer options have been attempted. The hospital must decide whether urgency should dominate, expected benefit should dominate, or patients with uncertain prognosis should receive time-limited trials and reassessment with lottery among otherwise similarly prioritized candidates.

**Detailed:** A tertiary hospital is operating at sustained high ICU occupancy during ordinary winter demand. After transfer attempts and creation of surge capacity, one staffed bed remains available while three adults meet reasonable ICU admission criteria. Patient A is physiologically the most unstable and could deteriorate rapidly without intensive support, but has a moderate probability of recovery. Patient B is somewhat less immediately unstable but has a higher estimated likelihood of short-term recovery if admitted. Patient C has uncertain reversibility; a 24-hour intensive-care trial could generate useful prognostic information. None is categorically excluded because of disability, age alone, social worth or socioeconomic status. The hospital needs a standing triage rule for ordinary scarcity: prioritize urgency/most immediate need; prioritize expected near-term benefit; or use time-limited trials/reassessment for uncertain cases and a lottery when ethically relevant clinical criteria do not distinguish remaining candidates.

## Evidence architecture

AMA Opinion 11.1.3 explicitly applies allocation ethics to chronically limited resources including ICU beds and recommends medically relevant criteria such as urgency, likelihood and anticipated duration of benefit while rejecting social worth. This is a multiprinciple professional architecture rather than a mandate for one simple score. Ordinary ICU triage literature also emphasizes reassessment and time-limited trials where prognosis is uncertain.

Direct patient/public evidence on routine ICU allocation priorities is less developed than pandemic triage research and requires focused review before execution.

## Frameworks

- **Urgency/worst-off:** scarce critical care should first rescue the person at greatest immediate risk when benefit remains meaningful.
- **Benefit maximization:** admitting a patient more likely to recover uses a scarce bed more effectively and can increase total benefit.
- **Fair uncertainty management:** uncertain prognosis should not become automatic exclusion → use time-limited trials and reassessment, with lottery for clinically similar cases.

## Construction risks

Keep ordinary scarcity distinct from M098 catastrophe. Do not use age, disability or social worth as categorical exclusions. Distinguish urgency from expected benefit. Make transfer/surge attempts explicit so scarcity is genuine.

## Executable judgment

`needs-additional-evidence`. Professional guidance is multiprinciple but direct affected/public policy evidence for ordinary ICU triage is insufficient.
