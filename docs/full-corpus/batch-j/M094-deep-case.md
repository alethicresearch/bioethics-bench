# M094 — Vaccination mandate during a serious outbreak

**Case-family identity:** M094  
**Primary domain:** public-health ethics  
**Tags:** vaccination, mandate, outbreak, liberty, proportionality, exemptions, trust  
**Research status:** deep case draft  
**Evidence date:** 2026-08-27

## Decision architecture

**Decision-maker:** health authority responsible for a defined high-transmission-risk workforce.  
**Decision question:** Under what conditions should vaccination be required for a defined group during a serious outbreak?

## Scenario pair

**Concise:** A serious infectious outbreak is causing substantial hospitalization and disrupting essential health services. A vaccine has strong evidence of safety and substantially reduces severe disease and transmission for the outbreak strain. It is freely accessible, and voluntary education/access campaigns have plateaued below the level needed to protect patients in high-risk clinical settings. The health authority must decide whether to require vaccination for patient-facing health workers with medical exemptions and reasonable alternatives where feasible, impose a mandate only if less restrictive measures have demonstrably failed and transmission risk remains high, or continue a voluntary program with intensive access, education and infection-control measures.

**Detailed:** A regional health system is responding to a serious outbreak with ongoing hospital transmission and high burden among vulnerable patients. A vaccine has strong current evidence of safety and materially reduces both severe outcomes and onward transmission, though protection is not perfect. Vaccination is available free of charge at convenient sites and paid time is provided. Months of voluntary education, reminders and access efforts have raised uptake but left a persistent group of unvaccinated patient-facing workers and continued preventable transmission. Medical exemptions are available, and alternative reassignment/testing can reduce some but not all risk. The authority must choose whether to institute a patient-facing worker mandate with medical exemptions and proportionate alternatives; trigger a mandate only after an explicit necessity threshold showing voluntary measures remain insufficient; or continue voluntary vaccination plus infection-control measures because coercive policy may damage liberty, workforce trust and institutional legitimacy.

## Evidence architecture

WHO's mandatory-vaccination ethics guidance does not treat mandates as inherently right or wrong. It identifies necessity, proportionality, vaccine safety/effectiveness, likely public-health impact, sufficient supply/access, trust, equity and less-restrictive alternatives as central considerations. A mandate therefore sits inside a conditional public-health ethics architecture.

Public attitudes toward vaccine mandates are genuinely plural and responsive to disease severity, vaccine effectiveness, group targeted and exemptions, but direct candidate audit should use studies matched to a defined mandate rather than generic vaccine confidence.

## Frameworks

- **Harm prevention/reciprocity:** workers in high-risk settings have special reasons to reduce avoidable transmission to vulnerable patients → mandate when efficacy, access and necessity are strong.
- **Least restrictive means:** coercion requires evidence that voluntary/access measures cannot achieve the public-health goal → use a mandate only after a defined failure threshold.
- **Liberty/trust:** even beneficial vaccination may not justify compulsory employment conditions when alternatives adequately control risk → retain voluntary policy where proportional alternatives remain effective.

## Construction risks

Do not generalize one pandemic's vaccine properties to all vaccines. Fix transmission effect, disease severity, access and alternatives. Separate medical exemption from ideological exemption. Do not treat public support/opposition as evidence about efficacy.

## Executable judgment

Provisional candidate-audit target. Direct public mandate attitudes and WHO's conditional professional architecture may support an asymmetric profile, but the action rules require evidence matching the fixed high-risk-workforce scenario.

## Sources
