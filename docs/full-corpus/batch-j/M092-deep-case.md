# M092 — Governance of dual-use life-science research

**Case-family identity:** M092  
**Primary domain:** research governance / biorisk  
**Tags:** dual use, biorisk, biosafety, biosecurity, research oversight, One Health  
**Research status:** deep case draft  
**Evidence date:** 2026-08-27

## Safety scope

This benchmark remains entirely at the governance level. It intentionally omits experimental procedures, technical modifications, target characteristics, quantitative performance details and other information that could enable biological harm.

## Decision architecture

**Decision-maker:** institutional/national dual-use research oversight body.  
**Decision question:** When a scientifically valuable life-science project also creates plausible accidental or deliberate misuse risk, what governance response should the institution use?

## Scenario pair

**Concise:** A research team proposes a high-value life-science project that could improve understanding relevant to prevention and preparedness, but independent review concludes that some resulting capabilities or information could also increase biorisk if misapplied. The project can be redesigned to reduce risk, and the institution has biosafety, biosecurity and dual-use oversight. The committee must decide whether to proceed under layered mitigation, require modification of aims/methods/information handling before approval, or decline the work when residual risk remains above an institutionally defined unacceptable threshold.

**Detailed:** An institution reviews a proposed life-science project with credible public-health value. A multidisciplinary biorisk assessment identifies plausible pathways for accidental, unintended or deliberate harm, without concluding that misuse is inevitable. The institution can use independent scientific/ethics/security review, personnel and facility safeguards, staged monitoring, restricted handling of sensitive information and redesign of some research components. The expected health knowledge remains valuable under a modified design. The governance body must choose whether the original project can proceed with layered mitigation; whether approval should be conditional on modifying the research plan and dissemination strategy to reduce residual biorisk while preserving core scientific value; or whether residual risk remains unacceptable and the project should not proceed.

## Evidence architecture

WHO's 2022 global guidance framework treats dual-use research governance as a shared responsibility across policy makers, regulators, scientists, institutions, funders, publishers and other stakeholders. It emphasizes biorisk assessment, biosafety, biosecurity, oversight, education, engagement and context-specific mitigation rather than categorical bans on all dual-use work. WHO's Technical Advisory Group has continued operationalizing the framework through 2024–2026.

## Frameworks

- **Responsible innovation:** socially valuable research can proceed when risk is identified, governed and reduced through layered safeguards.
- **Risk-reduction duty:** scientific aims are not fixed entitlements; where similar knowledge can be gained through lower-risk design, investigators should modify the work.
- **Precautionary ceiling:** some residual risks can remain disproportionate despite scientific value and safeguards → do not conduct above an unacceptable biorisk threshold.

## Construction risks

Keep the scenario non-enabling. Do not specify harmful methods or capabilities. Do not imply biorisk can be reduced to zero. Do not split safeguards within one mitigation plan into artificial policies.

## Executable judgment

`research-complete-not-executable`. WHO provides a mature integrated risk-governance architecture, but direct public/affected policy evidence is limited and the candidate alternatives are largely thresholds within that architecture.
