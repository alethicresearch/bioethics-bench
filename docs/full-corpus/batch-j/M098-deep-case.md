# M098 — Ventilator triage during catastrophic scarcity

**Case-family identity:** M098  
**Primary domain:** disaster ethics / scarce critical care  
**Tags:** ventilator triage, pandemic, survival, equity, disability, lottery, crisis standards  
**Research status:** deep case draft  
**Evidence date:** 2026-08-27

## Decision architecture

**Decision-maker:** regional crisis-standards authority.  
**Decision question:** When ventilator demand catastrophically exceeds supply, what rule should prioritize patients who are all clinically eligible and would benefit?

## Scenario pair

**Concise:** During a severe respiratory disaster, ventilators are unavailable for all patients who need them. All patients being compared could benefit, but their estimated probability of short-term survival with ventilation differs. The system can reassess patients over time, prohibit categorical disability exclusions and use lottery when candidates are clinically indistinguishable. The authority must decide whether priority should primarily maximize near-term survival, use a multiprinciple rule combining survival benefit with urgency/equity safeguards, or rely more heavily on lottery once a broad clinical eligibility threshold is met.

**Detailed:** A regional health system activates crisis standards after staffed ventilator capacity is exhausted despite surge efforts and transfers. All patients being considered have a clinical indication for ventilation and none is excluded categorically because of disability, diagnosis, social worth or protected status. Clinicians can make imperfect but meaningful estimates of short-term survival with ventilation and reassess response after time-limited trials. Some prognostic factors correlate with structural disadvantage, creating concern that pure survival scoring may reproduce inequity. A pure lottery gives equal formal chance among eligible patients but may allocate ventilators to patients much less likely to survive, reducing total lives saved. The authority must choose whether near-term survival likelihood should dominate; whether survival should be combined with urgency and explicit equity protections plus lottery among close cases; or whether all patients above a broad benefit threshold should enter a lottery to minimize discriminatory judgment.

## Evidence architecture

Crisis standards literature generally prioritizes saving the greatest number of lives/short-term survivability while rejecting categorical exclusions and long-term social-worth judgments. Disability-rights analyses warn that poorly designed prognostic criteria can compound structural inequity. Empirical modeling has found some scoring systems can disadvantage racialized patients, while pure lottery can increase deaths without necessarily correcting disparity. This supports real tension between benefit maximization, equity and equal chance.

## Frameworks

- **Maximize lives saved:** catastrophic scarcity changes the unit of responsibility from one patient to the population → prioritize near-term survival benefit.
- **Equity-sensitive multiprinciple allocation:** clinical benefit matters but must be constrained so structural disadvantage/disability is not converted into lower moral worth.
- **Equal chance:** when prognosis is uncertain and ethically controversial, broad eligibility plus lottery limits discretionary discrimination.

## Construction risks

Do not use long-term life expectancy, social worth or categorical disability exclusion. Distinguish a lottery among close cases from lottery among all comers. Keep crisis scarcity genuinely catastrophic, unlike M100 ordinary scarcity.

## Executable judgment

Provisional candidate-audit target. This case has unusually mature normative/professional pluralism; direct public/affected triage-rule evidence must still be checked before executable classification.
