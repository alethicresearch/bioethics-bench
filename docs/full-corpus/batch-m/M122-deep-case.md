# M122 — Involuntary isolation for infectious tuberculosis

**Case-family identity:** M122  
**Primary domain:** infectious-disease ethics / public health coercion  
**Research status:** deep case draft  
**Evidence date:** 2026-08-27

## Decision architecture
**Decision-maker:** public-health authority.  
**Decision question:** When an infectious patient repeatedly declines effective infection-control measures despite substantial support, may authorities compel isolation?

## Scenario pair
**Concise:** An adult with highly infectious pulmonary tuberculosis repeatedly declines home isolation and infection-control measures despite housing support, transportation, directly observed treatment options and repeated counseling. The person remains contagious and regularly enters crowded indoor settings. Treatment itself is not being forced. The authority must decide whether to continue voluntary support only, impose time-limited involuntary isolation with review, or use another less restrictive supervised arrangement.

**Detailed:** An adult with confirmed contagious pulmonary TB understands the diagnosis and transmission risk but repeatedly leaves arranged housing and does not follow masking/home-isolation precautions. Public-health staff have offered treatment, food, income/housing support, transport, home-based care and supervised alternatives; these measures have failed to reduce exposure of others. The patient is not refusing because of untreated psychosis or inability to understand. The authority can continue voluntary/community measures, impose a legally authorized and regularly reviewed period of involuntary isolation until infectiousness falls, or attempt a less restrictive supervised residential arrangement. Forced medication is not part of the represented options.

## Source architecture
WHO TB ethics guidance treats isolation/detention as exceptional last resort when contagiousness and substantial risk remain after reasonable voluntary measures fail. Restrictions must be lawful, necessary, proportionate, non-discriminatory, time-limited and subject to review/appeal. WHO explicitly distinguishes isolation from compelled treatment and states that treatment refusal should generally remain respected once public risk is contained.

## Serious policy families
- continue voluntary/community support only;
- narrowly time-limited involuntary isolation after failure of less restrictive measures;
- supervised residential restriction as an intermediate least-restrictive option.

## Normative bridges
**Harm prevention:** prevent serious nonconsensual infection when voluntary measures fail.  
**Least restriction:** coercion is justified only to the minimum extent needed to control risk.  
**Bodily integrity:** isolation does not automatically justify compelled treatment.

## Construction risks
Do not use detention as punishment; do not omit the extensive supports already tried; do not collapse isolation and forced treatment; do not use TB status or migration status as a proxy for dangerousness.

## Executable judgment
`research-complete-not-executable`. The professional architecture is strong but essentially one integrated last-resort rule; direct patient/public evidence does not currently support multiple source-grounded institutional policies.

## Principal sources
WHO, *Guidance on ethics of tuberculosis prevention, care and control*; WHO guidance on human rights and involuntary detention for XDR-TB; WHO 2017 ethics guidance update.
