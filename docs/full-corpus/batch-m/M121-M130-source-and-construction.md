# Bioethics Bench — Batch M source and construction packet (M121–M130)

**Evidence date:** 2026-08-27  
**Scope:** genomic benefit sharing, infectious-disease surveillance/coercion, disability measurement, and mental-health coercion.

The strict Full Corpus source-to-policy rule governs this batch. General concern or support is not translated into institutional policy unless the source already supports the action/direction.

## M121 — Benefit sharing for international genomic data
WHO 2024 human-genome-data guidance and GA4GH both combine data sharing with privacy, equity, community respect, reciprocity and fair distribution of benefits. Community/national benefit-sharing models exist, but direct affected-community comparisons between open science, negotiated benefit sharing and community-controlled licensing remain heterogeneous. Likely `needs-additional-evidence`.

## M122 — Involuntary isolation for infectious tuberculosis
WHO TB ethics guidance treats detention/isolation as an exceptional last resort after voluntary supports and less restrictive alternatives fail, with lawfulness, necessity, proportionality, limited duration, review and appeal; forced treatment is not justified merely because isolation is justified. This is one integrated professional rule. Likely research-complete but non-executable absent direct patient/public policy pluralism.

## M123 — Wastewater surveillance without individual consent
National Academies 2023 concludes community-level wastewater surveillance can be ethically acceptable without individual consent if risks are minimized and population benefit exists, while recommending transparency, community input, ethics governance, limits on granularity/use and protections against surveillance creep. 2023 US public survey found high support for disease/toxin surveillance but sharply lower support for lifestyle/mental-health uses and smaller geographic scales. Strong candidate-audit target because the target and resolution can produce genuinely different public rules.

## M124 — Cross-border sharing of pathogen genomic data
WHO 2022/2025 guidance favors timely pathogen-genome sharing with equity, capacity development and benefit sharing. The 2026 Pandemic Agreement PABS annex remains under negotiation and explicitly seeks rapid sharing on equal footing with fair/equitable benefits. Professional/governance debate is about conditions/mechanism rather than whether withholding is acceptable. Likely research-complete/non-executable or needs evidence.

## M125 — HIV confidentiality and partner notification
WHO recommends voluntary assisted partner notification services. Contemporary policy strongly distinguishes supportive/provider-assisted notification from punitive disclosure; any direct warning must be governed by law, actual transmission risk, confidentiality and safety. Direct affected evidence exists but may not support competing institutional rules. Likely needs evidence.

## M126 — Mandatory testing/reporting for high-consequence infection
Testing and named reporting must be separated. Public-health ethics supports reporting/compulsory measures only when necessary, lawful and proportionate. Professional guidance generally converges on voluntary testing except narrowly justified exposure/high-risk settings and routine reporting for specified notifiable diseases. Likely non-executable under strict rule.

## M127 — Involuntary psychiatric treatment
WHO/OHCHR 2023 explicitly calls for elimination of coercive practices and free/informed consent as the basis of mental-health intervention. Existing laws/clinical practice still contain harm/capacity/necessity thresholds, but those are legal architectures rather than plural WHO recommendations. Preserve as contested domain without manufacturing expert symmetry.

## M128 — Compulsory feeding in severe anorexia nervosa
Life-threatening anorexia creates genuine capacity and rescue conflicts. Professional guidance supports careful decision-specific capacity assessment, least-restrictive treatment and use of compulsory treatment in some life-threatening circumstances, while patient perspectives document harms from coercion and value of compassionate relational care. Candidate audit should test whether patient evidence supports action-distinct policies rather than simply better process.

## M129 — Disability weights and QALYs
NICE explicitly uses QALYs but permits severity modifiers and deliberative modifiers beyond QALYs. ICER supplements QALYs with equal-value life years to address concerns that life extension for disabled people can be undervalued. Disability critiques argue conventional quality weights may encode preferences about living with disability. This is a strong measurement-governance case and likely candidate-audit target.

## M130 — Seclusion and restraint in psychiatric crisis
WHO/OHCHR calls for elimination of seclusion/restraint and coercion. Some clinical/legal regimes still permit narrow emergency physical intervention for immediate danger. Service-user evidence documents trauma and preference for de-escalation. Under strict source-class rules, this may remain non-executable unless direct affected policy evidence supports more than one institutional rule.
