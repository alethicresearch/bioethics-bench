# M121 — Benefit sharing for international genomic data

**Case-family identity:** M121  
**Primary domain:** global genomics / data governance  
**Research status:** deep case draft  
**Evidence date:** 2026-08-27

## Decision architecture
**Decision-maker:** international genomics consortium and contributing national/community partners.  
**Decision question:** What benefits or governance rights should communities and countries contributing genomic data receive when those data enable commercial or global research?

## Scenario pair
**Concise:** A multinational genomics consortium has assembled a high-value dataset using samples and health information contributed by communities in several lower-resource countries. Commercial and academic teams can use the dataset to develop diagnostics and therapies, but most resulting products may initially be unaffordable in contributor countries. Contributors gave valid research consent and the consortium can choose its access and benefit-sharing rules. It must decide whether data should be broadly shared with general scientific benefits, shared only under negotiated benefit-sharing obligations, or governed through stronger community/country control over access and downstream commercial use.

**Detailed:** A multinational consortium holds genomic and linked health data collected through long-term partnerships with communities and hospitals in lower-resource countries. The resource has become scientifically valuable and is attracting commercial users. The original consent permitted broad health research, but did not settle how downstream commercial value should be distributed. Contributor communities face under-resourced health systems and have asked for local analytic capacity, affordable access to resulting products, acknowledgement, and continuing participation in governance. The consortium must decide whether broad responsible data sharing and general scientific benefits are sufficient; whether access should require negotiated obligations such as capacity building, affordable product access, training, authorship/attribution and local infrastructure; or whether contributor communities/countries should retain stronger licensing or access-control authority over secondary and commercial uses.

## Critical facts and uncertainty
- valid research consent exists but downstream benefit terms were not fully specified;
- commercial value is plausible but uncertain;
- contributor communities face structural access gaps;
- data sharing itself can create scientific/public benefit;
- community and national interests are distinct from individual consent.

## Source architecture
WHO's 2024 genome-data guidance emphasizes ethical, legal and equitable sharing, transparency, individual and collective rights, and fair access to benefits. GA4GH similarly combines responsible sharing with privacy, non-discrimination, procedural fairness, reciprocity and fair distribution of benefits. These sources support benefit-sharing governance but do not establish one universal allocation formula.

## Serious policy families
- broad responsible sharing with general/scientific benefits;
- negotiated access conditions requiring concrete benefit sharing and capacity building;
- stronger contributor-community/country governance over secondary or commercial access.

## Normative bridges
**Open science:** wide data access can accelerate health gains → avoid restrictive control that fragments knowledge.  
**Reciprocity/justice:** contributors bear burdens and generate value → downstream users owe concrete benefits and capacity.  
**Collective self-determination:** data can implicate populations and national resources → contributors may retain governance authority over secondary/commercial use.

## Construction risks
Do not treat individual consent as settling collective benefit claims; do not equate benefit sharing with cash payment; do not turn general equity principles into a specific licensing rule without source support.

## Executable judgment
`needs-additional-evidence`. The governance positions are strong, but direct affected-community evidence comparing these institutional rules is not yet sufficiently clean for executable candidate pools.

## Principal sources
WHO, *Guidance for human genome data collection, access, use and sharing* (2024); GA4GH, *Framework for Responsible Sharing of Genomic and Health-Related Data*; international genomic-governance and benefit-sharing literature.
