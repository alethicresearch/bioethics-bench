# Batch M candidate audit — M123 and M129

## M123 — Wastewater surveillance

### Public candidates
**pub1 — routine citywide public-health monitoring**  
`Use wastewater surveillance at city/community scale for infectious diseases and other clearly health-protective targets when results are useful for public-health action.`

**Support:** US national surveys directly found ~91–92% support for disease/toxin monitoring and ~76–85% support for citywide surveillance. This is direct support for the core action, not an editorial conversion from a generic privacy value.

**pub2 — restrict sensitive/small-area surveillance**  
`Do not routinely use wastewater surveillance for low-support lifestyle or mental-health targets or highly localized catchments; require a separate public-health justification and community-governance process before such monitoring.`

**Support:** surveys directly asked about targets and geographic scale: support was substantially lower for mental illness, healthy eating/lifestyle behaviors and small/local sites, with respondents identifying locations they would prohibit. **Bench-authored policy completion:** the separate-governance requirement operationalizes the directly supported restriction orientation using the National Academies governance recommendation.

### Expert candidate
**exp1 — governed proportional surveillance**  
`Permit community-level wastewater surveillance without individual consent when population benefit is established, while limiting resolution and secondary uses, protecting privacy, and using transparent ethics/community governance for expansion.`

**Support:** National Academies 2023.

### Framework candidates
**fw1 — public-health proportionality:** authorize nonconsensual surveillance when collective benefit is substantial and privacy burdens are minimized.  
**fw2 — privacy/anti-surveillance creep:** restrict targets, geographic resolution and secondary uses because nonconsensual health surveillance requires especially strong purpose limitation.

### Judgment
**Provisional `full-corpus-2x1x2-mean-v1`.** Survives strict source-to-policy audit.

## M129 — Disability weights and QALYs

The institutional measurement architectures are genuinely distinct (standard QALY, severity/modifier approaches, QALY + equal-value life-year reporting), but current Batch-M evidence does not contain sufficiently direct **affected disability-community policy evidence** supporting two candidate rules at this level.

Disability critiques establish the concern that health-state weights can undervalue lives lived with disability. They do not, in the present packet, directly choose among the represented HTA architectures. Promoting the critique itself into a specific replacement formula would violate the strict source-to-policy rule.

### Judgment
**`needs-additional-evidence`; do not execute yet.** This remains one of the most important measurement cases in the corpus and should be revisited with direct disability-community/HTA deliberative evidence.
