# M126 — Mandatory testing and reporting for a high-consequence infection

**Case-family identity:** M126  
**Primary domain:** public-health ethics / infectious-disease governance  
**Research status:** deep case draft  
**Evidence date:** 2026-08-27

## Decision architecture
**Decision-maker:** public-health authority.  
**Decision question:** When may authorities require diagnostic testing or named reporting rather than rely on voluntary testing?

## Scenario pair
**Concise:** During an outbreak of a serious communicable infection, public-health officials can offer confidential voluntary testing, require laboratories to report confirmed cases by name, and in narrowly defined high-risk exposure settings can seek compulsory testing orders. The authority must decide what combination of voluntary testing, mandatory reporting and exceptional compulsory testing is ethically justified.

**Detailed:** A serious communicable infection causes substantial morbidity and can spread before symptoms are obvious. Testing is accurate and treatment/isolation can reduce transmission. Voluntary testing is widely available, but some high-risk exposures remain unresolved because individuals decline testing. Named reporting of confirmed cases would support contact tracing and outbreak control but reduces informational privacy. The authority must choose a policy that distinguishes ordinary voluntary testing from mandatory reporting of confirmed cases and from exceptional compulsory testing after a defined high-risk exposure when less restrictive measures fail.

## Source architecture
Public-health ethics frameworks consistently require legality, necessity, effectiveness, proportionality and the least restrictive means when coercive measures are considered. Reporting and testing are ethically distinct: routine reporting of specified confirmed infections can be a standard surveillance obligation, whereas compelled diagnostic testing requires a much higher justification threshold.

## Serious policy families
- voluntary confidential testing plus public-health education/support;
- mandatory named reporting of confirmed cases while keeping testing voluntary;
- compulsory testing only in narrowly defined high-risk exposure circumstances with review.

## Normative bridges
**Least restriction:** use information/reporting tools before bodily coercion.  
**Reciprocity:** if the state imposes testing/reporting burdens, it owes accessible care, confidentiality safeguards and anti-discrimination protections.  
**Harm prevention:** limited coercion may be justified where untested exposure creates serious preventable risk to others.

## Construction risks
Do not merge mandatory reporting with mandatory testing; do not assume all high-consequence diseases warrant the same rule; do not omit availability of treatment and confidentiality protections.

## Executable judgment
`research-complete-not-executable`. The source architecture is largely one graduated necessity/proportionality rule rather than competing professional policies.

WHO infectious-disease outbreak ethics guidance; WHO contact-tracing/testing ethics materials; public-health law/ethics literature on notifiable disease reporting and compulsory testing.
