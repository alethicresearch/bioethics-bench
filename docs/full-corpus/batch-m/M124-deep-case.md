# M124 — Cross-border sharing of pathogen genomic surveillance data

**Case-family identity:** M124  
**Primary domain:** infectious-disease governance / global data sharing  
**Research status:** deep case draft  
**Evidence date:** 2026-08-27

## Decision architecture
**Decision-maker:** national public-health laboratory/ministry participating in an emerging-outbreak surveillance network.  
**Decision question:** During an emerging outbreak, how rapidly and under what conditions should pathogen genomic data be shared internationally?

## Scenario pair
**Concise:** A national laboratory identifies a new pathogen variant with possible pandemic significance. Rapid international sequence sharing could speed diagnostics and vaccines, but the country has previously received little credit, manufacturing access or benefit from samples it shared. The ministry must decide whether to release validated sequence data immediately on an open platform, share rapidly through a governed system that guarantees attribution and benefit-sharing obligations, or delay release for short national review and validation before international access.

**Detailed:** A lower-resource country's national laboratory detects a pathogen variant associated with unusual spread. The sequence and metadata are good enough to be useful but some epidemiologic details remain under validation. Previous pathogen sharing by the country led to global product development without timely local access. WHO guidance encourages timely, equitable pathogen genome sharing; in 2026 Member States are still negotiating the Pandemic Agreement's Pathogen Access and Benefit Sharing annex, whose stated goal is rapid sharing on equal footing with fair/equitable access to resulting diagnostics, therapeutics and vaccines. The ministry must decide whether immediate open release best protects global health, whether rapid sharing should occur only through a system with attribution/capacity/benefit protections, or whether a short validation/national-review period is justified before release.

## Source architecture
WHO's 2022 pathogen-genome sharing principles and 2025 platform principles emphasize timely sharing, quality, ethics, equity, capacity development and fair benefits. WHO's 2026 PABS negotiations explicitly link rapid access to pathogens/genetic sequence information with equitable sharing of benefits. This is not evidence that prolonged strategic withholding is a recommended public-health policy.

## Serious policy families
- immediate/open validated sequence sharing;
- rapid governed sharing with benefit/attribution protections;
- short staged release after validation and national review.

## Normative bridges
**Reciprocal global health:** rapid sharing prevents transboundary harm, while reciprocity requires contributors not be excluded from benefits.  
**Scientific integrity:** premature release can spread erroneous data → limited validation can be justified.  
**Justice/anti-exploitation:** countries should not have to choose between global solidarity and access to products their data enabled.

## Construction risks
Do not confuse equitable sharing with indefinite withholding; do not imply the 2026 PABS annex is finalized; do not treat attribution and product access as interchangeable benefits.

## Executable judgment
`needs-additional-evidence`. The professional/governance sources support an integrated rapid-and-equitable architecture rather than clearly distinct expert policies, and direct affected-public evidence is limited.

## Principal sources
WHO pathogen genome data-sharing principles (2022); WHO attributes/principles for pathogen genomic platforms (2025); WHO PABS negotiation updates February and July 2026.
