# Full Corpus construction-standard decision

**Decision date:** 2026-08-27  
**Applies to:** Bioethics Bench Full Corpus v1 (`benchmark` collection)  
**Does not modify:** released Featured v1 records

## Decision

The Full Corpus will use the stricter **source-to-policy construction standard** developed during the M001–M100 research pass.

For public and expert candidates, the source evidence must already support the candidate's **core action, direction or policy orientation**. Bench authoring may add a narrow operational clause that makes an already-supported orientation executable, but it may not create the orientation itself.

Permitted completion includes implementation detail such as a safeguard, continuity requirement, documentation step, review procedure or non-abandonment clause when removing that clause would leave the same underlying source-supported action intact.

The Full Corpus will not:

- convert general approval, concern, value preference, treatment-risk tolerance or personal acceptability into an institutional rule that the source did not support;
- combine separate empirical strands into a new composite rule and attribute that composite to the source pool;
- invent an opposing position to fill a candidate pool;
- split conditions nested within one professional recommendation into separate candidates;
- substitute institutional, philosophical or expert disagreement for missing affected-community disagreement.

When narrow Bench-authored completion is used, `provenance.summary` begins with:

`Bench-authored policy completion:`

No new provenance ontology or schema field is introduced.

## Featured v1 is a frozen legacy construction profile

Featured v1 was built under an earlier, explicitly documented convention that allowed **declared editorial translation** of affected-community evidence into actionable institutional rules. Its candidate-level provenance is honest about those translations. The records are released and frozen and will not be re-derived merely to satisfy the later Full Corpus rule.

The new greppable `Bench-authored policy completion:` prefix therefore applies prospectively to Full Corpus authoring. Featured v1 prose is not rewritten merely to add the prefix because that would alter released records and hashes without changing substantive provenance.

## Comparability consequence

Featured v1 executable yield and Full Corpus executable yield are **not directly comparable** because the construction standards differ. Any paper, report or resource documentation discussing both must say so.

In particular, the 20/20 executable Featured selection must not be treated as the first twenty observations in a single executable-yield series whose later value is the Full Corpus candidate-executable rate.

## E0 consequence

E0 is a valid execution of the released F11 record. F11 is an M098-origin Featured record built under the earlier Featured construction standard, and its candidate provenance declares editorial translation where used. The 24/24 QCCS run therefore measures that released represented object faithfully.

E0 must **not** be described as validation of the later Full Corpus source-to-policy construction standard, nor should F11 be presented as though it were authored under that standard.

This does not weaken the E0 calibration conclusion that the executable QCCS/SACRE pipeline ran end-to-end successfully.

## Why the stricter standard governs the Full Corpus

The Full Corpus is intended to function as a reusable source-grounded research resource, not merely as a curated demonstration set. At that scale, preserving source-class meaning is more important than maximizing executable yield. A lower executable yield is acceptable when it reflects genuine convergence, evidence gaps or lack of directly supportable policy alternatives.

Cases that are research-complete but non-executable remain first-class corpus content.

## Reassessment

Reassess this rule at the n=200 corpus review only if repeated source classes expose a concrete defect. Do not loosen it batch-by-batch to increase executable yield.
