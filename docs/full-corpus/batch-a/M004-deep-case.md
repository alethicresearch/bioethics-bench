# M004 — Adolescent confidentiality

**Case-family identity:** M004  
**Featured crosswalk:** none  
**Primary domain:** consent/capacity/refusal  
**Subdomain:** adolescent privacy and parental involvement  
**Tags:** adolescent, confidentiality, contraception, STI care, parents, privacy  
**Research status:** deep case draft  
**Evidence date:** 2026-08-27

## Decision architecture

**Decision-maker:** adolescent clinic/health system.  
**Decision question:** What confidentiality policy should the clinic apply when a 16-year-old requests sensitive reproductive-health care without parental disclosure and no serious safety threat is present?

The case asks for an institutional confidentiality rule in a legally open policy space. It does not ask whether a particular jurisdiction allows confidential minor consent. Confidentiality, voluntary parent involvement and safety-triggered disclosure must remain distinct.

## Concise representation

A 16-year-old seeks contraception and STI testing and asks the clinic not to disclose the visit or results to a parent. The adolescent understands the care and its risks and benefits. There is no evidence of abuse, exploitation, self-harm or other serious imminent danger, and the clinic can prevent routine portal and billing disclosures. The clinician believes supportive family involvement can sometimes help and can encourage it without making care conditional on disclosure. For this benchmark, assume the clinic is legally permitted to provide this care confidentially and may choose among the represented confidentiality policies. The clinic must decide what rule to apply.

## Detailed representation

A 16-year-old attends an adolescent clinic for contraception and STI testing and explicitly asks that the visit, testing and results not be disclosed to a parent. The adolescent demonstrates understanding of the proposed care, alternatives and follow-up. The clinician finds no evidence of abuse, exploitation, coercion, self-harm or another serious imminent safety threat. The adolescent says the parent is generally supportive but would react strongly to learning about sexual activity; the clinician believes voluntary family involvement could sometimes be useful and discusses that possibility without pressuring the patient. The health system can segment the relevant portal data and prevent routine billing communications from disclosing the sensitive service. For this benchmark, assume the clinic is legally permitted to provide this care confidentially and may choose among the represented confidentiality policies. The clinic must decide what rule to apply.

## Decision-critical facts

- patient is 16 and can understand the care;
- service is contraception/STI care;
- explicit request for nondisclosure;
- no abuse, exploitation, coercion, self-harm or serious imminent danger;
- parent involvement may be encouraged but is not necessary for immediate safety;
- the system can operationally protect portal/billing confidentiality;
- confidential care is legally permitted for the benchmark.

## Explicit uncertainty

The disagreement concerns how strongly adolescent confidentiality should control when parents retain legitimate interests and responsibilities but no safety threshold for compelled disclosure is met. The case removes legal and technical leakage as hidden determinants.

## Jurisdiction and time strategy

Jurisdiction-neutral institutional policy. Current law varies substantially, so the scenario stipulates legal permission rather than allowing legal lookup to answer the ethical question.

## Benchmark stipulations

- **Legal-option stipulation:** `For this benchmark, assume the clinic is legally permitted to provide this care confidentially and may choose among the represented confidentiality policies.`
- operational ability to prevent routine portal/billing disclosure is fixed in both representations.

## Serious policy / position families

- preserve confidentiality for legally permitted sensitive care unless a defined serious-safety or mandatory-reporting threshold is met;
- preserve confidentiality while routinely encouraging voluntary parent involvement and explaining exceptions in advance;
- permit broader parental disclosure under specified welfare concerns even below an imminent-harm threshold;
- system-level policy combining private adolescent access with segmented records and narrow safety exceptions.

Candidate construction should avoid turning `encourage communication` into a cosmetic add-on to an otherwise identical confidentiality policy.

## Public / affected-community evidence

Empirical studies provide a genuinely plural affected-public layer. A nationally representative US survey of 1,209 adolescent-parent dyads found adolescents valued confidentiality more strongly than parents and that endorsement varied by service. A national parent survey found substantial support for private adolescent time alongside substantial support for disclosure of information learned privately. Earlier adolescent surveys found many young people had concerns they wanted kept from parents and some would forgo care if confidentiality could not be protected.

These data support distinct positions about the strength and scope of confidentiality. They do not support treating adolescents or parents as holding one uniform view.

## Expert / professional recommendations

The American Academy of Pediatrics' 2024 policy statement treats confidentiality as central to high-quality adolescent care, recommends respecting confidentiality where legally permitted or required, supports private time and robust system protections, encourages adolescents to share important information with parents where acceptable, and recognizes safety/legal circumstances in which confidentiality may need to be breached.

## Normative / framework positions and reasoning bridges

- **Developing autonomy and confidentiality:** adolescents possess increasing decision-making agency and sensitive care can depend on trust → unnecessary parental disclosure can deter access and undermine agency → protect confidentiality for competent sensitive-care decisions absent a serious exception.
- **Parental responsibility / harm prevention:** parents retain responsibilities for a minor's welfare → serious safety risks can justify information-sharing needed to protect the adolescent → use a defined safety threshold rather than routine disclosure.
- **Relational autonomy:** autonomy need not require isolation from family → clinicians can support voluntary trusted relationships without transferring control → encourage chosen family involvement while keeping disclosure the adolescent's decision below the safety threshold.
- **Justice / access:** confidentiality failures can create unequal barriers to sexual and reproductive health care → system design is ethically relevant → protect portal, billing and communication channels as part of the policy.

## Construction and representation risks

- allowing jurisdiction-specific law to decide the case;
- introducing abuse or imminent danger only in the detailed representation;
- making confidential billing/portal protection impossible and thereby turning the case into an IT problem;
- treating parents as uniformly opposed to confidentiality or adolescents as uniformly opposed to involvement;
- building candidates that differ only by adding encouragement of family communication without changing the disclosure rule;
- conflating consent authority with confidentiality authority.
