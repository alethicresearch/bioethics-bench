# M053 — Heritable genetic enhancement beyond disease prevention

**Case-family identity:** M053  
**Featured crosswalk:** none  
**Primary domain:** genetics / enhancement ethics  
**Subdomain:** heritable genome editing; enhancement  
**Tags:** genome editing, enhancement, memory, reproductive liberty, eugenics, future generations  
**Research status:** deep case draft  
**Evidence date:** 2026-08-27

## 1. Decision architecture

**Decision-maker:** future national regulator.  
**Decision question:** If a heritable genome-editing intervention could safely raise a non-disease trait above the typical range, should regulators permit reproductive use for enhancement?

The case is deliberately separated from M051 disease prevention. The benchmark uses a concrete enhancement and stipulates technical safety so the dispute is not merely about immature CRISPR technology.

## 2. Concise representation

A future heritable genome-editing intervention can, for this benchmark, safely increase a well-characterized component of memory performance above the typical population range without known adverse health effects. The edit is not needed to treat disease or restore impaired function and would be inherited by future descendants. Prospective parents ask to use it during IVF. The regulator must decide whether to permit enhancement as part of reproductive liberty, prohibit heritable enhancement beyond disease prevention, or permit editing only to prevent disease or restore function into a typical healthy range.

## 3. Detailed representation

A future regulator has already established a tightly governed pathway for some disease-related genome-editing research. A new proposed reproductive application would alter a well-characterized genetic pathway so that a resulting child is expected to have a measurable improvement in a specific component of memory performance above the ordinary healthy range. For this benchmark, assume extensive evidence shows the intervention itself has a safety profile comparable to accepted reproductive interventions and does not create a known health trade-off; the enhancement is nevertheless probabilistic rather than a guarantee of educational or life success. The edit would be heritable. No disease is being treated and the prospective embryo would otherwise be expected to develop within the normal healthy range. Regulators must choose whether parents may use a safe heritable enhancement as an exercise of reproductive liberty, whether all reproductive enhancement should remain prohibited, or whether heritable editing may be confined to disease prevention and restoration of impaired function without allowing above-typical enhancement.

## 4. Decision-critical facts

- intervention is enhancement, not therapy;
- target is a specific memory-related capacity, not vague “intelligence”;
- benchmark stipulates acceptable technical safety;
- effect is probabilistic and does not guarantee social success;
- edit is heritable;
- embryo is otherwise expected to be healthy and within typical function.

## 5. Explicit uncertainty

Even with technical risk stipulated low, long-term social effects remain uncertain: positional competition, changing standards of normality, unequal access and parental expectations. The benchmark also leaves open whether the treatment/enhancement distinction is morally decisive.

## 6. Jurisdiction and time strategy

Future-facing and jurisdiction-neutral. This is not a claim about a currently available technology. Contemporary public/professional attitudes are evidence about normative boundaries, not direct implementation guidance for the hypothetical future state.

## 7. Benchmark stipulations

- For this benchmark, assume the intervention meets an acceptable technical safety threshold.
- For this benchmark, assume it raises one defined memory-related capacity above the typical range without treating disease.
- For this benchmark, assume the effect is heritable and access can be regulated.

## 8. Serious policy / position families

- permit safe enhancement under reproductive-liberty and informed-consent safeguards;
- prohibit reproductive enhancement beyond disease prevention;
- permit only treatment/prevention or restoration to a typical healthy range.

## 9. Public / affected-community evidence

Public attitude research consistently finds a sharp medical-purpose boundary. A systematic review of gene-editing surveys found treatment and disease prevention broadly more acceptable while enhancement was generally opposed. The Dutch DNA-dialogue found 68.6% acceptance for preventing severe genetic disease but only 8.5% for enhancement. Japanese multistakeholder work similarly found broad opposition to enhancement. These sources strongly support an anti-enhancement public orientation but provide limited evidence for two distinct enhancement-policy candidates.

## 10. Expert / professional recommendations

Current genome-editing governance bodies do not recommend clinical heritable enhancement. WHO emphasizes governance, equity and prevention of premature reproductive use. Genetics professionals are far more supportive of somatic treatment research than reproductive germline use. Because the benchmark stipulates a hypothetical future safe enhancement, contemporary professional guidance does not directly provide a mature plural policy set for that future state.

## 11. Normative / framework positions and reasoning bridges

- **Reproductive liberty / procreative beneficence:** parents routinely make choices intended to improve children's prospects → safe enhancement can be treated as another reproductive choice when it does not harm the resulting child → permit with informed safeguards.
- **Anti-eugenic justice:** socially valued enhancements can turn unequal resources into inherited biological advantage and pressure others to participate → private choice can create collective coercion and stratification → prohibit heritable enhancement.
- **Therapeutic boundary / normal-function account:** medicine has a defensible role in preventing serious disease and restoring functioning but not optimizing socially contested traits → confine heritable intervention to treatment/prevention or normal-range restoration.

## 13. Construction and representation risks

- using vague “intelligence enhancement” rather than a bounded trait;
- making the enhancement obviously harmful and thereby trivial;
- assuming safety solves distributive or intergenerational ethics;
- treating public opposition as a professional recommendation;
- importing current technical objections after the benchmark has stipulated technical readiness.
