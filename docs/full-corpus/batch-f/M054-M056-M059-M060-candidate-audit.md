# Batch F candidate audit — M054, M056, M059, M060

**Purpose:** determine whether the strongest M051–M060 deep cases support action-distinct source-grounded candidate pools. No JSON should be created before independent review.

## M054 — Secondary findings in adult clinical genomic sequencing

### Public / affected candidates

**pub1 — default actionable return with opt-out**  
`Analyze a defined set of medically actionable secondary findings by default and offer them with genetic counseling unless the patient makes an informed choice to opt out before analysis.`

**Support:** very high uptake of actionable secondary findings in clinical sequencing cohorts (97% in the 2023 cancer cohort; 93.5% of the first 200 clinical exome patients chose at least some secondary findings). This is a Bench policy translation from strong demand plus preserved refusal, not evidence that patients explicitly chose a default architecture.

**pub2 — explicit pre-analysis choice / opt-in**  
`Do not analyze unrelated secondary findings until the patient affirmatively selects which categories they want returned, then apply that preference during genomic analysis.`

**Support:** adult focus-group work underlying the PIGSR instrument found strong preference for autonomous category choice applied before analysis; a real-world international laboratory using explicit opt-in found 50.4% chose secondary-finding analysis. This directly supports a choice-before-analysis architecture more strongly than the earlier general preference literature alone.

**Distinctness:** pass. Default analysis with opt-out and no analysis before explicit opt-in can yield different results for nonresponders/ambivalent patients.

### Expert candidate

**exp1 — ACMG actionable-list + opt-out architecture**  
`Offer analysis and return of a professionally defined minimum list of medically actionable secondary findings with informed opt-out, confirmatory interpretation and pre/post-test genetic counseling.`

**Support:** ACMG 2021 policy; current SF v3.3 maintains the actionability-oriented minimum list. One professional architecture.

### Framework candidates

**fw1 — prevention/beneficence default**  
`Analyze highly actionable findings by default with an opt-out because preventable serious disease creates a strong reason to make beneficial risk information available unless the patient refuses it.`

**fw2 — informational autonomy / prior authorization**  
`Seek unrelated genomic risks only after explicit patient authorization because deciding whether to generate information that can reshape identity, anxiety and family obligations is itself an autonomy-sensitive choice.`

**Distinctness:** pass.

### Judgment

**Provisional profile: `full-corpus-2x1x2-mean-v1`.** Survives candidate audit. The key provenance caveat is that pub1's *default* structure is Bench policy translation from high actionable-result demand; the studies show preference for receiving results, not a randomized preference for opt-out defaults.

## M056 — Extending the 14-day embryo-research limit

### Public candidates

**pub1 — retain 14 days**  
`Keep 14 days as the maximum permitted culture period for intact human embryos, even when later development would be scientifically valuable, while allowing embryo models and other alternatives.`

**Support:** Japanese public survey directly found a substantial group opposed to culture beyond 14 days. This is a direct policy translation of maintaining the current boundary.

**pub2 — allow justified extension beyond 14 days**  
`Permit embryo culture beyond 14 days for scientifically important projects when specialized review confirms necessity, valid consent, lack of adequate alternatives and a predefined developmental endpoint.`

**Support:** 37.9% of the Japanese public expressly agreed that embryos could be cultured beyond 14 days. The safeguards are Bench policy completion informed by contemporary oversight, not content elicited from the public survey. Provenance should state: `Bench-authored policy completion: extension is conditioned on specialized review, scientific necessity, consent, alternatives and a predefined endpoint.`

**Distinctness:** pass.

### Expert candidate

**exp1 — specialized project-specific review**  
`Allow culture beyond the historical 14-day boundary only through specialized scientific and ethics review that evaluates scientific rationale, necessity, alternatives, consent and the minimum justified developmental endpoint.`

**Support:** current ISSCR governance after removal of the categorical 14-day prohibition. This is one professional architecture; ISSCR does not prescribe a new universal later day.

### Framework candidates

**fw1 — bright-line precaution**  
`Retain a fixed 14-day boundary because a stable, publicly intelligible limit constrains incremental expansion and protects legitimacy even when some scientifically valuable work is foregone.`

**fw2 — adaptive proportionality**  
`Replace the absolute 14-day boundary with tightly reviewed extensions to the minimum scientifically necessary developmental endpoint because ethically relevant oversight should track purpose, development and alternatives rather than a historic number alone.`

**Distinctness:** pass.

### Judgment

**Provisional profile: `full-corpus-2x1x2-mean-v1`.** Strong Batch-F candidate. No invented new day limit is needed; public contrast is retain versus extend, while the expert candidate specifies the current specialized-review architecture.

## M059 — Mitochondrial donation

### Candidate test

The UK affected-public record supports broad conditional acceptance of mitochondrial donation for severe disease, and affected families have strong reasons for access. HFEA's policy architecture is also unusually mature.

However, the two apparent professional candidates from the deep file do **not** survive distinctness review:

1. permit for people at very high risk of serious mitochondrial disease through licensed specialist treatment, individual approval and follow-up;
2. permit when alternatives offer little or no benefit.

The second is not a separate policy architecture. It is one eligibility condition nested inside the cautious case-by-case regime. Splitting it would recreate the M005 problem at the expert layer by scoring convergence between paraphrases of one rule.

A professional prohibition candidate would instead have to come from a different current governance authority. Present HFEA policy cannot supply it merely because evidence remains early.

At the public layer, broad support and caution are real, but the current packet does not yet give two direct action-distinct regulatory policies at sufficient granularity.

### Judgment

**`needs-additional-evidence`; do not execute.** This is a strong, complete case and a useful example where a real-world regulator has synthesized the ethical trade-off into one conditional architecture.

## M060 — Non-medical sex selection in IVF

### Public candidates

**pub1 — permit non-medical selection as reproductive choice**  
`Allow patients already undergoing IVF to select among otherwise suitable embryos by sex after counseling, provided the choice is voluntary and no medical priority among embryos is displaced.`

**Support:** US focus-group participants often expressed moral discomfort yet did not think harms justified governmental interference; a nationally representative US PGD survey found 21.1% support for sex selection. This is an adapted permissive policy orientation, not a majority claim.

**pub2 — do not permit non-medical sex selection**  
`Do not allow embryo sex to determine transfer when no medical indication exists, because non-medical selection is an inappropriate use of reproductive technology even when patients request it.`

**Support:** representative German survey found only 11% approved non-medical sex selection and 32% favored strict prohibition; Australian population surveys show large majorities opposed to legalizing IVF sex selection for elective or family-balancing reasons.

**Distinctness:** pass.

### Expert / professional candidates

**exp1 — clinic-permissive family-balancing policy**  
`Permit non-medical sex selection for patients already undergoing IVF, at least for family balancing, after counseling about medical burdens, coercion, stereotyping and social-justice concerns.`

**Support:** ASRM 2022 explicitly states practitioners are under no ethical obligation to refuse nonmedical sex selection and discusses reproductive-liberty arguments; ESHRE ethics analysis describes a cautious family-balancing exception as a possible responsible compromise. Provenance must label the exact family-balancing limitation as a synthesis across these professional ethics sources, not a direct ASRM mandate.

**exp2 — clinic-declines nonmedical selection**  
`Adopt a clinic policy declining embryo sex as a transfer criterion unless sex selection is medically indicated, while informing patients of the policy before treatment and applying it consistently.`

**Support:** ASRM 2022 explicitly states practitioners are under no ethical obligation to provide nonmedical sex selection, says the practice should not be encouraged and identifies discrimination, stereotyping, resource and social-justice concerns. This is a permissible professional policy directly contemplated by the opinion, though not a universal ASRM requirement.

**Distinctness:** pass.

### Framework candidates

**fw1 — reproductive liberty**  
`Permit non-medical sex selection when informed and voluntary because choosing among otherwise transferable embryos is part of reproductive planning and family-balancing motives need not express hostility toward either sex.`

**fw2 — anti-discrimination / open future**  
`Decline non-medical sex selection because clinical participation can reinforce sex stereotypes, unequal valuation and parental expectations that constrain children's open futures and wider equality.`

**Distinctness:** pass.

### Judgment

**Provisional profile: `featured-core-2x2x2-v1` by shape only; Full Corpus collection.** Survives candidate audit. M060 is the strongest Batch-F symmetric candidate. The independent reviewer should scrutinize whether exp1's family-balancing synthesis is sufficiently source-faithful and whether the public sources, though older and cross-national, are adequate for the jurisdiction-neutral case.
