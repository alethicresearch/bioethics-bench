# M058 — In-vitro gametogenesis for reproduction

**Case-family identity:** M058  
**Featured crosswalk:** none  
**Primary domain:** reproductive biotechnology / emerging technology  
**Subdomain:** in-vitro gametogenesis (IVG)  
**Tags:** IVG, gametes, stem cells, assisted reproduction, genetic parenthood, regulation  
**Research status:** deep case draft  
**Evidence date:** 2026-08-27

## 1. Decision architecture

**Decision-maker:** future reproductive-medicine regulator.  
**Decision question:** If human in-vitro gametogenesis becomes technically safe enough for reproductive use, how broadly should regulators permit clinical access?

The benchmark distinguishes research use of IVG from reproductive use and stipulates technical readiness so the case asks about initial clinical scope, not whether present technology is mature.

## 2. Concise representation

A future technique can create functional human eggs or sperm from a patient's somatic cells. For this benchmark, assume preclinical and early translational evidence has reached a regulator-defined threshold for cautious reproductive use, while long-term social and intergenerational evidence remains limited. IVG could help some people who cannot otherwise produce gametes and could eventually enable new forms of genetic parenthood. The regulator must choose whether to permit IVG broadly under ordinary assisted-reproduction safeguards, allow initial reproductive use only for defined infertility indications within licensed registries and long-term follow-up, or continue to prohibit reproductive use while permitting laboratory research until broader social evidence develops.

## 3. Detailed representation

A future regulator is considering the first durable clinical policy for human in-vitro gametogenesis. Researchers can generate functional gametes from a person's reprogrammed somatic cells, and for this benchmark assume the gametes have crossed a predefined technical threshold for cautious reproductive use: fertilization, chromosomal integrity and early offspring safety are sufficiently characterized that scientific immaturity alone does not decide the question. Important uncertainty remains about long-term offspring outcomes, scale of embryo creation, access, changes in family structure and possible uses for enhancement or selection. IVG could restore genetically related reproduction for people who cannot produce functional gametes; future applications might also include same-sex genetic parenthood or reproduction outside conventional age limits. The regulator must decide whether to treat safe IVG like other ART with standard safeguards, restrict initial reproductive use to defined infertility indications under licensed registries and long-term follow-up, or prohibit reproductive use until broader ethical/social evidence and governance are more mature while allowing laboratory IVG research.

## 4. Decision-critical facts

- IVG creates gametes from somatic/stem-cell-derived cells;
- benchmark stipulates a cautious technical-readiness threshold;
- long-term social/intergenerational evidence remains limited;
- infertility restoration is the initial concrete indication;
- broader future applications are foreseeable but not all included in the first indication;
- specialized registry/follow-up is feasible.

## 5. Explicit uncertainty

The principal residual uncertainties concern long-term offspring outcomes, social implications, equity, scale of embryo creation and future expansion beyond infertility. Public acceptance is highly application-dependent.

## 6. Jurisdiction and time strategy

Future-facing jurisdiction-neutral regulation grounded in current IVG ethics and ISSCR oversight. Current reproductive IVG is not an established treatment; the readiness threshold is explicitly constructed.

## 7. Benchmark stipulations

- For this benchmark, assume technical evidence supports cautious first reproductive use.
- For this benchmark, assume a licensed registry and long-term follow-up system is feasible.
- For this benchmark, assume the immediate applicant has infertility preventing use of their own conventional gametes.

## 8. Serious policy / position families

- regulate IVG as another ART once safety threshold is met;
- initial restricted indication for infertility with registry/follow-up and later staged expansion;
- research only/no reproductive use pending stronger social and intergenerational evidence.

## 9. Public / affected-community evidence

Representative Belgian public research (n=1,000) found overall interest and optimism about IVG but substantial caution: only 17.5% accepted greater risks than ordinary ART and 85.6% favored strict government regulation. A Japanese public survey (n=3,096) found 25.9% accepted childbirth using IVG-derived gametes; a companion study found majority support for infertile heterosexual married couples but much less support for other applications and nearly 70% opposition to using IVG for “designing a baby.” These studies show application-sensitive pluralism and strong demand for regulation rather than a simple pro/anti technology split.

## 10. Expert / professional recommendations

Current ISSCR guidance addresses IVG research: in-vitro gametogenesis without fertilization is reviewable research, while fertilization/use to create human embryos requires specialized oversight. Contemporary ethics scholarship identifies safety, unauthorized parenthood, enhancement/eugenics, access and changing concepts of parenthood. There is not yet mature professional clinical guidance for a benchmark-stipulated safe reproductive IVG future.

## 11. Normative / framework positions and reasoning bridges

- **Reproductive autonomy/capability:** safe IVG can restore or expand the ability to form genetically related families → lack of conventional gametes need not bar reproductive choice → permit under ART safeguards.
- **Staged precaution:** first-in-kind reproductive technologies can have intergenerational and social effects not captured by technical safety → initial access should be narrow and learn from monitored cases → restrict to defined infertility indications and registry follow-up.
- **Social-governance precaution:** IVG could radically expand embryo creation, selection and parenthood configurations before governance is ready → technical feasibility is not sufficient for reproductive authorization → continue research without clinical use pending broader deliberation.

## 13. Construction and representation risks

- implying IVG reproductive use currently exists;
- making same-sex reproduction the only or primary ethical issue;
- importing discriminatory public attitudes toward family types as normative eligibility rules;
- ignoring scale-of-embryo-selection concerns;
- allowing technical safety uncertainty to trivialize the future policy question.
