### 9. Public / affected-community evidence

For each source, record:

- population/community and setting;
- study or consultation design;
- what position or preference it actually supports;
- limits on translating it into an institutional policy candidate.

If evidence is absent or too weak, say so.

### 10. Expert / professional recommendations

Separate recommendations from empirical facts. Record the relevant professional body, guidance or documented expert disagreement and the policy architecture it supports.

### 11. Normative / framework positions and reasoning bridges

For each framework used, make the bridge explicit:

`normative commitment → case-relevant consideration → actionable policy direction`

Framework-derived executable sentences are Bench constructions, not quotations.

### 12. References and provenance

Group sources by source class where useful. Record enough bibliographic information or a stable URL/identifier to reconstruct the source packet.

### 13. Construction and representation risks

- hidden factual dependence;
- loaded language;
- candidate near-entailment;
- mismatched granularity;
- source-class duplication;
- stakeholder stereotyping or omission;
- jurisdictional drift;
- companion representations that differ in a load-bearing fact;
- overlap with an existing Bench family.

Add case-specific risks.

### 14. Rights / licensing notes

Bench-authored scenario and candidate text may be released under CC BY 4.0. Cited source material retains its original rights. Do not reproduce protected source prose beyond what is necessary for scholarship and provenance.

### 15. Possible uses

Examples: teaching, SACRE execution, representation-sensitivity runs, human/model QCCS, perturbation, RE-Iteration, comparison explanation, robustness analysis.

### 16. Executable-eligibility judgment

Choose one and give the reason:

- `executable-2x2x2`
- `executable-other-profile`
- `research-complete-not-executable`
- `needs-additional-evidence`

Do not use executable status as a proxy for philosophical importance.

### 17. Review requirements

Name the substantive review still required: clinical, legal, affected-community, disability, cultural, technical, animal-welfare, empirical-methods, provenance, or representation review.

### 18. Downstream record rule

If the family becomes executable, record which case-file material may be transcribed into machine-readable records and which remains research metadata. If the family already has a released Featured record, preserve that record unchanged unless a genuine defect requires an explicitly versioned correction.
