## Why Batch P adds zero executable cases

M151–M155 are emerging AI/data governance cases where professional guidance and technical debate are developing faster than direct affected-policy evidence. The strict rule refuses to turn privacy concern, AI trust or interest in access into institutional policies that were not actually elicited.

M156–M159 expose a different and potentially deeper problem. In animal research and xenotransplantation, nonhuman animals themselves bear direct harms, but the Bench's original source architecture is organized around human public/affected views, professional guidance and normative frameworks. Human surveys about animal research can describe social legitimacy; they cannot automatically serve as the animals' interests.

The corpus therefore records M156–M159 as first-class research cases while withholding executable status until a principled nonhuman affected-interest rule is developed or justified.

M160 likewise remains non-executable because contemporary antimicrobial-stewardship evidence largely converges on an integrated rule: preserve treatment of sick animals while restricting unnecessary use and reducing AMR through stewardship, surveillance and husbandry improvements.

## Methodological significance

The M156–M159 problem may matter beyond animal ethics. A computational ethics architecture that requires every affected interest to arrive through surveyable human testimony risks systematically underrepresenting beings who cannot supply such testimony. The alternative—having Bench authors infer their interests—creates a different representation and provenance problem.

This tension should be reviewed explicitly before the animal/One Health block is translated into executable records. It should not be resolved simply by relabeling public opinion as animal interest.
