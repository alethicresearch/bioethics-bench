# Batch O candidate audit — M141, M144, M146, M148, M149

## M141 — Commercial partnership using health-system data

### Public / patient candidates

**pub1 — permit governed commercial collaboration**  
`Allow health data to be used in a commercial research partnership when privacy is protected, the purpose is medically valuable, the partnership is transparent, and the health system or public receives a meaningful share of the resulting benefit.`

**Support:** empirical health-data studies include substantial patient willingness to cooperate with health-industry research when governance and health benefit are clear; systematic reviews show commercial involvement reduces but does not eliminate support. **Bench-authored policy completion:** the benefit-return clause operationalizes recurring directly elicited concerns about profit and public benefit without changing the permissive orientation.

**pub2 — require specific permission for commercial access**  
`Do not give a private or for-profit company secondary access to patient health data without specific patient permission for that commercial use.`

**Support:** UK public survey data directly found a major theme that health data should not be available to private/third-party companies without specific permission; Dutch/German data similarly show much lower willingness for industry access than university/public research.

**Distinctness:** pass.

### Expert candidate

**exp1 — controlled partnership with purpose and use restrictions**  
`Permit a commercial data partnership only through transparent governance, secure controlled access, contractual purpose limits, prohibition of unauthorized reuse, and an explicit public/clinical benefit rationale.`

**Support:** health-information governance policy, AHA data-sharing principles, and responsible secondary-use frameworks converge on transparent use restrictions and accountable governance.

### Framework candidates

**fw1 — social value/innovation:** permit a partnership when commercial capacity can create clinically useful tools and the public receives fair benefit.  
**fw2 — anti-exploitation/informational autonomy:** require specific permission because data collected in a care relationship should not be converted into private commercial value without the contributor's authorization.

### Judgment
**Provisional `full-corpus-2x1x2-mean-v1`.** Survives strict source-to-policy audit.

## M144 — Disclosure of AI involvement to patients

### Public / patient candidates

**pub1 — disclose every clinically material AI use**  
`Tell patients whenever AI materially contributes to diagnosis, triage or treatment planning, and explain the role it played in the clinician's decision.`

**Support:** US patient survey found 95%+ considered it important to be informed when AI played a big role in diagnosis/treatment; newer focus-group/survey work found two-thirds considered disclosure of every AI use important and emphasized understanding how AI affected care.

**pub2 — specific disclosure for consequential/high-impact AI use**  
`Give specific patient notice when AI plays a critical or consequential role in diagnosis, monitoring or treatment, while routine low-impact uses may be covered by general institutional transparency.`

**Support:** patient focus groups directly distinguished critical uses such as diagnosis/monitoring from minor functions such as scheduling, where some participants did not need specific notice. The core materiality threshold is directly elicited rather than constructed.

**Distinctness:** pass for low-to-moderate clinical AI contributions; both disclose high-impact use but differ on the scope of case-specific notice.

### Expert candidate

**exp1 — meaningful tiered transparency**  
`Use a tiered disclosure policy that gives patients specific notice of clinically consequential or autonomous AI use and clear general transparency for lower-impact tools, including who remains responsible and how decisions can be questioned.`

**Support:** AMA transparency/explainability policy and WHO transparency/human-autonomy principles. **Bench-authored policy completion:** the general-notice/specific-notice implementation makes the supported transparency principle operational without creating a new substantive orientation.

### Framework candidates

**fw1 — informational autonomy:** disclose all material AI involvement because patients may reasonably care about the epistemic process producing consequential medical advice.  
**fw2 — materiality/non-overload:** require specific disclosure only when AI changes clinically meaningful choice, risk or decision authority; indiscriminate notices can become meaningless boilerplate.

### Judgment
**Provisional `full-corpus-2x1x2-mean-v1`.** Survives strict audit.

## M146 — Ambient AI documentation

The strongest affected evidence supports **permission before use**, transparency about audio/data flows and an easy opt-out. The 2025 JAMA Network Open study's own recommendation is opt-in; UC Davis patient work likewise recommends explicit verbal permission before use. High acceptance and preference for future use do not support a separate default-use policy.

Creating `default use with notice/opt-out` as pub2 would therefore convert acceptability into a policy the affected sources did not endorse.

### Judgment
**`needs-additional-evidence`; do not execute.** Excellent implementation case, one strong affected-policy orientation.

## M148 — AI-driven prior authorization

AMA 2026 explicitly opposes autonomous or semiautonomous AI as a substitute for physician review in coverage determinations and calls for physician-led review and transparency. The branch does not yet contain direct patient/public evidence comparing `human review for denials`, `automated denial + appeal`, and `no high-stakes AI prior authorization` as institutional rules.

### Judgment
**`needs-additional-evidence`; do not execute.** Do not manufacture a pro-autonomous-denial expert candidate.

## M149 — Updating a deployed clinical AI model

FDA's PCCP architecture is a genuine governance innovation and the alternatives—full revalidation, bounded PCCP updates, or frozen periodic versions—are analytically distinct. But the current source layer is almost entirely regulator/professional. There is no adequate public/affected policy pool.

### Judgment
**`research-complete-not-executable`.** Valuable versioning analogue for the normative-computation program; not an executable Bench case under the strict source-class rule.
