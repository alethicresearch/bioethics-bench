# M141 — Commercial partnership using health-system data

**Case-family identity:** M141  
**Primary domain:** health data / commercial partnerships  
**Research status:** deep case draft  
**Evidence date:** 2026-08-27

## Decision architecture
**Decision-maker:** public or nonprofit health-system data-governance board.  
**Decision question:** Should a health system share patient data with a commercial company to build a clinically useful model, and under what benefit and consent conditions?

## Scenario pair
**Concise:** A nonprofit health system can partner with a technology company to develop a model expected to improve diagnosis. The company would receive coded patient data inside a controlled environment and would own some resulting intellectual property. The health system can permit the partnership under privacy/public-benefit conditions, require a nonexclusive or public-benefit licensing model, or require individual consent before any commercial secondary use.

**Detailed:** A nonprofit health system holds a large clinical dataset collected for care. A commercial company proposes using coded data in a secure environment to develop a clinically useful predictive model. The company will invest substantial engineering resources and seeks commercial rights; the hospital would receive discounted access and research collaboration but patients would not receive individual payments. Public-attitude reviews show that commercial involvement often lowers willingness to share and raises concerns about consent, privacy, control, profit and benefit sharing, while support can remain substantial when public benefit and governance are clear. The board must decide what conditions make the partnership acceptable.

## Source architecture
A 2024 systematic review of 113 empirical studies found commercial involvement in biobank/genomic data commonly raises concerns about consent, privacy/security, trust, benefit sharing and ownership/control. Broader health-data-sharing reviews likewise find willingness depends strongly on recipient, purpose, privacy, individual control and perceived public benefit.

## Serious policy families
- permit secure commercial partnership under transparent public-benefit/privacy terms;
- permit only under nonexclusive/public-benefit licensing and benefit-sharing conditions;
- require individual consent before commercial secondary use.

## Normative bridges
**Public benefit/innovation:** commercial expertise may produce useful tools that public systems cannot build alone.  
**Reciprocity:** publicly generated data should not become private value without fair return.  
**Informational autonomy/trust:** commercial use changes the social meaning of data collected for care.

## Construction risks
Do not equate coding with zero privacy risk; specify IP and access; distinguish commercial research from sale of identifiable records; do not infer permission from general research support.

## Executable judgment
Provisional candidate-audit target. Public evidence directly distinguishes commercial from noncommercial recipients and benefit/control conditions, while governance sources support concrete partnership rules.

## Principal sources
Walshe et al., 2024 systematic review of commercialization and public attitudes (PMID 38389329); 2024 systematic reviews of health-data sharing attitudes; responsible data-governance literature.
