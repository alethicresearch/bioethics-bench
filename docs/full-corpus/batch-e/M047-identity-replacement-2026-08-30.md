# M047 identity replacement — 2026-08-30

## Why the identity changed

The original M047, **conscientious objection in abortion care**, substantially duplicated M007. Both cases asked what disclosure, referral/facilitation and access duties apply when a clinician objects to abortion in a lawful setting where delay matters. Keeping both as independent Full Corpus families would create duplicate ontology rather than meaningful breadth.

The old M047 scholarship is therefore retained as development history and should be cross-referenced to M007. It must not be counted as a distinct case family in the frozen 200-case inventory.

## Replacement identity

M047 is replaced by **Permanent contraception requested by a young childfree adult**.

The replacement remains within reproductive ethics but presents a materially different policy object: whether a clinical service may impose age/parity-based barriers or additional mandatory safeguards on a competent adult seeking an irreversible contraceptive procedure.

## Source basis

- ACOG Committee Statement No. 8, *Permanent Contraception: Ethical Issues and Considerations* (2024), makes reproductive autonomy primary, recommends nonjudgmental shared decision making, emphasizes permanence and alternatives, supports longitudinal counseling, and explicitly states that respect for reproductive autonomy requires **not imposing thresholds based on age or parity**.
- Contemporary national regret evidence shows regret is more common among patients sterilized at younger ages, while concluding that age alone should not be used as a barrier to permanent contraception (PMID 35115436).
- Recent research focused on childfree people seeking permanent contraception documents access barriers and high satisfaction among respondents who underwent the procedure (PMID 39522633). This evidence is descriptive and must not be generalized into a universal policy preference.

## Provenance rule

The source-backed professional position is patient-centered access without age/parity vetoes, combined with careful informed counseling. Stronger mandatory waiting periods, compulsory second opinions, or age/parity refusal rules may be represented only as clearly labeled constructed or framework-derived comparators unless a source directly supports that exact policy.
