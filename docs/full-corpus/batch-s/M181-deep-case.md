# M181 — Recurrent nonadherence to chronic-disease treatment

**Domain:** chronic care / structural determinants  
**Evidence date:** 2026-08-27  
**Research status:** deep case draft

## Decision
**Decision-maker:** multidisciplinary chronic-care team.  
**Question:** How should the team respond when repeated medication nonadherence creates preventable risk but is driven partly by cost, unstable housing, work schedules and treatment burden?

## Scenario
A patient with diabetes and hypertension has repeated emergency visits after missing medications. Interviews reveal high copays, unstable housing, difficulty storing supplies, rotating work shifts and medication side effects. The patient understands the risks and wants treatment but cannot reliably follow the existing regimen. The team must choose whether to redesign care around barriers and patient priorities, impose stronger adherence conditions before escalating resource-intensive therapy, or simplify goals/regimens even if this accepts less aggressive disease targets.

## Evidence layers
Qualitative and chronic-care studies show medication adherence is strongly shaped by cost, housing, complexity, side effects, trust and communication. Patient-centered chronic-care models can improve adherence and reduce cost barriers.

## Candidate families
1. barrier-focused support and regimen redesign;
2. structured adherence conditions for selected high-risk therapies;
3. shared revision of treatment goals/regimen burden.

## Risks
Do not moralize nonadherence or treat structural barriers as character defects. Distinguish conditions needed for a particular high-risk treatment from conditions on ordinary access to care.

## Executable judgment
`needs-additional-evidence`. Strong affected evidence supports barrier-responsive care, but not multiple action-distinct institutional rules.
