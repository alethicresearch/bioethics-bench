# M183 — Suspected child abuse with uncertain evidence

**Domain:** pediatrics / safeguarding  
**Evidence date:** 2026-08-27  
**Research status:** deep case draft

## Decision
**Decision-maker:** pediatric clinical team.  
**Question:** When findings are concerning but not diagnostic of abuse, when should clinicians report to child-protection authorities?

## Scenario
A young child presents with bruising whose pattern is concerning but not pathognomonic for inflicted injury. The caregiver gives a plausible but incomplete history; the child is medically stable and a child-abuse pediatrician is available. For this benchmark, assume local law requires reporting on reasonable suspicion rather than proof. The team must decide how to combine immediate reporting, multidisciplinary evaluation and communication with the family.

## Evidence layers
AAP guidance is explicit that mandatory reporting does not require diagnostic certainty; reasonable suspicion/cause is sufficient. Multidisciplinary evaluation is valuable but is not a professional basis to delay a legally required report when reasonable suspicion already exists.

## Candidate families
1. report when reasonable suspicion threshold is met while continuing evaluation;
2. evaluate further before reporting only when current facts remain below that threshold and delay is safe;
3. communicate concerns/support the family while fulfilling the reporting duty.

## Risks
Do not create a fake 'wait for proof' expert candidate. Specify the threshold and the child's immediate safety.

## Executable judgment
`research-complete-not-executable`. Current professional/legal guidance forms one threshold-based process.
