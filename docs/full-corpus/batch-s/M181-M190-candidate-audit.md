## M189 — mammography screening when guidelines disagree

M189 has excellent professional pluralism. The 2024 USPSTF recommendation (biennial mammography from age 40 through 74) and current American Cancer Society schedule (annual screening beginning no later than 45, with an option to begin annually at 40–44) are genuinely action-distinct expert policies.

The affected evidence is also unusually good in one sense: national and other patient-preference studies show that women in their 40s differ substantially in preferred screening timing and frequency, and balanced decision information can shift preferences toward later screening.

But the strict construction question is narrower: do those studies directly support **institutional practice policies** such as “the practice should recommend routine biennial screening from 40” versus “the practice should recommend annual screening in the 40s” or “the practice should make frequency a shared decision”? They primarily elicit what respondents personally prefer for themselves, not what rule a practice should adopt for all similarly situated patients.

Turning personal screening preference into a clinic policy would be a core-action translation, not narrow operational completion. That is permitted in frozen Featured v1 but not in Full Corpus v1.

**Disposition:** `needs-additional-evidence`. Re-open if patient/public deliberative evidence directly compares the practice-level recommendation rules, or if a valid public source class can be grounded without converting personal choice into institutional policy.

This is intentionally conservative despite the real USPSTF/ACS expert disagreement.
