# M188 — Predictive genetic testing of a child for an adult-onset condition

**Domain:** pediatric genetics / future autonomy  
**Evidence date:** 2026-08-27  
**Research status:** deep case draft

## Decision
**Decision-maker:** pediatric genetics service.  
**Question:** Should parents be able to obtain predictive testing of an asymptomatic minor for a highly penetrant adult-onset disorder when no childhood intervention is available?

## Scenario
Parents with a familial pathogenic variant for an adult-onset cancer syndrome request testing of their healthy 12-year-old. No surveillance or treatment would begin before adulthood, and the child is not currently asking to know. The parents say certainty would help family planning and reduce anxiety. The service can defer testing until the young person can decide as an adolescent/adult, make a narrow exception where psychosocial burden is substantial and the mature adolescent concurs, or use individualized review of medical and psychosocial consequences.

## Evidence layers
AAP/ACMG ethics generally recommends deferring testing for adult-onset conditions without childhood intervention, with limited exceptions for significant family psychosocial burden and mature adolescent involvement.

## Candidate families
1. defer to preserve the child's future autonomy;
2. allow a narrow exception with mature adolescent assent and substantial psychosocial benefit;
3. individualized review when consequences differ from the canonical case.

## Risks
Do not treat parental anxiety alone as a medical indication or assume learning genetic risk is psychologically neutral.

## Executable judgment
`research-complete-not-executable`. The apparent alternatives are exception conditions within one prevailing professional architecture.
