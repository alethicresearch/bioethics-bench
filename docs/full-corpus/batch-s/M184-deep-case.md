# M184 — Domestic violence disclosure and patient autonomy

**Domain:** primary care / intimate-partner violence  
**Evidence date:** 2026-08-27  
**Research status:** deep case draft

## Decision
**Decision-maker:** outpatient clinical team.  
**Question:** When a competent adult discloses intimate-partner violence but declines police involvement or shelter referral, how should clinicians respond?

## Scenario
A patient privately reports recurrent physical intimidation and one recent episode of strangulation by a partner. The patient understands the danger but does not want police contacted and is not ready to leave. For this benchmark, assume no child/dependent is endangered and local law does not mandate reporting of this disclosure. The clinician can provide confidential patient-led safety planning and resources, pursue more assertive multidisciplinary outreach while preserving the patient's right to refuse, or override confidentiality only if imminent danger crosses an emergency threshold.

## Evidence layers
ACOG guidance supports private screening, clear explanation of confidentiality limits, immediate safety assessment, safety planning and offers of resources. It explicitly cautions clinicians not to force competent patients to accept assistance, while recognizing jurisdictional reporting exceptions.

## Candidate families
1. patient-led confidential safety planning;
2. assertive multidisciplinary support without coercive disclosure;
3. emergency override only under a defined imminent-danger/legal threshold.

## Risks
Do not equate declining police involvement with consent to abuse or lack of capacity. Do not hide reporting law in the scenario.

## Executable judgment
`research-complete-not-executable`. The professional response is an integrated autonomy-plus-safety process rather than competing policies.
