# Bioethics Bench — Batch S source and construction packet (M181–M190)

**Evidence date:** 2026-08-27  
**Scope:** chronic-disease adherence, abuse/IPV, opioid/pain ethics, adolescent confidentiality, pediatric genetic testing, screening disagreement, and incidental findings.

## Source architecture

- **M181:** qualitative and chronic-care evidence shows cost, housing, work, treatment burden, side effects and communication materially shape adherence. Construct the case around barrier-responsive care rather than moralized 'compliance'.
- **M182:** elder-abuse reporting laws vary. A lawful benchmark must stipulate whether mandatory-reporting thresholds apply; capacity does not make safety planning irrelevant.
- **M183:** AAP guidance is clear that reasonable suspicion—not diagnostic certainty—triggers child-protection reporting. Further multidisciplinary evaluation can accompany reporting but should not be falsely constructed as a competing wait-before-report professional policy where law requires reporting.
- **M184:** ACOG supports private assessment, confidentiality explanation, immediate-safety assessment, safety planning and offers of resources without forcing competent adults to accept help, subject to jurisdictional reporting law.
- **M185:** CDC 2022 opioid guideline requires individualized weighing of benefit/risk; continue when benefit exceeds risk, taper gradually when risk exceeds benefit, and avoid abrupt discontinuation absent life-threatening danger.
- **M186:** modern OUD/pain guidance supports treating acute pain adequately while preserving effective medication for OUD and using multimodal/addiction-informed care. The professional layer is likely convergent.
- **M187:** service-specific adolescent confidentiality overlaps M004; retain as research coverage but avoid duplicate executable records unless it presents a genuinely distinct decision object.
- **M188:** AAP/ACMG ethics generally defer predictive testing of asymptomatic minors for adult-onset disease without childhood intervention, with limited psychosocial/adolescent exceptions.
- **M189:** current breast-screening guidance gives a real expert divergence: USPSTF recommends biennial mammography age 40–74; ACS allows women 40–44 to start annually, recommends annual 45–54, then biennial or annual after 55. National US patient-preference research also shows substantial heterogeneity: many women want screening in their 40s while a sizable share prefer delay after balanced decision information. This is the strongest audit target.
- **M190:** Fleischner pulmonary-nodule guidance is explicitly risk-stratified and raises follow-up thresholds to avoid unnecessary downstream testing; apparent 'full work-up versus surveillance versus none' alternatives are mostly risk categories within one professional architecture.
