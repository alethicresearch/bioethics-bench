# M182 — Suspected elder abuse when a capacitated patient resists intervention

**Domain:** geriatrics / safeguarding / autonomy  
**Evidence date:** 2026-08-27  
**Research status:** deep case draft

## Decision
**Decision-maker:** clinical team.  
**Question:** When a capacitated older adult appears to be experiencing abuse but declines separation or police involvement, what process should the team use within applicable law?

## Scenario
An older adult has recurrent bruising and reports that an adult child sometimes threatens and grabs them during arguments. The patient understands the risks, wishes to return home and refuses police involvement or shelter placement. For this benchmark, assume local law requires reporting only if a defined statutory threshold is met and that threshold is not automatically triggered by the current facts. The team can respect refusal while conducting confidential safety planning and follow-up, intensify multidisciplinary assessment before discharge, or intervene/report if new facts cross the legal or imminent-danger threshold.

## Evidence layers
Safeguarding guidance emphasizes private assessment, capacity, danger, resources and jurisdiction-specific reporting requirements. Adult autonomy does not eliminate the duty to assess coercion or safety.

## Candidate families
1. respect refusal with safety planning;
2. intensive multidisciplinary assessment before honoring refusal;
3. mandatory intervention only when statutory/imminent-danger threshold is met.

## Risks
Do not assume age or dependence equals incapacity. Do not let unspecified law decide the case.
