# M185 — Chronic pain and opioid prescribing under emerging misuse risk

**Domain:** pain medicine / opioid stewardship  
**Evidence date:** 2026-08-27  
**Research status:** deep case draft

## Decision
**Decision-maker:** chronic-pain clinician.  
**Question:** Should opioid therapy continue when it improves function but emerging misuse indicators increase overdose and diversion risk?

## Scenario
A patient on long-term opioids reports meaningful functional benefit but has escalating early refill requests and one unexpected toxicology result. There is no current intoxication or imminent overdose. The clinician can continue therapy with intensified monitoring and risk mitigation, begin a collaborative gradual taper/transition to other pain and addiction treatments, or discontinue controlled prescribing only if risk becomes unmanageable while maintaining nonabandonment and treatment for pain/OUD as indicated.

## Evidence layers
CDC's 2022 guideline explicitly rejects abrupt discontinuation in stable physically dependent patients except for life-threatening danger, requires individualized benefit-risk assessment, and supports continuation when benefits outweigh risks or gradual taper when they do not.

## Candidate families
1. continue with structured monitoring when benefit remains favorable;
2. gradual patient-centered taper/transition when risks now exceed benefit;
3. stop controlled prescribing only under high-risk circumstances while continuing care.

## Risks
Do not make urine toxicology a moral test. Distinguish misuse, OUD, diversion and physiologic dependence. Do not construct abrupt forced taper as a serious expert policy.

`research-complete-not-executable`. The apparent options are branches inside one current conditional professional rule.
