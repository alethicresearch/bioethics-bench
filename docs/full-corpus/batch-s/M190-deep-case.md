# M190 — Incidental finding after low-value imaging

**Domain:** diagnostic stewardship / incidental findings  
**Evidence date:** 2026-08-27  
**Research status:** deep case draft

## Decision
**Decision-maker:** clinician managing an incidental pulmonary nodule.  
**Question:** After discretionary imaging reveals a small uncertain incidental abnormality, how aggressively should follow-up proceed?

## Scenario
A low-value CT obtained for nonspecific symptoms finds a 4-mm solid pulmonary nodule in a low-risk adult with no cancer history. The nodule is unlikely to be malignant, but the patient is anxious because it exists. Follow-up imaging creates radiation exposure, cost and further incidental-finding risk. The clinician can follow a risk-stratified guideline that recommends no routine follow-up below the relevant threshold, offer optional surveillance when individual risk/preferences justify it, or pursue more intensive work-up only if additional risk factors move the patient into a higher-risk category.

## Evidence layers
The Fleischner Society revised its pulmonary-nodule guideline to raise the size threshold for routine follow-up and use ranges that permit clinical discretion based on risk and preference. The apparent 'no follow-up / surveillance / full work-up' options are therefore risk branches of one evidence-based architecture rather than independent expert policies.

## Risks
Do not make prior low-value testing erase the obligation to manage a discovered finding responsibly. Do not use patient anxiety alone as proof that invasive work-up is indicated.

## Executable judgment
`research-complete-not-executable`. Current professional guidance is integrated and risk-stratified.
