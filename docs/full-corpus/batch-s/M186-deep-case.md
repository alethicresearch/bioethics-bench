# M186 — Treating acute pain in a patient with opioid use disorder

**Domain:** acute pain / addiction medicine  
**Evidence date:** 2026-08-27  
**Research status:** deep case draft

## Decision
**Decision-maker:** inpatient/surgical team.  
**Question:** How should clinicians treat severe acute pain in a patient with OUD who is stable on medication treatment?

## Scenario
A patient taking buprenorphine for OUD is admitted with a painful fracture requiring surgery. The patient has severe pain and is worried both about undertreatment and relapse. The team can continue medication for OUD and provide multimodal analgesia including additional opioids when clinically indicated, use an opioid-sparing plan with specialist consultation while retaining rescue opioids, or alter OUD therapy only for a specific perioperative reason under addiction/pain expertise.

## Evidence layers
Contemporary addiction and pain guidance increasingly supports continuing effective medication for OUD, treating pain adequately, using multimodal analgesia and avoiding stigmatizing undertreatment. The professional debate is about implementation details rather than whether patients with OUD deserve analgesia.

## Candidate families
1. full multimodal analgesia including opioids when indicated while continuing OUD treatment;
2. opioid-sparing multimodal plan with rescue opioid access and addiction consultation;
3. individualized temporary medication adjustment only when clinically justified.

## Risks
Do not equate OUD history with drug-seeking. Do not construct undertreatment as relapse prevention.

## Executable judgment
`research-complete-not-executable`. Professional guidance is strongly integrated and patient evidence does not supply competing institutional policies.
