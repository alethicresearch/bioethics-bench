## 4. Source-grounding worked examples

### M002 — WHO QualityRights

**Claim:** an earlier candidate used a “substitute decision-making only as a last resort” framing inconsistent with the QualityRights/CRPD Article 12 orientation; the current candidate was repaired toward supported interpretation of the person's will and preferences.

**Status:** PASS as a model-assisted source-review finding.

**Evidence:** current M002 record and direct-policy warrant review. The current provenance note explicitly records that the earlier wording attributed to WHO a last-resort framing that the source orientation rejects.

**Boundary:** the QualityRights module itself was not independently human-adjudicated for this manuscript; do not upgrade this to independent source validation.

### M031 — Oregon Death with Dignity

**Claim:** the Oregon Health Authority annual data report documented program operation but was not the source of the statutory safeguards encoded by the candidate; warrant was repaired to ORS 127.800–127.897.

**Status:** PASS.

**Evidence:** direct-policy warrant review and repaired record provenance.

### M041 — ESHRE IVF allocation

**Claim:** ESHRE's minimum-effectiveness threshold rule was genuinely supported by the cited document, but whole-document reading also revealed a childless-couple priority relevant to another axis in the case; the omission is now disclosed.

**Status:** PASS as a bounded whole-document review example.

**Boundary:** whole-document omission review has been performed by that method for five families only; no corpus-wide omission rate may be inferred.

### M028 — institutional policy versus personal preference

**Claim:** Ludka et al. asked both whether a hospital should be required to continue treatment when an informed family rejects brain death and what respondents would want for themselves; the corresponding reported figures were 41.9% and 24.4%.

**Status:** PASS.

**External reference:** Ludka N, Hurse D, Brummett A. *Neurocritical Care*. 2025;43(1):262-276. doi:10.1007/s12028-024-02196-8. The manuscript uses this as the clearest action-target-alignment example rather than as an ethical answer key.

### M060 — source independence

**Claim:** public candidates are grounded in population evidence (Dahl, Winkelman, Kippen) while expert candidates are grounded in ASRM professional guidance, avoiding a cross-role self-comparison; the ASRM opinion itself can license opposed clinic policies because it declines to require either provision or refusal.

**Status:** PASS as current record/review state.

## 7. V7 adjacent-literature claims

### Moral Machine

**Manuscript use:** a large-scale descriptive moral-preference precedent.

**Status:** PASS.

Awad et al. report the Moral Machine platform and 40 million decisions across 233 countries/territories. V7 makes the weaker, fully supported statement that it scaled descriptive moral-preference elicitation across countries and cultures.

Reference: Awad E, Dsouza S, Kim R, et al. *Nature*. 2018;563:59-64. doi:10.1038/s41586-018-0637-6.

### ETHICS

**Manuscript use:** benchmark for prediction of widespread ethical judgments spanning justice, deontology, virtue, utilitarianism/well-being, and commonsense morality.

**Status:** PASS.

Reference: Hendrycks D, Burns C, Basart S, et al. *Aligning AI with shared human values.* ICLR 2021.

### Delphi / Norm Bank

**Manuscript use:** descriptive human moral-judgment prediction, with an explicit distinction between representing participant judgments and endorsing moral truth.

**Status:** PASS.

Jiang et al. describe Delphi as a system trained to predict US participants' moral judgments and state that Norm Bank is released as a representation of particular participants' judgments without necessarily endorsing their correctness.

Reference: Jiang L, Hwang JD, Bhagavatula C, et al. *Nat Mach Intell.* 2025;7:145-160. doi:10.1038/s42256-024-00969-6.

### TRIAGE

**Manuscript use:** closest current medical machine-ethics benchmark neighbor: clinician-created mass-casualty dilemmas used to evaluate ethical decision-making by LLMs under ethical/adversarial prompts.

**Status:** PASS.

Reference: Kirch NM, Hebenstreit K, Samwald M. *Sci Rep.* 2025;15:30974. doi:10.1038/s41598-025-16716-9.

### HELM

**Manuscript use:** example of standardized scenarios/metrics/evaluation conditions for transparent comparable model assessment.

**Status:** PASS.

## 8. Submission-positioning claims

**Patterns Resource fit:** supported. Patterns formally launched the Resource article type in July 2025, describing it as flexible and open to software, datasets, and other resources, including technical and governance/history content. The format is a strong substantive fit.

**NeurIPS E&D alternative:** supported. The 2026 track explicitly positions evaluation as a scientific object and includes responsible dataset development, benchmarking methodology, audits, protocols, tools, and documentation; the May 6, 2026 deadline has passed, so the realistic alternative is a later cycle.

**Scientific Data Data Descriptor:** not recommended because current guidelines state Data Descriptors should not contain results, discussion, or analyses; those elements are central to Bioethics Bench.
