# Bioethics Bench — Casebook and Teaching-Case Source Map

Casebooks are a **first-class discovery source** for the master inventory. They solve a different sampling problem from guidelines, statutes, policy reports and headline controversies: they reveal which ethical problems repeatedly arise in ordinary clinical practice, professional education and case-based reasoning.

They are **not** treated as a license to copy case text. Bench scenarios are independently reconstructed, source-grounded representations written for the Bench. When a casebook leads us to a problem, the final case file should trace the underlying professional guidance, empirical literature, law/policy and normative scholarship as well.

The most useful casebook properties for this project are:

- bounded decision situations rather than broad topics;
- recurring everyday dilemmas, not only famous cases;
- explicit stakeholder conflict;
- examples of which factual details make an ethical answer change;
- cross-references to professional/legal sources;
- pedagogical evidence about what an informed reader can understand without a monograph.

The limitation is equally important: a pedagogically vivid case is not automatically a stable benchmark object. It may be jurisdiction-bound, written to teach a predetermined lesson, contain an implicit `correct` answer, combine several decisions, or rely on copyrighted narrative detail. The Bench therefore uses casebooks to **discover and triangulate**, then constructs its own versioned case.

## B01 — Jeremy Sugarman, *20 Common Problems: Ethics in Primary Care* (McGraw-Hill, 2000)

A particularly valuable source for **ordinary outpatient ethics**. Public bibliographic descriptions identify recurring topics including inappropriate requests for treatment/tests and for exemptions/privileges; complementary and alternative medicine; confidentiality; suspected abuse/neglect; treating minors; refusal of treatment; pain control; informed consent; genetic testing; preventive medicine; adherence/nonadherence; managed-care pressures and other primary-care conflicts.

Use for: mundane but high-frequency cases, clinician–patient boundary problems, documentation/certification, gatekeeping, conflicts of interest, adherence, confidentiality and prevention.

Do not assume 2000-era clinical/legal recommendations remain current. Use the case structure, then update facts and governing standards from current sources.

## B02 — Robert M. Veatch, Amy M. Haddad & Dan C. English, *Case Studies in Biomedical Ethics: Decision-Making, Principles, and Cases*, 2nd ed. (Oxford University Press, 2015)

More than 100 case studies drawn from current events, court cases and health-professional experience, spanning principles and practical topics including treatment refusal, mental health, human experimentation, abortion and other biomedical controversies.

Use for: breadth audit, classic case structures, identifying when one `topic` actually contains multiple distinct decisions.

## B03 — Albert R. Jonsen, Mark Siegler & William J. Winslade, *Clinical Ethics: A Practical Approach to Ethical Decisions in Clinical Medicine*, current 9th ed.

Foundational case-oriented clinical-ethics text using the four-topics/four-box approach: medical indications, patient preferences, quality of life and contextual features. Numerous realistic case examples illustrate how factual and contextual changes affect ethical analysis.

Use for: clinical scenario completeness; checking whether a case has stated the medical indication, patient preference/capacity, outcome/burden and contextual facts that actually drive the decision.

## B04 — Lewis Vaughn, *Bioethics: Principles, Issues, and Cases*, 6th ed. (Oxford University Press, 2025)

Current introductory text integrating moral reasoning, readings and classic/contemporary cases. Current contents include autonomy/paternalism, truth-telling/confidentiality, informed consent, human research, abortion, reproductive technology, genetic choices, euthanasia/assisted suicide, resource allocation, race/racial bias and disability ethics.

Use for: curriculum-level coverage audit and classic/contemporary teaching problems.

## B05 — Mathy D. Mezey et al. (eds.), *Ethical Patient Care: A Casebook for Geriatric Health Care Teams* (Johns Hopkins University Press, 2003)

Case-study teaching focused on multidisciplinary care of older adults and conflicts among patients, families, professionals and institutions.

Use for: geriatric ethics, team conflict, transitions of care, institutional responsibility, chronic multimorbidity and cases where several professionals legitimately see different parts of the problem.

## B06 — Jeffrey P. Spike & Rebecca Lunstroth, *A Casebook in Interprofessional Ethics* (Springer, 2016)

Integrates clinical, nursing, dental, research, public-health, informatics and organizational ethics in interprofessional cases.

Use for: non-physician professional roles, organizational systems, informatics, team authority and coordination failures.

## B07 — *Bioethics, Public Health, and the Social Sciences for the Medical Professions: An Integrated, Case-Based Approach* (Springer, 2019)

Nearly two dozen case vignettes integrating ethics, law, economics, public policy, social medicine and population health across patient populations and settings.

Use for: bridging bedside and population policy, and identifying cases where the ethically relevant decision belongs to an institution or public authority rather than the treating clinician.

## B08 — Beauchamp et al., *The Human Use of Animals: Case Studies in Ethical Choice* (Oxford University Press, 2008)

Case studies addressing difficult practical questions in human uses of animals.

Use for: animal research, food systems, companion/wild animals and the methodological question of representing nonhuman interests in SACRE.

# How casebooks enter provenance

A final Bench record should distinguish:

1. **Discovery provenance** — casebook/course/topic map that led us to examine the problem.
2. **Scenario provenance** — factual, legal, clinical and policy sources used to construct the represented facts.
3. **Candidate provenance** — public-preference, expert/professional and framework sources supporting each executable recommendation.
4. **Editorial transformation** — how the research team converted those sources into compact Bench language.

A casebook can appear in `references` as a `teaching-case` or book source where appropriate, but it should rarely be the sole provenance for an executable candidate.

---

# Sampling consequence

The master inventory should intentionally combine at least four discovery streams:

- **casebooks / teaching curricula** — recurring and pedagogically legible problems;
- **professional and policy guidance** — actual institutional decision architectures;
- **peer-reviewed normative and empirical bioethics literature** — arguments, public/professional attitudes and contested concepts;
- **current/emerging governance** — newly actionable questions not yet represented in classic casebooks.

No one stream defines `bioethics`. Their overlap and disagreement are useful evidence about what the Bench should contain.
