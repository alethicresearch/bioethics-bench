**Date:** 2026-08-30

## Purpose

The record-level Featured audit identified several cases where the current source-to-Policy construction might be compressing, overtranslating, or mismatching the normative material. This note reopens primary or near-primary literature for the highest-priority cases and records what that evidence implies for a future explicit record version.

The guiding question is not whether the current `decision_question` lists every possible branch. It is whether the **Scenario and the source-grounded Policy candidates correspond to the same normative object**, and whether the candidate field preserves the natural positions actually supported by the sources.

## F03 — Resuscitation at the threshold of viability

### Current concern

The current Public pair consists of:

1. present resuscitation and comfort care as legitimate options and follow informed parental preference;
2. use shared decision-making and provide a clinician recommendation when parents want guidance while preserving parental choice.

These are not obviously competing public Policy positions. They can readily be held together by the same parent: *I want meaningful decisional authority and I also want the clinician to tell me what they recommend.*

### Source re-open

The professional literature supports a genuine distinction between **individualized shared decision-making** and **standardized gestational guidance / thresholds with a parent-preference zone**.

- Krick & Feltman, *Journal of Perinatology* (2019), survey of 480 neonatology providers: most respondents wanted standardized upper and lower gestational limits, while parental preference often overrode guidelines in conflict scenarios; resuscitation versus comfort-care choices were nearly evenly split in the surveyed scenarios. DOI: `10.1038/s41372-019-0313-1`.
- A rapid review of periviable counseling (2025) describes the pre-birth choice as survival-focused versus comfort care and emphasizes transparency, collaboration, empowerment, individualized information, and integration of parental priorities.
- Earlier and later parent studies show that families make different *case-level choices* between intensive care and comfort care when given a shared-decision pathway. That variation is evidence about the importance of offering real choice; it is not automatically evidence for two different institutional Policy candidates.

### Reconstruction implication

**Do not manufacture “default resuscitation” and “default comfort care” Public candidates merely because the framing question names them.** The source evidence must support a *policy preference*, not merely document that different families choose different treatments.

The likely natural field after full source adjudication is:

- **Public: 1 principal Policy position** — offer balanced resuscitation and comfort-care options in the gray zone, preserve meaningful informed parental choice, and provide guidance/recommendation when wanted.
- **Expert: 2 positions** — individualized shared decision-making versus standardized gestational thresholds / zones with parental preference inside the gray zone.
- **Framework: 2 positions** — broad parental authority where both pathways are medically reasonable versus a fair consistently applied prognosis threshold limiting variation.

**Likely natural geometry: `1 × 2 × 2`.**

If further public-policy evidence identifies genuinely distinct procedural preferences, increase that count only on the evidence.

## F04 — Brain death and continued organ support after family objection

### Current concern

The current Expert pair may be two descriptions of one professional policy: provide reasonable time-limited accommodation and use a multidisciplinary disagreement-resolution / transfer process.

### Source re-open

The 2023 AAN/AAP/CNS/SCCM consensus guideline recommends that hospital policies:

- include a process to resolve disagreements with families; and
- consider providing a reasonable period of accommodation after BD/DNC determination.

The later AAN guidance on communication, objections, pregnancy, and public trust (2025) becomes more specific, including:

- a finite opportunity to arrange transfer;
- a brief and uniform accommodation period, described as under 48 hours as a reasonable policy;
- no escalation of treatment after BD/DNC determination; and
- discontinuation of organ support when needed for a living patient if no alternative bed is available.

### Reconstruction implication

There are two defensible ways to represent the expert evidence, and release review should choose between them explicitly:

1. **Current-consensus representation:** treat the 2025 guidance as the operative professional position and use **one** Expert candidate combining finite accommodation, transfer, no escalation, and a declared brief institutional time frame.
2. **Comparative professional-position representation:** retain a distinction between a case-sensitive “reasonable period” approach from the 2023 consensus and the later more standardized brief-uniform-period approach, but only if they are treated as genuinely distinct expert judgments rather than duplicate components of one procedure.

The current `exp2` should not remain merely as a multidisciplinary-process elaboration of `exp1`; that is the nested-caveat failure mode.

## F09 — Heritable genome editing to prevent serious disease

### Confirmed problem

The current Scenario stipulates a **future** condition in which heritable genome editing has reached a high independently verified safety and efficacy threshold. Current Expert `exp1`, however, says not to authorize clinical HHGE *at present* and relies on contemporary nonauthorization. That position does not cleanly answer the stipulated future Scenario.

### Source re-open

The International Commission on the Clinical Use of Human Germline Genome Editing, *Heritable Human Genome Editing* (2020), supplies a future-conditional professional pathway:

- **Recommendation 2:** extensive societal dialogue should precede a country's decision whether to permit clinical HHGE;
- **Recommendation 3:** clinical use should proceed incrementally only where a responsible translational pathway has been defined and a country has decided to permit it;
- **Recommendation 4:** initial uses, if permitted, should be restricted to serious monogenic disease and tightly specified reproductive circumstances;
- **Recommendations 5–6:** stringent preclinical safety/efficacy criteria, embryo evaluation, monitoring, and long-term follow-up;
- **Recommendation 8:** competent regulatory mechanisms, independent assessment, prospective science/ethics review, public transparency, and practice standards before clinical use.

WHO's standing position that proceeding clinically is irresponsible **“at this time”** is important current governance context but should not be represented as an Expert answer to a Scenario that explicitly assumes the relevant technical threshold has later been met.

### Reconstruction implication

Preferred approach: **retain the future conditional Scenario** because it preserves the interesting normative question after technical safety is no longer doing all the work.

Then reconstruct the Expert field around positions that genuinely apply to that future state.

The current evidence may naturally support **one integrated Expert policy** rather than two alternatives:

> Do not authorize clinical HHGE merely because the technical threshold has been met; require prior societal authorization and competent governance, and if a country permits initial clinical use, restrict it to a responsible translational pathway for serious monogenic disease under stringent preclinical review, transparency, monitoring, and long-term follow-up.

This is a coherent professional position, not two artificial slots.

If a second genuinely distinct future-facing Expert position is found in serious governance literature, add it. Otherwise preserve asymmetry.

**Likely natural geometry after current evidence: `2 Public × 1 Expert × 2 Framework`.**

Alternative approach: if the research team wants to preserve a present-day WHO prohibition as a candidate, then the Scenario must become a present-day Scenario and the future-safety stipulation must be removed. Do not mix the two temporal objects.

## F17 — Biomedical animal research: replacement and harm–benefit review

### Confirmed omission / compression

The current Framework pair is:

1. give animal suffering direct moral weight and use animals only when comparable human benefits cannot reasonably be pursued with less harmful methods;
2. animal research can be justified for sufficiently important health benefits when harms are minimized and no adequate alternative exists.

These are different in emphasis but both remain fundamentally **reformist / conditional-permission** positions. The field omits a major serious normative position that the literature explicitly recognizes.

### Source re-open

The Nuffield Council on Bioethics, *The Ethics of Research Involving Animals*, explicitly distinguishes multiple philosophical positions, including an **abolitionist view** under which there is no moral justification for harmful research on sentient animals that is not for their own benefit.

Tom Regan's animal-rights position is explicitly categorical in its implications for harmful animal experimentation: sentient animals possess moral rights that rule out using them as experimental means merely because doing so would benefit others.

This is not an invented extreme for demonstration purposes. It is a canonical, source-supported normative family that is directly relevant to the Scenario.

### Reconstruction implication

Rebuild the Framework pool around genuinely different theories rather than two grades of harm–benefit reasoning.

A strong two-candidate Framework field would be:

- **Consequentialist / equal-consideration position:** animal pain and distress count directly alongside human benefit; harmful animal research may be permissible only when expected benefits are sufficiently important and no less harmful method can achieve comparable benefit.
- **Rights-based abolitionist position:** do not approve harmful non-beneficial experimentation on sentient animals merely for benefits to others; replacement is required because the animal's moral status constrains use independently of aggregate benefit.

Primary theoretical source for the latter should include Tom Regan; the Nuffield report is useful as an explicit map of the abolitionist position and its implications.

This correction can likely retain a **`2 × 2 × 2` natural geometry**, but the Framework candidates would be substantively more faithful and much more analytically informative.

## F18 — Medically important antimicrobials in food-producing animals

### Source re-open

The low-to-medium demonstration richness appears to be substantially **real convergence**, not obvious construction failure.

WHO (2017) strongly recommends:

- overall reduction in medically important antimicrobial use;
- complete restriction for growth promotion;
- complete restriction for prevention of infectious disease not yet clinically diagnosed in healthy animals;
- conditional restrictions on critically important antimicrobials, with veterinary exceptions where needed to avoid animal-health and welfare harm.

WOAH's current prudent-use framework is somewhat more permissive and explicitly permits antimicrobial use for **treatment, control, and prevention** of infectious disease when clinically justified under veterinary responsibility, while rejecting use as compensation for inadequate husbandry and emphasizing vaccination, biosecurity, hygiene, and good husbandry.

### Reconstruction implication

The present Expert distinction between the stricter WHO prevention rule and the WOAH veterinary-stewardship / exceptional-prevention position is genuine.

Do not add a permissive “routine prevention is fine” candidate merely to widen the matrix. F18 is a good calibration example of a case that may be **SACRE-suitable but only moderately demonstration-rich because contemporary serious sources share substantial stewardship commitments.**

## F20 — Increasingly complex neural organoids

### Confirmed source-to-Policy overtranslation risk

The current Public candidates are:

1. continue research with enhanced review for transplantation / unusually long maturation / increased complexity;
2. pause the most complex experiments until welfare thresholds and protections are defined.

The available public evidence does not directly establish those two governance policies.

### Source re-open

Sawai, Koike & Kataoka (2026), Japanese online survey (`n = 326`), reports:

- very high support for basic, disease, treatment, and drug-discovery brain-organoid research (>90% across the research purposes reported);
- high concern about unanticipated risks, commercialization, cloning, and possible future morally relevant capacities;
- a conclusion emphasizing public engagement and informed discussion.

The study does **not** directly survey support for “enhanced review” versus “pause the most complex experiments.”

Inarimori et al. (2026) review 13 empirical stakeholder studies and conclude that stakeholders emphasize commercialization, reproductive cloning, informed consent, and uncertainty about consequences more often than consciousness; again, this maps concerns better than it identifies the two current Policy rules.

Most importantly, the Nuffield Council on Bioethics 2026 neural-organoid report itself recommends **robust additional public engagement** to understand which values should govern the research, acceptable uses, consent expectations, perceived ethical problems, and any public “red lines.” That recommendation is evidence that the present public-policy field is not yet mature enough to treat two specific governance rules as established public positions.

### Reconstruction implication

Do **not** retain the current two Public candidates as though they were directly source-grounded Policy positions.

Two defensible options remain:

1. **Hold the case outside the present SACRE executable collection** while retaining it as a research-complete Bench case with strong Expert and Framework material, pending stronger policy-specific public / affected-community evidence.
2. If further source review identifies one sufficiently direct public governance position, use the natural asymmetric field rather than inventing a second. Broad support for research plus concern is not, by itself, a policy on when enhanced oversight or a pause should trigger.

Given the current evidence, the conservative classification is **research-complete, not yet defensibly executable under the current three-source SACRE specification**.

## Consequences for the calibration standard

The priority re-open confirms that the corrected audit is doing useful work that the previous release checks did not fully capture:

- **F03:** a fixed geometry can split one coherent public procedural preference into two candidate slots.
- **F04:** multiple clauses of one professional procedure can masquerade as separate candidates.
- **F09:** candidates can be individually well sourced yet fail to address the same temporal Scenario.
- **F17:** a familiar limited framework canon can omit a major serious normative family and flatten philosophical disagreement.
- **F18:** low diversity can be genuine consensus and should remain low diversity.
- **F20:** public concerns can be overtranslated into actionable public Policy rules that the evidence did not actually elicit.

These failure modes should now be treated as explicit review questions in the all-200 Bench pass.
