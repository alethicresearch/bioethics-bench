# Bioethics Bench — Scenario, Candidate-Universe, and Projection Audit

**Status:** active completion gate  
**Date:** 2026-08-30  
**Applies to:** Full Corpus completion, future Featured releases, SACRE projections, demonstration selection, RE-Iteration suitability

## Why this audit exists

Bioethics Bench should preserve two things at once:

1. the **rich normative space** around a bioethical Scenario; and
2. the **evidential status** of each represented Policy candidate.

Those are not the same property.

A candidate can be analytically important even when it is not directly source-grounded. Conversely, a source-backed candidate can be redundant, overly compressed, or too cautious to represent a major normative position distinctly.

The Bench therefore separates three layers:

- **Scenario** — the practical normative situation;
- **candidate universe** — the serious Policy positions worth representing;
- **SACRE projection** — an explicit selection/role assignment from that universe for a particular analysis.

This separation prevents a source-completeness rule from becoming an unintended ceiling on the intellectual richness of the resource.

## SACRE relationship

SACRE itself operates on a Scenario and Policy candidates represented in Public, Expert, and Framework roles for the run. A `decision_question` and named decision-maker can remain useful metadata, but they are not formal SACRE primitives.

The Bench may contain more candidates than any one SACRE run uses.

A projection may be source-grounded, expanded, matched for a study, or curated for demonstration. The projection must declare what it is.

## Candidate provenance model

Every candidate should carry a provenance status.

### 1. Direct source

An external source substantially supports the represented Policy itself.

### 2. Source-informed

The source supports values, preferences, concerns, experience, professional reasoning, or adjacent policy structure, and the Bench makes an explicit bounded inference to the represented Policy.

### 3. Framework-derived

The Policy is derived from an identified Ethical Framework through an explicit reasoning bridge.

### 4. Constructed comparator

The Bench deliberately authors a serious, action-distinct Policy because it helps represent an important normative position, boundary, contrast, or experimental condition.

Constructed candidates are legitimate. They must never be presented as though an empirical public, affected community, professional body, or named author directly endorsed the exact Policy.

## Public source mark

The public interface may reduce the provenance complexity to a simple **✓ Source** indicator where external source grounding is defensible.

The check mark is a provenance signal, not a quality score and not a requirement for inclusion in the candidate universe.

Internally, retain the fuller provenance class above.

## Four separate audit questions

### A. Scenario adequacy

Does the Scenario give enough factual, institutional, temporal, and jurisdictional structure for the candidate positions to be interpreted consistently?

### B. Candidate-universe adequacy

Does the case preserve the important serious Policy positions needed to understand the normative space?

### C. Provenance accuracy

Is every candidate's source/provenance status represented truthfully?

### D. Projection validity

Does a declared SACRE projection make clear which candidates it uses, what roles they occupy, and what claims can legitimately be made from the result?

A case can pass A–C even when no canonical source-grounded SACRE projection is available.

## What counts as a substantively distinct Policy position

Distinctness should be judged at the level of normative commitment and practical implication. Useful axes include:

- recommended action: permit, require, prohibit, fund, withdraw, disclose, defer, prioritize;
- authority: patient, parent, clinician, institution, court, regulator, community, reviewer;
- threshold: when a position applies, escalates, changes, or permits an exception;
- scope: universal, targeted, discretionary, restricted-setting;
- allocation principle: need, benefit, equality, lottery, priority, desert, instrumental value;
- sequence: voluntary first, staged escalation, immediate mandate, reassessment;
- procedure: review, appeal, consultation, shared decision-making, authorization;
- exception structure: categorical rule versus defined exception or balancing rule;
- time horizon: immediate, delayed, reviewable, prospective;
- burden distribution: who bears risk, cost, restriction, sacrifice, or responsibility;
- normative commitment: positions that may coincide here but diverge under nearby conditions.

Different wording is not enough. Different sources are not enough. Different rationales are not enough if the represented Policy remains practically identical.

## Candidate-universe failure modes

### Near-paraphrase

Two candidates differ in wording but express substantially the same Policy.

### Nested-caveat duplication

One candidate is another plus a modest safeguard or procedural clause without a materially different commitment.

### Source-slot completion

A candidate exists because an editor wanted a fixed number of Public, Expert, or Framework items rather than because it adds a serious distinct position.

### Source-ceiling compression

An important normative position is omitted merely because direct source evidence for it is weak or absent, even though the candidate could be transparently represented as constructed or inferred.

### Decision-question overfitting

The candidate universe is forced to mirror an editorial framing question rather than the underlying normative problem.

### Constraint-induced collapse

Benchmark stipulations remove so much of the live normative structure that major positions become indistinguishable.

### Evidence-to-policy overcompression

Distinct source positions are translated into one cautious institutional compromise.

### Rationale diversity without Policy diversity

Different theories or sources all produce effectively the same candidate sentence.

### Position merging

Several important positions are collapsed into a synthetic compromise for neatness.

### Artificial extremity

A constructed comparator is written as a caricature merely to create large disagreement.

### Provenance laundering

A constructed or inferred Policy is visually or textually presented as though directly source-backed.

## Scenario audit

Ask:

1. Does the Scenario contain enough context for consistent interpretation?
2. Does any stipulation silently decide the normative question?
3. Do concise and detailed forms preserve the same normative Scenario?
4. Is the framing question helping comprehension rather than dictating candidate construction?
5. Are all candidates addressing the same temporal and institutional state?

## Candidate-universe audit

Ask:

1. What are the major serious Policy positions for this Scenario?
2. Which are externally sourced?
3. Which require bounded source-informed inference?
4. Which are Framework-derived?
5. Which important positions remain worth representing as constructed comparators?
6. Has a major serious normative family been omitted because it lacked a convenient source?
7. Have distinct positions been merged?
8. Are any candidates near-paraphrases, nested variants, or rhetorical extremes?
9. Is the level of abstraction reasonably comparable?
10. Would an informed reader recognize the universe as a fair map of the important normative alternatives?

The audit does **not** require every imaginable option to be included. Richness means adequate normative coverage, not combinatorial exhaustiveness.

## Source/provenance audit

For every candidate:

1. Is the ✓ Source state correct?
2. If direct-source, does the source materially support the Policy itself?
3. If source-informed, is the inferential bridge explicit and reasonable?
4. If Framework-derived, does the cited framework actually support the normative commitment used?
5. If constructed, is that construction clearly disclosed?
6. Is any source being used outside the scope of what it actually establishes?

## SACRE projection audit

A projection must state:

- projection name/version;
- candidate IDs selected;
- Public / Expert / Framework role assignment;
- provenance state of each selected candidate;
- geometry;
- aggregation rule;
- study/demonstration purpose;
- whether the projection supports claims about actual represented source positions or includes constructed comparators.

### Source-grounded projection

A projection intended to measure convergence among represented Public, Expert, and Framework source positions must use candidates with defensible grounding for those roles.

### Expanded projection

An expanded projection may include constructed or inferred comparators if they are explicitly labeled. The result then concerns the represented expanded normative field, not empirical consensus among the named source communities.

### Matched-study projection

A controlled study may select a fixed geometry from the candidate universe. The geometry is an experimental design property, not the ontology of the Bench.

### Demonstration projection

A public demonstration may select especially legible candidates, but must preserve provenance status and should not imply that omitted candidates do not exist.

## Demonstration richness

Demonstration richness is separate from validity.

A case or projection is especially demonstration-rich when it exposes several meaningful relations, such as:

- different actions or institutional rules;
- disagreement on multiple normative axes;
- mixtures of convergence and conflict;
- a meaningful opportunity for RE-Iteration;
- a Scenario whose trade-offs are understandable to readers.

A genuinely convergent source-grounded projection can be scientifically valuable even if an expanded projection is more useful for explaining SACRE.

## Do not force disagreement — but do not erase it either

Two opposite errors are possible.

**Artificial diversity:** inventing caricatures or false source positions to make the matrix interesting.

**Artificial compression:** omitting serious positions because the sources converge, or translating distinct positions into one cautious consensus formulation.

The candidate-universe model avoids both: preserve the source evidence accurately and separately preserve important serious comparators with honest provenance.

## Featured calibration and all-200 audit

The 20 released Featured cases are the calibration set. Use them to identify:

- where the released source-grounded candidate field is already rich;
- where two sourced candidates are effectively duplicates;
- where a major serious position is missing from the candidate universe;
- where an expanded projection would be useful even if the source-grounded projection is narrow;
- where the current record has a genuine Scenario/provenance defect requiring a new explicit version.

Then apply the same audit to **all 200 planned cases before final Full Corpus freeze**.

A previous executable label, valid JSON, resolved citations, or passing regression suite does not exempt a case from this review.

## RE-Iteration implication

RE-Iteration should be tested on more than one projection type.

A narrow source-grounded projection and a richer expanded projection may legitimately yield different revision behavior. That difference is itself scientifically useful and should be studied before diagnosing the revision prompt as defective.

The current RE-Iteration prompt remains historically stable in its core instruction to preserve the candidate's essential normative commitment and source perspective while making the smallest sufficient revision.

## Public-language rule

Public pages should not read like this audit document.

Prefer ordinary language:

- **Scenario** or **case**;
- **Policy candidates** or **positions**;
- **✓ Source** for visibly grounded candidates;
- **constructed** only when a reader opens provenance/details;
- **analysis set** or **SACRE set** rather than *projection* where technical precision is unnecessary;
- concise and detailed versions of the same case.

Do not expose internal release gates, calibration stages, audit queues, or implementation terminology on the main public-facing page unless a reader explicitly opens methods/provenance material.
