# Master Case Inventory — M051–M100

**Genetics and embryo research · human-subject research · research governance · public health · scarcity**

Inventory IDs are editorial handles, not frozen Bench case IDs. `C/M/F` = canonical/core, methodologically informative, frontier/contemporary.

## Genetics, embryo research and reproductive biotechnology

| ID | Provisional case | Decision to represent | Position families | Anchors / use | Main construction issue |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **M051** | Heritable human genome editing to prevent serious disease | Should regulators permit clinical heritable genome editing once specified safety thresholds are met? | Permit for serious disease under international oversight; research only/no reproductive use; permit only when no reasonable reproductive alternative exists | A14 · **C,F,M** · v0#23 | Separate present safety uncertainty from the deeper question of whether heritable editing should ever become permissible. |
| **M052** | Polygenic embryo screening for non-disease traits | Should fertility clinics offer embryo ranking using polygenic scores for traits such as height, cognition proxies or educational attainment? | Prohibit non-medical ranking; permit informed reproductive choice; permit only validated disease-risk applications | A10,A14 · **F,M** · v0#24 | Fast-moving evidence; specify predictive validity and avoid treating contested social traits as simple biological endpoints. |
| **M053** | Genetic enhancement beyond disease prevention | If a safe germline intervention could enhance a trait beyond the typical range, should regulators permit its reproductive use? | Permit reproductive liberty; prohibit enhancement; permit only therapeutic/normal-range restoration | A01,A14 · **C,F** · **LEGACY:genetic-enhancement** · v0#25 | The legacy version was abstract. A future record needs a concrete regulator/clinic decision and a specific trait. |
| **M054** | Secondary findings in clinical genomic sequencing | What policy should a health system use for medically actionable findings unrelated to the original reason for sequencing? | Routine return with opt-out; return only with prior opt-in; restrict to high-actionability findings under expert criteria | A15 · **C,M** · v0#26 | Specify adult/minor context and actionability; do not collapse patient preference and professional duty. |
| **M055** | Direct-to-consumer genetics and relatives' interests | When a consumer genetic result has important implications for relatives, what disclosure/support obligations should the testing service have? | Consumer controls; encourage mediated disclosure; permit/require narrow warning pathways for serious preventable harm | A15,A30 · **F,M** · v0#27 | Distinguish company's duty from clinician duty and account for probabilistic rather than deterministic findings. |
| **M056** | Extending the 14-day limit for human embryo research | Should law/oversight permit culture of human embryos beyond 14 days for defined high-value research? | Retain strict limit; extend to a new fixed limit with specialized review; replace fixed day limit with staged case-by-case oversight | A11,A12 · **C,F,M** | One of the strongest contemporary governance cases; scenario should stipulate what research becomes possible and what oversight exists. |
| **M057** | Stem-cell-based embryo models | What oversight should apply to increasingly complete human stem-cell-based embryo models? | Specialized oversight for all 3D models; tiered oversight by developmental potential; restrict specified high-integration/end-point experiments | A11,A12 · **F,M** | Use current ISSCR terminology and do not imply embryo models are embryos; define the model capability that triggers the decision. |
| **M058** | In vitro gametogenesis for reproduction | If IVG becomes sufficiently safe, under what conditions should clinical use be permitted? | Permit broadly with standard ART safeguards; initial restricted indications/registry; prohibit reproductive use pending stronger social evidence | A13 · **F,M** | Future-facing; scenario must explicitly stipulate safety/effectiveness so the ethical question is not merely premature technology adoption. |
| **M059** | Mitochondrial donation | Should regulators permit mitochondrial replacement to avoid serious mitochondrial disease? | Permit for serious disease with follow-up; restrict to research/specified indications; prohibit germline reproductive modification | A11,A14 · **C,F** | Must distinguish mitochondrial donation from nuclear genome editing and state inheritance/follow-up implications. |
| **M060** | Non-medical sex selection in IVF | Should fertility programs permit embryo sex selection when no sex-linked disease is at issue? | Permit reproductive choice; decline non-medical selection; allow limited family-balancing policy | A10 · **C,M** | ASRM explicitly identifies the practice as ethically controversial; avoid assuming all preferences arise from sex discrimination. |
| **M061** | Compensation and autonomy in gestational surrogacy | What policy should govern compensation and medical decision-making in gestational-carrier arrangements? | Permit compensation with independent counsel and carrier control; permit only expense reimbursement; prohibit commercial arrangements | A10 · **C,M** | Keep two questions linked but not conflated: compensation/exploitation and the carrier's sole authority over her medical care. |
| **M062** | Intrafamilial gamete donation or gestational carrying | When should fertility programs accept arrangements involving adult family members? | Permit with enhanced counseling; prohibit specified kinship/coercion patterns; decline all intrafamilial arrangements | A10 · **C,M** | Strong coercion/relational-autonomy case; specify relationship and avoid simulated-incest edge cases unless that is the intended decision. |
| **M063** | Reproductive cloning | If reproductive cloning became technically safe enough for clinical consideration, should it be permitted? | Prohibit; permit under reproductive-liberty safeguards; permit only narrow infertility indications | A11,A14 · **C,F** | A classic practical-ethics problem but hypothetical safety assumptions must be explicit. |
| **M064** | Ectogenesis / artificial-womb support | If partial or more complete ectogenesis becomes clinically feasible, what eligibility and parental decision rules should govern its use? | Offer as neonatal/reproductive technology under consent; restrict to rescue of extreme prematurity; broader reproductive use only after separate social review | A11,A13 · **F,M** | Distinguish partial ectogenesis for prematurity from complete gestational substitution. |
| **M065** | Embryo selection to create an HLA-matched sibling donor | May parents use PGT to select an embryo partly so the resulting child can provide compatible tissue for an existing sick sibling? | Permit with limits on donor-child burdens; prohibit instrumental selection; permit only when child-donor interventions stay minimal risk | A10 · **C,M** | Excellent nontrivial case if the anticipated donation type is specified. |
| **M066** | Prenatal whole-genome sequencing and incidental findings | What information should clinicians offer/return when prenatal sequencing produces uncertain or adult-onset findings unrelated to the indication? | Broad return with parental choice; restricted clinically actionable prenatal findings; defer adult-onset/uncertain findings to protect future autonomy | A15 · **F,M** | The meaning of `actionable` differs before birth; state analytical validity and uncertainty. |
| **M067** | Fertility preservation for minors facing gonadotoxic therapy | When should clinicians offer invasive fertility-preservation procedures to a child who cannot fully consent? | Offer when future benefit plausible with assent; restrict to low-burden established options; defer experimental procedures to research | A10 · **C,M** | Specify treatment urgency and procedural burden so `preserve future options` is not costless. |
| **M068** | Transfer of an embryo known to carry a serious monogenic condition | Should an IVF clinic honor a patient's request to transfer an embryo predicted to develop a serious genetic disorder? | Respect reproductive choice; decline transfer for serious predictable harm; case-by-case severity/probability threshold | A10 · **F,M** | ASRM 2025 opinion creates strong source architecture; severity and penetrance must be stipulated. |
| **M069** | Disclosure of errors involving gametes or embryos | What should an ART program disclose after a gamete/embryo loss, damage, mix-up or clinically relevant near miss? | Full clinically significant disclosure; disclose all errors/near misses; staged disclosure tied to confirmed facts and materiality | A10 · **C,M** | Distinctive because errors may implicate identity, reproductive plans and future offspring, not only immediate physical harm. |
| **M070** | Equitable access to curative somatic gene therapy | When an approved one-time gene therapy is extremely expensive and capacity-limited, how should a public program decide access? | Clinical benefit priority; severity/worst-off priority; lottery after eligibility; cost-effectiveness constrained allocation | A14,A33 · **F,M** | Bridges genetics and allocation; use a hypothetical price/capacity rather than a named drug if durability changes quickly. |

## Human-subject research and research governance

| ID | Provisional case | Decision to represent | Position families | Anchors / use | Main construction issue |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **M071** | Placebo control when effective treatment exists | May investigators use placebo rather than best proven therapy when placebo would improve scientific interpretability? | Active control only; placebo under Helsinki-style compelling-methodology/no-serious-harm exception; placebo with rescue/short duration | A03,A04 · **C,M** · **LEGACY:placebo-trial** · v0#32 | Preserve the conditions that make placebo genuinely contested rather than obviously impermissible. |
| **M072** | Controlled human infection study | Should an ethics committee approve deliberate infection of healthy volunteers for an important infectious-disease question? | Approve under stringent criteria; approve only when effective rescue treatment exists; reject intentional infection above a risk threshold | A05 · **C,M** · v0#33 | Excellent research-ethics benchmark because WHO explicitly treats CHIS as potentially acceptable under conditions. |
| **M073** | Emergency research without prospective consent | Should an emergency trial enroll incapacitated patients before surrogate consent can be obtained? | Exception from consent with community/independent safeguards; surrogate consent only; deferred consent with narrow risk/urgency criteria | A03,A04 · **C,M** · v0#34 | Specify why prospective consent is infeasible and whether treatment is time-critical. |
| **M074** | Control standard in a lower-resource setting | May a trial use the local standard of care as comparator when a more effective intervention is routinely available in wealthy settings but not locally accessible? | Best global proven standard; local standard if responsive/socially valuable with fair benefits; intermediate enhanced standard | A04 · **C,M** · v0#35 | A canonical exploitation/social-value case; avoid falsely treating `local standard` as ethically neutral. |
| **M075** | Broad consent for biobank research | What consent model should a biobank use for future research that cannot yet be specified? | Broad consent + governance; dynamic/recontact consent; specific consent/opt-out for residual materials under defined conditions | A04 · **C,M** · v0#36 | CIOMS makes clear broad consent is bounded, not blanket consent; governance details belong in scenario. |
| **M076** | Payment to healthy research participants | How should an ethics committee judge high payment for a burdensome but scientifically valuable study? | Allow market-level compensation with risk limits; cap payment to avoid undue influence; structure payment by time/burden with independent review | A04 · **C,M** | Separate payment from permissible risk: high payment must not make excessive risk acceptable. |
| **M077** | Pediatric research with no direct benefit | When may children be enrolled in research that offers them no prospect of direct clinical benefit? | Minimal-risk only; minor increase over minimal risk for important child-health knowledge; broader research with stringent assent/permission and social-value criteria | A04 · **C,M** | Define risk and scientific necessity rather than using vague `low risk`. |
| **M078** | Inclusion of pregnant people in clinical trials | Should a trial actively include pregnant participants when evidence is limited and exclusion perpetuates evidence gaps? | Presumptive inclusion with pregnancy-specific safeguards; staged inclusion after nonpregnant data; exclude where fetal uncertainty exceeds threshold | A03,A04 · **C,M** | Avoid treating exclusion as automatically protective; specify preclinical/clinical evidence. |
| **M079** | Research involving prisoners | Under what conditions should incarcerated people be eligible for a study relevant to their health needs? | Allow with enhanced protections and relevance; restrict to minimal risk/direct benefit; categorical exclusion from specified trials | A04 · **C,M** | Coercive environment and fair access cut in opposite directions. |
| **M080** | Post-trial access to a beneficial intervention | What obligations do sponsors/researchers have to participants who still need an intervention shown beneficial in the trial? | Guarantee continuing access; time-limited transition to health system; no special entitlement beyond pre-agreed fair-benefit plan | A03,A04 · **C,M** | Use a setting where access after trial is realistically uncertain, especially low-resource settings. |
| **M081** | Publication of negative and inconclusive trial results | What policy should institutions/sponsors enforce when completed human research produces commercially unfavorable or null results? | Mandatory timely public reporting; registry summary plus publication discretion; publication obligations proportionate to social value/confidentiality | A03 · **C,M** | Helsinki 2024 makes nonpublication hard to defend; preserve a real disagreement by focusing on timing/detail/proprietary data rather than whether to hide safety results. |
| **M082** | Cluster-randomized public-health trial and community authorization | When an intervention is assigned by school, village or clinic, what combination of individual consent and community engagement is required? | Individual consent wherever feasible; gatekeeper/community authorization + opt-out; waiver for low-risk system interventions with public notice | A04 · **C,M** | Identify which components are research interventions versus routine policy. |
| **M083** | Return of individual research findings | When research sequencing reveals a potentially actionable finding, what should investigators offer participants? | Return validated actionable findings with consent; return only findings specified at enrollment; no individual return unless study is clinically certified | A04,A15 · **C,M** | Distinguish research validity, clinical confirmation and participants' right not to know. |
| **M084** | Sponsor control over publication | May a commercial sponsor delay or constrain publication to protect legitimate IP/confidentiality interests? | Investigators retain timely publication right; short defined review/delay; broader sponsor control under contract | A03 · **C,M** | Use a concrete maximum delay; avoid an obviously unethical indefinite suppression option. |
| **M085** | First-in-human high-risk gene/cell therapy | Should an ethics committee authorize first-in-human intervention when animal evidence is promising but irreversible harms remain uncertain? | Proceed in severely affected participants under enhanced oversight; require stronger preclinical evidence; restrict to participants with no alternatives and staged dosing | A11,A14 · **C,F,M** | Strong uncertainty case; define disease severity, reversibility and monitoring. |
| **M086** | Scarce trial slots versus expanded access | When an experimental therapy is scarce, should available doses go to randomized trial participants or desperate patients outside the trial? | Trial priority to generate knowledge; expanded-access priority for worst-off; split allocation with transparent quotas | A03,A04 · **C,M** | Distinct from M011 because scarcity creates a direct conflict between care and evidence generation. |
| **M087** | Sham surgery as a control | May a trial expose participants to anesthesia/incision without therapeutic intent to establish whether an invasive procedure works? | Permit if necessary and low incremental risk; active/usual-care control only; permit with enhanced consent and rescue criteria | A03,A04 · **C,M** | Specify why blinding matters scientifically and the actual sham risk. |
| **M088** | Adaptive platform trial during a public-health emergency | Should an emergency research program use adaptive randomization/drop arms rapidly, accepting greater design complexity for speed and learning? | Adaptive platform; conventional fixed trial; adaptive design only with predefined independent oversight/equipoise triggers | A02,A03 · **F,M** | Ethical issue is not statistics alone: changing allocation probabilities affects participants and public trust. |
| **M089** | Setting a national health-research agenda | How should a funder allocate a fixed research budget between high-burden diseases, neglected populations, transformative science and near-term implementation research? | Burden/expected health gain; worst-off/neglect priority; participatory portfolio with reserved exploratory funding | A06 · **C,M** | Excellent structural case because WHO explicitly frames research priority setting as distributive ethics. |
| **M090** | Community governance of genomic research | When a genomic cohort concerns an Indigenous or historically exploited community, what authority should community governance have over secondary use and sharing? | Individual consent sufficient; community governance/collective veto for specified uses; layered individual + community stewardship | A15,A30 · **C,F,M** | Avoid generic `Indigenous` scenario; final case should be co-designed or use a clearly fictionalized governance setting grounded in real frameworks. |
| **M091** | Environmental footprint of a clinical trial | Should funders require a lower-carbon trial design when it increases cost or reduces some logistical convenience but preserves scientific validity? | Mandatory emissions criterion; scientific/participant welfare priority with voluntary mitigation; proportional requirement based on avoidable emissions | A08,A34,A35 · **F,M** | New WHO 2026 work makes this a timely research-governance case rather than a generic climate question. |
| **M092** | Dual-use pathogen research | Should an institution authorize research likely to yield major health knowledge but also increase capabilities for accidental or deliberate biological harm? | Proceed under layered risk mitigation; modify aims/methods; do not conduct above a biorisk threshold | A07 · **C,F,M** | State benefits and misuse pathways at a level that supports ethical comparison without reproducing dangerous technical detail. |
| **M093** | Publication of dual-use methods | After ethically approved research produces information with plausible misuse potential, how much methodological detail should a journal release? | Full publication; redacted/controlled-access detail; do not publish dangerous enabling information | A07 · **C,F,M** | Keep discussion at governance level; never place operationally harmful procedural details into the Bench scenario. |

## Public health and scarcity

| ID | Provisional case | Decision to represent | Position families | Anchors / use | Main construction issue |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **M094** | Vaccination mandate during a serious outbreak | Under what conditions should a government or institution require vaccination for a defined group? | Mandatory vaccination with exemptions; conditional mandate only after necessity/effectiveness threshold; voluntary program with strong access/education | A20 · **C,M** · v0#37 | Specify disease severity, transmission effect and vaccine safety/effectiveness; otherwise mandate ethics floats free of facts. |
| **M095** | Quarantine or isolation for infectious risk | When may public health authorities compel isolation of a person who poses a substantial transmission risk? | Compulsory isolation as last resort; supported voluntary isolation first with legal backstop; no coercion absent extreme/imminent risk | A21,A22 · **C,M** · v0#38 | State infectiousness, available support, duration and less restrictive alternatives. |
| **M096** | Expansion of newborn screening | Should a newborn-screening program add a condition when early identification may help some children but evidence or treatment benefit is uncertain? | Add broadly; add only when effective early intervention exists; pilot/research pathway before population screening | A02 · **C,M** · v0#39 | A strong screening-criteria case if condition characteristics are stipulated rather than named and assumed. |
| **M097** | Supervised consumption / harm-reduction site | Should a city establish supervised consumption services for people who use high-risk drugs? | Establish as harm reduction; do not establish and prioritize abstinence/treatment; conditional pilot with evaluation and community safeguards | A02 · **C,M** · v0#40 | Include evidence assumptions about overdose prevention and neighborhood effects without turning the scenario into advocacy. |
| **M098** | Ventilator triage during catastrophic scarcity | When not all patients can receive ventilation, what allocation rule should the health system use? | Maximize near-term survival; priority to worst-off/young life-cycle or other equity criterion; lottery among clinically eligible patients; multiprinciple rule | A17 · **C,M** · **LEGACY:pandemic-triage** · v0#42 | One of the best RE-Iteration cases: explicit criteria can be revised after disability/equity challenge and rerun. |
| **M099** | Public reimbursement of a very high-cost therapy | Should a health system fund a therapy with substantial benefit for a small group when doing so displaces other effective services? | Fund based on severity/rarity; apply ordinary cost-effectiveness threshold; negotiate/conditional coverage with evidence development | A33 · **C,M** · v0#43 | Use a hypothetical therapy and explicit opportunity cost rather than making a named pricing controversy go stale. |
| **M100** | Routine ICU admission under ordinary scarcity | When ICU beds are scarce but not in disaster conditions, how should competing patients be prioritized? | Expected benefit; urgency/worst-off; time-limited trial + reassessment; lottery among similarly eligible patients | A17 · **C,M** · v0#44 | Important complement to pandemic triage: tests whether emergency moral intuitions carry into everyday rationing. |

## Notes for the next research pass

The embryo/genetics group deliberately preserves both canonical and frontier questions. The research-ethics group is broader than the original longlist because WMA 2024, CIOMS and WHO's newer work provide concrete, globally relevant decision problems beyond placebo and consent.

M098 should remain a priority for P2/RE-Iteration demonstration research, but it should not be allowed to define the collection. The master inventory now treats scarcity as one domain among many rather than the implicit center of applied bioethics.
