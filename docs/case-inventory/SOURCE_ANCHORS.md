# Master Case Inventory — Research Anchor Map

These are **anchor source families**, not substitutes for case-level research. They establish that the master inventory is built against real guidance, scholarship, policy, casebooks and documented controversies rather than from free association. Every final case receives a smaller, case-specific bibliography and provenance record.

For teaching/casebook discovery sources, see [`../CASEBOOK_SOURCE_MAP.md`](../CASEBOOK_SOURCE_MAP.md).

## General practical ethics and bioethics

**A01 — Practical Ethics / PSai course map.** `alethicresearch/psai`, especially `content/course.json` and the lecture summaries. Covers ethical theories; death and brain death; abortion; killing/letting die and double effect; disabled and premature infants; euthanasia; global poverty; climate change and geoengineering; animals and animal research; food systems; biodiversity, feral animals and predation. Used as a map of established practical-ethics questions and blind spots, not as independent evidence for a policy candidate.

**A02 — WHO Health Ethics & Governance.** Cross-cutting WHO ethics work across research, outbreaks, emerging technologies, disease-specific ethics, AI, genome editing, climate/health and surveillance.

## Clinical research and research governance

**A03 — WMA Declaration of Helsinki, 2024 revision.** Current international research-ethics declaration, including placebo use, post-trial provisions, registration/publication of negative and inconclusive results, and unproven interventions in clinical practice.

**A04 — CIOMS, _International Ethical Guidelines for Health-related Research Involving Humans_, 2016 (republished CC in 2025).** Social value, low-resource settings, vulnerable groups, biological samples, health data, broad consent and governance.

**A05 — WHO controlled human infection study guidance, 2022; WHO emergency CHIS criteria, 2025.** Intentional infection, risk, rescue treatment, social value, participant selection and emergency context.

**A06 — WHO guidance on ethics of health-research priority setting, 2025.** Research agenda setting as distributive choice over scarce potential benefits.

**A07 — WHO global guidance framework for responsible use of the life sciences, 2022.** Biorisk and dual-use governance across researchers, institutions, funders, publishers, regulators and industry.

**A08 — WHO, _Clinical trials and environmental sustainability_, 2026.** Climate mitigation/adaptation across trial design and conduct.

## Reproduction, embryos and genetics

**A09 — WHO Abortion Care Guideline, 2022.** Clinical, service-delivery, law and policy recommendations across varying jurisdictions.

**A10 — ASRM Ethics Committee opinions.** Posthumous gamete/embryo use; donor-conceived persons; gestational carriers; intrafamilial donation; unclaimed embryos; non-medical sex selection; transfer of embryos affected by monogenic conditions; ART error disclosure and related reproductive ethics.

**A11 — ISSCR Guidelines for Stem Cell Research and Clinical Translation, 2025 v1.2.** Embryos, stem-cell-based embryo models, chimeras, organoids, genome editing and related research; 2025 update revises embryo-model governance.

**A12 — Nuffield Council on Bioethics, review of the 14-day rule, 2025–26.** Scientific, ethical and public-dialogue work on extending human embryo culture limits.

**A13 — Nuffield Council on Bioethics, _In vitro gametogenesis: A review of ethical and policy questions_, 2025.** Safety, genetic relatedness, family formation, consent, equity and regulation.

**A14 — WHO human genome editing governance framework and recommendations, 2021.** Somatic, germline and heritable editing; international oversight, registries, medical travel, IP and public engagement.

**A15 — WHO, _Guidance for human genome data collection, access, use and sharing_, 2024.** Ethical, legal and equitable genomic-data governance, including collective interests.

## Death, critical care and allocation

**A16 — AAN/AAP/CNS/SCCM Pediatric and Adult Brain Death/Death by Neurologic Criteria Consensus Practice Guideline, 2023.** Standardized determination, communication, ancillary testing and contested areas.

**A17 — WHO ethics guidance on resource allocation and priority setting.** Equality, best outcomes, worst-off, reciprocity/instrumental value, fair process and global allocation.

**A18 — WHO transplantation work and 2026 draft Global Strategy on Donation and Transplantation.** Access disparities, donation, transplantation, trafficking, global governance and emerging alternatives.

**A19 — OPTN Ethics Committee white papers.** Allocation principles, candidacy, living non-directed donation, multi-organ transplantation, continuous distribution, transparency and normothermic regional perfusion.

## Public health, infectious disease and surveillance

**A20 — WHO mandatory-vaccination ethics, 2022.** Necessity, effectiveness, proportionality, exemptions, trust and transparency.

**A21 — WHO ethics of public-health surveillance, 2024.** Trust, legitimate purpose, secondary use, law-enforcement access, identifiable data and oversight.

**A22 — WHO TB ethics guidance / End TB ethics guidance.** Voluntary treatment, adherence support, involuntary isolation as last resort, migrants/prisoners, non-abandonment and palliative care.

**A23 — WHO ethics and vector-borne diseases, 2020; WHO genetically modified mosquito guidance, 2021.** Community engagement, health/environmental risk, governance and field testing.

**A24 — WHO antimicrobial-resistance work, including 2025 global call to action.** Stewardship, preservation of effectiveness, system obligations and future populations.

## Mental health, disability and neuroethics

**A25 — WHO/OHCHR, _Mental health, human rights and legislation: guidance and practice_, 2023.** Rights-based law reform, supported decision-making, deinstitutionalization and coercion.

**A26 — Nuffield Council on Bioethics, _Neural organoids: Ethical and governance considerations_, 2026.** Governance gaps and increasing complexity of human neural organoids.

**A27 — UNESCO neurotechnology ethics work and 2025 Recommendation process.** Mental privacy, autonomy, identity, dignity and human–computer interfaces.

## AI, digital health and data

**A28 — WHO, _Ethics and governance of artificial intelligence for health_, 2021.** Autonomy, safety, transparency, accountability, equity, sustainability and governance across design/deployment/use.

**A29 — WHO current guidance on AI/large multimodal models for health.** General-purpose/generative model risks, governance, deployment and accountability.

**A30 — GA4GH Consent Policy.** Consent and responsible international sharing of genomic and related health data.

## Global and structural health ethics

**A31 — WHO Global Code of Practice on International Recruitment of Health Personnel, amended 2026.** Worker mobility and rights, source-country health-system impacts, ethical recruitment and co-investment.

**A32 — WHO refugee and migrant health work, including 2026 global report.** Equitable access, migration status, discrimination, continuity of care and UHC.

**A33 — WHO/WIPO/WTO work on access to medical technologies, IP and trade.** Innovation incentives, generics, neglected diseases and equitable access.

## Climate, environment and animals

**A34 — WHO Ethics in Health and Climate Change workstream, 2023–26.** Priority setting, vulnerable populations, acceptable trade-offs, health-system decisions and regionally diverse case-study development.

**A35 — WHO low-carbon sustainable health-system guidance, 2024–25.** Decarbonization targets, health-sector emissions and sustainability.

**A36 — Nuffield Council on Bioethics, _The ethics of research involving animals_, 2005.** Ethical framework for animal research, alternatives, harms and benefits; pair with current welfare/3Rs standards when a case is built.

**A37 — PSai animal/environment modules.** Moral status of animals, equal consideration, speciesism, animals in research, factory farming, environmental effects of food, endangered species, biodiversity, feral animals and wild-animal suffering. Discovery map, not candidate evidence.

## Case-level research rule

Before an inventory topic becomes an executable case:

1. identify the actual decision-maker and decision;
2. assemble case-specific primary/professional sources plus serious normative literature;
3. identify public-preference evidence where it exists, without inventing consensus;
4. distinguish empirical facts from expert policy recommendations;
5. derive framework recommendations explicitly rather than presenting theories as if they literally authored a candidate sentence;
6. specify jurisdiction/time when it matters;
7. separate factual uncertainty from normative disagreement;
8. build under `CASE_CONSTRUCTION_STANDARD.md`;
9. independently review scenario and candidate provenance before release.
