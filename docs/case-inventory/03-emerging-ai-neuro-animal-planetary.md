# Why these boundary domains belong in the master inventory

The Bench should not silently equate `bioethics` with individual decisions inside affluent hospitals. WHO's current ethics work explicitly spans climate and health, surveillance, migration, emerging technology and global governance. The Practical Ethics course likewise treats human health, animals, food, climate and global poverty as connected questions about whose interests count and how institutions should act.

That does **not** mean every boundary case belongs in a given Featured release. It means the master inventory preserves them for research rather than excluding them before testing whether SACRE can represent the affected interests faithfully.

Animal/One Health cases are especially methodologically useful. If the public/expert/framework architecture cannot represent nonhuman interests without forcing every candidate through a human-preference proxy, that is a finding about the method and its source design—not a reason to hide the problem by leaving animals out of the corpus.
