# Master Case Inventory — M176–M200

**Everyday clinical ethics · professional roles · primary care · geriatric and interprofessional cases**

These entries are deliberately less dominated by headline controversies. They are informed by recurring problem structures in clinical ethics casebooks, especially Sugarman's *20 Common Problems: Ethics in Primary Care*, Veatch/Haddad/English, Jonsen/Siegler/Winslade, Vaughn, and geriatric/interprofessional casebooks. The eventual Bench scenarios must still be independently researched and reconstructed.

Inventory IDs are editorial handles, not frozen Bench case IDs. `C/M/F` = canonical/core, methodologically informative, frontier/contemporary.

| ID | Provisional case | Decision to represent | Position families | Casebook/use | Main construction issue |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **M176** | Patient requests an unnecessary diagnostic test | When a patient strongly requests imaging/laboratory testing with very low expected clinical value but some downstream harm/cost, should the clinician order it? | Decline and explain; offer only after shared decision under explicit uncertainty; order when reassurance value plausibly outweighs harms | B01,B03 · **C,M** | Avoid a clearly useless test; specify false-positive/downstream risk and the reason for the request. |
| **M177** | Patient requests a medically inappropriate treatment | How should a clinician respond to a requested intervention that has little expected benefit but meaningful risk and is not professionally indicated? | Refuse nonbeneficial treatment; negotiate a lower-risk alternative; provide if informed patient preference falls within a reasonable professional range | B01,B03 · **C,M** | Distinguish `not recommended` from `outside acceptable practice`; otherwise the answer is predetermined. |
| **M178** | Request for a medical exemption or certification not supported by evidence | Should a clinician sign a school/work/travel exemption when the patient strongly desires it but the medical criteria are not met? | Decline inaccurate certification; document relevant limitations without endorsing exemption; broader patient-advocacy discretion when criteria are genuinely ambiguous | B01 · **C,M** | Excellent honesty/fiduciary-role case if criteria are not made completely black-and-white. |
| **M179** | Complementary therapy alongside effective conventional treatment | When a patient wants an alternative therapy with uncertain benefit but low direct risk, how should the clinician respond? | Respect use while monitoring; discourage and recommend evidence-based care only; integrate if it does not replace effective treatment and interactions are addressed | B01,B03 · **C,M** | Need a specific therapy/evidence level; avoid lumping all complementary medicine together. |
| **M180** | Alternative therapy replacing effective treatment | When a capacitated patient wants to stop a proven treatment in favor of an unproven alternative, what should the clinician do? | Respect refusal after robust informed consent; continue relationship and harm-reduction monitoring; consider ending non-emergency treatment relationship if demands conflict with professional integrity | B01,B03 · **C,M** | Keep respect for refusal separate from duty to provide the requested alternative. |
| **M181** | Recurrent nonadherence to chronic disease treatment | How should a care team respond when a patient's repeated nonadherence creates preventable risk but is driven partly by cost, work, housing or treatment burden? | Supportive barrier-focused care; stronger structured adherence conditions for high-risk treatment; preserve patient choice while changing goals/regimen | B01,B03,B07 · **C,M** | Do not moralize adherence; explicitly represent structural causes. |
| **M182** | Suspected elder abuse when the patient resists intervention | When a capacitated older adult appears to be experiencing abuse but declines reporting or separation from the suspected abuser, what should the team do? | Respect refusal with safety planning; report/intervene where statutory threshold applies; intensive multidisciplinary assessment before honoring refusal | B01,B05 · **C,M** | Jurisdiction-specific mandatory reporting can decide the legal question; either stipulate law or frame institutional policy within lawful options. |
| **M183** | Suspected child abuse with uncertain evidence | When findings raise concern but are not diagnostic of abuse, when should clinicians report to child protection authorities? | Report on reasonable suspicion; further multidisciplinary evaluation before reporting if safe; disclose concern/support family while meeting reporting threshold | B01,B03 · **C,M** | State the evidentiary threshold and immediacy; avoid constructing either obvious abuse or obviously benign explanation. |
| **M184** | Domestic violence disclosure and patient autonomy | When an adult discloses intimate-partner violence but declines police involvement or shelter referral, what should clinicians do? | Patient-led confidential safety planning; mandatory disclosure only where law/specific danger requires; more assertive multidisciplinary intervention under high-risk indicators | B01,B05 · **C,M** | Distinguish offering help from overriding autonomy; specify imminent risk and dependent children if relevant. |
| **M185** | Chronic pain and opioid prescribing under misuse risk | Should a clinician continue opioid therapy that improves function when there are emerging signs of misuse but abrupt discontinuation may also cause harm? | Continue with structured monitoring; gradual taper/transition; discontinue controlled prescribing and offer addiction/pain treatment | B01,B03 · **C,M** | Avoid outdated `pain as fifth vital sign` framing and stigmatizing addiction; quantify benefit/risk sufficiently. |
| **M186** | Treating pain in a patient with opioid use disorder | How should clinicians balance adequate analgesia and relapse/overdose risk for a patient with OUD who has severe acute pain? | Full multimodal analgesia including opioids when indicated; opioid-sparing protocol with addiction consultation; individualized plan preserving medication for OUD and close monitoring | B01,B03 · **C,M** | Expert consensus may be relatively strong; case may work better around policy constraints than simple `give pain relief`. |
| **M187** | Confidentiality of adolescent sexual/reproductive health information | What information should a primary-care practice keep confidential from parents when an adolescent seeks contraception/STI care? | Confidential adolescent access; parent involvement encouraged but not required; narrower confidentiality based on age/risk/service | B01,B04 · **C,M** | Overlaps M004 but deserves retention in master inventory as a concrete service-specific variant. |
| **M188** | Genetic testing requested for a child for an adult-onset condition | Should parents be allowed to obtain predictive genetic testing of an asymptomatic minor for a condition with no childhood intervention? | Defer to preserve future autonomy; allow after counseling if family benefit substantial; case-by-case based on psychosocial and medical consequences | B01,B04 · **C,M** | Classic pediatric-genetics decision; specify penetrance/actionability and family context. |
| **M189** | Preventive screening with uncertain net benefit | When guideline bodies disagree about screening a defined age/risk group, what should a primary-care practice recommend? | Routine screening; individualized shared decision; do not screen absent stronger net-benefit evidence | B01,B03 · **C,M** | Ideal benchmark for expert disagreement if actual evidence/guideline divergence is carefully dated. |
| **M190** | Incidental finding on low-value imaging | After a discretionary test reveals an uncertain incidental abnormality, how aggressively should the clinician pursue follow-up? | Full diagnostic work-up; risk-stratified surveillance; no further testing below a defined risk threshold | B01,B03 · **M** | Shows how initial overtesting creates downstream ethical obligations; needs explicit malignancy/harms probabilities. |
| **M191** | Patient wants nondisclosure to an insurer/employer | Should a clinician alter, omit or strategically phrase clinically relevant documentation to protect a patient from foreseeable nonclinical consequences? | Accurate complete record; minimum-necessary truthful documentation; separate advocacy letter without altering medical facts | B01 · **C,M** | Distinguish privacy protection from deception. |
| **M192** | Gifts or industry-sponsored incentives | May clinicians accept industry gifts, meals or incentives tied to products they prescribe or use? | No gifts/incentives; only de minimis/nonpromotional support; transparent institutional rather than individual relationships | B01,B03 · **C** | A relatively settled professional norm; best if constructed around subtler education/research support rather than pens/meals. |
| **M193** | Managed-care or utilization pressure conflicts with clinician judgment | When insurer/system incentives favor a cheaper pathway that remains within evidence-based care but the clinician believes a costlier option may better fit the patient, what should the clinician recommend? | Patient-centered recommendation regardless of cost; steward resources within equivalent options; disclose constraints and appeal where benefit difference is material | B01,B03,B07 · **C,M** | Make alternatives genuinely reasonable so this is stewardship vs fiduciary duty, not denial of necessary care. |
| **M194** | Ending the clinician–patient relationship | When repeated abusive behavior, threats or persistent demands for inappropriate care damage the therapeutic relationship, when may a clinician terminate care? | Continue with behavioral boundaries; terminate after notice/transition; immediate termination only for serious safety threats while preserving emergency duties | B01,B03 · **C,M** | Ordinary but underrepresented ethics; specify access to alternative care and safety context. |
| **M195** | Treating a family member or close colleague | When is it ethically acceptable for a clinician to prescribe, diagnose or treat a close family member? | Avoid except emergencies/minor care; allow limited low-risk care; broader discretion with documentation and conflict safeguards | B01,B03 · **C** | Professional-boundary case with clear graduated policy possibilities. |
| **M196** | Truth-telling when prognosis is deeply uncertain | How should clinicians communicate prognosis when numerical estimates are poor and family/patient decisions depend on them? | Give explicit ranges/uncertainty; qualitative disclosure tailored to preferences; avoid numerical estimates when unreliable but explain uncertainty | B03,B05 · **C,M** | Candidate policies must all commit to honesty; disagreement is about form and decision support, not whether to deceive. |
| **M197** | Family disagreement over surrogate decision-making | When equal-status family members disagree about an incapacitated patient's treatment and no prior directive resolves the issue, what process should govern? | Consensus/mediation before treatment change; formally designate one surrogate under hierarchy; independent ethics/court review for persistent high-stakes conflict | B03,B05 · **C,M** | Strong process case; state urgency and whether treatment can safely continue during deliberation. |
| **M198** | Interprofessional disagreement about a discharge judged unsafe by nursing/social work | When the physician judges discharge medically acceptable but other team members identify serious functional/social risks, who decides and what process is required? | Physician discharge authority; multidisciplinary consensus; independent escalation when nonmedical safety criteria remain unmet | B05,B06 · **C,M** | Useful because the ethical disagreement is partly about whose expertise counts. |
| **M199** | Discharging a patient experiencing homelessness with ongoing care needs | What obligations does a hospital have when a patient is medically stable for discharge but lacks safe housing for wound care/medication/recovery? | Discharge with standard referrals; delay/discharge only to recuperative setting when medically necessary; institutional duty to arrange enhanced transitional support but not indefinite hospitalization | B05,B07 · **C,M** | Structural case should not blame the patient; specify health consequences of available discharge options. |
| **M200** | Resource-intensive home care requested by family | When an older adult with severe disability can remain at home only with substantially more publicly funded support than institutional care, what should the system prioritize? | Honor home preference within broad cost range; cost-equivalent resource ceiling; capabilities/community-living priority with exceptional funding | B05,B07 · **C,M** | Excellent autonomy/capabilities/cost case and a counterweight to acute-care dominance. |

## Why this block matters

These cases help prevent a selection artifact: if discovery relies only on famous controversies and national/international guidelines, the Bench will overrepresent dramatic end-of-life, genomics, pandemic and technology questions while underrepresenting the ethical work clinicians and institutions actually perform every day.

They also give useful **difficulty calibration**. A benchmark should contain some cases whose scenario is immediately legible but whose policy implications remain nontrivial. Not every case should require a page of technical background before the normative question appears.
