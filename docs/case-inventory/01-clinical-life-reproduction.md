# Master Case Inventory — M001–M050

**Clinical decisions · pediatrics · death and dying · assisted dying · reproduction**

Inventory IDs are editorial handles, not frozen Bench case IDs. `C/M/F` = canonical/core, methodologically informative, frontier/contemporary.

## Clinical decision-making, consent and professional duties

| ID | Provisional case | Decision to represent | Position families | Anchors / use | Main construction issue |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **M001** | Competent adult refuses blood transfusion | Should clinicians honor a capacitated adult's refusal of a lifesaving transfusion made for religious reasons? | Honor refusal; seek all bloodless alternatives but respect refusal; override only if capacity/voluntariness fails | A02 · **C,M** · v0#1 | Keep capacity and immediacy explicit so the case is autonomy vs beneficence rather than a hidden capacity test. |
| **M002** | Capacity, risk and supported decision-making | When a patient has impaired but fluctuating decision-making ability, should a high-stakes treatment decision be made through supported decision-making, substitute judgment, or clinician override? | Maximize support; surrogate/substitute decision; temporary protective override | A25 · **C,M** · v0#2 | Must state the functional abilities actually impaired; avoid treating diagnosis as incapacity. |
| **M003** | Emergency treatment when wishes are unknown | Should emergency clinicians provide an immediately lifesaving intervention when the unconscious patient's preferences cannot be established in time? | Presume emergency consent; delay briefly for records/surrogate if feasible; withhold if reliable prior refusal evidence exists | A02 · **C** · v0#3 | Specify time window and what records are actually available. |
| **M004** | Adolescent confidentiality | Should a clinic keep an adolescent's sensitive health information confidential from parents when disclosure is not needed to prevent serious imminent harm? | Strong adolescent confidentiality; conditional parental disclosure; shared-decision approach with narrow exceptions | A02 · **C,M** · v0#4 | Jurisdiction and age rules can dominate; best as a jurisdiction-neutral institutional policy with legal options stipulated. |
| **M005** | Family asks clinicians not to disclose a serious diagnosis | Should clinicians disclose the diagnosis directly to a capacitated patient when family members request nondisclosure for cultural or protective reasons? | Direct disclosure; ask patient about desired information/family role first; family-mediated disclosure if patient has delegated it | A02 · **C,M** · v0#5 | Represent relational autonomy without stereotyping cultures. |
| **M006** | Disclosure of a consequential medical error | After a preventable error has caused or may cause harm, what disclosure policy should the institution require? | Full prompt disclosure including known consequences; staged disclosure as facts emerge; disclose material harm with structured apology/remediation | A02 · **C,M** | Distinguish uncertainty about facts from temptation to withhold information. |
| **M007** | Clinician conscientious objection and referral | When a lawful, professionally recognized service conflicts with a clinician's conscience, what must the clinician do? | Provide; decline but timely refer/transfer; decline without facilitation subject to emergency/non-abandonment limits | A02 · **C,M** | Needs a specific service and access context; otherwise it becomes abstract conscience theory. |
| **M008** | Confidentiality versus serious preventable harm to a third party | When confidential patient information identifies a serious foreseeable risk to another person, should clinicians warn or disclose? | Preserve confidentiality; targeted disclosure after threshold; supported patient-mediated disclosure first | A02 · **C,M** | Specify magnitude/imminence and whether the third party is identifiable. |
| **M009** | Requested CPR judged medically non-beneficial | Should a hospital offer CPR when patient/family requests it but the clinical team judges it unable to achieve the agreed physiological goal? | Honor request; allow unilateral non-offer after process; require independent review/transfer before limitation | A02 · **C,M** | Avoid loading the scenario with the contested word `futile`; stipulate the relevant outcome probabilities/goals. |
| **M010** | Family members as interpreters for consequential consent | Should a hospital permit family interpretation for a high-stakes consent discussion when a qualified interpreter is available but the patient prefers family assistance? | Require professional interpreter; allow patient-chosen family interpreter; dual approach with professional verification | A02 · **C** | Keep language access, privacy and relational autonomy distinct. |
| **M011** | Expanded access to an unproven intervention | Should a clinician/company provide an investigational intervention outside a trial to a seriously ill patient who cannot enroll? | Provide under expanded access; prioritize trial enrollment/evidence generation; conditional access with registry/data obligations | A03,A04 · **C,M** | State safety evidence, alternatives and trial eligibility; otherwise one option becomes obviously dominant. |
| **M012** | Referral after unproven commercial stem-cell treatment | Should clinicians assist a patient seeking an unproven commercial stem-cell intervention abroad, and if so how? | Counsel against and do not facilitate; harm-reduction counseling without endorsement; assist only if minimum evidence/oversight threshold met | A11 · **F,M** | Keep it from becoming `is fraud bad?`; use a plausible but uncertain intervention with nontrivial evidence. |
| **M013** | Consent to trainee participation | When should patients be told that students/residents will perform material parts of an examination or procedure? | Explicit opt-in; general team-based consent with specific disclosure for invasive steps; clinician discretion for low-risk participation | A02 · **C** | Define what participation changes and avoid conflating observation with independent procedure performance. |
| **M014** | Pregnant patient's refusal of recommended treatment | Should clinicians honor a capacitated pregnant patient's refusal of an intervention expected to benefit the fetus but carrying burdens for her? | Honor refusal; intensive shared decision without coercion; override only under an exceptional threshold | A02,A09 · **C,M** | State maternal risks, fetal benefit and capacity; do not frame the fetus or pregnant patient as merely instrumental. |
| **M015** | Parental refusal of routine childhood vaccination | When parents refuse a highly effective childhood vaccine, what should the pediatric practice or health system require? | Continue care/counsel; require vaccination for certain settings; dismiss/refuse non-emergency practice enrollment only under defined conditions | A20 · **C,M** | Separate clinical relationship policy from state mandate. |
| **M016** | Parents refuse curative cancer therapy for a child | When parents reject a treatment with a high probability of cure, should clinicians seek state intervention? | Override refusal; intensive negotiation with short delay; respect refusal only below a defined benefit/harm threshold | A02 · **C,M** | Stipulate prognosis with and without treatment so the ethical threshold is visible. |
| **M017** | Last-resort antibiotic for one patient versus antimicrobial stewardship | Should a hospital restrict a reserve antimicrobial that may benefit the present patient in order to preserve effectiveness and reduce resistance? | Individual-patient priority; stewardship restriction; conditional use with infectious-disease review/criteria | A24 · **C,M** · related to v0#41 | Excellent future-person/externality case; must state expected benefit and resistance implications without pretending they are precisely known. |
| **M018** | Patient-requested antibiotics with low expected benefit | Should clinicians prescribe antibiotics when bacterial infection is unlikely but the patient strongly prefers treatment? | Do not prescribe; delayed/back-up prescription; prescribe after shared decision under defined uncertainty | A24 · **C** | Avoid triviality by giving genuine diagnostic uncertainty rather than a clearly viral illness. |
| **M019** | Telemedicine access versus in-person diagnostic safety | When remote care substantially improves access but omits parts of the physical examination, when should a service require in-person assessment? | Remote-first; risk-triggered in-person rule; broad in-person requirement for specified presentations | A02,A28 · **F,M** | Needs a concrete presentation and evidence level; otherwise it is generic technology policy. |
| **M020** | Clinician duty to work during a dangerous outbreak | What level of occupational risk may a health system reasonably require clinicians to accept during an infectious emergency? | Strong duty with reciprocal protections; duty only below a defined risk/protection threshold; voluntary staffing with incentives | A02,A17 · **C,M** | Specify PPE availability, personal vulnerability and reciprocity obligations. |

## Death, dying and assisted dying

| ID | Provisional case | Decision to represent | Position families | Anchors / use | Main construction issue |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **M026** | Withdrawal of life-sustaining treatment over family objection | When treatment no longer offers the patient a realistic path to the agreed goals, may clinicians withdraw life support despite surrogate objection? | Continue at surrogate request; withdraw after fair-process review; seek transfer/time-limited trial before withdrawal | A02 · **C,M** · **LEGACY:withdrawal-life-support** · v0#10 | Replace legacy `persistent vegetative state`/`futility` shortcuts with current diagnostic/prognostic facts and explicit fair process. |
| **M027** | Advance directive versus current contentment in dementia | Should a prior directive refusing life-prolonging treatment control when a person with advanced dementia now appears comfortable and engaged? | Precedent autonomy controls; current experiential interests control; context-sensitive threshold/review | A01,A02 · **C,M** · **LEGACY:dementia-advance-directive** · v0#11 | One of the best cases for genuine nontrivial convergence; distinguish simple antibiotics from highly burdensome treatment if used. |
| **M028** | Brain death and continued somatic support | After death by neurologic criteria has been validly determined, how long should a hospital continue organ support when the family rejects the determination? | Discontinue after reasonable accommodation; continue while transfer/religious accommodation sought; extended support under defined criteria | A01,A16 · **C,M** · v0#12 | Separate the scientific determination from the normative accommodation policy. Jurisdiction can matter greatly. |
| **M029** | Palliative sedation for refractory suffering | When symptoms cannot otherwise be relieved near the end of life, when is deep continuous sedation ethically permissible? | Permit with proportionality/consent safeguards; reserve until imminently dying; prefer lighter/intermittent sedation unless exceptional | A01,A02 · **C,M** · v0#13 | State intention, prognosis and alternatives; avoid collapsing into euthanasia by wording. |
| **M030** | Concurrent disease-directed treatment and hospice support | Should a payer/program allow hospice-level supportive services while a terminally ill patient continues selected disease-directed treatment? | Concurrent care; traditional comfort-only eligibility; time-limited concurrent model based on need/value | A02 · **C,M** · **LEGACY:hospice-transition** · v0#14 | Legacy case was US reimbursement specific; better as a program-design question not `should the patient stop chemotherapy?`. |
| **M031** | Medical aid in dying for terminal illness | Should a health system permit clinician participation in assisted dying for a capacitated adult with terminal illness under safeguards? | Permit under safeguards; prohibit clinician participation; permit with narrower eligibility/mandatory palliative pathway | A01,A02 · **C,M** · **LEGACY:medical-aid-in-dying** · v0#15 | Jurisdiction-neutral institutional policy is preferable; do not make legality the answer. |
| **M032** | Assisted dying for treatment-resistant psychiatric illness | Should eligibility for assisted dying extend to a capacitated adult whose sole underlying condition is a severe, enduring psychiatric disorder? | Equal eligibility with stringent criteria; categorical exclusion; exceptional pathway with prolonged independent review | A25 · **F,M** · v0#16 | Hard prognosis/capacity problem; requires very careful evidence and no claim that irremediability is easily known. |
| **M033** | Advance request for assisted dying after loss of capacity | Should an earlier competent request for assisted dying be actionable after advanced dementia removes contemporaneous decision-making capacity? | Honor qualifying advance request; require contemporaneous consent; narrow activation only with specified observable conditions/review | A25 · **F,M** · v0#17 | Distinct from M027 because the requested act is assisted death rather than refusal of treatment. |
| **M034** | Artificial nutrition and hydration in profound neurologic impairment | Should clinically assisted nutrition/hydration be continued when a patient lacks awareness and prior wishes are uncertain? | Continue as basic care; treat as medical treatment subject to benefit/burden; time-limited trial with surrogate/fair-process review | A01,A02 · **C,M** | Define diagnosis, prognosis and mode of feeding; avoid assumptions about symbolic meaning. |
| **M035** | Voluntary stopping of eating and drinking | How should clinicians respond when a capacitated adult with serious illness chooses to stop eating and drinking to hasten death? | Respect and provide symptom care; require heightened capacity/palliative review; decline active clinical facilitation while continuing ordinary care | A02 · **C,M** | Distinguish respecting refusal from providing assisted death. |
| **M036** | Withholding versus withdrawing the same treatment | Should a policy treat stopping an already-started life-sustaining treatment as ethically different from not starting it when prognosis and goals are otherwise identical? | Treat as equivalent; require stronger process for withdrawal; use time-limited trials with explicit stopping criteria | A01 · **C,M** | Excellent teaching case if embedded in a concrete ICU decision rather than asked abstractly. |
| **M037** | Unilateral limitation of non-beneficial treatment | What fair process should govern a clinician's decision not to offer continued intensive treatment requested by a surrogate? | Surrogate controls; clinician may limit after institutional due process; external review/transfer required | A02 · **C,M** | Use as process design, not a disguised claim that clinicians are always right about benefit. |
| **M038** | Somatic support after brain death during pregnancy | After a pregnant patient is declared dead by neurologic criteria, should somatic support continue to permit fetal development? | Continue if plausible fetal benefit; follow prior wishes/surrogate and discontinue; conditional support under gestational/prognostic thresholds | A16 · **F,M** | Technically fact-sensitive and legally variable; scenario must stipulate gestation, fetal prognosis and prior wishes. |
| **M039** | Organ donation after assisted dying | May organs be recovered from a patient who dies through a lawful assisted-dying process, and what safeguards should separate the two decisions? | Permit with strict separation; prohibit to protect voluntariness/trust; permit only after independent donation consent and no clinician overlap | A18,A19 · **F,M** | Strong interaction of two ethically accepted/contested practices; prevent donation benefit from entering the assisted-dying decision. |
| **M040** | Family objection to brain-death testing | When prerequisites are met but family members object to the apnea test or formal death determination, should clinicians proceed? | Proceed after communication; short accommodation/delay; require alternative ancillary testing or external review | A16 · **C,M** | Different from M028: this concerns whether/how to perform the determination, not support after determination. |

## Reproduction and family formation

| ID | Provisional case | Decision to represent | Position families | Anchors / use | Main construction issue |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **M041** | Publicly funded IVF eligibility | How should a public health system allocate a limited number of funded IVF cycles among eligible patients? | Clinical-prognosis priority; equal access/lottery after minimum criteria; priority incorporating prior children/age/cost-effectiveness | A10 · **C,M** · v0#18 | Choose criteria that are genuinely contested and stipulate resource scarcity. |
| **M042** | Prenatal screening and disability justice | What policy should a prenatal screening program use to offer testing while avoiding pressure that treats disability as a life not worth living? | Universal opt-in with nondirective counseling; routine offer with robust disability information; narrower targeted testing | A09,A10 · **C,M** · v0#19 | Separate access to information from selective abortion; include disability-community perspectives in the public pool. |
| **M043** | Selective reduction in multifetal pregnancy | Under what conditions should a fertility program support fetal reduction after a high-order multiple pregnancy? | Respect pregnant patient's choice; recommend reduction above risk threshold; restrict non-medically indicated reduction | A10 · **C,M** · v0#20 | Maternal/fetal risk data and gestational facts must be explicit. |
| **M044** | Uterus transplantation | Should a transplant program offer uterus transplantation when it can enable gestation but exposes a healthy recipient to major surgery and immunosuppression? | Offer to informed eligible patients; research-only/limited rollout; do not offer where safer family-building alternatives exist | A10,A18 · **F,M** · v0#21 | Compare reproductive benefit without presuming genetic/gestational parenthood is either trivial or overriding. |
| **M045** | Posthumous use of stored gametes or embryos | May a clinic use stored reproductive material after one genetic contributor has died? | Require explicit written consent; allow surviving partner under strong evidence of wishes; prohibit absent contemporaneous consent | A10 · **C,M** · v0#22 | ASRM provides unusually clear source architecture; distinguish retrieval from later reproductive use. |
| **M046** | Institutional abortion-service policy | Within a jurisdiction where several policies are legally available, what abortion services should a hospital or health system provide? | Broad access within clinical/legal limits; narrower gestational/indication policy; referral-centered policy for services the institution declines | A01,A09 · **C,M** | Highly salient but must be constructed without importing partisan labels; specify legal choice set and clinical setting. |
| **M047** | Conscientious objection in abortion care | When clinicians object to providing abortion, what referral, disclosure and emergency duties should the institution require? | Provide despite objection; allow objection with timely effective referral; allow nonparticipation with emergency/non-abandonment boundaries | A09 · **C,M** | Keep distinct from M007 by grounding in reproductive access and time sensitivity. |
| **M048** | Disposition of embryos after separation | When former partners disagree about previously created embryos, what default policy should a clinic follow if prior consent forms are ambiguous? | Require contemporaneous mutual consent for use; enforce prior written directive; favor avoiding genetic parenthood without current consent | A10 · **C,M** | Legal doctrine varies; best framed as prospective clinic consent policy rather than adjudicating a real divorce. |
| **M049** | Unclaimed cryopreserved embryos | After prolonged loss of contact, when may a clinic stop storing embryos and what dispositions are permissible? | Indefinite storage unless explicit instruction; disposal after defined notice/process; permit research/donation only with prior specific authorization | A10 · **C,M** | Strong policy-design case with clear ASRM anchor; specify contact attempts and prior consent. |
| **M050** | Donor anonymity and donor-conceived persons' access to identity | What information should gamete/embryo donation programs promise to release to donor-conceived adults? | Open identity by default; non-identifying information with mutual-consent contact; preserve donor anonymity where originally promised | A10 · **C,M** | Technology already undermines practical anonymity; distinguish disclosure of donor conception from disclosure of donor identity. |

## Notes for the next research pass

M021, M027, M031 and M036 are especially useful for comparing canonical teaching value with executable clarity. M024 is deliberately retained despite high representational risk: the master inventory should not erase difficult public controversies, but public release requires stronger dated evidence and subject-matter review than most cases.

The end-of-life cluster remains intentionally broad here. The master inventory is not a portfolio and therefore should not be thinned merely to correct the skew of the old nine-case example set. Portfolio balance is a property of a release, not a reason to delete distinct research questions.
