Source-grounded research infrastructure for computational bioethics

A bioethics case, its policy options, and the evidence behind them.

Each Bioethics Bench case asks a concrete decision question. The record includes a scenario, several policy options, the sources and reasoning used to construct those options, explicit assumptions used for the benchmark, a concise and detailed version of the case, and a versioned review history. Researchers can then apply a defined comparison task and record which model or human evaluator produced the result.

How the Bench works

Start with the case. Then specify the comparison. Then record the evaluator.

Keeping these three pieces separate makes it possible to reuse the same case with different computational methods, models, human evaluators, prompts, or experimental conditions while preserving exactly what was held fixed.

Policy options

Each case keeps the policy options that its evidence can support.

The 34 executable families contain different numbers of Public, Expert and Framework options. They occupy eight candidate-set geometries. Eight families have a 2×2×2 structure; the remaining 26 have asymmetric sets. Under sacre-qccs-v1, the current corpus generates 428 cross-source comparisons for one representation and 856 across the matched concise and detailed records.

Where an option comes from

Each candidate records its policy text and provenance. The record distinguishes policies stated closely by a source, policies inferred from source evidence, and policies constructed from a named normative framework.

Two versions of the same case

The concise and detailed records keep the same decision question, policy options and benchmark assumptions while changing the amount of scenario detail. This allows representation detail to be studied directly.

Assumptions are visible

Some decisions require facts to be fixed before the policy options can be compared. Those benchmark stipulations are written into the scenario so a reader can see exactly what the analysis assumes.

Evidence so far

Two results are currently available.

First, the full 68-record corpus has been checked to confirm that the explicit Bench task adapter reproduces SACRE's executable task semantics. Second, a smaller developmental tranche has been used to study model execution, representation and repeat behavior.

Sources and policy construction

A citation should show how it supports the policy option.

For a sourced policy, the important questions are concrete: What source is being cited? What does it actually say? Which part of that source matters for this case? How did the Bench turn that evidence into the policy wording shown in the record? The provenance fields preserve those steps so they can be reviewed and revised.

What the provenance records

Source type, the claim taken from the source, the role that claim plays, the direction and scope of support, the Bench's policy bridge, and the identity of the review performed on that relationship.

Current review coverage

Canonical source locators are now complete across the 68 Full Corpus records. The current corpus contains 242 unique citations; 130 carry PMIDs and all 130 resolve consistently against PubMed. Independent corpus-wide human source-to-policy review remains a later quality and release step.

Reproducible research

A result should say exactly which case, task and evaluator produced it.

For a computational or human study, report the record ID, version and content hash; the task protocol used to compare the policies; and the evaluator, prompt and configuration used for that run. Those three pieces are enough to identify the object studied and the procedure that produced the result.
