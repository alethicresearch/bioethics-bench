Bioethics Bench is a source-grounded library of practical ethical decisions designed so the same case can be studied by people, AI systems, and computational ethics methods. Each case states the decision, the relevant context, serious policy options, and where those options came from.

The basic idea

Make the ethical problem reusable without hiding how it was constructed.

Bioethics research often discusses rich cases in prose, while computational evaluation needs stable inputs. The Bench connects those worlds: it turns bounded practical decisions into inspectable research objects without pretending that ethical judgment is reducible to a single answer key.

01

A bounded decision

Who has to decide what? A case is framed around an actual policy or practice choice rather than a broad topic such as “autonomy” or “AI ethics.”

02

Grounded policy options

Public or affected-community evidence, professional guidance, and ethical frameworks are used to construct serious policy alternatives with visible provenance.

03

A reproducible object

Versioning, hashes, sources, assumptions, and controlled case wording make it possible to compare evaluators or rerun a study without silently changing the case.

A stronger completion standard

Good sources are necessary. They are not enough.

A computational ethics case also needs a meaningful policy space. Six well-cited sentences can still be a weak SACRE example if they are minor variations on the same institutional rule.

Source diversity ≠ policy diversity. Genuine agreement across public, expert, and ethical sources should be preserved. But when a case contains several serious operative alternatives, the construction process should not compress them into near-paraphrases simply to make the provenance tidy.
Different actionspermit, prohibit, require, fund, withdraw, disclose, defer
Different authoritypatient, family, clinician, court, regulator, independent review
Different thresholdswhen a rule applies, escalates, or can be overridden
Different scopeuniversal, targeted, staged, case-by-case, high-risk settings only
Different allocation rulesneed, benefit, equality, priority, lottery, instrumental value
Different proceduresconsultation, appeal, reassessment, shared decision-making, safeguards

Completion work now includes a policy-space audit. A case may be source-grounded and research-complete yet still be classified as a poor demonstration case. We do not manufacture disagreement to make a prettier matrix; we choose demonstration cases whose real policy alternatives make the method worth seeing.

From Bench to SACRE

The resource and the method stay separate.

The Bench supplies the represented decision and policy options. SACRE is one method that can operate on those objects. Keeping those layers distinct lets the same cases support other models, human studies, or future methods.

CASE

Represent the decision

Scenario, decision question, assumptions, sources, and policy options are frozen as a versioned record.

COMPARE

Measure relationships

SACRE compares policy options across normative sources using the declared QCCS procedure.

STRUCTURE

Build a coherence profile

Pairwise relations are aggregated into an inspectable ranking over the represented candidates.

ITERATE

Test revision

RE-Iteration can revise selected positions and rerun the analysis while preserving the lineage of what changed.

What the completed resource enables

One case library, many empirical questions.

The value of a stable case resource is not a single benchmark score. It is the ability to hold the decision fixed while varying the evaluator, representation, task, or revision process.

Repeated model runsMeasure stability when the same model evaluates the same frozen case repeatedly.
Model comparisonCompare providers or model versions on exactly the same policy relationships.
Human comparisonField matched questions to human evaluators and study human-human or human-model correspondence.
Representation effectsCompare concise versus detailed versions while holding the underlying decision and policy options fixed.
Robustness studiesTest controlled perturbations, pair order, near-ties, or other changes without rebuilding the case from scratch.
Reflective revisionStudy whether directed revisions improve coherence, preserve the original commitment, or change the provisional policy.
