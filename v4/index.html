A useful ethics benchmark should preserve the structure of a normative problem: the Scenario, the serious Policy positions around it, and enough provenance to see where those positions came from.

Bioethics Bench · computational bioethics research resource

Inside a Case

Scenarios, candidates, and evidence

Each case starts with a bounded bioethical Scenario and develops a richer set of serious Policy positions around it. Source grounding is shown rather than assumed.

01 · Scenario

A stable normative problem

Concise and detailed versions preserve the same load-bearing facts while varying the amount of context available to the evaluator.

02 · Candidate Universe

A richer field of Policy positions

The Bench can preserve sourced positions, framework-derived positions, and serious constructed comparators without pretending they have identical evidential status.

03 · Provenance

Source status stays visible

Candidates with defensible external grounding are marked ✓ Source. Detailed provenance remains available for research and review.

What It Provides

A shared infrastructure for normative research

The Bench is designed to make bioethical reasoning reusable, comparable, and inspectable without reducing ethics to a single answer key.

📚

Large-Scale Corpus

A planned 200-case scholarly resource spanning clinical, research, public-health, technology, animal, and global-health ethics.

🧭

Rich Candidate Spaces

Cases preserve important Policy alternatives rather than compressing every debate into a small fixed geometry.

✓

Visible Source Grounding

Source-backed candidates are marked clearly; inferred and constructed positions remain distinguishable in detailed provenance.

↔

Controlled Representations

Concise and detailed forms support representation-sensitivity studies while preserving the same normative object.

⚙

Reusable Analysis Sets

Different studies can select source-grounded, expanded, matched, or demonstration sets from the same researched case.

◎

Human & Model Comparison

The resource supports repeated computational runs, human comparison, robustness analysis, and future empirical validation.

Example

Age as an organ-allocation criterion

A current public case shows how one Scenario can contain several distinct positions across public preferences, professional policy, and Ethical Frameworks.

Scenario
Two transplant candidates have similar urgency and expected graft benefit but differ substantially in age. The allocation system must decide what role, if any, chronological age should play.
Domain
Allocation & Transplantation
Public
✓ Source
Use younger age only as a limited tie-breaker when candidates are otherwise closely matched.
Public
✓ Source
Do not give chronological age independent weight; use urgency, expected benefit, and other declared criteria.
Expert
✓ Source
Use age only when it contributes to a validated estimate of medically relevant outcomes.
Expert
✓ Source
When clinical criteria leave candidates tied, use a transparent neutral tie-break such as lottery.
Framework
✓ Source
Allow a modest life-cycle tie-break because a younger person has had less opportunity to pass through life's stages.
Framework
✓ Source
Give persons equal moral status regardless of age; priority should track relevant need or benefit instead.
